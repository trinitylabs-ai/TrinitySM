## Problem
IMO 2026 Problem 1. There are 2026 integers greater than 1 written on a blackboard, not necessarily different. In one move, Confucius chooses two integers m > 1 and n > 1 from different positions on the blackboard and replaces them by gcd(m,n) and lcm(m,n)/gcd(m,n). He continues making moves for as long as this is possible. (1) Prove that, regardless of Confucius's choices, after finitely many moves exactly one integer M on the blackboard is greater than 1. (2) Prove that the value of M does not depend on Confucius's choices. Here gcd(x,y) denotes the greatest common divisor of positive integers x,y, and lcm(x,y) denotes their least common multiple.

## Candidate proof
To solve the problem, we analyze the operation by looking at the prime valuations of the integers on the blackboard.

### 1. Analysis of the Operation
Let the integers on the blackboard be $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_{2026}$. For any prime $p$, let $v_p(n)$ denote the exponent of $p$ in the prime factorization of $n$.
When Confucius chooses two integers $m, n > 1$ and replaces them by $g = \gcd(m, n)$ and $l = \text{lcm}(m, n)/\gcd(m, n)$, the new valuations for any prime $p$ are:
$$v_p(g) = \min(v_p(m), v_p(n)), \quad v_p(l) = v_p(\text{lcm}(m, n)) - v_p(\gcd(m, n)) = \max(v_p(m), v_p(n)) - \min(v_p(m), v_p(n)) = |v_p(m) - v_p(n)|$$
Let $a = v_p(m)$ and $b = v_p(n)$. The pair of valuations $(a, b)$ is replaced by $(\min(a, b), |a - b|)$.

### 2. Termination of the Process
Define a potential function $S$ as the sum of all prime valuations of all integers on the blackboard:
$$S = \sum_p \sum_{i=1}^{2026} v_p(x_i)$$
In each move on $m, n$, the change in $S$ for a specific prime $p$ is:
$$\Delta S_p = (\min(a, b) + |a - b|) - (a + b) = \max(a, b) - (a + b) = -\min(a, b)$$
The total change in $S$ is $\Delta S = \sum_p -\min(v_p(m), v_p(n)) = -\sum_p v_p(\gcd(m, n)) = -\Omega(\gcd(m, n))$, where $\Omega(k)$ is the total number of prime factors of $k$.
- If $\gcd(m, n) > 1$, then $\Omega(\gcd(m, n)) \ge 1$, so $S$ strictly decreases.
- If $\gcd(m, n) = 1$, then $S$ remains constant. However, the operation replaces $m, n > 1$ with $g = 1$ and $l = mn > 1$. This reduces the number of integers greater than 1 on the blackboard by exactly one.

Since $S \ge 0$ and the number of integers is finite, the process must terminate. Termination occurs when no two integers $m, n > 1$ can be chosen, meaning at most one integer $M$ on the blackboard is greater than 1.

### 3. Invariance of $M$
For a fixed prime $p$, let the valuations be $\{v_{p,1}, v_{p,2}, \dots, v_{p,2026}\}$. The operation transforms a pair $(a, b)$ into $(\min(a, b), |a-b|)$. 
From the property of the Euclidean algorithm, $\gcd(a, b) = \gcd(a, b-a)$ for $b \ge a$. Since $\min(a, b)$ and $|a-b|$ are exactly $a$ and $b-a$ (assuming $b \ge a$), we have:
$$\gcd(\min(a, b), |a - b|) = \gcd(a, b)$$
Thus, for each prime $p$, the greatest common divisor of the valuations $g_p = \gcd(v_{p,1}, v_{p,2}, \dots, v_{p,2026})$ is invariant under the operation.
When the process terminates, only one integer $M$ is greater than 1. For any prime $p$, all other valuations $v_{p,i}$ are 0. The invariant $g_p$ then satisfies:
$$g_p = \gcd(v_p(M), 0, \dots, 0) = v_p(M)$$
The final integer $M$ is therefore:
$$M = \prod_p p^{g_p} = \prod_p p^{\gcd(v_{p,1}, v_{p,2}, \dots, v_{p,2026})}$$
Since the original integers were all greater than 1, for any $x_i$ there is some prime $p$ such that $v_p(x_i) \ge 1$, ensuring $g_p \ge 1$ for that $p$, so $M > 1$. This value depends only on the initial integers.

### Local critic
The solution attempts to analyze the operation using prime valuations and a potential function. However, there are several errors in the reasoning.

First, the potential function \(S\) is defined as the sum of all prime valuations. The change in \(S\) for a specific prime \(p\) is given as \(\Delta S_p = -\min(v_p(m), v_p(n))\). This is incorrect because the operation replaces two numbers with two new numbers, and the change in \(S\) should account for the sum over all numbers and all primes. The calculation for \(\Delta S_p\) is flawed, and the conclusion that \(S\) strictly decreases when \(\gcd(m,n) > 1\) is not necessarily true because the operation affects multiple primes simultaneously. Moreover, the case where \(\gcd(m,n) = 1\) is handled incorrectly: the operation replaces \(m, n > 1\) with \(g = 1\) and \(l = mn > 1\), but the potential function change is not properly considered. The argument that the process terminates because \(S\) decreases and the number of integers is finite is invalid due to the incorrect definition and change of \(S\).

Second, the invariance argument for the final \(M\) is flawed. The solution claims that for each prime \(p\), the gcd of the valuations is invariant, but the operation does not preserve the multiset of valuations for each prime. The transformation of a pair \((a, b)\) into \((\min(a, b), |a-b|)\) is not a standard Euclidean algorithm step, and the invariant \(g_p = \gcd(v_{p,1}, v_{p,2}, \dots, v_{p,2026})\) is not maintained. In fact, the operation can change the multiset of valuations for each prime independently, and the final \(M\) is not necessarily the product of primes raised to the gcd of their initial valuations. The solution incorrectly assumes that the operation preserves the gcd for each prime, but this is not the case.

The errors in the potential function and the invariance argument are both substantial and require new mathematics to fix. Therefore, the score is capped at 3 due to these fallacies.
