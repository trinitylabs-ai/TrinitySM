INDICTMENT

The claimed classification is not rigorously established. The definition of W is ambiguous/circular as used, the theta = 90 degrees case is asserted without proof, and the converse overlooks angles created at the split vertex.

ROUND 1

Inquisitor: Conditions (i) and (ii) are written conjunctively, contradicting the base case that a theta-angle alone puts T in W. The later “inductive assumption” for W has no stated finite-horizon parameter.

Architect: The converse needs a well-founded induction on forced remaining moves; otherwise recursive W-membership does not itself certify a finite win.

Pre-mortem: An unqualified recursive closure can be self-supporting along an indefinitely continuing branch, so it is not automatically the finite-winning set.

Defender: The opening sentence intends W to mean actual finite-winning positions.

Chief Grader: That charitable interpretation repairs wording, not the missing backward induction. This is a fallacy.

ROUND 2

Inquisitor: For n = 2, theta = 90 degrees, an acute triangle such as 70-60-50 has no angle C at least theta. The stated interval-length argument therefore does not apply, yet the proof says “in any case.” Also, angle APC ranges over (B, B+C), not (A, A+C).

Architect: One advertised case is unproved, and the cut-angle parameter is misidentified.

Pre-mortem: In a triangle with A = 40, B = 60, C = 80, angle APC ranges from 60 to 140 degrees, not from 40 to 120 degrees; selecting a multiple from the stated interval may select an unavailable angle.

Defender: Relabeling the P-angle fixes the interval, and a separate right-angle/acute case could handle n = 2.

Chief Grader: The relabeling is a slip, but the n = 2 argument is new and absent from the submission. This is an incomplete essential case.

ROUND 3

Inquisitor: In the nonintegral case, the proof says the two S-angles in the children must be phi and 180-phi. It ignores the two angles x and C-x produced by splitting the chosen vertex.

Architect: Mixed possibilities must be excluded. For example, one child could use x = k theta and the other A+x = m theta; they are not supplementary P-angles.

Pre-mortem: Only the omitted calculation A = (m-k) theta rules out that mixed branch by forcing an original S-angle. Without checking it and the analogous cases, “This requires” does not follow.

Defender: Angle bookkeeping can eliminate all such cases.

Chief Grader: That bookkeeping is essential new case analysis, not mathematics already written. The converse remains unproved. The statement that theta = 120 degrees divides 180 degrees evenly is an additional removable slip.

ERROR CLASSIFICATION

Fallacies/incompleteness: unsupported finite-winning induction; missing n = 2 proof; omitted split-vertex and mixed-angle cases in the converse.

Slips: incorrect P-angle interval/name; erroneous theta = 120 degrees remark; ambiguous “and” in the W definition.

VERDICT

There is a substantial usable idea for the integral cases n at least 3, but central parts of both sufficiency and necessity require added arguments. The fallacy cap applies.
