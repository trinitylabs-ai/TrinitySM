To prove that $C$ is the midpoint of the segment $XY$, we use a coordinate geometry approach.

Let $C$ be the origin $(0,0)$ and let the line $BC$ be the $x$-axis. Let the coordinates of the vertices be $C(0,0)$, $B(a, 0)$, and $A(b \cos C, b \sin C)$. Since the triangle is acute, the coordinates are well-defined and $C$ is an acute angle.

**1. Coordinates of $F$ and $H$**
$F$ is the foot of the altitude from $C$ to $AB$. In $\triangle BCF$, $\angle BFC = 90^\circ$, so $\angle FCB = 90^\circ - B$. The length $CF$ is given by $CF = a \sin B$. Thus, the coordinates of $F$ are:
\[ F = (a \sin B \cos(90^\circ - B), a \sin B \sin(90^\circ - B)) = (a \sin^2 B, a \sin B \cos B) \]
$H$ is the orthocenter of $\triangle ABC$. The altitude from $A$ to $BC$ is the vertical line $x = b \cos C$. The altitude from $C$ to $AB$ is the line $CF$, which passes through the origin and $F$, so its slope is $\frac{a \sin B \cos B}{a \sin^2 B} = \cot B$. The equation of line $CF$ is $y = (\cot B)x$. The intersection of these two lines gives $H$:
\[ H = (b \cos C, b \cos C \cot B) \]

**2. Coordinates of $P$**
$P$ is the reflection of $H$ across the line $BC$ (the $x$-axis). Thus, the coordinates of $P$ are:
\[ P = (b \cos C, -b \cos C \cot B) \]

**3. The Center of the Circumcircle of $\triangle AFP$**
Let $O' = (x_0, y_0)$ be the center of the circumcircle $\Gamma$ of $\triangle AFP$. $O'$ is the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of $AP$ and $AF$.
Since $A = (b \cos C, b \sin C)$ and $P = (b \cos C, -b \cos C \cot B)$, the segment $AP$ is vertical. Its perpendicular bisector is the horizontal line:
\[ y_0 = \frac{b \sin C - b \cos C \cot B}{2} \]
The perpendicular bisector of $AF$ passes through the midpoint $M_{AF} = \left(\frac{b \cos C + a \sin^2 B}{2}, \frac{b \sin C + a \sin B \cos B}{2}\right)$ and has a slope $m_{\perp}$ perpendicular to the slope of $AF$. The slope of $AF$ is $m_{AF} = \frac{b \sin C - a \sin B \cos B}{b \cos C - a \sin^2 B}$, so:
\[ m_{\perp} = -\frac{b \cos C - a \sin^2 B}{b \sin C - a \sin B \cos B} \]
The equation of the perpendicular bisector of $AF$ is:
\[ y - \frac{b \sin C + a \sin B \cos B}{2} = -\frac{b \cos C - a \sin^2 B}{b \sin C - a \sin B \cos B} \left(x - \frac{b \cos C + a \sin^2 B}{2}\right) \]

**4. Midpoint of $XY$**
The points $X$ and $Y$ are the intersections of $\Gamma$ with the line $BC$ (the $x$-axis). The midpoint of the chord $XY$ is the projection of the center $O'$ onto the $x$-axis, which is $(x_0, 0)$. To prove that $C(0,0)$ is the midpoint of $XY$, we must show that $x_0 = 0$.
Substituting $x = 0$ and $y = y_0$ into the perpendicular bisector equation:
\[ \frac{b \sin C - b \cos C \cot B - (b \sin C + a \sin B \cos B)}{2} = \frac{b \cos C - a \sin^2 B}{b \sin C - a \sin B \cos B} \cdot \frac{b \cos C + a \sin^2 B}{2} \]
Simplifying the left side:
\[ \frac{-b \cos C \cot B - a \sin B \cos B}{2} = -\frac{\cos B (b \cos C + a \sin^2 B)}{2 \sin B} \]
Dividing both sides by $\frac{b \cos C + a \sin^2 B}{2}$ (which is non-zero for an acute triangle):
\[ -\frac{\cos B}{\sin B} = \frac{b \cos C - a \sin^2 B}{b \sin C - a \sin B \cos B} \]
Cross-multiplying:
\[ -\cos B (b \sin C - a \sin B \cos B) = \sin B (b \cos C - a \sin^2 B) \]
\[ -b \sin C \cos B + a \sin B \cos^2 B = b \cos C \sin B - a \sin^3 B \]
\[ a \sin B (\cos^2 B + \sin^2 B) = b (\sin B \cos C + \cos B \sin C) \]
\[ a \sin B = b \sin(B + C) = b \sin A \]
This is the Law of Sines, which is always true. Thus, $x_0 = 0$.

Since $O' = (0, y_0)$, its projection onto the line $BC$ is $C(0,0)$. Therefore, $C$ is the midpoint of the chord $XY$.

\(\square\)
