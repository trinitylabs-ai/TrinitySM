To prove that we can choose two diagonals of a convex $(18n+2)$-gon $P$ such that the three parts of $P$ each contain at least $3n$ and at most $9n$ triangles, we represent the triangulation using its dual graph $G$.

In the dual graph $G$, each vertex corresponds to a triangle in $P$, and an edge exists between two vertices if the corresponding triangles share a diagonal. Because $P$ is a convex polygon and is divided into $18n$ triangles by $18n-1$ diagonals, $G$ is a tree with $N = 18n$ vertices. Furthermore, since each triangle has only three sides, each vertex in $G$ has a maximum degree of 3. Removing an edge in $G$ corresponds to choosing a diagonal in $P$ that splits the polygon into two parts. Removing two edges in $G$ splits the tree into three components, which corresponds to dividing $P$ into three parts using two non-intersecting diagonals.

We seek two edges in $G$ whose removal splits the tree into three components $S_1, S_2, S_3$ such that $3n \le |S_i| \le 9n$ for $i=1, 2, 3$.

Let $v$ be a centroid of $G$. A centroid is a vertex such that every component of $G \setminus \{v\}$ has size at most $N/2$. Let the components of $G \setminus \{v\}$ be $T_1, T_2, \dots, T_k$ with sizes $s_1, s_2, \dots, s_k$. Since the maximum degree of $G$ is 3, $k \le 3$. We have $s_i \le 9n$ for all $i$ and $\sum_{i=1}^k s_i = N-1 = 18n-1$.

**Case 1: All $s_i \ge 3n$.**
If $k=3$, we have $s_1, s_2, s_3 \ge 3n$. We choose the two edges incident to $v$ leading to $T_1$ and $T_2$. The sizes of the three resulting components are $S_1 = s_1$, $S_2 = s_2$, and $S_3 = s_3 + 1$. Since $s_i \le 9n$, we have $S_1, S_2 \le 9n$. If $s_3 \le 9n-1$, then $S_3 \le 9n$ and we are done. If $s_3 = 9n$, then $s_1 + s_2 = 9n-1$. In this case, we choose the edges incident to $v$ leading to $T_1$ and $T_3$. The sizes are $S_1 = s_1$, $S_2 = s_3 = 9n$, and $S_3 = s_2 + 1$. Since $s_2 \ge 3n$, $S_3 \ge 3n+1$, and since $s_2 \le 9n-1-s_1 \le 6n-1$, $S_3 \le 6n$. Thus, all components are within $[3n, 9n]$.

**Case 2: At least one $s_i < 3n$.**
Assume $s_1 < 3n$. Then $s_2 + s_3 = 18n - 1 - s_1 > 15n - 1$. Since $s_2, s_3 \le 9n$, it follows that $s_2 \ge 6n-1$ and $s_3 \ge 6n-1$.
Consider the subtree $T' = G \setminus T_3$, which contains $T_1, T_2$, and the vertex $v$. The size of $T'$ is $S = s_1 + s_2 + 1 = 18n - s_3$. Given $6n-1 \le s_3 \le 9n$, we have $9n \le S \le 12n+1$.
Let $v'$ be a centroid of $T'$. The components $C_1, \dots, C_m$ of $T' \setminus \{v'\}$ satisfy $|C_j| \le S/2$ and $m \le 3$. Thus, at least one component $C_j$ must satisfy $|C_j| \ge \frac{S-1}{3}$. Let $x = |C_j|$.
We split $G$ using the edge in $T'$ that defines $C_j$ and the edge connecting $T'$ to $T_3$. The sizes of the three parts are $S_1 = x$, $S_2 = S - x$, and $S_3 = s_3$.
1. For $S_3$: Since $6n-1 \le s_3 \le 9n$, $S_3 \ge 3n$ for all $n \ge 1$.
2. For $S_1$: $x \ge \frac{S-1}{3} \ge \frac{9n-1}{3} = 3n - \frac{1}{3} \implies x \ge 3n$. Also $x \le \frac{S}{2} \le \frac{12n+1}{2} = 6n + 0.5 \le 9n$.
3. For $S_2$: $S-x \ge S - \frac{S}{2} = \frac{S}{2} \ge \frac{9n}{2} = 4.5n \ge 3n$. Also $S-x \le S - \frac{S-1}{3} = \frac{2S+1}{3} \le \frac{2(12n+1)+1}{3} = \frac{24n+3}{3} = 8n+1 \le 9n$.

In both cases, we have found two diagonals such that the three parts each contain between $3n$ and $9n$ triangles.

\(\square\)
