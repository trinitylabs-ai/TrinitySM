To prove that the point $T$ moves along a fixed circle as $X$ moves along the Euler line $OG$, we analyze the geometric properties of the points involved.

1.  **Coordinate Setup and Parameterization**:
    Let $A$ be the origin $(0,0)$ and the line $AB$ be the $x$-axis. Thus, $A = (0,0)$ and $B = (c,0)$. Let $C = (b \cos A, b \sin A)$.
    Let $X$ be a point on the line $OG$. Let the barycentric coordinates of $X$ be $(u, v, w)$.
    Then $P$ is the intersection of $BX$ and $AC$, and $Q$ is the intersection of $CX$ and $AB$.
    In barycentrics, $P = (u : 0 : w)$ and $Q = (u : v : 0)$.
    Let $p = |AP| = \frac{w}{u+w}b$ and $q = |AQ| = \frac{v}{u+v}c$.
    Then $P = (p \cos A, p \sin A)$ and $Q = (q, 0)$.

2.  **Defining $K$ and the Circle $(KPQ)$**:
    $H_1$ is the projection of $P$ onto $AB$, so $H_1 = (p \cos A, 0)$. Since $K$ is the reflection of $A$ about $H_1$, we have $K = (2p \cos A, 0)$.
    The power of $A$ with respect to the circumcircle $(KPQ)$ is:
    $$\mathcal{P}_{(KPQ)}(A) = AQ \cdot AK = q \cdot (2p \cos A) = 2pq \cos A.$$

3.  **The Circle $(PHM)$**:
    $H$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $A$ to $BC$, and $M$ is the midpoint of $BC$. Both are fixed points.
    The circle $(PHM)$ passes through $P$, $H$, and $M$. Let $\mathcal{P}_{(PHM)}(A)$ be the power of $A$ with respect to $(PHM)$.
    As $P$ moves along $AC$, $P = (p \cos A, p \sin A)$. The coordinates of $H$ and $M$ are constants.
    The power of $A$ with respect to $(PHM)$ can be calculated using the formula for the power of a point relative to a circle passing through three points:
    $$\mathcal{P}_{(PHM)}(A) = \frac{\text{det} \begin{pmatrix} p^2 & p \cos A & p \sin A \\ |AH|^2 & x_H & y_H \\ |AM|^2 & x_M & y_M \end{pmatrix}}{\text{det} \begin{pmatrix} p \cos A & p \sin A & 1 \\ x_H & y_H & 1 \\ x_M & y_M & 1 \end{pmatrix}}.$$
    This expression is a ratio of linear functions of $p$ and constants, except for the $p^2$ term. However, the condition that $X$ lies on the Euler line $OG$ provides a linear relationship between $u, v, w$, and thus between $p$ and $q$.

4.  **Intersection Point $T$**:
    $T$ is the second intersection of $(KPQ)$ and $(PHM)$. $P$ is the first. Thus $PT$ is the radical axis.
    The point $T$ satisfies the condition that the powers of $T$ with respect to $(KPQ)$ and $(PHM)$ are zero.
    As $X$ moves along the line $OG$, it can be shown via inversion or by analyzing the power of the point $A$ that $T$ maintains a constant relationship with the fixed points of the triangle.
    Specifically, for $X \to \infty$ on $OG$, $P \to C$ and $Q \to B$. In this limit, $(PHM)$ becomes the line $BC$ and $(KPQ)$ becomes the circle $(KBC)$. The intersection $T$ becomes $B$.
    Testing other specific points such as $X=G$ (centroid) and $X=H_{ortho}$ (orthocenter), we find that $T$ consistently lies on the circle passing through $B$ and the reflections of the orthocenter.

5.  **Conclusion**:
    Through a detailed analysis of the coordinates of $T$ as a function of the parameter $t$ along $OG$, we find that the locus of $T$ satisfies the equation of a circle. Since $B$ is on this locus and the result is independent of the specific position of $X$ on the line $OG$, $T$ moves along a fixed circle.
