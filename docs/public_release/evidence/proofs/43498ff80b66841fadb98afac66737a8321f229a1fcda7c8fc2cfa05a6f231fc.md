To prove that line $IP$ is parallel to line $XY$, we first establish the geometric properties of the points $X$ and $Y$.

1. **Projections of the Incenter**: 
Let $I$ be the incenter of $\triangle ABC$. Line $BI$ and $CI$ are angle bisectors, intersecting $AC$ at $E$ and $AB$ at $F$. By the Angle Bisector Theorem, $AE = \frac{bc}{a+c}$ and $AF = \frac{bc}{a+b}$, where $a, b, c$ are the side lengths $BC, AC, AB$. Points $U$ and $V$ are on $AB$ and $AC$ such that $AU = AE$ and $AV = AF$. 
The point $L$ is the intersection of the line $BC$ and the line through $I$ perpendicular to $AI$. The circumcircle of $\triangle ILC$ passes through $I, L, C$. Since $X$ is the intersection of this circle and the line $LU$, we have $\angle LXI = \angle LCI = \angle BCI = C/2$.
Similarly, since $Y$ is on the circumcircle of $\triangle ILB$ and on the line $LV$, we have $\angle LYI = \angle LBI = \angle ABI = B/2$.

2. **The Circle with Diameter $LI$**:
In $\triangle ABC$, let $\angle A = 2\alpha$. Since $LI \perp AI$ and $AI$ is the angle bisector of $\angle A$, the angle $\angle ALI = 90^\circ - \alpha$. 
Consider the projection of $I$ onto the line $LU$, denoted $X'$. Then $\angle LX'I = 90^\circ$. In the circle $\omega_{ILC}$, the property $\angle LXI = \angle LCI$ implies that $X$ is the point on $LU$ such that $LX = LI \cos \angle ILX$. The projection $X'$ also satisfies $LX' = LI \cos \angle ILX$. Thus, $X = X'$. Similarly, $Y$ is the projection of $I$ onto the line $LV$.
Consequently, $X$ and $Y$ both lie on the circle $\omega$ with diameter $LI$.

3. **Parallelism of $IP$ and $XY$**:
Let $L$ be the origin $(0,0)$ and let the line $LI$ be the $y$-axis, so $I = (0, d)$. The circle $\omega$ is $x^2 + y^2 - dy = 0$. Let the lines $LU$ and $LV$ make angles $\theta_X$ and $\theta_Y$ with $LI$. The coordinates of $X$ and $Y$ are:
$X = (d \sin \theta_X \cos \theta_X, d \cos^2 \theta_X)$ and $Y = (d \sin \theta_Y \cos \theta_Y, d \cos^2 \theta_Y)$.
The slope of line $XY$ is:
\[ m_{XY} = \frac{\cos^2 \theta_Y - \cos^2 \theta_X}{\sin \theta_Y \cos \theta_Y - \sin \theta_X \cos \theta_X} = \frac{\cos 2\theta_Y - \cos 2\theta_X}{\sin 2\theta_Y - \sin 2\theta_X} = -\cot(\theta_X + \theta_Y). \]
Point $P$ is the intersection of lines $YB$ and $XC$. Given that $L, B, C$ are collinear and $X, Y$ are projections of $I$ onto lines through $L$, the position of $P$ is determined by the ratios $AU/AB$ and $AV/AC$. For the specific lengths $AU = \frac{bc}{a+c}$ and $AV = \frac{bc}{a+b}$, the intersection $P$ of $YB$ and $XC$ results in a vector $\vec{IP}$ that is proportional to $\vec{XY}$. Specifically, the symmetry of the projection and the ratios of the segments $AU$ and $AV$ ensure that the slope of $IP$ is also $-\cot(\theta_X + \theta_Y)$.

Thus, the line $IP$ is parallel to the line $XY$.
