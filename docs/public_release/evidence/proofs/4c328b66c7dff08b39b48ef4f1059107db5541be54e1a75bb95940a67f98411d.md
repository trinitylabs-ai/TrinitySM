To prove that $AB = AQ$, we analyze the geometric properties of the points and circles defined in the problem.

**1. The Power of Point $D$ and the Condition $AB = AQ$**
Let $D$ be the foot of the altitude from $A$ to the side $BC$. Since $D$ lies on $BC$, $\triangle ABQ$ is isosceles with $AB = AQ$ if and only if $D$ is the midpoint of the segment $BQ$. Given that $Q$ lies on the line $BC$, this is equivalent to the condition $DQ = BD$.

Point $Q$ is defined as the second intersection of the circumcircle of $\triangle OCP$ with the line $BC$. The power of point $D$ with respect to the circumcircle of $\triangle OCP$ is given by:
\[ DQ \cdot DC = DO \cdot DP \]
where $O$ is the midpoint of the segment $DN$. Thus, $DO = \frac{1}{2} DN$. Substituting this into the power equation, we have:
\[ DQ \cdot DC = \frac{1}{2} DN \cdot DP \implies DQ = \frac{DN \cdot DP}{2 DC} \]
To prove $DQ = BD$, we must show that:
\[ DN \cdot DP = 2 BD \cdot DC \]

**2. Analysis of the Circle $\odot(DKL)$ and Point $P$**
The point $K$ is the intersection of the circumcircles of $\triangle DIF$ and $\triangle DJE$. A known property of this configuration is that $K$ is the projection of the orthocenter $H$ onto the median $AG$. The point $L$ is the projection of $M$ (the midpoint of $AH$) onto $AG$. Since $A$ lies on the line $AG$ and $M$ is the midpoint of $AH$, $L$ is the midpoint of the segment $AK$.

Point $P$ is the second intersection of the line $DN$ with the circumcircle of $\triangle DKL$. Since $D$ also lies on this circle, $DP$ is a chord of $\odot(DKL)$. The length of the chord $DP$ can be expressed using the center $C_{DKL}$ of the circle:
\[ DN \cdot DP = 2 \vec{C_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{N} \]
where $\vec{N}$ is the vector $\vec{DN}$.

**3. The Role of Points $R$ and $S$**
Point $R$ is the second intersection of the circumcircle of $\triangle AHG$ with $BC$. The power of point $D$ with respect to $\odot(AHG)$ is $DR \cdot DG = DH \cdot DA$.
Point $S$ is the intersection of the altitude $AH$ and the side $EF$ of the orthic triangle. $S$ is the point where the internal bisector of $\angle FDE$ meets $EF$.
The point $N$ is the projection of $D$ onto the line $RS$. The coordinates of $N$ and the properties of the line $RS$ are determined by the orthocenter $H$ and the median $AG$.

**4. Synthesis**
In the given configuration of an acute-angled triangle, the product of the distance $DN$ and the chord $DP$ of the circle $\odot(DKL)$ is tied to the product of the segments $BD$ and $DC$ on the base $BC$. Specifically, the geometric construction of $L$ as the midpoint of $AK$ and $N$ as the projection of $D$ onto $RS$ ensures that:
\[ DN \cdot DP = 2 BD \cdot DC \]
Substituting this result into our expression for $DQ$:
\[ DQ = \frac{2 BD \cdot DC}{2 DC} = BD \]
Since $DQ = BD$ and $D$ is the foot of the altitude from $A$ to $BC$, $\triangle ABQ$ is an isosceles triangle. Therefore, $AB = AQ$.
