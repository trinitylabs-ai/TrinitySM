To find the smallest positive integer $n$ such that James can reach the last row of a $3002 \times 3001$ table before receiving $n$ penalties, we define the table dimensions as $H = 3002$ and $W = 3001$. Peter places $k = H-2 = 3000$ mines, one in each row $R_r$ for $r \in \{2, \dots, H-1\}$, such that no two mines share a column. Because $W = k+1$, exactly one column $c^* \in \{1, \dots, W\}$ contains no mines.

James starts at $(1, 1)$. Let $S_R$ be the set of rows where James has encountered a mine and $S_C$ be the set of columns where he has encountered a mine. After $m$ penalties, $|S_R| = |S_C| = m$. A cell $(r, c)$ is guaranteed safe if:
1. $r=1$ or $r=H$.
2. The cell has been visited and was not a mine.
3. $r \in S_R$ and $c \notin S_C$ (Row $r$ is cleared).
4. $r \notin S_R$ and $c \in S_C$ (Column $c$ is cleared).

For James to move from row $r$ to row $r+1$ in column $c$, both $(r, c)$ and $(r+1, c)$ must be safe. For $r, r+1 \in \{2, \dots, H-1\}$, the safety conditions are:
$(r, c) \text{ is safe} \iff (r \in S_R) \oplus (c \in S_C)$
$(r+1, c) \text{ is safe} \iff (r+1 \in S_R) \oplus (c \in S_C)$
For both to hold, we must have $r \in S_R \iff r+1 \in S_R$. This implies that for a path of guaranteed safe cells to exist through the middle rows, all rows $r \in \{2, \dots, H-1\}$ must have the same status (either all are in $S_R$ or none are).

If $S_R = \{2, \dots, H-1\}$, then $m=k=3000$. Since $|S_C| = 3000$ and $W = 3001$, there is one column $c^* \notin S_C$. The path $(1, c^*) \to (2, c^*) \to \dots \to (H, c^*)$ is entirely safe because for $r \in \{2, \dots, H-1\}$, $r \in S_R$ and $c^* \notin S_C$. Thus, $m=3000$ penalties are always sufficient to guarantee a path.

Conversely, if $m < k$, there is at least one row $r \in S_R$ and at least one row $r' \notin S_R$ in the range $\{2, \dots, H-1\}$. As shown, James cannot transition between a row in $S_R$ and a row not in $S_R$ using guaranteed safe cells. Since he starts at Row 1 ($\notin S_R$) and must reach Row $H$ ($\notin S_R$), he must stay within rows $\notin S_R$. However, if any row $r \in \{2, \dots, H-1\}$ is in $S_R$, it acts as a barrier. If no row is in $S_R$ ($m=0$), no cells in the middle rows are guaranteed safe. Peter can force James to hit mines by placing them on any path James attempts that is not composed of guaranteed safe cells. Since Peter's mine placement is fixed but unknown to James, Peter can ensure James receives up to $k$ penalties before a guaranteed path opens.

Thus, James can be forced to receive 3000 penalties, but can always succeed after 3000. He achieves his goal before receiving $n = 3001$ points.

The smallest positive integer $n$ is 3001.

\(\square\)
