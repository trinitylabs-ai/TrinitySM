Let $S$ be the set of $n = 120$ students. The set $F$ is the power set $\mathcal{P}(S)$, containing $2^{120}$ elements. The function $f: F \to F$ is defined such that for any $X \in F$, $f(X)$ is the set of students loved by one or more students in $X$. Let $R$ be the binary relation on $S$ where $xRy$ if student $x$ loves student $y$. Then:
\[ f(X) = \{y \in S \mid \exists x \in X, xRy\} \]
The function $f$ distributes over the union of sets:
\[ f(X \cup Y) = \{y \in S \mid \exists x \in X \cup Y, xRy\} = \{y \in S \mid \exists x \in X, xRy\} \cup \{y \in S \mid \exists x \in Y, xRy\} = f(X) \cup f(Y) \]
By induction, for any $k \in \mathbb{N}$, $f^k(X) = \bigcup_{x \in X} f^k(\{x\})$.

We are looking for the maximum possible value $M(f)$ of $t$ such that there exist distinct elements $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_t \in F$ where for any $1 \le i < j \le t$, $A_i$ and $A_j$ have a lovely relationship. By definition, $A_i$ and $A_j$ have a lovely relationship if there exists a natural number $k \ge 1$ such that $f^k(A_i) = A_j$.
This condition implies that the elements must form a chain:
\[ A_1 \xrightarrow{f^{k_1}} A_2 \xrightarrow{f^{k_2}} A_3 \dots \xrightarrow{f^{k_{t-1}}} A_t \]
where each $k_i \ge 1$. Consequently, every $A_j$ for $j > 1$ is an image of $A_1$ under some iteration of $f$. Specifically, $A_j = f^{K_j}(A_1)$ where $K_j = \sum_{m=1}^{j-1} k_m$. Since the $A_i$ are distinct, the values $K_j$ must be distinct. Thus, $t$ is bounded by the number of distinct elements in the orbit of $A_1$ under $f$, which is the set $O(A_1) = \{A_1, f(A_1), f^2(A_1), \dots\}$.

For any $x \in S$, let $S_k(x) = f^k(\{x\})$. The sequence $S_k(x)$ for $k=0, 1, 2, \dots$ eventually becomes periodic. The pre-period $m(x)$ and period $p(x)$ of this sequence are determined by the structure of the directed graph $G = (S, R)$. The period $p(x)$ is the least common multiple (LCM) of the periods of the strongly connected components (SCCs) reachable from $x$. The period of an SCC is the GCD of its cycle lengths, which is at most the size of the SCC. Thus, $p(x) \le g(n)$, where $g(n)$ is Landau's function (the maximum LCM of a partition of $n$). For $n=120$, $g(120) \approx 2 \cdot 10^{10}$.
The pre-period $m(x)$ is the time it takes for the sequence to enter its cycle, which is bounded by the index of convergence of the adjacency matrix of $G$. A known bound for the index of convergence of a Boolean matrix of size $n \times n$ is $(n-1)^2 + 1$. For $n=120$, $m(x) \le 119^2 + 1 = 14162$.

For any $X \in F$, the sequence $f^k(X) = \bigcup_{x \in X} S_k(x)$ becomes periodic when all $S_k(x)$ for $x \in X$ have become periodic. The pre-period $m(X)$ of the sequence $f^k(X)$ is $\max_{x \in X} m(x) \le n^2$, and the period $p(X)$ is $\text{lcm}_{x \in X} p(x) \le g(n)$.
The total number of distinct elements in the orbit of $A_1$ is $m(A_1) + p(A_1)$. Therefore:
\[ M(f) \le n^2 + g(n) \le 120^2 + g(120) \]
Given $g(120) \approx 2 \cdot 10^{10}$ and $120^2 = 14400$, we have:
\[ M(f) \approx 2 \cdot 10^{10} \]
Since $2^{70} = (2^{10})^7 \approx (10^3)^7 = 10^{21}$, it is clear that:
\[ M(f) \le 2 \cdot 10^{10} \ll 10^{21} \le 2^{70} \]
Thus, $M(f) \le 2^{70}$ for any such function $f$.
