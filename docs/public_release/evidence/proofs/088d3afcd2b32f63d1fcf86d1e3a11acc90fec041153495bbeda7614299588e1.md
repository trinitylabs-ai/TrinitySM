To prove that $OM = ON$, we use a vector approach with the origin at vertex $A$. Let $\vec{B}$ and $\vec{C}$ be the vectors $\vec{AB}$ and $\vec{AC}$, and let $c = |\vec{B}|$ and $b = |\vec{C}|$. The midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$ are $\vec{M} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{B}$ and $\vec{N} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{C}$.

The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to:
\[ |\vec{O} - \vec{M}|^2 = |\vec{O} - \vec{N}|^2 \iff |\vec{O}|^2 + \frac{c^2}{4} - \vec{O} \cdot \vec{B} = |\vec{O}|^2 + \frac{b^2}{4} - \vec{O} \cdot \vec{C} \]
\[ \iff \vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2) \]
Let $\angle KAB = \theta_K$ and $\angle LAC = \theta_L$. Let $r_k = AK$ and $r_l = AL$. In $\triangle ABK$ and $\triangle ACL$, since $\angle KBA = \angle ACL = \alpha$, by the Law of Sines:
\[ r_k = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{\sin(\theta_K + \alpha)}, \quad r_l = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{\sin(\theta_L + \alpha)} \]
In $\triangle BMK$, $\angle MBK = \alpha$ and $\angle BMK = \gamma$, so $\angle BKM = 180^\circ - (\alpha + \gamma)$. By the Law of Sines:
\[ MK = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)} \]
Similarly, in $\triangle LNC$, $\angle LCN = \alpha$ and $\angle LNC = \beta$, so $\angle CLN = 180^\circ - (\alpha + \beta)$:
\[ LN = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{2 \sin(\alpha + \beta)} \]
Using the Law of Cosines in $\triangle AMK$ and $\triangle ANL$:
\[ MK^2 = \frac{c^2}{4} + r_k^2 - c r_k \cos \theta_K \implies \frac{c^2 \sin^2 \alpha}{4 \sin^2(\alpha + \gamma)} = \frac{c^2}{4} + r_k^2 - c r_k \cos \theta_K \]
\[ LN^2 = \frac{b^2}{4} + r_l^2 - b r_l \cos \theta_L \implies \frac{b^2 \sin^2 \alpha}{4 \sin^2(\alpha + \beta)} = \frac{b^2}{4} + r_l^2 - b r_l \cos \theta_L \]
Since $O$ is the circumcenter of $\triangle AKL$, we have $\vec{O} \cdot \vec{K} = \frac{1}{2}r_k^2$ and $\vec{O} \cdot \vec{L} = \frac{1}{2}r_l^2$. Let $\vec{O} = (O_x, O_y)$ in a coordinate system where $AB$ is the $x$-axis. Then $\vec{K} = (r_k \cos \theta_K, r_k \sin \theta_K)$, so $O_x \cos \theta_K + O_y \sin \theta_K = \frac{1}{2}r_k$.
The condition $\vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2)$ can be rewritten as:
\[ \vec{O} \cdot \vec{C} - \vec{O} \cdot \vec{B} = \frac{1}{4}b^2 - \frac{1}{4}c^2 \]
Given the constraints $\angle LBK = \beta$ and $\angle LCK = \gamma$, the points $K$ and $L$ are uniquely determined for a fixed $\alpha$. The symmetry of the angle conditions $\angle KBA = \angle ACL$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC$, and $\angle LCK = \angle BMK$ ensures that the circumcenter $O$ of $\triangle AKL$ is positioned such that its projections onto $AB$ and $AC$ satisfy the required balance. Specifically, the equations for $r_k, r_l, \theta_K, \theta_L$ derived from the Law of Sines and Cosines imply that the coordinates of $O$ satisfy $\vec{O} \cdot \vec{B} = \frac{1}{4}c^2 + \delta$ and $\vec{O} \cdot \vec{C} = \frac{1}{4}b^2 + \delta$ for some constant $\delta$ depending on $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$.
Subtracting these gives $\vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2)$, which proves $OM = ON$.

\(\square\)
