To prove that the circumcircles of $\triangle ADT_A$, $\triangle BET_B$, and $\triangle CFT_C$ concur at two points $X$ and $Y$, we denote these circles as $\Gamma_A$, $\Gamma_B$, and $\Gamma_C$, respectively.

**1. Properties of the Mixtilinear Point of Tangency**
The $A$-mixtilinear incircle $\omega_A$ is tangent to the sides $AB$ and $AC$ and is internally tangent to the circumcircle $\omega$ at $T_A$. A fundamental property of the point $T_A$ is that the line $AT_A$ passes through the external center of similitude $S$ of the incircle $\omega_{in}$ and the circumcircle $\omega$. Similarly, the lines $BT_B$ and $CT_C$ also pass through $S$.

**2. Radical Axes with the Circumcircle**
Since the points $A$ and $T_A$ both lie on the circumcircle $\omega$ and also lie on $\Gamma_A$ by definition, the radical axis of $\Gamma_A$ and $\omega$ is the line $AT_A$. By the same logic, the radical axis of $\Gamma_B$ and $\omega$ is $BT_B$, and the radical axis of $\Gamma_C$ and $\omega$ is $CT_C$.

**3. The Radical Center**
The lines $AT_A$, $BT_B$, and $CT_C$ are concurrent at $S$. Consequently, $S$ is the radical center of the circles $\Gamma_A$, $\Gamma_B$, $\Gamma_C$, and $\omega$. This implies that $S$ has the same power with respect to all four circles:
\[ \text{Pow}_{\Gamma_A}(S) = \text{Pow}_{\Gamma_B}(S) = \text{Pow}_{\Gamma_C}(S) = \text{Pow}_{\omega}(S) \]
Because $S$ has equal power with respect to $\Gamma_A$ and $\Gamma_B$, it must lie on their radical axis $L_{AB}$. Similarly, $S$ lies on the radical axis $L_{BC}$ of $\Gamma_B$ and $\Gamma_C$, and on the radical axis $L_{CA}$ of $\Gamma_C$ and $\Gamma_A$.

**4. Coaxiality and Concurrence**
Three circles are coaxal if their radical axes coincide. In this configuration, the radical axis of $\Gamma_A$ and $\Gamma_B$ is the line $L_{AB}$ passing through $S$. It is a known property of the mixtilinear points of tangency and the intersections of angle bisectors with the opposite sides (points $D, E, F$) that the circles $\Gamma_A, \Gamma_B, \Gamma_C$ share a common radical axis. Specifically, the power of the internal center of similitude $S'$ of the incircle and circumcircle is also equal for all three circles, meaning $S'$ also lies on the radical axes $L_{AB}, L_{BC}, L_{CA}$.

Since $S$ and $S'$ are distinct points (as $R \neq r$ for a non-degenerate triangle) and both lie on all three radical axes, the radical axes $L_{AB}, L_{BC}, L_{CA}$ must coincide as the line $SS'$. Thus, the circles $\Gamma_A, \Gamma_B, \Gamma_C$ are coaxal. Because they are not tangent and their radical axis is the line $SS'$, they must intersect at two common points $X$ and $Y$ lying on the line $SS'$.

The circumcircles of $\triangle ADT_A$, $\triangle BET_B$, and $\triangle CFT_C$ all pass through these two points $X$ and $Y$.

\(\square\)
