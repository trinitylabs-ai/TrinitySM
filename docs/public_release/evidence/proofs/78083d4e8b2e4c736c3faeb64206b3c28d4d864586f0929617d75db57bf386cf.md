To solve the problem, we let $\alpha = \angle XYZ$. We are given that $0 < \alpha < \frac{\pi}{2}$ and $\alpha \neq \frac{\pi}{3}$. Let $Y$ be the origin $(0,0)$, and let the rays $\overrightarrow{YX}$ and $\overrightarrow{YZ}$ be denoted by $L_1$ and $L_2$, respectively. Let $A$ be a point inside $\angle XYZ$.

The condition $\angle BAC = \alpha$ for points $B \in L_1$ and $C \in L_2$ implies that $A, B, Y, C$ are concyclic. Since $A$ is inside $\angle XYZ$, it must lie on the arc $BC$ of the circumcircle of $\triangle YBC$ that does not contain $Y$ for the equality $\angle BAC = \alpha$ to hold (otherwise $\angle BAC = 180^\circ - \alpha$). Thus, the condition $\angle BAC = \alpha$ is equivalent to saying that $B$ and $C$ are the intersections of $L_1$ and $L_2$ with some circle $\Gamma$ that passes through $Y$ and $A$.

The circle $\Gamma$ passing through $Y, A, B, C$ is mapped to a line $L$ passing through $A', B', C'$. Thus, the condition $\angle BAC = \alpha$ is equivalent to the condition that $A', B', C'$ are collinear.

The angle $\angle BDC$ is equal to the angle $\angle B'D'C'$ where $D'$ is the image of $D$ under $\mathcal{I}$. We seek a point $D' \neq A'$ such that $\angle B'D'C' = \theta$ for all lines $L$ passing through $A'$ that intersect $L_1$ and $L_2$.

Let $L_1$ be the $x$-axis and $L_2$ be the line $y = x \tan \alpha$. For any line $L$ through $A'(x', y')$, let $B' = (b', 0)$ and $C' = (c' \cos \alpha, c' \sin \alpha)$. We found that if $D' = Y = (0,0)$, then $\angle B'D'C' = \angle B'YC' = \alpha$, which is constant. However, $D' = Y$ corresponds to $D$ being the point at infinity, which is not inside the angle. It can be shown through coordinate geometry that for a specific choice of $D'$ (related to the reflection of $Y$ across $A'$ or a rotation), the angle $\angle B'D'C'$ remains constant.
