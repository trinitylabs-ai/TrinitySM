Let the sequence $\{F_n\}_{n \geq 1}$ be defined by $F_1 = 1$ and $F_n = n^{F_{n-1}}$ for $n \geq 2$. We wish to prove that for any positive integers $a, c$ and integer $b$, there exists a positive integer $n$ such that $a^{F_n} + n \equiv b \pmod{c}$.

We proceed by induction on $c$. For $c=1$, the congruence $a^{F_n} + n \equiv b \pmod{1}$ is trivially satisfied for any $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.
Assume that for all $m < c$, for any positive integer $a'$, integer $b'$, and any $M \in \mathbb{Z}^+$, there exists $n > M$ such that $a'^{F_n} + n \equiv b' \pmod{m}$.

Consider the chain of totients starting at $c$: let $c_0 = c, c_1 = \phi(c_0), c_2 = \phi(c_1), \dots, c_k = 1$. Let $L_1 = \text{lcm}(c_1, c_2, \dots, c_k)$.
By the generalized Euler's Totient Theorem, for any $m$ and $x \geq \phi(m)$, $a^x \equiv a^{x \pmod{\phi(m)} + \phi(m)} \pmod{m}$.
For $n$ sufficiently large, $F_n \pmod{c_i}$ depends on $n \pmod{c_i}$ and $F_{n-1} \pmod{c_{i+1}}$. Specifically, $F_n \equiv n^{F_{n-1} \pmod{c_{i+1}} + c_{i+1}} \pmod{c_i}$.
By iterating this, for $n$ sufficiently large, $F_n \pmod{c_1}$ is determined by the residues of $n \pmod{c_1}, (n-1) \pmod{c_2}, \dots, (n-k+1) \pmod{c_k}$.
If $n \equiv \rho \pmod{L_1}$, then $n-j \equiv \rho-j \pmod{c_{j+1}}$ for all $j \in \{0, \dots, k-1\}$. Thus, for $n$ sufficiently large and $n \equiv \rho \pmod{L_1}$, the value $F_n \pmod{c_1}$ is a constant $V_1(\rho)$.

Let $g = \gcd(c, L_1)$. We first show that $g < c$ for $c > 1$. Let $p$ be the largest prime dividing $c$. The exponent of $p$ in $\phi(m)$ is $v_p(\phi(m)) = v_p(m) - 1 + \sum_{q|m, q \equiv 1 \pmod{p}} v_p(q-1)$. Since $p$ is the largest prime dividing $c$, for any $q|c$, $q \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$ is impossible unless $q > p$, which contradicts the choice of $p$. Thus $v_p(\phi(c)) = v_p(c) - 1$. By induction, $v_p(c_i) = v_p(c) - i$ until the exponent becomes 0. Thus $v_p(L_1) = \max_i(v_p(c_i)) = v_p(c) - 1 < v_p(c)$. Hence $L_1$ cannot be a multiple of $c$, so $g < c$.

By the strengthened inductive hypothesis, for any $M \in \mathbb{Z}^+$, there exists $n' > M$ such that $a^{F_{n'}} + n' \equiv b \pmod{g}$.
Choose $M$ large enough such that for any $n' > M$, $F_{n'} \pmod{c_1}$ has reached its stable value $V_1(\rho_1)$ (where $\rho_1 = n' \pmod{L_1}$) and $F_{n'} \geq \phi(g)$.
Since $\phi(g)$ divides $\phi(c) = c_1$, we have $F_{n'} \equiv V_1(\rho_1) \pmod{\phi(g)}$. By the generalized Euler's Theorem, $a^{F_{n'}} \equiv a^{V_1(\rho_1) \pmod{\phi(g)} + \phi(g)} \pmod{g}$.
Also, since $c_1$ is a multiple of $\phi(g)$ and $V_1(\rho_1) + c_1 \geq \phi(g)$, we have $a^{V_1(\rho_1) + c_1} \equiv a^{(V_1(\rho_1) + c_1) \pmod{\phi(g)} + \phi(g)} \pmod{g}$.
Because $(V_1(\rho_1) + c_1) \equiv V_1(\rho_1) \pmod{\phi(g)}$, it follows that $a^{F_{n'}} \equiv a^{V_1(\rho_1) + c_1} \pmod{g}$.
Thus, $a^{F_{n'}} + n' \equiv a^{V_1(\rho_1) + c_1} + \rho_1 \equiv b \pmod{g}$.

We now seek $\rho$ such that:
1) $\rho \equiv \rho_1 \pmod{L_1}$
2) $\rho \equiv b - a^{V_1(\rho_1) + c_1} \pmod{c}$
By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, such a $\rho$ exists if and only if $\rho_1 \equiv b - a^{V_1(\rho_1) + c_1} \pmod{\gcd(L_1, c)}$.
Since $\gcd(L_1, c) = g$, this condition is $a^{V_1(\rho_1) + c_1} + \rho_1 \equiv b \pmod{g}$, which we have already established.

Finally, for $n$ sufficiently large and $n \equiv \rho \pmod{\text{lcm}(c, L_1)}$, we have $n \equiv \rho_1 \pmod{L_1}$, so $F_n \equiv V_1(\rho_1) \pmod{c_1}$.
Since $F_n \geq \phi(c) = c_1$ for large $n$, by the generalized Euler's Theorem:
$a^{F_n} \equiv a^{F_n \pmod{c_1} + c_1} \equiv a^{V_1(\rho_1) + c_1} \pmod{c}$.
Then $a^{F_n} + n \equiv a^{V_1(\rho_1) + c_1} + \rho \pmod{c}$.
Substituting $\rho \equiv b - a^{V_1(\rho_1) + c_1} \pmod{c}$, we obtain:
$a^{F_n} + n \equiv a^{V_1(\rho_1) + c_1} + b - a^{V_1(\rho_1) + c_1} \equiv b \pmod{c}$.
Thus, $\frac{a^{F_n} + n - b}{c}$ is an integer.
