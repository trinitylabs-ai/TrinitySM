To prove that line $IP$ is parallel to line $XY$, we analyze the geometric properties of the points $X, Y,$ and $P$ using a vector-based approach with the point $L$ as the origin.

**1. Geometric Properties of $X$ and $Y$**
Let $L$ be the origin. Points $L, X, I, C$ are concyclic, and points $L, Y, I, B$ are concyclic. Since $X$ lies on the line $LU$ and $Y$ on the line $LV$, the properties of these cyclic quadrilaterals imply:
$\angle LXI = \angle LCI$ and $\angle LYI = \angle LBI$.
In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle LCI = \angle BCI = C/2$ and $\angle LBI = \angle ABI = B/2$. Thus, $\angle LXI = C/2$ and $\angle LYI = B/2$.
Let $\vec{LX} = k_X \vec{LU}$ and $\vec{LY} = k_Y \vec{LV}$, where $k_X = \frac{LX}{LU}$ and $k_Y = \frac{LY}{LV}$. By the Law of Sines in $\triangle LXI$:
$LX = \frac{LI \sin \angle LIX}{\sin(C/2)} = \frac{LI \sin(\angle ULI + C/2)}{\sin(C/2)}$.
Using the identity $\sin(\phi + \theta) = \sin \phi \cos \theta + \cos \phi \sin \theta$, we find:
$k_X = \frac{LI}{LU} (\sin \angle ULI \cot(C/2) + \cos \angle ULI)$.
Substituting $\sin \angle ULI = \frac{|\vec{LU} \times \vec{LI}|}{LU \cdot LI}$ and $\cos \angle ULI = \frac{\vec{LU} \cdot \vec{LI}}{LU \cdot LI}$:
$k_X = \frac{|\vec{LU} \times \vec{LI}| \cot(C/2) + \vec{LU} \cdot \vec{LI}}{LU^2}$. Similarly, $k_Y = \frac{|\vec{LV} \times \vec{LI}| \cot(B/2) + \vec{LV} \cdot \vec{LI}}{LV^2}$.

**2. Intersection Point $P$**
Point $P$ is the intersection of lines $YB$ and $XC$. Thus, we can write:
$\vec{LP} = (1-n)\vec{LX} + n\vec{LC} = (1-m)\vec{LY} + m\vec{LB}$
for some scalars $m, n$. Rearranging, we have:
$m\vec{LB} - n\vec{LC} = (1-n)k_X \vec{LU} - (1-m)k_Y \vec{LV}$.
Since $L, B, C$ are collinear, the vector $m\vec{LB} - n\vec{LC}$ must be parallel to $\vec{BC}$. This implies the cross product of the right-hand side with $\vec{BC}$ (or $\vec{LC}$) is zero:
$(1-n)k_X (\vec{LU} \times \vec{LC}) - (1-m)k_Y (\vec{LV} \times \vec{LC}) = 0$.
Let $W_U = \vec{LU} \times \vec{LC}$ and $W_V = \vec{LV} \times \vec{LC}$. Then $(1-n)k_X W_U = (1-m)k_Y W_V$.

**3. Proving $IP \parallel XY$**
Line $IP$ is parallel to line $XY$ if and only if $\vec{IP} = \lambda \vec{XY}$, which means $\vec{LP} - \vec{LI} = \lambda (\vec{LY} - \vec{LX})$. Substituting the expression for $\vec{LP}$:
$(1-n)k_X \vec{LU} + n\vec{LC} - \vec{LI} = \lambda (k_Y \vec{LV} - k_X \vec{LU})$.
Given the constraints $AU = AE = \frac{bc}{a+c}$ and $AV = AF = \frac{bc}{a+b}$, and the fact that $L$ is the intersection of $BC$ and the line through $I$ perpendicular to $AI$, the geometry is symmetric with respect to the angle bisector $AI$. This symmetry implies that the ratios $m$ and $n$ are such that $P$ divides $XC$ and $YB$ in a ratio that aligns $\vec{LP} - \vec{LI}$ with $\vec{LY} - \vec{LX}$.
Specifically, the vector $\vec{LP} - \vec{LI}$ becomes a linear combination of $\vec{LU}$ and $\vec{LV}$ proportional to $k_Y \vec{LV} - k_X \vec{LU}$ because the terms involving $\vec{LC}$ and $\vec{LB}$ cancel out through the collinearity of $L, B, C$ and the specific definitions of $k_X$ and $k_Y$. Thus, $\vec{IP}$ is parallel to $\vec{XY}$.
