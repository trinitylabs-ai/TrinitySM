To prove that $C$ is the midpoint of $XY$, we use a coordinate geometry approach. Let $B$ be the origin $(0,0)$ and let the line $BC$ lie along the $x$-axis. Let $a$ be the length of $BC$ and $c$ be the length of $AB$. We denote the angle at $B$ as $\angle B$.

The coordinates of the vertices of $\triangle ABC$ are:
- $B = (0, 0)$
- $C = (a, 0)$
- $A = (c \cos B, c \sin B)$

$F$ is the foot of the altitude from $C$ to $AB$. Since $F$ lies on the line $AB$ (which has slope $\tan B$) and $CF$ is perpendicular to $AB$, $F$ is the projection of $C$ onto $AB$. The distance $BF = BC \cos B = a \cos B$. Thus, the coordinates of $F$ are:
$F = (a \cos B \cdot \cos B, a \cos B \cdot \sin B) = (a \cos^2 B, a \sin B \cos B)$.

Let $H$ be the orthocenter. $H$ is the intersection of the altitudes. The altitude from $A$ to $BC$ is the vertical line $x = c \cos B$. The altitude from $C$ to $AB$ is the line passing through $C(a, 0)$ with slope $-\cot B$:
$y - 0 = -\cot B(x - a) \implies y = (a - x) \cot B$.
Substituting $x = c \cos B$, we find the $y$-coordinate of $H$:
$y_H = (a - c \cos B) \cot B$.
$P$ is the reflection of $H$ across the line $BC$ (the $x$-axis), so:
$P = (c \cos B, -(a - c \cos B) \cot B) = (c \cos B, (c \cos B - a) \cot B)$.

Let $\Gamma$ be the circumcircle of $\triangle AFP$. The center $O' = (x_0, y_0)$ of $\Gamma$ is the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of $AP$ and $AF$.
The segment $AP$ is vertical because $x_A = x_P = c \cos B$. Its perpendicular bisector is the horizontal line $y = y_0$, where:
$y_0 = \frac{y_A + y_P}{2} = \frac{c \sin B + (c \cos B - a) \cot B}{2}$.

The midpoint of $AF$ is $M_{AF} = \left( \frac{c \cos B + a \cos^2 B}{2}, \frac{c \sin B + a \sin B \cos B}{2} \right)$. Since the slope of $AF$ is $\tan B$, the perpendicular bisector of $AF$ has slope $-\cot B$. Its equation is:
$y - \frac{c \sin B + a \sin B \cos B}{2} = -\cot B \left( x - \frac{c \cos B + a \cos^2 B}{2} \right)$.

The x-coordinate $x_0$ of the center $O'$ is found by substituting $y = y_0$ into this equation:
$y_0 - \frac{c \sin B + a \sin B \cos B}{2} = -\cot B \left( x_0 - \frac{c \cos B + a \cos^2 B}{2} \right)$.
Substituting $y_0$:
$\frac{c \sin B + (c \cos B - a) \cot B - c \sin B - a \sin B \cos B}{2} = -\cot B \left( x_0 - \frac{c \cos B + a \cos^2 B}{2} \right)$
$\frac{(c \cos B - a) \cot B - a \sin B \cos B}{2} = -\cot B \left( x_0 - \frac{c \cos B + a \cos^2 B}{2} \right)$.

We test if $x_0 = a$:
$\text{RHS} = -\cot B \left( a - \frac{c \cos B + a \cos^2 B}{2} \right) = \frac{\cot B (c \cos B + a \cos^2 B - 2a)}{2}$
$= \frac{c \cos B \cot B + a \cos^2 B \cot B - 2a \cot B}{2}$.
Comparing the numerators:
$\text{LHS num} = c \cos B \cot B - a \cot B - a \sin B \cos B$.
$\text{RHS num} = c \cos B \cot B + a \cos^2 B \cot B - 2a \cot B$.
Difference: $\text{RHS num} - \text{LHS num} = a \cos^2 B \cot B - a \cot B + a \sin B \cos B$
$= a \cot B (\cos^2 B - 1) + a \sin B \cos B = a \cot B (-\sin^2 B) + a \sin B \cos B$
$= -a \cos B \sin B + a \sin B \cos B = 0$.
Thus, $x_0 = a$.

The points $X$ and $Y$ are the intersections of the circle $\Gamma$ with the line $BC$ (the $x$-axis). For any circle, the x-coordinate of the center is the midpoint of the x-coordinates of the points where the circle intersects the $x$-axis. Since $x_0 = a$ and the x-coordinate of $C$ is $a$, $C$ is the midpoint of $XY$.
