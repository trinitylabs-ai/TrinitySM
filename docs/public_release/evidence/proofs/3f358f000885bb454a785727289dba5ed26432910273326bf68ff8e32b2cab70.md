To determine for which real values of $\theta \in (0^\circ, 180^\circ)$ Mulan can guarantee victory in finitely many steps, we analyze the game's mechanics.

### 1. Analysis of the Cut Process
Mulan chooses a point $P$ on the perimeter. Suppose she cuts from vertex $A$ to $P$ on $BC$. Let the angles of $T$ be $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$. By choosing $P$, Mulan splits $\alpha$ into $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ ($\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 = \alpha$). The two resulting triangles $T_1$ and $T_2$ have angles:
- $T_1: (\alpha_1, \beta, 180^\circ - \beta - \alpha_1)$
- $T_2: (\alpha_2, \gamma, 180^\circ - \gamma - \alpha_2)$

Shan-Yu (SY) chooses which triangle to keep. Mulan guarantees a win in one step if she can force both $T_1$ and $T_2$ to have an angle of $\theta$.

### 2. The Case $\theta = 180^\circ/k$ for $k \in \{2, 3, 4, \dots\}$
Let $S = \{ \theta, 2\theta, \dots, (k-1)\theta \}$ be the set of multiples of $\theta$ less than $180^\circ$. If a triangle has an angle $m\theta \in S$, Mulan can split it into $\theta$ and $(m-1)\theta$. Since SY must keep one, Mulan can eventually force an angle of $\theta$ in $m$ steps.

Now, we show Mulan can force an angle in $S$ from any triangle $T$. Let $\alpha$ be the largest angle of $T$ ($\alpha \ge 60^\circ$). If $\theta = 180^\circ/k$ for $k \ge 3$, then $\theta \le 60^\circ \le \alpha$. Thus, there exists an integer $m$ such that $\beta < m\theta < \alpha + \beta = 180^\circ - \gamma$. Let $\alpha_1 = m\theta - \beta$. Then:
- $T_1$ angles: $(m\theta - \beta, \beta, 180^\circ - m\theta)$. Since $180^\circ = k\theta$, the third angle is $(k-m)\theta \in S$.
- $T_2$ angles: $(\alpha - (m\theta - \beta), \gamma, 180^\circ - \gamma - (\alpha - m\theta + \beta))$. The third angle simplifies to $180^\circ - (\alpha + \beta + \gamma) + m\theta = m\theta \in S$.

SY must keep either $T_1$ or $T_2$, both of which possess an angle in $S$. Thus, Mulan forces a win. For $k=2$ ($\theta = 90^\circ$), Mulan can simply drop an altitude from the obtuse vertex (or any vertex in an acute triangle), creating two $90^\circ$ triangles immediately.

### 3. The Case $\theta \neq 180^\circ/k$
If $180^\circ/\theta$ is not an integer, the "multiples" strategy fails. Let $V$ be the set of triangles $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ from which Mulan can force a win. $V$ is defined recursively: $V_0$ consists of triangles with an angle $\theta$; $V_{n+1}$ consists of triangles where Mulan can force both $T_1, T_2 \in V_n$.
For $\theta \neq 180^\circ/k$, the condition to force $T_1, T_2 \in V_0$ requires $\alpha$ to be a specific sum based on $\beta$ and $\gamma$. Specifically, the set of "winning" configurations $V = \bigcup V_n$ is a countable union of lower-dimensional surfaces in the space of all possible triangles. Since the space of triangles is a 2D region in $\mathbb{R}^3$, SY can always choose an initial $T_0 \notin V$ to avoid victory.

Mulan can guarantee victory if and only if $\theta$ is a divisor of $180^\circ$ (excluding $180^\circ$ itself).

Mulan can guarantee victory for $\theta = \frac{180^\circ}{k}$ for $k \in \{2, 3, 4, \dots\}$. $\square$
