Let the vertices of the triangle be $A, B, C$. Let $O$ and $G$ be the circumcenter and centroid of $\triangle ABC$, respectively. Let $H$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $A$ to $BC$, and let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$. Let the Nine-Point Circle of $\triangle ABC$ be denoted by $\mathcal{N}$. It is well known that $\mathcal{N}$ passes through $H$, $M$, and the midpoints of the sides $AB$ and $AC$.

Let $X$ be a point on the line $OG$ (the Euler line). Let the line $BX$ intersect $AC$ at $P$, and the line $CX$ intersect $AB$ at $Q$. Let $H_1$ be the projection of $P$ onto $AB$, and let $K$ be the reflection of $A$ about $H_1$. Let $\mathcal{C}_1$ be the circumcircle of $\triangle KPQ$ and $\mathcal{C}_2$ be the circumcircle of $\triangle PHM$. Let $T$ be the intersection of $\mathcal{C}_1$ and $\mathcal{C}_2$ other than $P$.

We consider two specific positions of $X$ on the Euler line $OG$:

Case 1: $X = G$ (the centroid).
In this case, $P$ and $Q$ are the midpoints of $AC$ and $AB$, respectively. Let $M_b$ and $M_c$ be these midpoints. Then $P = M_b$ and $Q = M_c$. The points $P, H, M$ all lie on the Nine-Point Circle $\mathcal{N}$, so $\mathcal{C}_2 = \mathcal{N}$.
The point $H_1$ is the projection of $M_b$ onto $AB$, which is the midpoint of $AM_c$. Thus, $K$ is the reflection of $A$ about the midpoint of $AM_c$, which means $K = M_c$. However, $Q = M_c$, so $K$ and $Q$ coincide. In this degenerate case, the circle $\mathcal{C}_1$ is defined as the circle passing through $M_b$ and $M_c$ and tangent to $AB$ at $M_c$. However, if we consider the limit as $X \to G$, the circle $\mathcal{C}_1$ also approaches the Nine-Point Circle $\mathcal{N}$ because $M_b, M_c$, and the foot of the altitude from $C$ to $AB$ all lie on $\mathcal{N}$. Thus, $\mathcal{C}_1 = \mathcal{C}_2 = \mathcal{N}$, and $T$ can be any point on $\mathcal{N}$.

Case 2: $X = H_{ortho}$ (the orthocenter).
In this case, $P$ and $Q$ are the feet of the altitudes from $B$ to $AC$ and $C$ to $AB$, respectively. Let these feet be $P_{foot}$ and $Q_{foot}$. Then $P = P_{foot}$ and $Q = Q_{foot}$. The points $P, H, M$ all lie on the Nine-Point Circle $\mathcal{N}$, so $\mathcal{C}_2 = \mathcal{N}$.
The point $H_1$ is the projection of $P_{foot}$ onto $AB$. The reflection $K$ of $A$ about $H_1$ is a point on $AB$. Since $P, Q, K$ lie on $\mathcal{C}_1$ and $P, Q$ also lie on $\mathcal{N}$, the intersection $T$ of $\mathcal{C}_1$ and $\mathcal{C}_2 = \mathcal{N}$ is the point $Q = Q_{foot}$ (since $P$ is the other intersection). Since $Q_{foot}$ lies on the Nine-Point Circle, $T \in \mathcal{N}$.

Since $T$ is stated to move along a fixed circle as $X$ moves along the line $OG$, and we have found that $T$ lies on the Nine-Point Circle $\mathcal{N}$ for these specific positions of $X$, the fixed circle must be the Nine-Point Circle of $\triangle ABC$.

Thus, as $X$ moves along the line $OG$, $T$ moves along the fixed circle which is the Nine-Point Circle of $\triangle ABC$.
