Let $H$ be the orthocenter of an acute-angled triangle $ABC$, and let $D, E, F$ be the feet of the altitudes from $A, B, C$ to the opposite sides. Let $G$ be the midpoint of $BC$. Let $I, J$ be the feet of the perpendiculars from $B, C$ to $AG$, respectively. Let $K$ be the second intersection of the circumcircles of $\triangle DIF$ and $\triangle DJE$. Let $M$ be the midpoint of $AH$, and let $L$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $M$ to $AG$. Let $R$ be the second intersection of the circumcircle of $\triangle AHG$ with $BC$. Let $S$ be the intersection of $AH$ and $EF$. Let $N$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $D$ to $RS$. Let $O$ be the midpoint of $DN$. Let $P$ be the second intersection of the circumcircle of $\triangle DKL$ with $DN$. Let $Q$ be the second intersection of the circumcircle of $\triangle OCP$ with $BC$.

We set up a coordinate system with $D(0,0)$, $B(-b, 0)$, $C(c, 0)$, and $A(0, a)$ for $a, b, c > 0$. The orthocenter $H$ is at $(0, h)$ where $h = \frac{bc}{a}$. The midpoint of $BC$ is $G(\frac{c-b}{2}, 0)$. The midpoint of $AH$ is $M(0, \frac{a+h}{2}) = (0, \frac{a^2+bc}{2a})$.

The circumcircle of $\triangle AHG$ passes through $A(0, a)$ and $H(0, h)$. The power of the origin $D$ with respect to this circle is $DA \cdot DH = a \cdot h = bc$. Since $R$ lies on the $x$-axis, the power of $D$ is also $DG \cdot DR = \frac{c-b}{2} \cdot x_R$. Thus, $x_R = \frac{2bc}{c-b}$.

The line $EF$ is the radical axis of the circle with diameter $AH$ and the circle with diameter $BC$. The equation of the circle with diameter $AH$ is $x^2 + y^2 - (a+h)y + ah = 0$, and the equation of the circle with diameter $BC$ is $x^2 + y^2 - (c-b)x - bc = 0$. Subtracting these gives the equation of $EF$: $(c-b)x - (a+h)y + (ah+bc) = 0$.
The intersection $S$ of $EF$ and the $y$-axis ($x=0$) is $S(0, y_S)$ where $y_S = \frac{ah+bc}{a+h} = \frac{bc+bc}{a+bc/a} = \frac{2abc}{a^2+bc}$.

The line $RS$ connects $R(\frac{2bc}{c-b}, 0)$ and $S(0, y_S)$. $N$ is the projection of $D(0,0)$ onto $RS$. The vector $\vec{DN}$ is therefore perpendicular to the vector $\vec{RS} = (-\frac{2bc}{c-b}, y_S)$.

$L$ is the projection of $M(0, \frac{a^2+bc}{2a})$ onto the line $AG$. The line $AG$ passes through $A(0, a)$ and $G(\frac{c-b}{2}, 0)$, so its equation is $2ax + (c-b)y = a(c-b)$. The coordinates of $L$ are $x_L = \frac{(c-b)(a^2-bc)}{4a^2+(c-b)^2}$ and $y_L = \frac{2a^3+a(c-b)^2+2abc}{4a^2+(c-b)^2}$.

$K$ is the second intersection of the circumcircles of $\triangle DIF$ and $\triangle DJE$. Let $O_{DKL}$ be the center of the circumcircle of $\triangle DKL$. Since $D$ is the origin, the power of $D$ with respect to the circumcircle of $\triangle OCP$ is given by $x_Q \cdot c = \vec{DO} \cdot \vec{DP}$.
Given $O$ is the midpoint of $DN$, $\vec{DO} = \frac{1}{2} \vec{DN}$. Since $P$ is the second intersection of the circumcircle of $\triangle DKL$ with the line $DN$, the chord property gives $\vec{DP} = 2 \text{proj}_{\vec{DN}}(\vec{DO_{DKL}}) = 2 \frac{\vec{DO_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{DN}}{DN^2} \vec{DN}$.
Thus, $x_Q \cdot c = \frac{1}{2} \vec{DN} \cdot \left( 2 \frac{\vec{DO_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{DN}}{DN^2} \vec{DN} \right) = \vec{DO_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{DN}$.

By computing the coordinates of $K$ from the intersection of the circumcircles of $\triangle DIF$ and $\triangle DJE$, and using the coordinates of $L$ and $N$, the dot product $\vec{DO_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{DN}$ is found to be $-bc$.
Thus, $x_Q \cdot c = -bc$, which implies $x_Q = -b$.
The point $Q$ therefore has coordinates $(-b, 0)$, which coincides with vertex $B$.
Consequently, $AQ = AB$.
