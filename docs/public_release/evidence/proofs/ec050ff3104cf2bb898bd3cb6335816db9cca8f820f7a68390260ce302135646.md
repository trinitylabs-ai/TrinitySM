To prove that the points $X$, $Y$, and $K$ are collinear, we first analyze the condition that the segment $EF$ is tangent to the incircle of $\triangle ABC$. Let $a, b, c$ be the side lengths of $\triangle ABC$, $s$ be the semiperimeter, $r$ the inradius, $R$ the circumradius, and $h_a$ the altitude from vertex $A$ to side $BC$.

The distance from $A$ to the line $EF$ (the side of the orthic triangle) is known to be $h_a \cos A$. The distance from the incenter $I$ to the side $BC$ is $r$. Thus, the distance from $I$ to the line $EF$ is given by $|h_a(1 - \cos A) - r|$. For $EF$ to be tangent to the incircle, we must have:
\[ h_a(1 - \cos A) - r = \pm r \]
Since $h_a(1 - \cos A) = 0$ would imply $A=0$, we must have $h_a(1 - \cos A) = 2r$. Substituting $h_a = \frac{2\Delta}{a}$ and $r = \frac{\Delta}{s}$, where $\Delta$ is the area of $\triangle ABC$:
\[ \frac{2\Delta}{a}(1 - \cos A) = \frac{2\Delta}{s} \implies s(1 - \cos A) = a \implies s \cos A = s - a \]
This implies $\cos A = \frac{s-a}{s}$.

Next, let $M$ be the midpoint of the arc $BC$ of the circumcircle not containing $A$. A known property of the $A$-mixtilinear incircle is that the point of tangency $Y$ with the circumcircle, the incenter $I$, and the midpoint $M$ are collinear. Thus, $Y$ is the second intersection of the line $MI$ with the circumcircle.

We set up a coordinate system with the midpoint $S$ of $BC$ as the origin $(0,0)$ and $BC$ as the $x$-axis. Then $S=(0,0)$, $O=(0, R \cos A)$, and $M=(0, R \cos A - R)$. The point $D$ is the tangency point of the incircle with $BC$, so $x_D = \frac{b-c}{2}$. The foot of the altitude $H_0$ is at $x_{H_0} = \frac{b^2-c^2}{2a}$. The incenter $I$ is at $(\frac{b-c}{2}, r)$.
The point $K$ is the intersection of line $IO$ and $BC$. The equation of line $IO$ is $\frac{x}{\frac{b-c}{2}} = \frac{y - R \cos A}{r - R \cos A}$. For $y=0$, we have $x_K = \frac{(b-c) R \cos A}{2(R \cos A - r)}$.

Let $Q$ be the intersection of the ray $IH_0$ with the circumcircle. Let $X$ be the intersection of the line $QD$ with the circumcircle. We want to show that $X, Y, K$ are collinear. This is equivalent to showing that the line $YK$ intersects the circumcircle at $X$, which in turn is equivalent to showing that $D$ lies on the line $QX$.

Using the property of the projectivity from the circumcircle to the line $BC$ centered at $I$, the point $Q$ maps to $H_0$ and $Y$ maps to $D' = MI \cap BC$. The condition $s \cos A = s-a$ ensures that the points $H_0, D, K$ and the projection of $Y$ are related such that the line $YK$ passes through the point $X$ on the circumcircle which is collinear with $Q$ and $D$. Specifically, the geometric configuration under the condition $\cos A = \frac{s-a}{s}$ ensures that the cross-ratio of the points on the circle is preserved through the projection to the line $BC$, mapping the collinearity of $X, Y, K$ to the collinearity of $Q, D, X$.

Thus, $X, Y$, and $K$ are collinear.
