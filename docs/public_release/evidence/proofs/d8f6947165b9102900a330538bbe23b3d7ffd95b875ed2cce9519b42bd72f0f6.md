To solve the problem, we analyze the effect of the operation on the prime factorizations of the integers on the blackboard.

### Part (1): Termination of the Process

Let the integers on the blackboard at any time $t$ be $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_{2026}$. A move consists of choosing two integers $m, n > 1$ and replacing them with $g = \gcd(m, n)$ and $L = \text{lcm}(m, n)/\gcd(m, n)$. 

Let $v_p(x)$ denote the exponent of the prime $p$ in the prime factorization of $x$. For any prime $p$, the exponents of $m$ and $n$ are $a = v_p(m)$ and $b = v_p(n)$. The exponents of the new integers $g$ and $L$ are:
- $v_p(g) = \min(a, b)$
- $v_p(L) = v_p(\text{lcm}(m, n)) - v_p(\gcd(m, n)) = \max(a, b) - \min(a, b) = |a - b|$

To prove the process terminates, we define two quantities:
1. $N(t)$: The number of integers on the blackboard greater than 1.
2. $S(t) = \sum_p \sum_{i=1}^{2026} v_p(x_i)$: The total sum of all prime exponents of all integers on the blackboard.

In each move $(m, n) \to (g, L)$:
- If $\gcd(m, n) = 1$, then $g = 1$. Since $m, n > 1$, the number of integers greater than 1 decreases: $N(t+1) = N(t) - 1$. The total sum $S(t)$ remains unchanged because $v_p(g) + v_p(L) = 0 + v_p(mn) = v_p(m) + v_p(n)$.
- If $\gcd(m, n) > 1$, there exists at least one prime $p$ such that $\min(v_p(m), v_p(n)) \ge 1$. The total sum $S(t)$ changes by:
  $$\Delta S = \sum_p (\min(a, b) + |a - b| - (a + b)) = \sum_p (\max(a, b) - (a + b)) = -\sum_p \min(a, b)$$
  Since $\gcd(m, n) > 1$, $\Delta S < 0$, so $S(t+1) < S(t)$. In this case, $N(t+1)$ is either $N(t)$ (if $L > 1$) or $N(t) - 1$ (if $L = 1$).

Since $(N(t), S(t))$ is a strictly decreasing sequence in lexicographical order and $N(t) \ge 1, S(t) \ge 0$, the process must terminate in finitely many moves. The process stops when no two integers $m, n > 1$ can be chosen, meaning at most one integer is greater than 1. Since at least one of $g, L$ is always greater than 1 (if $m=n$, $g=m>1$; if $m \neq n$, either $\gcd(m, n)>1$ or $\text{lcm}(m, n)/\gcd(m, n)>1$), and we start with $x_i > 1$, exactly one integer $M > 1$ remains.

### Part (2): Invariance of $M$

For a fixed prime $p$, let the exponents of the integers on the blackboard be $\{a_1, a_2, \dots, a_{2026}\}$. The operation replaces two exponents $(a, b)$ with $(\min(a, b), |a - b|)$. 
We observe that the greatest common divisor of the exponents is invariant:
$$\gcd(a, b) = \gcd(\min(a, b), |a - b|)$$
Indeed, any divisor of $a$ and $b$ divides $\min(a, b)$ and $|a - b|$. Conversely, any divisor $d$ of $\min(a, b)$ and $|a - b|$ also divides their sum $\min(a, b) + |a - b| = \max(a, b)$, and thus divides both $a$ and $b$.

Therefore, for every prime $p$, the value $G_p = \gcd(v_p(x_1), v_p(x_2), \dots, v_p(x_{2026}))$ is invariant throughout the process. When the process terminates, only one integer $M$ is greater than 1, so all other exponents are 0. The final GCD for prime $p$ is:
$$\gcd(v_p(M), 0, \dots, 0) = v_p(M)$$
Thus, $v_p(M) = G_p$ for all $p$. This means $M = \prod_p p^{G_p}$, which depends only on the initial integers $x_i$ and not on the choices made. $\square$
