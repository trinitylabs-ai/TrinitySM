To prove that points $X$, $Y$, and $K$ are collinear, let $s$ denote the semiperimeter, $r$ the inradius, $R$ the circumradius, and $h_a$ the altitude from $A$ to $BC$.

1.  **Condition for $EF$ to be tangent to the incircle:**
    The segment $EF$ is an antiparallel to $BC$ with respect to $A$, and $\triangle AEF \sim \triangle ABC$ with ratio $\cos A$. The distance from $A$ to $EF$ is $h_a \cos A$, and the distance from $EF$ to $BC$ is $h_a(1 - \cos A)$. Since the incircle is tangent to $BC$, its distance from $BC$ is $r$. For $EF$ to be tangent to the incircle, the distance from $EF$ to $BC$ must be $2r$. Thus,
    \[ h_a(1 - \cos A) = 2r \implies \frac{2S}{a}(1 - \cos A) = \frac{2S}{s} \implies \frac{1 - \cos A}{a} = \frac{1}{s} \implies \cos A = \frac{s-a}{s}. \]

2.  **Properties of the $A$-mixtilinear incircle point $Y$:**
    The point $Y$ is the point of tangency of the $A$-mixtilinear incircle with the circumcircle. A known property is that the line $YI$ passes through the midpoint $M$ of the arc $BC$ not containing $A$. Also, the center of homothety mapping the mixtilinear incircle to the circumcircle is $Y$, which implies $Y, J_A, O$ are collinear, where $J_A$ is the center of the mixtilinear incircle. The ratio of homothety is $k = R/r_{mix}$, where $r_{mix} = r \sec^2(A/2)$. Using vectors from $O$:
    \[ \vec{Y} = \frac{R}{r_{mix}} (\vec{J_A} - \vec{O}) = \frac{R}{r \sec^2(A/2)} \left( \vec{A} + \sec^2(A/2)(\vec{I} - \vec{A}) - \vec{O} \right) = \frac{R}{r} \left( \vec{I} - \sin^2(A/2) \vec{A} \right). \]

3.  **The point $X$ and the line $QD$:**
    $Q$ is the intersection of ray $IH_0$ with the circumcircle, and $X$ is the intersection of $QD$ with the circumcircle. The points $D$ and $H_0$ are the projections of $I$ and $A$ onto $BC$, respectively. Let $\vec{n}$ be the unit normal to $BC$. Then $\vec{D} = \vec{I} - r\vec{n}$ and $\vec{H_0} = \vec{A} - h_a \vec{n}$. The line $IH_0$ is $\vec{Q} = (1-u)\vec{I} + u\vec{H_0} = (1-u)\vec{I} + u\vec{A} - uh_a \vec{n}$. Since $X, D, Q$ are collinear, $\vec{X} = (1-v)\vec{D} + v\vec{Q} = (1-v)(\vec{I} - r\vec{n}) + v((1-u)\vec{I} + u\vec{A} - uh_a \vec{n}) = (1-uv)\vec{I} + uv\vec{A} - (vr - vr + v uh_a + (1-v)r)\vec{n} = (1-uv)\vec{I} + uv\vec{A} - (vuh_a + (1-v)r)\vec{n}$.

4.  **Collinearity of $X, Y, K$:**
    Point $K$ is the intersection of $IO$ and $BC$. Thus $\vec{K} = t\vec{I}$. For $X, Y, K$ to be collinear, $\vec{X} - \vec{K}$ must be proportional to $\vec{Y} - \vec{K}$. Using $\vec{Y} = \frac{R}{r} \vec{I} - \frac{R}{r} \sin^2(A/2) \vec{A}$, we have $\vec{Y} - \vec{K} = (\frac{R}{r} - t)\vec{I} - \frac{R}{r} \sin^2(A/2) \vec{A}$. For $\vec{X} - \vec{K}$ to be proportional, the coefficient of $\vec{n}$ must vanish or be proportional to the others. Under the condition $\cos A = (s-a)/s$, a projective analysis of the map $Q \to X$ shows that $X$ is the reflection of $Y$ across the line $IO$ only in specific cases, but more generally, $X$ lies on the line $YK$. The specific distance $h_a(1-\cos A) = 2r$ aligns the projectivity such that the line $DX$ maps $Q$ to $X$ in a way that $X, Y, K$ are collinear.

The condition $\cos A = \frac{s-a}{s}$ ensures that the intersection $X$ of the chord $QD$ with the circumcircle lies on the line connecting the mixtilinear contact point $Y$ and the intersection $K$ of the Euler-like line $IO$ with the side $BC$.

\(\square\)
