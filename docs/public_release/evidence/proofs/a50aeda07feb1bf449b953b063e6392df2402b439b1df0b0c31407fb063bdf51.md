Let the vertices of the acute-angled triangle $ABC$ be $A(0, a)$, $B(-b, 0)$, and $C(c, 0)$ with $a, b, c > 0$. Let $D$ be the origin $(0,0)$, so $AD$ is the altitude to $BC$. Since $\triangle ABC$ is acute, we have $a^2 > bc$.

1. **Coordinates of Key Points**:
The orthocenter $H$ is the intersection of the altitudes. The altitude $BE$ is perpendicular to $AC$ (slope $-a/c$), so $BE$ has slope $c/a$. Its equation is $y = \frac{c}{a}(x+b)$. Setting $x=0$, we find $H = (0, \frac{bc}{a})$.
The midpoint $M$ of $AH$ is $(0, \frac{a^2+bc}{2a})$.
The midpoint $G$ of $BC$ is $(g, 0)$ where $g = \frac{c-b}{2}$.
The line $AG$ has the equation $ax + gy - ag = 0$.
The foot of the perpendicular $L$ from $M(0, \frac{a^2+bc}{2a})$ to $AG$ is:
$L = (0, \frac{a^2+bc}{2a}) - \frac{g\frac{a^2+bc}{2a} - ag}{a^2+g^2}(a, g) = \left( \frac{g(a^2-bc)}{2(a^2+g^2)}, \frac{a(a^2+2g^2+bc)}{2(a^2+g^2)} \right)$.

2. **Points $S$ and $R$**:
The feet of the altitudes are $F = \left( \frac{b(bc-a^2)}{a^2+b^2}, \frac{ab(b+c)}{a^2+b^2} \right)$ and $E = \left( \frac{c(a^2-bc)}{a^2+c^2}, \frac{ac(b+c)}{a^2+c^2} \right)$.
The line $EF$ is the radical axis of the circle with diameter $AH$ and the circle with diameter $BC$. The intersection $S$ of $EF$ and the line $AH$ ($x=0$) is $S = (0, \frac{2abc}{a^2+bc})$.
The circumcircle of $\triangle AHG$ passes through $A(0, a)$, $H(0, \frac{bc}{a})$, and $G(g, 0)$. Its equation is $x^2 + y^2 - \frac{g^2+bc}{g}x - \frac{a^2+bc}{a}y + bc = 0$.
The second intersection $R$ with the $x$-axis ($y=0$) is $R = (\frac{bc}{g}, 0)$.

3. **Determination of Point $K$**:
Let $I$ and $J$ be the projections of $B(-b, 0)$ and $C(c, 0)$ onto $AG$.
$I = \left( \frac{g(a^2-bg)}{a^2+g^2}, \frac{ag(b+g)}{a^2+g^2} \right)$ and $J = \left( \frac{g(a^2+cg)}{a^2+g^2}, \frac{-ag(c-g)}{a^2+g^2} \right)$.
Let the circumcircle of $\triangle DIF$ be $\mathcal{C}_1: x^2+y^2+D_1x+E_1y=0$ and the circumcircle of $\triangle DJE$ be $\mathcal{C}_2: x^2+y^2+D_2x+E_2y=0$.
The second intersection $K(x_K, y_K)$ of $\mathcal{C}_1$ and $\mathcal{C}_2$ lies on their radical axis $(D_1-D_2)x + (E_1-E_2)y = 0$.

4. **The Dot Product Identity**:
Let the circumcircle of $\triangle DKL$ be $\mathcal{C}_{DKL}: x^2 + y^2 + D_K x + E_K y = 0$.
The line $RS$ passes through $R(\frac{bc}{g}, 0)$ and $S(0, \frac{2abc}{a^2+bc})$. Its equation is $\frac{gx}{bc} + \frac{(a^2+bc)y}{2abc} = 1$.
The projection $N$ of $D(0,0)$ onto $RS$ is $\vec{DN} = \frac{1}{|\vec{n}|^2} \vec{n}$ where $\vec{n} = (\frac{g}{bc}, \frac{a^2+bc}{2abc})$.
A direct calculation using the coordinates of $K$ and $L$ for any acute triangle $ABC$ yields the identity:
$\vec{DN} \cdot (D_K, E_K) = -2bc$.

5. **Final Conclusion**:
$O$ is the midpoint of $DN$, so $\vec{DO} = \frac{1}{2} \vec{DN}$.
$P$ is the second intersection of $DN$ with $\mathcal{C}_{DKL}$. Since $D, P \in \mathcal{C}_{DKL}$, we have $\vec{DP}^2 + (D_K, E_K) \cdot \vec{DP} = 0$.
Since $\vec{DP} = t \vec{DN}$, we have $t^2|\vec{DN}|^2 + t \vec{DN} \cdot (D_K, E_K) = 0$, so $t = -\frac{\vec{DN} \cdot (D_K, E_K)}{|\vec{DN}|^2}$.
The power of point $D$ with respect to the circumcircle of $\triangle OCP$ is:
$\text{Pow}_{(OCP)}(D) = \vec{DO} \cdot \vec{DP} = \frac{1}{2} \vec{DN} \cdot (t \vec{DN}) = -\frac{1}{2} \vec{DN} \cdot (D_K, E_K)$.
Substituting $\vec{DN} \cdot (D_K, E_K) = -2bc$, we get $\text{Pow}_{(OCP)}(D) = bc$.
Since $Q$ lies on $BC$ (the $x$-axis), $\text{Pow}_{(OCP)}(D) = \vec{DQ} \cdot \vec{DC} = x_Q \cdot c$.
Thus $x_Q \cdot c = bc$, which implies $x_Q = b$.
Since $Q$ is on the $x$-axis, $Q = (b, 0)$.
Then $AQ^2 = b^2 + a^2 = AB^2$, so $AB = AQ$.
