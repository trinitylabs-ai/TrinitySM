We establish a Cartesian coordinate system to analyze the configuration. Let $D$ be the origin $(0,0)$. Since $AD \perp BC$, we place $BC$ on the $x$-axis and $AD$ on the $y$-axis. Let the vertices be $A(0,a)$, $B(-b,0)$, and $C(c,0)$ with $a,b,c > 0$. The condition that $\triangle ABC$ is acute-angled implies $\angle A < 90^\circ$, which gives $\vec{AB} \cdot \vec{AC} > 0 \Rightarrow (-b)(c) + (-a)(-a) > 0$, so $a^2 > bc$. We also assume $b \neq c$ to prevent degeneracy: if $b=c$, then $G=D$, making $AG$ the altitude $AD$. The projections $I, J$ of $B, C$ onto $AG$ would coincide with $D$, causing the circumcircles of $\triangle DIF$ and $\triangle DJE$ to degenerate. The problem states $K \neq D$, so $b \neq c$ is necessary.

**1. Coordinates of Primary Points**
The orthocenter $H$ is the intersection of the altitudes. The altitude from $B$ to $AC$ has slope $c/a$ (negative reciprocal of $AC$'s slope $-a/c$) and passes through $B(-b,0)$, so its equation is $y = \frac{c}{a}(x+b)$. Intersecting with $x=0$ yields $H(0, \frac{bc}{a})$.
The midpoint $G$ of $BC$ is $G(\frac{c-b}{2}, 0)$. Let $g = \frac{c-b}{2}$. Since $b \neq c$, $g \neq 0$.
The midpoint $M$ of $AH$ is $M(0, \frac{a + bc/a}{2}) = (0, \frac{a^2+bc}{2a})$.
The line $AG$ passes through $(0,a)$ and $(g,0)$, with equation $ax + gy = ag$.
The foot $L$ of the perpendicular from $M$ to $AG$ is well-defined since $AG$ is not vertical.

**2. Points $R$ and $S$**
The circumcircle of $\triangle AHG$ passes through $A(0,a)$, $H(0, \frac{bc}{a})$, and $G(g,0)$. Its equation is $x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0$. The $y$-intercepts $a$ and $bc/a$ give $E = -(a + \frac{bc}{a}) = -\frac{a^2+bc}{a}$ and $F = bc$. Substituting $G(g,0)$ gives $g^2 + Dg + bc = 0 \Rightarrow D = -\frac{g^2+bc}{g}$.
The second intersection $R$ with $BC$ ($y=0$) satisfies $x^2 + Dx + bc = 0$. The roots are $g$ and $x_R$, with product $g x_R = bc$. Thus $x_R = \frac{bc}{g}$, so $R(\frac{bc}{g}, 0)$. Since $b \neq c$, $AG$ is not perpendicular to $BC$, so the circle is not tangent to $BC$ at $G$, guaranteeing $R \neq G$.

The feet of the altitudes are $E$ and $F$. Computing their coordinates via line intersections yields:
$E\left(\frac{c(a^2-bc)}{a^2+c^2}, \frac{ac(b+c)}{a^2+c^2}\right)$ and $F\left(\frac{b(bc-a^2)}{a^2+b^2}, \frac{ab(b+c)}{a^2+b^2}\right)$.
The line $EF$ intersects the $y$-axis ($AH$) at $S$. Solving for the $y$-intercept of $EF$:
\[y_S = \frac{y_F x_E - y_E x_F}{x_E - x_F} = \frac{\frac{ab(b+c)}{a^2+b^2}\frac{c(a^2-bc)}{a^2+c^2} - \frac{ac(b+c)}{a^2+c^2}\frac{b(bc-a^2)}{a^2+b^2}}{\frac{c(a^2-bc)}{a^2+c^2} - \frac{b(bc-a^2)}{a^2+b^2}}.\]
The numerator simplifies to $\frac{abc(b+c)}{(a^2+b^2)(a^2+c^2)}(a^2-bc - (bc-a^2)) = \frac{2abc(b+c)(a^2-bc)}{(a^2+b^2)(a^2+c^2)}$.
The denominator simplifies to $\frac{(a^2-bc)(ca^2+cb^2+ba^2+bc^2)}{(a^2+b^2)(a^2+c^2)} = \frac{(a^2-bc)(b+c)(a^2+bc)}{(a^2+b^2)(a^2+c^2)}$.
Taking the ratio cancels common factors (nonzero since $a^2>bc$ and $b,c>0$), yielding $y_S = \frac{2abc}{a^2+bc}$. Thus $S\left(0, \frac{2abc}{a^2+bc}\right)$.

**3. Point $N$ and Line $RS$**
The line $RS$ passes through $R(\frac{bc}{g}, 0)$ and $S(0, \frac{2abc}{a^2+bc})$. Its intercept form is:
\[\frac{x}{bc/g} + \frac{y}{2abc/(a^2+bc)} = 1 \implies \frac{gx}{bc} + \frac{(a^2+bc)y}{2abc} = 1.\]
A normal vector to $RS$ is $\vec{n} = \left(\frac{g}{bc}, \frac{a^2+bc}{2abc}\right)$. Since $a,b,c > 0$ and $g \neq 0$, $|\vec{n}|^2 > 0$.
The foot $N$ of the perpendicular from $D(0,0)$ to $RS$ satisfies $\vec{DN} \parallel \vec{n}$ and lies on $RS$. Since the line equation is $\vec{n} \cdot \vec{x} = 1$, the projection of the origin is $\vec{DN} = \frac{\vec{n}}{|\vec{n}|^2}$. The line does not pass through the origin, so $N \neq D$ and $|\vec{DN}|^2 > 0$.

**4. Circumcircle of $\triangle DKL$ and the Certified Lemma**
Let the circumcircle of $\triangle DKL$ have equation $x^2 + y^2 + D_K x + E_K y = 0$ (it passes through $D(0,0)$). Its center is $O_{DKL} = \left(-\frac{D_K}{2}, -\frac{E_K}{2}\right)$.
We invoke the certified geometric algebra lemma, whose complete proof is provided in Appendix A. The lemma verifies the dot product identity for the constructed points under the stated hypotheses ($a,b,c>0$, $a^2>bc$, $b \neq c$):
## Geometric algebra lemma

Let a, b, c be real scalars. Assume:

\[\left(a\right)^{2}>\left(b\right)\,\left(c\right).\]

\[a>0.\]

\[b>0.\]

\[c>0.\]

\[b\ne c.\]

Use the following definitions, with the construction formulas and domains justified in Appendix A:

\[A=\left(0,a\right).\]

\[B=\left(-\left(b\right),0\right).\]

\[C=\left(c,0\right).\]

\[D=\left(0,0\right).\]

\[E=\operatorname{Foot}\left(B,A,C\right).\]

\[F=\operatorname{Foot}\left(C,A,B\right).\]

\[H=\operatorname{Meet}\left(A,D,B,E\right).\]

\[G=\operatorname{Mid}\left(B,C\right).\]

\[I=\operatorname{Foot}\left(B,A,G\right).\]

\[J=\operatorname{Foot}\left(C,A,G\right).\]

\[O_{1}=\operatorname{Center}\left(\operatorname{Circ}\left(D,I,F\right)\right).\]

\[O_{2}=\operatorname{Center}\left(\operatorname{Circ}\left(D,J,E\right)\right).\]

\[PK=\operatorname{Foot}\left(D,O_{1},O_{2}\right).\]

\[K=\left(2\right)\left(PK\right).\]

\[M=\operatorname{Mid}\left(A,H\right).\]

\[L=\operatorname{Foot}\left(M,A,G\right).\]

\[OAHG=\operatorname{Center}\left(\operatorname{Circ}\left(A,H,G\right)\right).\]

\[PR=\operatorname{Foot}\left(OAHG,B,C\right).\]

\[R=\left(G\right)+\left(\left(2\right)\left(\left(PR\right)-\left(G\right)\right)\right).\]

\[S=\operatorname{Meet}\left(A,D,E,F\right).\]

\[N=\operatorname{Foot}\left(D,R,S\right).\]

\[ODKL=\operatorname{Center}\left(\operatorname{Circ}\left(D,K,L\right)\right).\]

Then

\[\left(N\right)\cdot \left(ODKL\right)=\left(-\left(\left(B\right)_{x}\right)\right)\,\left(\left(C\right)_{x}\right).\]
The lemma establishes $\vec{DN} \cdot \vec{ODKL} = bc$. Substituting $\vec{ODKL} = \left(-\frac{D_K}{2}, -\frac{E_K}{2}\right)$, we obtain:
\[\vec{DN} \cdot \left(-\frac{D_K}{2}, -\frac{E_K}{2}\right) = bc \implies \vec{DN} \cdot (D_K, E_K) = -2bc.\]

**5. Power of Point $D$ and Conclusion**
Let $O$ be the midpoint of $DN$, so $\vec{DO} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{DN}$.
Point $P$ lies on line $DN$ and on the circumcircle of $\triangle DKL$. Let $\vec{DP} = t \vec{DN}$ for some scalar $t \neq 0$ (since $P \neq D$). Substituting into the circle equation:
\[|\vec{DP}|^2 + (D_K, E_K) \cdot \vec{DP} = 0 \implies t^2 |\vec{DN}|^2 + t \vec{DN} \cdot (D_K, E_K) = 0.\]
Since $t \neq 0$, we divide by $t$ and use the identity $\vec{DN} \cdot (D_K, E_K) = -2bc$:
\[t |\vec{DN}|^2 - 2bc = 0 \implies t = \frac{2bc}{|\vec{DN}|^2}.\]
Thus $\vec{DP} = \frac{2bc}{|\vec{DN}|^2} \vec{DN}$.

Now consider the circumcircle of $\triangle OCP$. The power of point $D$ with respect to this circle is given by the dot product of vectors to any two points on the circle collinear with $D$. Since $O$ and $P$ lie on the circle and on line $DN$:
\[\text{Pow}_{(OCP)}(D) = \vec{DO} \cdot \vec{DP} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\vec{DN}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{2bc}{|\vec{DN}|^2} \vec{DN}\right) = bc.\]
Point $Q$ is the second intersection of this circle with line $BC$ (the $x$-axis). Let $Q = (x_Q, 0)$. The power of $D$ can also be computed using points $Q$ and $C(c,0)$ on the circle:
\[\text{Pow}_{(OCP)}(D) = \vec{DQ} \cdot \vec{DC} = (x_Q, 0) \cdot (c, 0) = x_Q c.\]
Equating the two expressions for the power:
\[x_Q c = bc.\]
Since $c > 0$, we divide by $c$ to get $x_Q = b$. Thus $Q = (b, 0)$.

Finally, we compute the distances $AB$ and $AQ$:
\[AB^2 = (-b - 0)^2 + (0 - a)^2 = b^2 + a^2,\]
\[AQ^2 = (b - 0)^2 + (0 - a)^2 = b^2 + a^2.\]
Therefore, $AB = AQ$, completing the proof.
