To prove that the points $X, Y,$ and $K$ are collinear, we begin by analyzing the condition that the segment $EF$ is tangent to the incircle of $\triangle ABC$.

Let $h_a$ be the altitude from $A$ to $BC$, $r$ the inradius, and $R$ the circumradius. The distance from $A$ to the line $EF$ is $h_a \cos A$. The distance from $I$ to $BC$ is $r$, and the distance from $A$ to $BC$ is $h_a$. Since $EF$ is between $A$ and $BC$, the distance from $I$ to $EF$ is given by $d(I, EF) = |h_a - r - h_a \cos A| = |h_a(1 - \cos A) - r|$. For $EF$ to be tangent to the incircle, we must have $d(I, EF) = r$, which implies $h_a(1 - \cos A) = 2r$.
Substituting $h_a = 2R \sin B \sin C$ and $1 - \cos A = 2 \sin^2(A/2)$, we obtain:
$4R \sin B \sin C \sin^2(A/2) = 2r$.
Using the identity $r = 4R \sin(A/2) \sin(B/2) \sin(C/2)$, this simplifies to:
$\sin B \sin C \sin(A/2) = 2 \sin(B/2) \sin(C/2)$.
Expanding $\sin B$ and $\sin C$ using double-angle formulas:
$(2 \sin \frac{B}{2} \cos \frac{B}{2})(2 \sin \frac{C}{2} \cos \frac{C}{2}) \sin \frac{A}{2} = 2 \sin \frac{B}{2} \sin \frac{C}{2} \implies 2 \cos \frac{B}{2} \cos \frac{C}{2} \sin \frac{A}{2} = 1$.
Using $\sin(A/2) = \cos((B+C)/2)$, the condition $2 \cos \frac{B}{2} \cos \frac{C}{2} \sin \frac{A}{2} = 1$ is equivalent to $\cos B + \cos C - \cos A = 1$. This is further equivalent to the relation $r = 2R \cos A$.

We now set up a coordinate system with $D$ as the origin $(0,0)$ and the side $BC$ along the $x$-axis. The incenter $I$ is at $(0, r)$. The projection of the circumcenter $O$ on $BC$ is the midpoint $M'$ of $BC$, and the distance $OM' = R \cos A$. Since $r = 2R \cos A$, we have $OM' = r/2$. Thus, $O = (d, r/2)$ where $d = (b-c)/2$.
The line $IO$ passes through $(0, r)$ and $(d, r/2)$. Its equation is $y - r = \frac{r/2 - r}{d - 0} x = -\frac{r}{2d} x$.
The point $K$ is the intersection of $IO$ with $BC$ ($y=0$), so $-r = -\frac{r}{2d} x \implies x = 2d$. Thus, $K = (2d, 0)$.
The point $H_0$ is the foot of the altitude from $A$. The distance $DH_0 = \frac{|b-c|s}{a}$. Using $s = 2R \cot(A/2)$ and $a = 2R \sin A$, we find $s/a = 1/(1 - \cos A)$. Thus, $H_0 = (\frac{b-c}{1 - \cos A}, 0)$.
The point $Q$ is the intersection of the ray $IH_0$ with the circumcircle $\Gamma$. Let $X$ be the other intersection of the line $QD$ with $\Gamma$. Since $D$ is the origin, $X$ is the image of $Q$ under the projectivity $f(P) = \text{line } PD \cap \Gamma$.
The point $Y$ is the tangency point of the $A$-mixtilinear incircle with $\Gamma$. It is a known property that $Y, I,$ and $M$ (the midpoint of arc $BC$ not containing $A$) are collinear. $M$ has coordinates $(d, r/2 - R)$. The line $YI$ is $y - r = \frac{r/2 - R - r}{d - 0} x = -\frac{2R+r}{2d} x$.
Under the condition $r = 2R \cos A$, the points $X, Y,$ and $K$ are collinear. This can be verified by observing that the projectivity $f$ maps $Q$ to $X$ such that the line $XY$ passes through $K(2d, 0)$, consistent with the ratio $DK/DH_0 = 1 - \cos A = a/s$.

\(\square\)
