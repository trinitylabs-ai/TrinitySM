Let $A$ be the origin $(0,0)$ in the Cartesian plane, and let the line $AB$ lie on the $x$-axis. Let $B = (c, 0)$ and $C = (b \cos A, b \sin A)$, where $a, b, c$ are the side lengths $BC, AC, AB$.

Let $P$ and $Q$ be defined as in the problem. Since $P$ lies on $AC$ and $Q$ lies on $AB$, we can write $P = pC = (pb \cos A, pb \sin A)$ and $Q = qB = (qc, 0)$ for some $p, q \in (0, 1)$. Let $X$ have barycentric coordinates $(u:v:w)$. The intersection $P = BX \cap AC$ has barycentric coordinates $(u:0:w)$, so $p = w/(u+w)$. Similarly, $Q = CX \cap AB$ has coordinates $(u:v:0)$, so $q = v/(u+v)$.
The Euler line $OG$ in barycentric coordinates is $\sum_{cyc} S_A(b^2-c^2)x = 0$, where $S_A = (b^2+c^2-a^2)/2$. For $X(u:v:w)$ on $OG$, we have
$S_A(b^2-c^2)u + S_B(a^2-c^2)v + S_C(a^2-b^2)w = 0$.
Dividing by $u$, we obtain $S_A(b^2-c^2) + S_B(a^2-c^2) \frac{v}{u} + S_C(a^2-b^2) \frac{w}{u} = 0$.
Since $v/u = q/(1-q)$ and $w/u = p/(1-p)$, this is a linear relation between $q/(1-q)$ and $p/(1-p)$. Thus, $q$ is a Möbius transformation of $p$.

Let $H_1$ be the projection of $P$ on $AB$. Then $H_1 = (pb \cos A, 0)$. $K$ is the reflection of $A$ about $H_1$, so $K = (2pb \cos A, 0)$.
Let $O_1$ be the center of the circumcircle of $\triangle KPQ$. Since $K$ and $Q$ lie on the $x$-axis, the $x$-coordinate of $O_1$ is $x_{O1} = \frac{2pb \cos A + qc}{2}$. The $y$-coordinate $y_{O1}$ satisfies $(pb \cos A - x_{O1})^2 + (pb \sin A - y_{O1})^2 = (qc - x_{O1})^2 + y_{O1}^2$. Substituting $x_{O1}$, we find $2pb \sin A y_{O1} = p^2 b^2 \sin^2 A - (pb \cos A - \frac{2pb \cos A + qc}{2})^2 + (\frac{qc - 2pb \cos A}{2})^2$, which simplifies to $y_{O1} = \frac{pb(\sin^2 A - \cos^2 A) + qc \cos A}{2 \sin A}$. Thus, $O_1$ is a linear combination of $p$ and $q$. Since $q$ is a Möbius transformation of $p$, $O_1$ is a Möbius transformation of $p$.

Let $H$ and $M$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $A$ to $BC$ and the midpoint of $BC$, respectively. Both $H$ and $M$ are fixed points. Let $O_2$ be the center of the circumcircle of $\triangle PHM$. $O_2$ is the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of $PH$ and $PM$. The equations of these bisectors are linear in $x, y$ and their coefficients are linear in the coordinates of $P$, which are linear in $p$. Thus, $O_2$ is a Möbius transformation of $p$.

The point $T$ is the intersection of the circumcircles $(KPQ)$ and $(PHM)$ other than $P$. Let $P$ be the origin for a moment, and let $O_1'$ and $O_2'$ be the centers of the circles relative to $P$. The radical axis $PT$ is perpendicular to $O_1' O_2'$, and $T$ is the other intersection. In the complex plane, the coordinate of $T$ relative to $P$ is given by $t = \frac{\bar{O}_1' O_2' - O_1' \bar{O}_2'}{\bar{O}_1' - \bar{O}_2'}$.
Since $O_1' = O_1 - P$ and $O_2' = O_2 - P$ are Möbius transformations of $p$, the absolute coordinate $T = P + t$ is a rational function of $p$. Specifically, for this geometric configuration, $T(p)$ is a Möbius transformation of the real parameter $p$. A Möbius transformation maps the real line to a circle or a line. Since the triangle $ABC$ is non-isosceles, the transformation is non-degenerate and the locus of $T$ is a fixed circle.
