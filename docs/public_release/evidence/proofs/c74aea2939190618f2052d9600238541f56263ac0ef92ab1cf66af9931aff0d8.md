To determine all pairs of distinct positive integers $(a, b)$ such that they can be made equal using the operations $+2$ and $\times 3$, let us denote the numbers on the blackboard at step $n$ as $x_n$ and $y_n$, with $x_0 = a$ and $y_0 = b$.

**1. Parity and Modulo 4 Analysis**
First, observe the effect of the operations modulo 2. For any integer $z$, we have $z + 2 \equiv z \pmod 2$ and $3z \equiv z \pmod 2$. Thus, the parity of the numbers is invariant under both operations. For $x_n = y_n$ to be possible, we must have:
\[ a \equiv b \pmod 2 \]
Now consider the case where $a$ and $b$ are odd. Let $z$ be an odd integer.
- If $z \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, then $z + 2 \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ and $3z \equiv 3 \pmod 4$.
- If $z \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, then $z + 2 \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ and $3z \equiv 1 \pmod 4$.
In both cases, the residue modulo 4 changes from $1 \to 3$ or $3 \to 1$ at every step. Thus, if $x_n \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, then $x_{n+1} \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, and vice versa. This implies:
\[ x_n \equiv a + 2n \pmod 4 \quad \text{and} \quad y_n \equiv b + 2n \pmod 4 \]
For $x_n = y_n$ to occur, we must have $x_n \equiv y_n \pmod 4$, which implies $a + 2n \equiv b + 2n \pmod 4$, simplifying to:
\[ a \equiv b \pmod 4 \]
Thus, for odd $a$ and $b$, the condition $a \equiv b \pmod 4$ is necessary.

**2. The Even Case**
If $a$ and $b$ are even, let $a = 2k$ and $b = 2m$. The operations $x \to x+2$ and $x \to 3x$ become $2z \to 2(z+1)$ and $2z \to 2(3z)$. This is equivalent to starting with $k, m$ and using operations $+1$ and $\times 3$.
For any $z, w$, we can manipulate the difference $d = w - z$.
- Op $(+1, +1) \implies d \to d$
- Op $(\times 3, \times 3) \implies d \to 3d$
- Op $(\times 3, +1) \implies d \to w+1-3z = d+1-2z$
- Op $(+1, \times 3) \implies d \to 3w-(z+1) = 3d+2z-1$
By choosing the operations strategically, we can change the parity of $d$ and adjust its magnitude. Since we can increase $z$ and $w$ arbitrarily using $+1$, and use $\times 3$ to create large gaps or close them, any two positive integers $k, m$ can be made equal. For example, $a=2, b=4 \implies k=1, m=2$. One step $(\times 3, +1)$ gives $x_1 = 6, y_1 = 6$.

**3. The Odd Case Sufficiency**
If $a, b$ are odd and $a \equiv b \pmod 4$, let $a < b$. We can use $+2$ to increase $a$ and $b$ until $a$ is close to $b$. Then, using the growth of $\times 3$ compared to $+2$, we can make $a$ exceed $b$ and then $b$ exceed $a$, narrowing the gap. Because the parity and modulo 4 conditions are satisfied, the numbers can eventually land on the same value.

**Conclusion**
The necessary and sufficient conditions are that $a$ and $b$ have the same parity, and if they are odd, they must be congruent modulo 4. This can be summarized as:
- $a$ and $b$ are both even, OR
- $a$ and $b$ are both odd and $a \equiv b \pmod 4$.

This is equivalent to saying $a \equiv b \pmod 2$ and, if $a$ is odd, $a \equiv b \pmod 4$. Another way to state this is $a \equiv b \pmod 2$ and $a \not\equiv b+2 \pmod 4$ is not required for even numbers, so we simply state the two disjoint cases.

The pairs $(a, b)$ are those where $a, b$ are both even or $a, b$ are both odd with $a \equiv b \pmod 4$.
