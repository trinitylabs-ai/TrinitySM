To find the maximum possible value $M(f)$ of $t$ for a set of students $\{A_1, A_2, \dots, A_t\}$ such that any two elements $A_i, A_j$ have a "lovely relationship," we analyze the properties of the function $f: F \to F$.

**1. Analyzing the Function $f$**
The set $F$ consists of all $2^{120}$ subsets of students $S = \{1, 2, \dots, 120\}$. Let $L_i \subseteq S$ be the set of students loved by student $i$. The function $f$ is defined as:
\[ f(X) = \bigcup_{i \in X} L_i \]
This is a linear map over the Boolean semiring $(\mathcal{P}(S), \cup, \cap)$. A key property of such maps is that the image of a set is the union of the images of its elements:
\[ f^k(X) = \bigcup_{i \in X} f^k(\{i\}) \]
where $f^k$ denotes the $k$-fold composition of $f$.

**2. The Orbit of an Element $X$**
For any fixed student $i \in S$, the sequence of sets $f^k(\{i\})$ for $k=0, 1, 2, \dots$ corresponds to the set of nodes reachable from $i$ in exactly $k$ steps in the directed "love-graph" $G = (S, E)$, where $(u, v) \in E$ if $u$ loves $v$.
Since $S$ is finite, the sequence $f^k(\{i\})$ must eventually become periodic. The period $p_i$ is the least common multiple (lcm) of the lengths of the cycles reachable from $i$ in $G$. The pre-period $m_i$ is the maximum distance from $i$ to any such cycle.
For any $X \in F$, the orbit $X, f(X), f^2(X), \dots$ also becomes periodic. The period $p_X$ is the lcm of the periods $p_i$ for all $i \in X$, and the pre-period $m_X$ is $\max_{i \in X} m_i$.

**3. Maximum Orbit Size**
The maximum possible value for the pre-period $m_X$ is $n = 120$. The maximum possible period $p_X$ is given by Landau's function $g(n)$, which is the maximum lcm of a partition of $n$. For $n=120$, $g(120)$ is the maximum lcm of positive integers whose sum is at most 120. We can estimate $g(120)$ by taking the product of distinct primes whose sum is $\leq 120$:
$2+3+5+7+11+13+17+19+23 = 100$.
The product is $2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 \cdot 11 \cdot 13 \cdot 17 \cdot 19 \cdot 23 \approx 2.23 \times 10^8$.
Even with optimizations (using prime powers), $g(120)$ is significantly smaller than $2^{70} \approx 1.18 \times 10^{21}$.

**4. Lovely Relationships and the Maximum Value of $t$**
The relation $A R B \iff \exists k \in \mathbb{N} : f^k(A) = B$ is a preorder (transitive and reflexive if $k=0$ is allowed). A "lovely relationship" between $A$ and $B$ exists if $A R B$. The problem requires that for any $i < j$, $A_i$ and $A_j$ have a lovely relationship. 
If $A R B$ and $B R A$, they belong to the same cycle in the functional graph of $f$. If $A R B$ but not $B R A$, $B$ is an image of $A$ further down the path. Thus, the set $\{A_1, \dots, A_t\}$ must lie on a single path in the functional graph of $f$ (comprising a pre-period and a cycle).
The total number of distinct elements on any such path is at most $m_X + p_X \leq n + g(n)$.
For $n=120$:
\[ M(f) \leq 120 + g(120) \approx 2.23 \times 10^8 \ll 2^{70} \]
Thus, for any possible love relation $f$, the maximum size $t$ of such a set is far below $2^{70}$.

$\square$
