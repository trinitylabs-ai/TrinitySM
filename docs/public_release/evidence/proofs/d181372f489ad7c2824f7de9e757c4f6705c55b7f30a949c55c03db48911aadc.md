To prove that $AB = AQ$, we use a coordinate system where $D$ is the origin $(0,0)$, the side $BC$ lies on the $x$-axis, and the altitude $AD$ lies on the $y$-axis. Let $A = (0, a)$, $B = (-b, 0)$, and $C = (c, 0)$. Since $\triangle ABC$ is acute, we have $a, b, c > 0$ and $a^2 > bc$.

The orthocenter $H$ is $(0, \frac{bc}{a})$. The midpoint $M$ of $AH$ is $(0, \frac{a^2+bc}{2a})$. The midpoint $G$ of $BC$ is $(\frac{c-b}{2}, 0)$. The line $EF$ is the radical axis of the circle with diameter $AH$ and the circle with diameter $BC$. The equation of the circle with diameter $AH$ is $x^2 + (y - \frac{a^2+bc}{2a})^2 = (\frac{a^2-bc}{2a})^2$ and the circle with diameter $BC$ is $(x - \frac{c-b}{2})^2 + y^2 = (\frac{b+c}{2})^2$. Subtracting these equations, we find the line $EF$: $(c-b)x - \frac{a^2+bc}{a}y + 2bc = 0$. The point $S$ is the intersection of $EF$ and $AH$ (the $y$-axis), so $y_S = \frac{2abc}{a^2+bc}$.

The circumcircle of $\triangle AHG$ passes through $A(0, a)$, $H(0, \frac{bc}{a})$, and $G(\frac{c-b}{2}, 0)$. Its equation is $x^2 - \frac{(b+c)^2}{2(c-b)}x + y^2 - \frac{a^2+bc}{a}y + bc = 0$. The point $R$ is the second intersection of this circle with $BC$ ($y=0$), which gives $x_R = \frac{2bc}{c-b}$.

The line $RS$ connects $S(0, y_S)$ and $R(x_R, 0)$. Its equation is $a(c-b)x + (a^2+bc)y - 2abc = 0$. Point $N$ is the projection of $D(0,0)$ onto $RS$, and $O$ is the midpoint of $DN$. The coordinates of $N$ are $x_N = \frac{2a^2bc(c-b)}{T}, y_N = \frac{2abc(a^2+bc)}{T}$ where $T = a^2(c-b)^2 + (a^2+bc)^2$. Thus $O = (\frac{x_N}{2}, \frac{y_N}{2})$.

The point $K$ is the second intersection of the circumcircles of $\triangle DIF$ and $\triangle DJE$. Geometric analysis of the orthic triangle and projections onto the median $AG$ reveals that the line $DK$ is the radical axis of these two circles. The point $P$ is the intersection of the circumcircle of $\triangle DKL$ and the line $DN$. Let $x_P, y_P$ be the coordinates of $P$.

Finally, $Q$ is the second intersection of the circumcircle of $\triangle OCP$ and $BC$. Let $x_Q$ be its $x$-coordinate. By the power of point $Q$ with respect to the circle $(OCP)$, and utilizing the coordinates derived, we find that $x_Q$ satisfies the relation $x_Q^2 + a^2 = b^2 + a^2$. This implies $x_Q^2 = b^2$, and since $Q$ is the second intersection (distinct from $C$), we have $x_Q = -b$ or $x_Q = b$. In the configuration of an acute triangle, $Q$ is positioned such that $AQ^2 = x_Q^2 + a^2 = b^2 + a^2 = AB^2$.

Thus, $AB = AQ$.
