To determine which player has a winning strategy, we analyze the conditions under which a road is constructed between two cities $A$ and $B$. Let the set of cities be $\mathcal{C} = \{C_i\}_{i=1}^\infty$, represented by complex numbers $c_i$. Alice chooses points $P, Q$ and a set $S$. Let $\Sigma = \{ \frac{r-p}{q-p} : R \in S \}$. A road exists between $A$ and $B$ if and only if for every city $C \in \mathcal{C} \setminus \{A, B\}$, $\triangle PQR$ is directly similar to $\triangle ABC$ or $\triangle BAC$ for some $R \in S$. This is equivalent to the condition:
\[ \frac{c-a}{b-a} \in \Sigma \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{c-b}{a-b} \in \Sigma. \]
Using the identity $\frac{c-b}{a-b} = 1 - \frac{c-a}{b-a}$, we define $\Omega = \Sigma \cup \{1-s : s \in \Sigma\}$. The condition for a road $AB$ is that for every $C \in \mathcal{C} \setminus \{A, B\}$, $\frac{c-a}{b-a} \in \Omega$. Let $W_{AB} = \{ \frac{c-a}{b-a} : c \in \mathcal{C} \setminus \{A, B\} \}$. A road exists between $A$ and $B$ if and only if $W_{AB} \subseteq \Omega$.

Bob wins if he can choose $\mathcal{C}$ such that the resulting graph is either disconnected or non-planar, while satisfying $dist(C_i, C_j) > 1$ and ensuring no three cities are collinear. Note that the "no three collinear" constraint implies that for any $A, B, C \in \mathcal{C}$, $\frac{c-a}{b-a} \notin \mathbb{R}$.

We consider two cases based on the set $\Omega^c \setminus \mathbb{R}$ (the set of non-real numbers not in $\Omega$):

**Case 1: $\Omega^c \setminus \mathbb{R}$ is infinite.**
Bob can ensure that no roads exist by making the graph disconnected. He constructs $\mathcal{C}$ iteratively. Starting with two cities $C_1, C_2$, he identifies all pairs $(C_i, C_j)$ currently in the set. For each pair, he adds a new city $C_k = c_i + w(c_j - c_i)$ where $w \in \Omega^c \setminus \mathbb{R}$. Since $w \notin \mathbb{R}$, $C_k$ is not collinear with $C_i$ and $C_j$. Since $\Omega^c \setminus \mathbb{R}$ is infinite, Bob can choose $w$ such that $C_k$ is not collinear with any other pair of existing cities and $dist(C_k, C_m) > 1$ for all $m$. By adding such a city for every pair, he ensures $W_{AB} \not\subseteq \Omega$ for all $A, B$. Even as new cities are added, creating new pairs, Bob can repeat this process in countably many stages to kill all potential roads. Thus, the graph is disconnected, and Bob wins.

**Case 2: $\Omega^c \setminus \mathbb{R}$ is finite.**
Let $\Omega^c \setminus \mathbb{R} = \{z_1, z_2, \dots, z_m\}$. Bob can ensure that every pair of cities is connected by a road. He constructs $\mathcal{C}$ inductively. For the $n$-th city $C_n$, he ensures:
1. $dist(C_n, C_i) > 1$ for all $i < n$.
2. $C_n$ does not lie on any line $C_i C_j$ for $i, j < n$.
3. For all $i, j < n$, $\frac{c_n-c_i}{c_j-c_i} \notin \{z_1, \dots, z_m\}$.
Since the set of forbidden points and lines is finite, such a $C_n$ always exists in the plane. Under these conditions, for any three cities $A, B, C \in \mathcal{C}$, we have $\frac{c-a}{b-a} \notin \mathbb{R}$ (by the collinearity constraint) and $\frac{c-a}{b-a} \notin \{z_1, \dots, z_m\}$. Therefore, $\frac{c-a}{b-a} \in \Omega$ for all $A, B, C$. This implies $W_{AB} \subseteq \Omega$ for all pairs $A, B$, making the resulting graph the complete graph $K_\infty$. Since $K_5$ is non-planar, $K_\infty$ is non-planar, and Bob wins.

In both cases, Bob has a winning strategy regardless of Alice's choice of $P, Q$, and $S$.

Bob has a winning strategy. \(\square\)
