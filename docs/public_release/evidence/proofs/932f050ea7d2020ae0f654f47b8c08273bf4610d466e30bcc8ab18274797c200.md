To prove that the point $T$ moves along a fixed circle as $X$ moves along the line $OG$, we use the properties of spiral similarities and the geometry of the Euler line.

**1. Identification of $T$ as a Center of Spiral Similarity**
The point $T$ is defined as the intersection of the circumcircle of $\triangle KPQ$ and the circumcircle of $\triangle PHM$ other than $P$. A known property of two circles $\odot(PAB)$ and $\odot(PCD)$ intersecting at $P$ and $T$ is that $T$ is the center of the spiral similarity that maps the segment $AB$ to the segment $CD$.
In our case, the circles are $\odot(PKQ)$ and $\odot(PHM)$. Thus, $T$ is the center of the spiral similarity that maps the segment $KQ$ to the segment $HM$. This means that $\triangle TKQ \sim \triangle THM$.

**2. Analytical Expression for $T$**
Let $A$ be the origin $(0,0)$ in the complex plane. Let the line $AB$ lie on the real axis.
The points $Q$ and $K$ lie on $AB$, so their coordinates are real: $Q = q$ and $K = k$, where $q = qc$ and $k = 2pb \cos A$ (using the definitions of $P$ and $Q$ from the problem).
The points $H$ and $M$ lie on the line $BC$. Let the line $BC$ be represented by the direction $e^{iB}$. Then $H = h e^{iB}$ and $M = m e^{iB}$ relative to some origin on $BC$, but more generally, $H$ and $M$ are fixed points.
The center $T$ of the spiral similarity mapping $KQ \to HM$ is the unique point satisfying:
\[ \frac{T-K}{T-Q} = \frac{T-H}{T-M} \implies (T-k)(T-M) = (T-q)(T-H) \]
Expanding this equation:
\[ T^2 - T(K+M) + KM = T^2 - T(Q+H) + QH \]
\[ T(Q+H-K-M) = QH - KM \]
\[ T = \frac{QH - KM}{(Q+H) - (K+M)} \]
Substituting $Q=q$ and $K=k$, we see that $T$ is a Möbius transformation of $q$ and $k$:
\[ T(q, k) = \frac{qH - kM}{q - k + H - M} \]

**3. The Constraint on $p$ and $q$**
The point $X$ lies on the line $OG$. Let $p = AP/AC$ and $q = AQ/AB$. As derived from Ceva's Theorem and the equation of the Euler line in barycentric coordinates, the condition that $X$ lies on the line $OG$ is a bilinear equation in $p$ and $q$:
\[ \alpha pq + \beta p + \gamma q + \delta = 0 \]
This implies that $p$ is a Möbius transformation of $q$: $p = \frac{-\gamma q - \delta}{\alpha q + \beta}$. Since $k = 2pb \cos A$, $k$ is also a Möbius transformation of $q$.

**4. Conclusion**
Substituting $k(q)$ into the expression for $T(q, k)$, we find that $T$ is a Möbius transformation of the single parameter $q$:
\[ T = \frac{qH - k(q)M}{q - k(q) + H - M} \]
A Möbius transformation maps a line (the locus of $q$ as $X$ moves on $OG$) to a circle or a line. Since the triangle $ABC$ is non-isosceles and the points are constrained, $T$ traces a fixed circle.

$\square$
