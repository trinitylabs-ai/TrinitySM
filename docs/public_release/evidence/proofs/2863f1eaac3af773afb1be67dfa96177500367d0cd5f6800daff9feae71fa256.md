To find all pairs of distinct positive integers $(a, b)$ such that they can be made equal after a finite number of steps using the operations $x \mapsto x+2$ and $x \mapsto 3x$, we analyze the invariants and necessary conditions.

### 1. Parity Analysis
Observe that both operations $f_1(x) = x+2$ and $f_2(x) = 3x$ preserve parity:
- $x+2 \equiv x \pmod 2$
- $3x \equiv x \pmod 2$
If $a$ and $b$ have different parities, they will always have different parities regardless of the operations chosen. Thus, a necessary condition for $a$ and $b$ to become equal is $a \equiv b \pmod 2$.

### 2. Analysis for Odd Integers
Suppose $a$ and $b$ are odd. Consider their values modulo 4.
- If $x \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, then $x+2 \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ and $3x \equiv 3 \pmod 4$.
- If $x \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, then $x+2 \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ and $3x \equiv 1 \pmod 4$.
In both cases, the value of $x \pmod 4$ flips between 1 and 3 at every step. Thus, if $a$ and $b$ are odd, then $a_n \equiv b_n \pmod 4$ if and only if $a_0 \equiv b_0 \pmod 4$. For the numbers to be equal, we must have $a_n = b_n$, which implies $a_n \equiv b_n \pmod 4$. Therefore, $a \equiv b \pmod 4$ is a necessary condition for odd $a, b$.

### 3. Analysis for Even Integers
Suppose $a$ and $b$ are even. Let $a=2m$ and $b=2n$. The operations $x \mapsto x+2$ and $x \mapsto 3x$ correspond to operations on $m, n$:
- $2m \mapsto 2m+2 = 2(m+1) \implies m \mapsto m+1$
- $2m \mapsto 3(2m) = 2(3m) \implies m \mapsto 3m$
We wish to determine if any two positive integers $m, n$ can be made equal using $x \mapsto x+1$ and $x \mapsto 3x$. Let $f(m, n) = n-3m$.
- The operation $(m+1, n+1)$ gives $f \to (n+1)-3(m+1) = f-2$.
- The operation $(3m, n+1)$ gives $f \to (n+1)-9m = f-6m+1$.
- The operation $(m+1, 3n)$ gives $f \to 3n-3(m+1) = 3(n-m-1)$.

If $f < -1$, we can make $f$ positive by repeatedly applying $m \mapsto m+1$ and $n \mapsto 3n$. After $k$ such steps, $n_k = 3^k n_0$ and $m_k = m_0 + k$, so $f_k = 3^k n_0 - 3(m_0 + k)$, which is positive for sufficiently large $k$. Once $f > -1$, if $f$ is even, the operation $(3m, n+1)$ makes $f$ odd since $f-6m+1 \equiv 1 \pmod 2$. If $f$ is odd and $f > -1$, we can repeatedly apply $(m+1, n+1)$ to reach $f=-1$ because the sequence $f, f-2, f-4, \dots$ will eventually hit $-1$. Once $f=-1$, we have $n=3m-1$. In the next step, applying $m \mapsto 3m$ and $n \mapsto n+1$ yields $3m = (3m-1)+1 = 3m$, achieving equality. Thus, all pairs of even integers can be made equal.

### 4. Sufficiency for Odd Integers
If $a, b$ are odd and $a \equiv b \pmod 4$, let $a=2m-1$ and $b=2n-1$. The condition $a \equiv b \pmod 4$ implies $m \equiv n \pmod 2$. The operations are:
- $2m-1 \mapsto 2m+1 \implies m \mapsto m+1$
- $2m-1 \mapsto 3(2m-1) = 6m-3 = 2(3m-1)-1 \implies m \mapsto 3m-1$

Let $f(m, n) = n-3m+2$. Since $m \equiv n \pmod 2$, $f$ is always even. The operations on $f$ are:
- $(m+1, n+1) \to (n+1)-3(m+1)+2 = f-2$.
- $(m+1, 3n-1) \to (3n-1)-3(m+1)+2 = 3n-3m-2 = 3(n-m)-2$.
By repeatedly applying $(m+1, 3n-1)$, we can make $f$ positive. Then, by repeatedly applying $(m+1, n+1)$, we can reach $f=0$ (since $f$ is even), meaning $n=3m-2$. Finally, applying the operations $m \to 3m-1$ and $n \to n+1$ yields $m = 3m-1$ and $n = (3m-2)+1 = 3m-1$, achieving equality.

### Conclusion
The necessary and sufficient conditions are that $a$ and $b$ must have the same parity, and if they are odd, they must be congruent modulo 4. This can be stated as: $a, b$ are both even, or $a, b$ are both odd and $a \equiv b \pmod 4$.
