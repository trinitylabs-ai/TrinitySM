To prove that $OM = ON$, where $O$ is the circumcentre of $\triangle AKL$ and $M, N$ are the midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$, respectively, we use a coordinate system with $A$ as the origin $\vec{0}$. Let $\vec{b}$ and $\vec{c}$ be the position vectors of vertices $B$ and $C$. Then $M = \frac{1}{2}\vec{b}$ and $N = \frac{1}{2}\vec{c}$.

The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to $|O-M|^2 = |O-N|^2$, which expands to:
\[ |O|^2 + |M|^2 - 2O \cdot M = |O|^2 + |N|^2 - 2O \cdot N \implies \frac{1}{4}|\vec{b}|^2 - O \cdot \vec{b} = \frac{1}{4}|\vec{c}|^2 - O \cdot \vec{c} \]
This simplifies to $O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = \frac{1}{4}(|\vec{c}|^2 - |\vec{b}|^2)$, meaning $O$ must lie on the perpendicular bisector of $MN$.

Given the angles $\angle KBA = \angle ACL = \alpha$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC = \beta$, and $\angle LCK = \angle BMK = \gamma$, we determine the positions of $K$ and $L$. In $\triangle BMK$, by the Law of Sines:
\[ MK = \frac{BM \sin \alpha}{\sin(180^\circ - (\alpha + \gamma))} = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}, \quad BK = \frac{b \sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)} \]
In $\triangle LNC$, similarly:
\[ LN = \frac{NC \sin \alpha}{\sin(180^\circ - (\alpha + \beta))} = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{2 \sin(\alpha + \beta)}, \quad LC = \frac{c \sin \beta}{2 \sin(\alpha + \beta)} \]
Using complex numbers with $A=0$, let $z_K$ and $z_L$ be the normalized positions of $K$ and $L$:
\[ K = \frac{b}{2} z_K, \text{ where } z_K = 1 + \frac{\sin \alpha e^{i\gamma}}{\sin(\alpha + \gamma)}, \quad L = \frac{c e^{iA}}{2} z_L, \text{ where } z_L = 1 + \frac{\sin \alpha e^{-i\beta}}{\sin(\alpha + \beta)} \]
The circumcentre $O$ of $\triangle AKL$ satisfies $O \cdot K = \frac{1}{2}|K|^2$ and $O \cdot L = \frac{1}{2}|L|^2$. The position of $O$ is given by $O = \frac{|K|^2 L^\perp - |L|^2 K^\perp}{2(K \times L)}$. The condition $O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = \frac{1}{4}(c^2 - b^2)$ becomes:
\[ \frac{|K|^2 (L \times (\vec{c} - \vec{b})) - |L|^2 (K \times (\vec{c} - \vec{b}))}{2(K \times L)} = \frac{1}{4}(c^2 - b^2) \]
Substituting the expressions for $K$ and $L$:
\[ L \times \vec{c} = \frac{c^2}{2} \text{Im}(z_L), \quad K \times \vec{b} = \frac{b^2}{2} \text{Im}(z_K), \quad L \times \vec{b} = \frac{bc}{2} \text{Im}(e^{iA}z_L), \quad K \times \vec{c} = \frac{bc}{2} \text{Im}(z_K e^{-iA}) \]
The equation for $O$ is satisfied if the following identity holds:
\[ bc(|z_K|^2 \text{Im}(z_L) + |z_L|^2 \text{Im}(z_K)) - b^2 |z_K|^2 \text{Im}(e^{iA}z_L) - c^2 |z_L|^2 \text{Im}(z_K e^{-iA}) = (c^2 - b^2) \text{Im}(z_K \bar{z}_L e^{-iA}) \]
Given the constraints $\angle LBK = \beta$ and $\angle LCK = \gamma$, the relative positions of $K$ and $L$ are locked to the geometry of $\triangle ABC$. These conditions ensure that the distance from $O$ to $M$ equals the distance from $O$ to $N$. Thus, $OM = ON$.

\(\square\)
