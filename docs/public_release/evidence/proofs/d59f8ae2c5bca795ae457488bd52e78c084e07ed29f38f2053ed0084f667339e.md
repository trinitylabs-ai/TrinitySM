To prove that we can choose two diagonals of a convex $(18n+2)$-gon $P$, divided into $18n$ triangles, such that the three resulting parts each contain at least $3n$ and at most $9n$ triangles, we analyze the dual graph of the triangulation.

### 1. Dual Graph Representation
A triangulation of a convex polygon $P$ can be represented by its dual graph $T$. In this graph, each triangle in the triangulation corresponds to a vertex, and an edge exists between two vertices if the corresponding triangles share a common diagonal. 
For a convex $(18n+2)$-gon divided into $18n$ triangles, the dual graph $T$ is a tree with $N = 18n$ vertices. Furthermore, since each triangle has at most three sides, each vertex in $T$ has a degree of at most 3.

Removing a diagonal of $P$ corresponds to removing an edge of $T$, which splits the tree into two connected components. The number of triangles in each resulting part of $P$ is equal to the number of vertices in the corresponding connected component of $T$.

### 2. The First Diagonal
A centroid of a tree $T$ is a vertex $v$ such that every connected component of $T \setminus \{v\}$ has a size of at most $N/2$. For our tree $T$ with $N=18n$ vertices, there exists a centroid $v$ such that any component $C_i$ resulting from the removal of $v$ satisfies $|C_i| \le 9n$.

Since the total number of vertices in the components is $\sum |C_i| = 18n - 1$ and there are at most three components (because the degree of $v$ is at most 3), by the Pigeonhole Principle, at least one component $C_1$ must satisfy:
$$|C_1| \ge \left\lceil \frac{18n-1}{3} \right\rceil = 6n.$$
Let $e_1$ be the edge in $T$ connecting $v$ to the root of $C_1$. Removing $e_1$ splits $T$ into two subtrees, $T_A$ and $T_B$. Let $s_A$ and $s_B$ be their respective sizes.
Based on the bounds for $|C_1|$, we have:
$$6n \le s_A \le 9n \quad \text{and} \quad s_B = 18n - s_A \in [9n, 12n].$$

### 3. The Second Diagonal
We now search for a second edge $e_2$ within the subtree $T_B$ to split it into two components $T_{B1}$ and $T_{B2}$ with sizes $s_1$ and $s_2$.
Let $v'$ be the centroid of $T_B$. Removing $v'$ splits $T_B$ into components $C'_i$ such that $|C'_i| \le s_B/2$. Since $s_B \le 12n$, we have $|C'_i| \le 6n$.
The total size of these components is $\sum |C'_i| = s_B - 1$. Again, since the degree of $v'$ is at most 3, at least one component $C'_j$ must satisfy:
$$|C'_j| \ge \left\lceil \frac{s_B-1}{3} \right\rceil \ge \left\lceil \frac{9n-1}{3} \right\rceil = 3n.$$
Let $e_2$ be the edge connecting $v'$ to the root of $C'_j$. Removing $e_2$ splits $T_B$ into $T_{B1}$ and $T_{B2}$ with sizes:
$$s_1 = |C'_j| \in [3n, 6n] \quad \text{and} \quad s_2 = s_B - s_1.$$

### 4. Final Size Verification
The two diagonals corresponding to edges $e_1$ and $e_2$ divide $P$ into three parts with sizes $s_1, s_2,$ and $s_3 = s_A$. We verify the bounds:
1.  $s_3 = s_A \in [6n, 9n]$, which satisfies $3n \le s_3 \le 9n$.
2.  $s_1 \in [3n, 6n]$, which satisfies $3n \le s_1 \le 9n$.
3.  For $s_2$, we use the bounds $s_B \in [9n, 12n]$ and $s_1 \in [3n, 6n]$:
    - Minimum $s_2 = \min(s_B) - \max(s_1) = 9n - 6n = 3n$.
    - Maximum $s_2 = \max(s_B) - \min(s_1) = 12n - 3n = 9n$.
    Thus, $s_2 \in [3n, 9n]$.

All three parts contain at least $3n$ and at most $9n$ triangles. $\square$
