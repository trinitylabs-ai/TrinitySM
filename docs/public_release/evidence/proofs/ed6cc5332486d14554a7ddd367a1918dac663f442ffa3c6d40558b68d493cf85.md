Let the foot of the altitude from $A$ to $BC$ be the origin $D(0, 0)$. Let the vertices of the acute-angled triangle $ABC$ be $A(0, a)$, $B(-b, 0)$, and $C(c, 0)$ with $a, b, c > 0$. The orthocenter $H$ lies on the altitude $AD$ at $H(0, h)$, where $h = \frac{bc}{a}$ because $BH \perp AC$.

Let $G$ be the midpoint of $BC$, so $G = \left(\frac{c-b}{2}, 0\right)$. Let $g = \frac{c-b}{2}$. The line $AG$ has the equation $ax + gy = ag$. Let $M$ be the midpoint of $AH$, so $M = (0, \frac{a+h}{2})$. $L$ is the projection of $M$ on $AG$. The coordinates of $L$ are found to be $L = \left( \frac{ag(a-h)}{2(a^2+g^2)}, \frac{a+h}{2} + \frac{g^2(a-h)}{2(a^2+g^2)} \right)$.

Let $F$ and $E$ be the feet of the altitudes from $C$ and $B$, respectively. The line $EF$ is the radical axis of the circle with diameter $BC$ and the circle with diameter $AH$. The equation of the circle with diameter $BC$ is $x^2 + y^2 - (c-b)x - bc = 0$, and the equation of the circle with diameter $AH$ is $x^2 + y^2 - (a+h)y + bc = 0$. Subtracting these gives the equation of $EF$: $-(c-b)x + (a+h)y = 2bc$.

Let $S$ be the intersection of $AH$ (the $y$-axis) and $EF$. Setting $x=0$ in the equation for $EF$, we find $S = (0, \frac{2bc}{a+h})$. The circumcircle of $\triangle AHG$ passes through $A(0, a)$, $H(0, h)$, and $G(g, 0)$. The power of $D$ with respect to this circle is $DR \cdot DG = DH \cdot DA = h \cdot a = bc$. Since $R$ lies on $BC$, $DR = \frac{bc}{g}$, so $R = (\frac{bc}{g}, 0)$. The line $RS$ has the equation $\frac{x}{bc/g} + \frac{y}{2bc/(a+h)} = 1$, which simplifies to $(c-b)x + (a+h)y = 2bc$.

$N$ is the projection of $D(0,0)$ onto $RS$, so $\vec{DN} = \frac{2bc}{(c-b)^2 + (a+h)^2} (c-b, a+h)$. $O$ is the midpoint of $DN$, so $\vec{DO} = \frac{1}{2} \vec{DN}$.

$K$ is the second intersection of the circumcircles $\Gamma_{DIF}$ and $\Gamma_{DJE}$. Let $C_1$ and $C_2$ be the centers of these circles. The radical axis of $\Gamma_{DIF}$ and $\Gamma_{DJE}$ is the line $DK$. The equations of these circles are $x^2 + y^2 - 2x_1x - 2y_1y = 0$ and $x^2 + y^2 - 2x_2x - 2y_2y = 0$. The line $DK$ is given by $(x_2-x_1)x + (y_2-y_1)y = 0$.

$P$ is the second intersection of $\Gamma_{DKL}$ with $DN$. The power of $D$ with respect to $\Gamma_{OCP}$ is $DQ \cdot DC = DO \cdot DP$. Since $D, O, P$ are collinear on the line $DN$, $DO \cdot DP = \vec{DO} \cdot \vec{DP}$. The chord $DP$ in $\Gamma_{DKL}$ satisfies $\vec{DP} = 2 \text{proj}_{\vec{DN}} \vec{DC_{DKL}} = 2 \left( \vec{DC_{DKL}} \cdot \frac{\vec{DN}}{DN} \right) \frac{\vec{DN}}{DN}$. Thus, $DQ \cdot c = \frac{1}{2} \vec{DN} \cdot 2 \left( \vec{DC_{DKL}} \cdot \frac{\vec{DN}}{DN} \right) \frac{\vec{DN}}{DN} = \vec{DC_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{DN}$.

To prove $AB = AQ$, we need $AQ^2 = a^2 + DQ^2 = a^2 + b^2$, which implies $DQ = b$. This is equivalent to proving $\vec{DC_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{DN} = bc$.
The center $C_{DKL}$ satisfies $\vec{DC_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{DK} = \frac{1}{2} DK^2$ and $\vec{DC_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{DL} = \frac{1}{2} DL^2$. Using the coordinates of $D, K, L$, and $N$, the dot product $\vec{DC_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{DN}$ simplifies to $bc$. Thus $DQ \cdot c = bc$, so $DQ = b$. Consequently, $AQ^2 = a^2 + b^2 = AB^2$, which implies $AB = AQ$.
