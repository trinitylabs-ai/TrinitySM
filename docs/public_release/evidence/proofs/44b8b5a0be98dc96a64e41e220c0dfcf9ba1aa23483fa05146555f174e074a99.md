Let $a, b, c$ be the side lengths of $\triangle ABC$, $s$ its semiperimeter, $r$ its inradius, and $R$ its circumradius. Let $I$ and $O$ be the incenter and circumcenter, and $H_0$ the foot of the altitude from $A$ to $BC$.

First, we derive the condition for the segment $EF$ to be tangent to the incircle. The triangle $AEF$ is similar to $\triangle ABC$ with a ratio of $\cos A$, and the line $EF$ is perpendicular to the line $AO$. The distance from $A$ to the line $EF$ is $h_a \cos A$. The distance from the incenter $I$ to the line $EF$ is given by
\[ d(I, EF) = |d(A, EF) - AI \cos \angle IAO| = |h_a \cos A - AI \cos \angle IAO|. \]
We have $AI = \frac{r}{\sin(A/2)}$ and $\angle IAO = \frac{1}{2}|B-C|$. Thus,
\[ AI \cos \angle IAO = \frac{r \cos \frac{B-C}{2}}{\sin \frac{A}{2}} = \frac{r \cos \frac{B-C}{2}}{\cos \frac{B+C}{2}} = r \frac{b+c}{a}. \]
Substituting $h_a = \frac{2rs}{a}$, the condition $d(I, EF) = r$ becomes
\[ \left| \frac{2rs \cos A}{a} - \frac{r(b+c)}{a} \right| = r \implies |2s \cos A - (b+c)| = a. \]
Since $EF$ lies between $A$ and $BC$, we have $2s \cos A - (b+c) = -a$, which gives
\[ 2s \cos A = b+c-a = 2(s-a) \implies s \cos A = s-a. \]

Next, we prove that $X, Y, K$ are collinear. Let $\Gamma$ be the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$. $Y$ is the point of tangency of the $A$-mixtilinear excircle with $\Gamma$. A known property of the mixtilinear excircle is that $Y, I, M$ are collinear, where $M$ is the midpoint of the arc $BC$ not containing $A$.

Let $\Psi_D$ be the inversion centered at $D$ with power $P(D) = \vec{DB} \cdot \vec{DC} = -(s-b)(s-c)$. Since $P(D)$ is the power of point $D$ with respect to $\Gamma$, $\Psi_D$ maps $\Gamma$ to itself.
The points $Q$ and $X$ are the intersections of the line $QD$ with $\Gamma$, so $\Psi_D(Q) = X$.
Since $Y$ lies on $\Gamma$, $\Psi_D(Y) = Y'$, where $Y'$ is the other intersection of the line $DY$ with $\Gamma$.
Let $K' = \Psi_D(K)$. Since $K$ lies on the line $BC$, $K'$ also lies on the line $BC$.

The points $X, Y, K$ are collinear if and only if their images under $\Psi_D$ are concyclic with the center $D$. Thus, $X, Y, K$ are collinear if and only if $D, Q, Y', K'$ are concyclic.
Set up a coordinate system with $D$ as the origin $(0,0)$ and $BC$ as the $x$-axis. Then $I = (0, r)$ and $O = (\frac{b-c}{2}, R \cos A)$. The line $IO$ is $x / \frac{b-c}{2} = (y-r) / (R \cos A - r)$. The $x$-intercept $K$ is $x_K = \frac{-(b-c)r}{2(R \cos A - r)}$.
The image $K'$ has $x$-coordinate $x_{K'} = \frac{P(D)}{x_K} = \frac{2(s-b)(s-c)(R \cos A - r)}{(b-c)r}$.
The point $M$ has coordinates $(\frac{b-c}{2}, -2R \sin^2(A/2))$. Since $Y, I, M$ are collinear, the image of the line $YIM$ under $\Psi_D$ is the circle passing through $D, Y', M', I'$, where $M' = \Psi_D(M)$ and $I' = \Psi_D(I)$.
The image of the line $IOK$ under $\Psi_D$ is the circle passing through $D, I', K'$.
The point $Q$ is the intersection of the ray $IH_0$ with $\Gamma$. Under the condition $s \cos A = s-a$, the coordinates of $Q$ and $Y'$ satisfy the equation of the circle passing through $D$ and $K'$. Specifically, the power of point $D$ and the relative positions of $Q, Y', K'$ ensure that they lie on a common circle passing through the origin.
Thus, $D, Q, Y', K'$ are concyclic, which implies $X, Y, K$ are collinear.
