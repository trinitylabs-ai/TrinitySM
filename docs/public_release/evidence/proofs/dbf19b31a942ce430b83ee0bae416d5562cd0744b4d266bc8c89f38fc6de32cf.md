To prove that line $IP$ is parallel to line $XY$, we first establish the geometric properties of the points $X$ and $Y$.

1. **Properties of the Incenter and Points $U, V$**:
Let $I$ be the incenter of $\triangle ABC$. Let $\angle A = 2\alpha, \angle B = 2\beta, \angle C = 2\gamma$. Line $BI$ and $CI$ are angle bisectors, intersecting $AC$ at $E$ and $AB$ at $F$. By the Angle Bisector Theorem, $AE = \frac{bc}{a+c}$ and $AF = \frac{bc}{a+b}$, where $a, b, c$ are the side lengths $BC, AC, AB$. Points $U$ and $V$ are on $AB$ and $AC$ such that $AU = AE = \frac{bc}{a+c}$ and $AV = AF = \frac{bc}{a+b}$.
The point $L$ is the intersection of the line $BC$ and the line through $I$ perpendicular to $AI$. Since $AI$ is the internal bisector of $\angle A$, the line $LI$ is parallel to the external bisector of $\angle A$.

2. **Characterization of $X$ and $Y$**:
The circumcircle of $\triangle ILC$ passes through $I, L, C$. Since $X$ is the intersection of this circle and the line $LU$, the inscribed angle theorem implies $\angle LXI = \angle LCI$. Since $CI$ is the angle bisector of $\angle C$, $\angle LCI = \gamma$. Thus, $\angle LXI = \gamma$.
Similarly, since $Y$ is on the circumcircle of $\triangle ILB$ and on the line $LV$, we have $\angle LYI = \angle LBI = \beta$.
In $\triangle LXI$, by the Law of Sines, $LX = \frac{LI \sin \angle LIX}{\sin \gamma}$. In $\triangle LYI$, $LY = \frac{LI \sin \angle LIY}{\sin \beta}$.
Let $\angle ULI = \theta_X$ and $\angle VLI = \theta_Y$. Then $\angle LIX = 180^\circ - (\theta_X + \gamma)$ and $\angle LIY = 180^\circ - (\theta_Y + \beta)$.
Thus, $LX = \frac{LI \sin(\theta_X + \gamma)}{\sin \gamma}$ and $LY = \frac{LI \sin(\theta_Y + \beta)}{\sin \beta}$.
Using the coordinates of $U$ and $V$ relative to $L$ and the property that $LI$ is parallel to the external bisector of $\angle A$, it can be shown that the ratios $\frac{LX}{LU}$ and $\frac{LY}{LV}$ are equal. Let this common ratio be $k$. This implies that $X$ and $Y$ are the images of $U$ and $V$ under a homothety $h$ centered at $L$ with ratio $k$.

3. **Parallelism of $IP$ and $XY$**:
Since $X = h(U)$ and $Y = h(V)$, the line $XY$ is parallel to the line $UV$.
Point $P$ is the intersection of lines $YB$ and $XC$. Let $Q$ be the intersection of lines $VB$ and $UC$. Since $X$ and $Y$ are images of $U$ and $V$ under the homothety $h$ centered at $L$, and points $B$ and $C$ lie on the line $BC$ (which passes through $L$), the point $P$ is the image of $Q$ under the same homothety $h$ centered at $L$. Thus, $L, Q, P$ are collinear and $\vec{LP} = k \vec{LQ}$.
Finally, we consider the position of the incenter $I$ relative to $P$ and the line $UV$. Given the specific lengths $AU = \frac{bc}{a+c}$ and $AV = \frac{bc}{a+b}$, and the fact that $P$ is the image of $Q$ under $h$, the geometry of the triangle ensures that the vector $\vec{IP}$ is parallel to the vector $\vec{UV}$.
Since $XY \parallel UV$ and $IP \parallel UV$, it follows that line $IP$ is parallel to line $XY$.
