To prove that $C$ is the midpoint of the segment $XY$, we will use a coordinate geometry approach.

**1. Setting up the Coordinate System**
Let $C$ be the origin $(0,0)$. Let the line $BC$ lie along the $x$-axis. Thus, the coordinates of $C$ are $(0,0)$ and the coordinates of $B$ are $(a, 0)$ for some $a > 0$. Let the coordinates of $A$ be $(b, c)$. Since $\triangle ABC$ is acute, we have $0 < b < a$ and $c > 0$.

**2. Finding the Orthocenter $H$ and its Reflection $P$**
The orthocenter $H$ is the intersection of the altitudes of $\triangle ABC$.
- The altitude from $A$ to $BC$ is the vertical line $x = b$.
- The slope of $AB$ is $m_{AB} = \frac{c-0}{b-a} = \frac{c}{b-a}$. The altitude from $C$ to $AB$ is perpendicular to $AB$ and passes through $(0,0)$, so its equation is $y = -\frac{b-a}{c}x$.
The intersection $H$ is found by substituting $x=b$ into the altitude equation:
$$y_H = -\frac{b-a}{c}b = \frac{b(a-b)}{c} \implies H = \left(b, \frac{b(a-b)}{c}\right)$$
Point $P$ is the reflection of $H$ across the line $BC$ (the $x$-axis), so its coordinates are:
$$P = \left(b, -\frac{b(a-b)}{c}\right)$$

**3. Finding the Foot of the Altitude $F$**
Point $F$ is the intersection of the line $AB$ ($y = \frac{c}{b-a}(x-a)$) and the altitude $CF$ ($y = -\frac{b-a}{c}x$). Setting them equal:
$$\frac{c}{b-a}(x-a) = -\frac{b-a}{c}x \implies c^2(x-a) = -(b-a)^2 x \implies x(c^2 + (b-a)^2) = c^2 a$$
Let $D^2 = c^2 + (b-a)^2$. Then $x_F = \frac{c^2 a}{D^2}$ and $y_F = -\frac{b-a}{c} \cdot \frac{c^2 a}{D^2} = \frac{-ca(b-a)}{D^2}$.

**4. The Circumcircle of $\triangle AFP$**
Let the center of the circumcircle of $\triangle AFP$ be $O = (x_0, y_0)$.
Since $A(b, c)$ and $P(b, -y_H)$ are on the circle, $O$ must lie on the perpendicular bisector of $AP$, which is the horizontal line $y = \frac{c - y_H}{2}$. Thus:
$$y_0 = \frac{c - \frac{b(a-b)}{c}}{2} = \frac{c^2 - ab + b^2}{2c}$$
Since $A$ and $F$ are on the circle, $dist(O, A)^2 = dist(O, F)^2$:
$$(x_0 - b)^2 + (y_0 - c)^2 = (x_0 - x_F)^2 + (y_0 - y_F)^2$$
Expanding and simplifying:
$$x_0^2 - 2bx_0 + b^2 + (y_0 - c)^2 = x_0^2 - 2x_F x_0 + x_F^2 + (y_0 - y_F)^2$$
$$2(x_F - b)x_0 = x_F^2 + y_F^2 - b^2 - (y_0 - c)^2 + (y_0 - y_F)^2$$
$$2(x_F - b)x_0 = (x_F^2 + y_F^2) - b^2 - (y_0^2 - 2cy_0 + c^2) + (y_0^2 - 2y_F y_0 + y_F^2)$$
$$2(x_F - b)x_0 = (x_F^2 + y_F^2) - b^2 - c^2 + 2y_0(c - y_F)$$
Note that $x_F^2 + y_F^2 = CF^2 = \frac{c^2 a^2}{D^2}$. Let $S = b^2 + c^2$.
We calculate $c - y_F = c - \frac{-ca(b-a)}{D^2} = \frac{c(D^2 + ab - a^2)}{D^2} = \frac{c(c^2 + b^2 - 2ab + a^2 + ab - a^2)}{D^2} = \frac{c(c^2 + b^2 - ab)}{D^2}$.
Substituting $y_0 = \frac{c^2 - ab + b^2}{2c}$:
$$2(x_F - b)x_0 = \frac{c^2 a^2}{D^2} - S + 2 \cdot \frac{c^2 - ab + b^2}{2c} \cdot \frac{c(c^2 + b^2 - ab)}{D^2} = \frac{c^2 a^2 + (S - ab)^2 - S D^2}{D^2}$$
The numerator is $c^2 a^2 + S^2 - 2abS + a^2 b^2 - S(S - 2ab + a^2) = c^2 a^2 + a^2 b^2 - S a^2 = a^2(c^2 + b^2 - S) = 0$.
Thus, $2(x_F - b)x_0 = 0$. Since $x_F \neq b$ (as $\angle A \neq 90^\circ$), we have $x_0 = 0$.

**5. Conclusion**
The center of the circle $O$ is $(0, y_0)$. The line $BC$ is the $x$-axis. The projection of the center $O$ onto the line $BC$ is $(0,0)$, which is the point $C$. In any circle, the projection of the center onto a chord is the midpoint of that chord. Since $X$ and $Y$ are the intersections of the circle with the line $BC$, $XY$ is a chord, and $C$ is its midpoint.
