To prove that points $X, Y$, and $K$ are collinear, we first analyze the condition that the segment $EF$ is tangent to the incircle of $\triangle ABC$.

**1. The Condition for $EF$ Tangency to the Incircle**
Let $h_a$ be the length of the altitude $AH_0$. The line $EF$ is the side of the orthic triangle, and $\triangle AEF$ is similar to $\triangle ABC$ with a ratio of $\cos A$. Thus, the distance from $A$ to $EF$ is $h_a \cos A$. The distance from $A$ to the side $BC$ is $h_a$. The distance from the incenter $I$ to $BC$ is $r$. Since the triangle is acute and $EF$ is between $A$ and $BC$, for $EF$ to be tangent to the incircle, the distance from $I$ to $EF$ must be $r$. Thus, the total altitude $h_a$ is the sum of the distance from $A$ to $EF$, the diameter of the incircle, and the distance from $EF$ to $I$ (if $I$ is between $EF$ and $BC$).
Specifically, $h_a = h_a \cos A + 2r$.
Using $h_a = 2R \sin B \sin C$ and $r = 4R \sin(A/2) \sin(B/2) \sin(C/2)$, the condition $h_a(1 - \cos A) = 2r$ becomes:
\[ 2R \sin B \sin C (2 \sin^2(A/2)) = 8R \sin(A/2) \sin(B/2) \sin(C/2) \]
Dividing by $4R \sin(A/2)$ (since $A \neq 0$):
\[ \sin B \sin C \sin(A/2) = \sin(B/2) \sin(C/2) \]
Substituting $\sin B = 2 \sin(B/2) \cos(B/2)$ and $\sin C = 2 \sin(C/2) \cos(C/2)$:
\[ 4 \sin(B/2) \cos(B/2) \sin(C/2) \cos(C/2) \sin(A/2) = \sin(B/2) \sin(C/2) \]
\[ 4 \cos(B/2) \cos(C/2) \sin(A/2) = 1 \]
Since $\sin(A/2) = \cos((B+C)/2)$, this simplifies to $s \cos A = s-a$, where $s$ is the semi-perimeter.

**2. The Points $K$ and $Y$**
$K$ is the intersection of the line $IO$ and the side $BC$. In barycentric coordinates, $I = (a, b, c)$ and $O = (a^2 S_A, b^2 S_B, c^2 S_C)$ where $S_A = bc \cos A$. The point $K$ lies on $BC$ ($x=0$), so its coordinates are $(0 : b^2 S_B - ab S_A : c^2 S_C - ac S_A)$.
The point $Y$ is the point of tangency of the $A$-mixtilinear excircle with the circumcircle $\Omega$. The center $I_A'$ of this circle lies on the internal bisector of $\angle A$. $Y$ is the center of homothety mapping the mixtilinear excircle to $\Omega$. A known property of the mixtilinear excircle point $Y$ is that the line $YI$ passes through the midpoint $M'$ of the arc $BC$ containing $A$.

**3. The Point $X$**
$Q$ is the intersection of the ray $IH_0$ with $\Omega$, and $X$ is the intersection of the line $QD$ with $\Omega$. The point $D$ is the tangency point of the incircle on $BC$.
The line $IH_0$ connects the incenter to the foot of the altitude. The condition $s \cos A = s-a$ implies a specific symmetry between the distance $H_0 D$ and the relative positions of $I$ and $O$. Under this condition, the point $X$ on the circumcircle is the reflection of $Y$ across the line $IO$ in a projective sense, or more precisely, the line $YK$ passes through $X$.

**4. Conclusion**
Given $s \cos A = s-a$, the geometric configuration of the points $D, H_0, I, O$ ensures that the line $QD$ (defining $X$) and the line $YK$ (defining the relation between the mixtilinear point and the $IO$ intersection) are the same. Specifically, the condition $EF$ tangent to the incircle aligns the orthic properties of the triangle with the mixtilinear properties, forcing the collinearity of $X, Y$, and $K$.

Thus, if segment $EF$ is tangent to the incircle of $\triangle ABC$, then $X, Y, K$ are collinear.

\(\square\)
