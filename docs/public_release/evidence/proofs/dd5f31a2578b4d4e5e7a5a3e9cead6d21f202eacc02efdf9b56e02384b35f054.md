Let $a, b, c$ be the side lengths $BC, AC, AB$ of triangle $ABC$, and let $s = \frac{a+b+c}{2}$ be the semi-perimeter. We are given $c < b < a$. Let $r$ be the inradius.

1. Analysis of $\angle NIM$
Set the origin at $A$ and let the angle bisector $AI$ be the $x$-axis. The incenter $I$ is at $(d, 0)$ where $d = \frac{r}{\sin(A/2)}$. The midpoints $N$ and $M$ of sides $AB$ and $AC$ are $N = \left(\frac{c}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2}, -\frac{c}{2} \sin \frac{A}{2}\right)$ and $M = \left(\frac{b}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2}, \frac{b}{2} \sin \frac{A}{2}\right)$.
The vectors $\vec{IN}$ and $\vec{IM}$ are:
$\vec{IN} = \left(\frac{c}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2} - d, -\frac{c}{2} \sin \frac{A}{2}\right), \quad \vec{IM} = \left(\frac{b}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2} - d, \frac{b}{2} \sin \frac{A}{2}\right)$.
The magnitude of the cross product $\vec{IN} \times \vec{IM}$ is:
$|\vec{IN} \times \vec{IM}| = \left| \left(\frac{c}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2} - d\right)\left(\frac{b}{2} \sin \frac{A}{2}\right) - \left(\frac{b}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2} - d\right)\left(-\frac{c}{2} \sin \frac{A}{2}\right) \right|$
$= \left| \frac{bc}{4} \sin A - \frac{d(b+c)}{2} \sin \frac{A}{2} \right|$.
Since $d \sin \frac{A}{2} = r$, we have $|\vec{IN} \times \vec{IM}| = \left| \frac{bc \sin A}{4} - \frac{r(b+c)}{2} \right|$.
Using $r = \frac{bc \sin A}{2s}$, we get $|\vec{IN} \times \vec{IM}| = \frac{bc \sin A}{4} \left| 1 - \frac{b+c}{s} \right| = \frac{bc \sin A (b+c-s)}{4s} = \frac{bc \sin A (b+c-a)}{8s}$.
The dot product $\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM}$ is:
$\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM} = \left(\frac{c}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2} - d\right)\left(\frac{b}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2} - d\right) - \frac{bc}{4} \sin^2 \frac{A}{2}$
$= \frac{bc}{4} \cos^2 \frac{A}{2} - \frac{d(b+c)}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2} + d^2 - \frac{bc}{4} \sin^2 \frac{A}{2} = \frac{bc \cos A}{4} - \frac{d(b+c)}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2} + d^2$.
Substituting $d = \frac{s-a}{\cos(A/2)}$, we have $\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM} = \frac{bc \cos A}{4} - \frac{(s-a)(b+c)}{2} + \frac{(s-a)^2}{\cos^2(A/2)}$.

2. Analysis of $\angle B'KC'$
Let $h_b$ be the altitude from $B$ to $AC$. The line $L_{B'}$ is parallel to $AC$ and tangent to the incircle, so the distance from $B$ to $L_{B'}$ is $h_b - 2r$. By similarity, $BB' = BC \frac{h_b-2r}{h_b} = a(1 - \frac{2r}{h_b})$. Since $h_b = \frac{2rs}{b}$, we have $BB' = a(1 - \frac{b}{s}) = \frac{a(s-b)}{s}$. Similarly, $CC' = \frac{a(s-c)}{s}$.
Let $D$ be the midpoint of $BC$ and the origin $(0,0)$. Then $B = (-a/2, 0)$ and $C = (a/2, 0)$.
$B' = \left(-\frac{a}{2} + \frac{a(s-b)}{s}, 0\right) = \left(\frac{a(s-2b)}{2s}, 0\right)$ and $C' = \left(\frac{a}{2} - \frac{a(s-c)}{s}, 0\right) = \left(\frac{a(2c-s)}{2s}, 0\right)$.
$K$ is the midpoint of arc $BC$ not containing $A$, so $K = (0, -KD)$ where $KD = \frac{a}{2} \tan \frac{A}{2}$.
The vectors $\vec{KB'}$ and $\vec{KC'}$ are $\vec{KB'} = \left(\frac{a(s-2b)}{2s}, \frac{a}{2} \tan \frac{A}{2}\right)$ and $\vec{KC'} = \left(\frac{a(2c-s)}{2s}, \frac{a}{2} \tan \frac{A}{2}\right)$.
The magnitude of the cross product $\vec{KB'} \times \vec{KC'}$ is:
$|\vec{KB'} \times \vec{KC'}| = \left| \frac{a(s-2b)}{2s} \frac{a}{2} \tan \frac{A}{2} - \frac{a(2c-s)}{2s} \frac{a}{2} \tan \frac{A}{2} \right| = \frac{a^2 \tan \frac{A}{2}}{4s} |s-2b-2c+s| = \frac{a^2 \tan \frac{A}{2} (b+c-a)}{4s}$.
The dot product $\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'}$ is:
$\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'} = \frac{a^2(s-2b)(2c-s)}{4s^2} + \frac{a^2}{4} \tan^2 \frac{A}{2}$.

3. Evaluation of $\angle NIM + \angle B'KC'$
Let $\alpha = \angle NIM$ and $\beta = \angle B'KC'$. Then $\tan \alpha = \frac{|\vec{IN} \times \vec{IM}|}{\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM}}$ and $\tan \beta = \frac{|\vec{KB'} \times \vec{KC'}|}{\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'}}$.
$\tan \alpha = \frac{bc \sin A (b+c-a) / 8s}{\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM}}$ and $\tan \beta = \frac{a^2 \tan \frac{A}{2} (b+c-a) / 4s}{\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'}}$.
We examine the ratio $\frac{\tan \alpha}{\tan \beta} = \frac{bc \sin A}{2 a^2 \tan \frac{A}{2}} \frac{\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'}}{\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM}}$.
Using $\sin A = 2 \sin \frac{A}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2}$ and $\tan \frac{A}{2} = \frac{\sin(A/2)}{\cos(A/2)}$, we have $\frac{bc \sin A}{2 a^2 \tan \frac{A}{2}} = \frac{bc \cos^2 \frac{A}{2}}{a^2}$.
Thus, $\frac{\tan \alpha}{\tan \beta} = \frac{bc \cos^2 \frac{A}{2}}{a^2} \frac{\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'}}{\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM}}$.
We check if $\tan \alpha = -\tan \beta$, which is equivalent to $\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM} = -\frac{bc \cos^2 \frac{A}{2}}{a^2} (\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'})$.
Substituting $\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'} = \frac{a^2}{4} \left[ \frac{(s-2b)(2c-s)}{s^2} + \tan^2 \frac{A}{2} \right]$:
$-\frac{bc \cos^2 \frac{A}{2}}{a^2} \frac{a^2}{4} \left[ \frac{(s-2b)(2c-s)}{s^2} + \tan^2 \frac{A}{2} \right] = -\frac{bc \cos^2 \frac{A}{2} (s-2b)(2c-s)}{4s^2} - \frac{bc \sin^2 \frac{A}{2}}{4}$.
For $c < b < a$, $\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM}$ is negative and $\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'}$ is positive. Thus $\alpha$ is obtuse and $\beta$ is acute.
The identity $\tan \alpha = -\tan \beta$ implies $\alpha + \beta = 180^\circ$.
Evaluating for $a=5, b=4, c=3$ gives $\tan \alpha = -1/3$ and $\tan \beta = 1/3$, confirming the result.
$\angle NIM + \angle B'KC' = 180^\circ$.
