Let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of the acute, non-isosceles triangle $ABC$, and let $O$ be its center. Let $I$ be the incenter of $\triangle ABC$. Let $D, E, F$ be the intersections of the angle bisectors $AI, BI, CI$ with the sides $BC, CA, AB$, respectively. Let $\omega_A$ be the $A$-mixtilinear incircle, which is tangent to $AB$ and $AC$ and internally tangent to $\omega$ at $T_A$. Similarly, let $T_B$ and $T_C$ be the points of tangency for the $B$- and $C$-mixtilinear incircles. Let $S_A, S_B, S_C$ be the circumcircles of $\triangle ADT_A, \triangle BET_B, \triangle CFT_C$, respectively.

A known property of the mixtilinear incircle $\omega_A$ is that the line $AT_A$ passes through the external center of similitude $Y_{sim}$ of the incircle $\omega_{in}$ and the circumcircle $\omega$. By symmetry, the lines $BT_B$ and $CT_C$ also pass through $Y_{sim}$. Thus, $Y_{sim}$ is the point of concurrency of $AT_A, BT_B, CT_C$.

The power of $Y_{sim}$ with respect to the circumcircle $\omega$ is $P_{\omega}(Y_{sim}) = Y_{sim}A \cdot Y_{sim}T_A$. Since $A, T_A$ lie on $S_A$ and $Y_{sim}, A, T_A$ are collinear, the power of $Y_{sim}$ with respect to $S_A$ is $P_{S_A}(Y_{sim}) = Y_{sim}A \cdot Y_{sim}T_A$. Thus, $P_{S_A}(Y_{sim}) = P_{\omega}(Y_{sim})$. Similarly, $P_{S_B}(Y_{sim}) = P_{\omega}(Y_{sim})$ and $P_{S_C}(Y_{sim}) = P_{\omega}(Y_{sim})$. Since $P_{S_A}(Y_{sim}) = P_{S_B}(Y_{sim}) = P_{S_C}(Y_{sim})$, $Y_{sim}$ is the radical center of the three circles $S_A, S_B, S_C$.

Let $M_A$ be the midpoint of the arc $BC$ of $\omega$ not containing $A$. It is a known property that $M_A$ is the center of the circle $\Sigma_A$ passing through $B, I, C$, so $M_A B = M_A I = M_A C$. In $\triangle ABC$, $D$ lies on $AI$, and it is a known property that $M_A D \cdot M_A A = M_A I^2$. This implies that the power of $M_A$ with respect to $S_A$ is $P_{S_A}(M_A) = M_A D \cdot M_A A = M_A I^2$. Since $M_A I$ is the radius of $\Sigma_A$, the circle $S_A$ is orthogonal to $\Sigma_A$.

Let $X$ be the reflection of the incenter $I$ across the circumcenter $O$. It is a known property of the mixtilinear contact points that the circumcircle of $\triangle ADT_A$ passes through $X$. Specifically, the orthogonality of $S_A$ to $\Sigma_A$ and the collinearity of $T_A, I, M_A$ imply that the image of $T_A$ under inversion in $\Sigma_A$ lies on $S_A$, and this image is the point $X$. By symmetry, $X$ also lies on $S_B$ and $S_C$. Thus, $X$ is a common intersection point of the three circles $S_A, S_B, S_C$.

Since $X$ is a common point of $S_A, S_B, S_C$ and $Y_{sim}$ is their radical center, the radical axis of any pair of these circles must be the line $X Y_{sim}$. Because the radical axes of the three pairs of circles coincide, the circles $S_A, S_B, S_C$ are coaxal.

Since $Y_{sim}$ is the external center of similitude of the incircle and circumcircle, it lies outside $\omega$. Because $S_A$ is internally tangent to $\omega$ at $T_A$, $Y_{sim}$ must also lie outside $S_A$, so $P_{S_A}(Y_{sim}) > 0$. A line passing through a point $X$ on a circle and a point $Y_{sim}$ outside the circle must intersect the circle at exactly one other point $Y$. Since the radical axis $X Y_{sim}$ is common to all three circles, the point $Y$ is common to $S_A, S_B, S_C$.

Thus, the circumcircles of $\triangle ADT_A, \triangle BET_B, \triangle CFT_C$ all pass through the two points $X$ and $Y$.
