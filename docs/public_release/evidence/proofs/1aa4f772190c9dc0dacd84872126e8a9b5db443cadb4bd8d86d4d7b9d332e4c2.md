To prove that we can choose two diagonals of a convex $(18n+2)$-gon $P$ such that the three resulting parts each contain at least $3n$ and at most $9n$ triangles, we utilize the dual graph of the triangulation.

**1. Dual Graph Representation**
A triangulation of a convex polygon with $18n+2$ vertices consists of $T = 18n$ triangles. The dual graph $G$ of this triangulation is a tree where each node represents a triangle and an edge exists between two nodes if the corresponding triangles share a diagonal. In such a triangulation, every node in $G$ has a degree of at most 3. Removing a diagonal from the triangulation corresponds to removing an edge from the tree $G$. Removing two diagonals partitions $G$ into three connected components $C_1, C_2, C_3$. We must prove there exist two edges whose removal results in $3n \le |C_i| \le 9n$ for $i=1, 2, 3$.

**2. Centroid Analysis**
Let $v$ be the centroid of the tree $G$. By the property of the centroid, removing $v$ splits $G$ into components $T_1, T_2, T_3$ (some may be empty) with sizes $s_1 \ge s_2 \ge s_3 \ge 0$ such that $s_1 \le \frac{18n}{2} = 9n$.
Since $s_1 + s_2 + s_3 = 18n - 1$ and $s_1$ is the largest component, we have $3s_1 \ge s_1 + s_2 + s_3 = 18n - 1$, which implies $s_1 \ge \frac{18n-1}{3} = 6n - \frac{1}{3}$. Since $s_1$ is an integer, $s_1 \ge 6n$. Thus, $s_1 \in [6n, 9n]$.

**3. Partitioning the Tree**
We consider two cases based on the size of the smallest component $T_3$:

*   **Case 1: $s_3 \ge 3n$.**
    We remove the edges $(v, w_1)$ and $(v, w_2)$, where $w_1, w_2$ are the roots of $T_1$ and $T_2$ respectively. The resulting components are $C_1 = T_1$, $C_2 = T_2$, and $C_3 = T_3 \cup \{v\}$. Their sizes are $s_1, s_2, s_3+1$.
    - Since $s_1 \in [6n, 9n]$, we have $3n \le s_1 \le 9n$.
    - Since $s_3 \le s_2 \le s_1 \le 9n$, we have $s_2 \le 9n$. Also $s_2 \ge s_3 \ge 3n$.
    - For $C_3$, $s_3+1 \ge 3n+1 \ge 3n$. Since $s_1+s_2+s_3 = 18n-1$ and $s_1 \ge 6n, s_2 \ge 3n$, we have $s_3 \le 18n-1-9n = 9n-1$, so $s_3+1 \le 9n$.
    Thus, all three components satisfy the required size constraints.

*   **Case 2: $s_3 < 3n$.**
    We first remove the edge $e_1 = (v, w_1)$, splitting $G$ into $C_1 = T_1$ and a remaining tree $T' = G \setminus T_1$. The size of $C_1$ is $s_1 \in [6n, 9n]$, which is within the required range. The size of $T'$ is $M = 18n - s_1$. Since $s_1 \in [6n, 9n]$, we have $M \in [9n, 12n]$.
    We now find an edge $e_2$ in $T'$ that splits it into $C_2, C_3$ such that $3n \le |C_2|, |C_3| \le 9n$. Let $v'$ be the centroid of $T'$, and let the components formed by removing $v'$ from $T'$ be $U_1, U_2, U_3$ with sizes $u_1 \ge u_2 \ge u_3 \ge 0$.
    By the property of the centroid, $u_1 \le \frac{M}{2}$. Since $u_1$ is the largest of the three components and $u_1 + u_2 + u_3 = M-1$, we have $3u_1 \ge M-1$, so $u_1 \ge \frac{M-1}{3}$.
    We choose $e_2$ to be the edge connecting $v'$ to the root of $U_1$, so $|C_2| = u_1$ and $|C_3| = M - u_1$.
    We verify the bounds for $|C_2|$:
    - $u_1 \ge \frac{M-1}{3} \ge \frac{9n-1}{3} = 3n - \frac{1}{3} \implies u_1 \ge 3n$.
    - $u_1 \le \frac{M}{2} \le \frac{12n}{2} = 6n \le 9n$.
    We verify the bounds for $|C_3|$:
    - $M - u_1 \ge M - \frac{M}{2} = \frac{M}{2} \ge \frac{9n}{2} = 4.5n \ge 3n$.
    - $M - u_1 \le M - \frac{M-1}{3} = \frac{2M+1}{3} \le \frac{2(12n)+1}{3} = 8n + \frac{1}{3} \implies M - u_1 \le 8n \le 9n$.
    Thus, $|C_2|$ and $|C_3|$ both fall within the range $[3n, 9n]$.

In both cases, we can choose two diagonals such that the three resulting parts each contain between $3n$ and $9n$ triangles.
