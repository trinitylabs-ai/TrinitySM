Let the two operations be $f(x) = x+2$ and $g(x) = 3x$. Let $x_n$ and $y_n$ be the values of the two numbers after $n$ steps.

**1. Necessary Conditions**
First, observe that both operations preserve parity: $f(x) \equiv x \pmod 2$ and $g(x) \equiv x \pmod 2$. Thus, $x_n \equiv a \pmod 2$ and $y_n \equiv b \pmod 2$ for all $n$. For $x_n = y_n$ to be possible, we must have $a \equiv b \pmod 2$.

If $a$ and $b$ are odd, we consider the values modulo 4. If $x \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, then $f(x) = x+2 \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ and $g(x) = 3x \equiv 3 \pmod 4$. If $x \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, then $f(x) = x+2 \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ and $g(x) = 3x \equiv 1 \pmod 4$. In both cases, the value modulo 4 flips every step. Thus, $x_n \equiv a + 2n \pmod 4$ and $y_n \equiv b + 2n \pmod 4$. For $x_n = y_n$, we must have $a + 2n \equiv b + 2n \pmod 4$, which implies $a \equiv b \pmod 4$.

**2. Reachability Lemma**
Consider a system where we start with $m$ and apply $k$ operations, where $j$ of these are multiplications by 3 and $k-j$ are additions of $c$. The final value $Z$ is given by $Z = 3^j m + \sum_{i=0}^j a_i 3^i \cdot c$, where $\sum_{i=0}^j a_i = k-j$ and $a_i \ge 0$.
For fixed $j, Z, c$, let $X = (Z - 3^j m)/c$. If $X$ is not a non-negative integer, no such $k$ exists. If $X$ is a non-negative integer, the possible values of $S = \sum a_i$ such that $\sum_{i=0}^j a_i 3^i = X$ are $\{ \text{min\_sum}(X, j), \text{min\_sum}(X, j) + 2, \dots, X \}$, where $\text{min\_sum}(X, j) = \lfloor X/3^j \rfloor + S_3(X \pmod{3^j})$ and $S_3(n)$ is the sum of digits of $n$ in base 3. The total number of steps is $k = j + S$.

**3. Sufficiency for Even Integers**
Suppose $a$ and $b$ are even. Let $a=2m$ and $b=2n$. The operations $x \to x+2$ and $x \to 3x$ correspond to $m \to m+1$ and $m \to 3m$ on the halved numbers. Here $c=1$.
A value $Z$ is reachable from $m$ in $k$ steps if there exists $j_m$ such that $k \in [j_m + \text{min\_sum}(X_m, j_m), j_m + X_m]$ with step 2, where $X_m = Z - 3^{j_m} m$.
The parity of $k$ is $k \equiv j_m + X_m \equiv j_m + Z - 3^{j_m} m \equiv j_m + Z - m \pmod 2$.
To make $a$ and $b$ equal, we need to find $Z, j_m, j_n$ such that the intervals for $k$ overlap and $j_m + Z - m \equiv j_n + Z - n \pmod 2$. This parity condition simplifies to $j_m - m \equiv j_n - n \pmod 2$.
If $m \equiv n \pmod 2$, we can pick $j_m=2, j_n=2$. If $m \not\equiv n \pmod 2$, we can pick $j_m=2, j_n=1$. In either case, the parity condition is satisfied. For a sufficiently large $Z$, the intervals $[j_m + \text{min\_sum}(X_m, j_m), j_m + X_m]$ and $[j_n + \text{min\_sum}(X_n, j_n), j_n + X_n]$ will overlap because the upper bounds grow linearly with $Z$ while the lower bounds grow as $Z/3^{j}$. Thus, any two even $a, b$ can be made equal.

**4. Sufficiency for Odd Integers**
Suppose $a$ and $b$ are odd and $a \equiv b \pmod 4$. Let $x = 2y+1$. The operations $x \to x+2$ and $x \to 3x$ correspond to $y \to y+1$ and $y \to 3y+1$.
Let $y = z - 1/2$. Then $y \to y+1 \implies z \to z+1$ and $y \to 3y+1 \implies z \to 3z$.
Starting from $z_a = a/2$ and $z_b = b/2$, a value $Z$ is reachable from $z_a$ in $k$ steps if there exists $j_a$ such that $Z = 3^{j_a}(a/2) + \sum_{i=0}^{j_a} a_i 3^i$, which is $2Z = 3^{j_a} a + 2 \sum a_i 3^i$.
Let $W = 2Z$ and $X_a = (W - 3^{j_a} a)/2$. The possible values for $k$ are $k \in [j_a + \text{min\_sum}(X_a, j_a), j_a + X_a]$ with step 2.
The parity of $k$ is $k \equiv j_a + X_a = j_a + \frac{W - 3^{j_a} a}{2} \pmod 2$.
Pick $j_a=2$ and $j_b=2$. Then $k \equiv 2 + \frac{W - 9a}{2} \equiv \frac{W - a}{2} \pmod 2$ and $k \equiv 2 + \frac{W - 9b}{2} \equiv \frac{W - b}{2} \pmod 2$.
Since $a \equiv b \pmod 4$, we have $(W-a)/2 \equiv (W-b)/2 \pmod 2$, so the parity conditions match. For sufficiently large $W$, the intervals for $k$ overlap.

**Conclusion**
The pairs $(a, b)$ are those such that $a \equiv b \pmod 2$, and if $a$ is odd, $a \equiv b \pmod 4$. This is equivalent to saying $a$ and $b$ are both even, or $a$ and $b$ are both odd and $a \equiv b \pmod 4$.
