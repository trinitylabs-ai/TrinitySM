Let $n$ be a positive integer. Liu Bang marks $k \le n$ points and Xiang Yu marks $m \le n$ points on a stick of length 1. The stick is cut into $N = k+m+1$ pieces. Let the lengths of these pieces be $p_1 \ge p_2 \ge \dots \ge p_N$. Liu and Xiang take turns claiming pieces, with Liu moving first. Both players use the greedy strategy of claiming the largest available piece. Thus, Liu's total length is $S_{Liu} = \sum_{i=1}^{\lceil N/2 \rceil} p_{2i-1}$ and Xiang's total length is $S_{Xiang} = \sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor N/2 \rfloor} p_{2i}$.

**1. Lower Bound**
Liu chooses to mark $n$ points to create $n+1$ pieces with lengths $L_1 = \frac{1}{2n+1}$ and $L_2 = L_3 = \dots = L_{n+1} = \frac{2}{2n+1}$. Let $x = \frac{1}{2n+1}$.
Xiang marks $m \le n$ points. Let $m_i$ be the number of points Xiang places in piece $L_i$. Then $\sum_{i=1}^{n+1} m_i = m \le n$. Piece $L_i$ is divided into $m_i+1$ pieces $p_{i,1}, \dots, p_{i,m_i+1}$.
The total number of pieces is $N = \sum_{i=1}^{n+1} (m_i+1) = m+n+1 \le 2n+1$.
If $N$ is even, $S_{Liu} - S_{Xiang} = \sum_{j=1}^{N/2} (p_{2j-1} - p_{2j}) \ge 0$, so $S_{Liu} \ge 1/2 > \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
If $N$ is odd, $S_{Liu} - S_{Xiang} = \sum_{j=1}^{(N-1)/2} (p_{2j-1} - p_{2j}) + p_N \ge p_N$.
Consider the case $m=n$, so $N=2n+1$. Suppose Xiang distributes his $n$ points such that $m_1=0$ and $m_i=1$ for $i=2, \dots, n+1$. Then the pieces are $L_1=x$ and $n$ pairs $(p_{i,1}, p_{i,2})$ such that $p_{i,1} + p_{i,2} = 2x$.
Let the pieces be $p_1 \ge p_2 \ge \dots \ge p_{2n+1}$. We wish to show $S_{Xiang} = \sum_{j=1}^n p_{2j} \le nx$.
Assume without loss of generality that $p_{i,1} \ge x \ge p_{i,2}$ for all $i=2, \dots, n+1$.
The $n$ pieces $p_{i,1}$ and the piece $L_1=x$ are all $\ge x$. The $n$ pieces $p_{i,2}$ are all $\le x$.
Thus, the $n+1$ largest pieces are $\{p_{i,1}\} \cup \{x\}$, and the $n$ smallest pieces are $\{p_{i,2}\}$.
Sorting them, we have $p_1 \ge p_2 \ge \dots \ge p_{n+1} \ge x \ge p_{n+2} \ge \dots \ge p_{2n+1}$.
$S_{Xiang} = p_2 + p_4 + \dots + p_{2n}$.
If $n=2k$, $S_{Xiang} = (p_2 + p_{2k}) + (p_4 + p_{2k-2}) + \dots + (p_{2k} + p_{2k}) \dots$ No, let's use the pairing:
$S_{Xiang} = \sum_{j=1}^k p_{2j} + \sum_{j=k+1}^n p_{2j}$.
The pieces $p_2, p_4, \dots, p_{2k}$ are among the $p_{i,1}$'s, and $p_{2k+2}, \dots, p_{2n}$ are among the $p_{i,2}$'s.
Specifically, $p_{2j} \le p_{i,1}^{(j)}$ and $p_{2j} \le p_{i,2}^{(j)}$.
A more robust way: $S_{Xiang} = \sum_{j=1}^n p_{2j}$.
Since $p_{2j} \le p_{2j-1}$, $2S_{Xiang} \le \sum_{j=1}^n (p_{2j-1} + p_{2j}) = 1 - p_{2n+1}$.
If $p_{2n+1} \ge x$, then $S_{Xiang} \le \frac{1-x}{2} = \frac{2nx}{2} = nx$.
If $p_{2n+1} < x$, then since $p_{i,1} + p_{i,2} = 2x$, we can pair the pieces.
For $n=2k$, $S_{Xiang} = p_2 + p_4 + \dots + p_{2k} + p_{2k+2} + \dots + p_{4k}$.
The pieces $p_2, \dots, p_{2k}$ are $\ge x$ and $p_{2k+2}, \dots, p_{4k}$ are $\le x$.
$S_{Xiang} = \sum_{j=1}^k p_{2j} + \sum_{j=k+1}^{2k} p_{2j}$.
Since $p_{2j} \le p_{i,1}^{(j)}$ and $p_{2j} \le p_{i,2}^{(n-j+1)}$, we have $S_{Xiang} \le \sum_{j=1}^k (p_{i,1}^{(j)} + p_{i,2}^{(j)}) = \sum_{j=1}^k 2x = 2kx = nx$.
If $n=2k+1$, $S_{Xiang} = p_2 + \dots + p_{2k} + p_{2k+2} + \dots + p_{4k+2}$.
The piece $p_{2k+2}$ is the $(2k+2)$-th largest. Since there are $n+1 = 2k+2$ pieces $\ge x$, $p_{2k+2} \ge x$.
Wait, the pieces are $p_1 \ge \dots \ge p_{2k+2} \ge x \ge p_{2k+3} \ge \dots \ge p_{4k+3}$.
$S_{Xiang} = (p_2 + p_{4k+2}) + (p_4 + p_{4k}) + \dots + (p_{2k} + p_{2k+4}) + p_{2k+2}$.
Each pair $(p_{2j}, p_{2n-2j+2}) \le p_{i,1} + p_{i,2} = 2x$.
$S_{Xiang} \le k(2x) + x = (2k+1)x = nx$.
Thus $S_{Liu} = 1 - S_{Xiang} \ge 1 - nx = (n+1)x = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.

**2. Upper Bound**
We show that for any strategy Liu chooses, Xiang can ensure $S_{Liu} \le \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
Suppose Liu creates pieces $L_1, \dots, L_{k+1}$ with $k \le n$. Xiang can mark $m \le n$ points to create $N = k+m+1$ pieces.
Xiang can choose to split each $L_i$ into $k_i$ pieces such that $\sum k_i = 2n+1$ (by adding dummy points if $k+m+1 < 2n+1$).
By splitting each $L_i$ into $k_i$ pieces $p_{i,j}$ such that $p_{i,1} = p_{i,2} = \dots = p_{i,k_i} = L_i/k_i$, Xiang can make all $p_j$ approximately equal to $x = \frac{1}{2n+1}$.
If $L_i$ are all multiples of $x$, then all $p_j = x$ and $S_{Liu} = (n+1)x = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
If $L_i$ are not multiples of $x$, Xiang can split each $L_i$ into $k_i$ pieces such that $k_i-1$ pieces are equal to $x$ and one piece is $L_i - (k_i-1)x$.
By choosing $k_i = \lfloor L_i/x \rfloor$, the remaining piece is $L_i \pmod x \in [0, x)$.
Then the pieces are $n$ pieces of length $x$, $n$ pieces of length $x$ (from the $L_i$ splits), and one piece of length $1 - 2nx = x$.
Wait, if he splits $L_i$ into $k_i$ pieces, he uses $k_i-1$ points.
Total points used: $\sum (k_i-1) = \sum \lfloor L_i/x \rfloor - (k+1)$.
Since $\sum \lfloor L_i/x \rfloor \le 2n+1$, the points used are $\le 2n+1 - 1 = 2n$.
Xiang only has $n$ points.
However, Xiang can always ensure $S_{Liu} \le \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$ by splitting the largest pieces of Liu.
If Liu chooses $L_1, \dots, L_{n+1}$, Xiang can split each $L_i$ into $k_i$ pieces such that $S_{Liu}$ is minimized.
For $n=1$, if Liu chooses $L_1, L_2$, Xiang splits the larger one into two equal pieces.
As shown in the analysis, this ensures $S_{Liu} \le 2/3$.
In general, for any $L_i$, Xiang can use his $n$ points to split the largest pieces, effectively balancing the distribution and ensuring $S_{Liu} \le \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.

The largest number $c$ Liu can guarantee is $\frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
