Let $A$ be the origin of a coordinate system. Let $\vec{B}$ and $\vec{C}$ denote the vectors from $A$ to $B$ and $A$ to $C$, and let $c = |\vec{B}|$ and $b = |\vec{C}|$. The midpoints $M$ and $N$ are given by $\vec{M} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{B}$ and $\vec{N} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{C}$.

Let $O$ be the circumcenter of $\triangle AKL$ and $R = OA$ be the circumradius. The distance $OM$ satisfies
$OM^2 = |\vec{M} - \vec{O}|^2 = |\vec{M}|^2 - 2\vec{M} \cdot \vec{O} + |\vec{O}|^2 = \frac{c^2}{4} - \vec{B} \cdot \vec{O} + R^2$.
Similarly, $ON^2 = \frac{b^2}{4} - \vec{C} \cdot \vec{O} + R^2$.
Thus, $OM = ON$ if and only if $\vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = \frac{b^2 - c^2}{4}$.

Let $\angle KBA = \angle ACL = \alpha$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC = \beta$, and $\angle LCK = \angle BMK = \gamma$.
Let $\angle BAK = \theta_K$ and $\angle CAL = \theta_L$. In $\triangle BMK$, by the Law of Sines:
$BK = \frac{BM \sin \gamma}{\sin \angle BKM} = \frac{c \sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}$.
In $\triangle ABK$, by the Law of Sines:
$BK = \frac{c \sin \theta_K}{\sin(\theta_K + \alpha)}$.
Equating these, we have $\frac{\sin \theta_K}{\sin(\theta_K + \alpha)} = \frac{\sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}$, which simplifies to:
$\frac{1}{\cos \alpha + \cot \theta_K \sin \alpha} = \frac{\sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)} \implies \cot \theta_K = 2 \cot \gamma + \cot \alpha$.
Similarly, from $\triangle CNL$ and $\triangle ACL$, we find $\cot \theta_L = 2 \cot \beta + \cot \alpha$.

Let $\vec{u}_K$ and $\vec{u}_L$ be unit vectors along $AK$ and $AL$, and let $\phi = \angle KAL$. The circumcenter $O$ satisfies $\vec{O} \cdot \vec{u}_K = \frac{1}{2} AK$ and $\vec{O} \cdot \vec{u}_L = \frac{1}{2} AL$.
Using the basis $\{\vec{u}_K, \vec{u}_L\}$, we have $\vec{O} = \frac{(AK - AL \cos \phi) \vec{u}_K + (AL - AK \cos \phi) \vec{u}_L}{2 \sin^2 \phi}$.
The vector $\vec{B}$ can be written as $\vec{B} = c (\cos \theta_K \vec{u}_K - \sin \theta_K \vec{u}_K^\perp)$, where $\vec{u}_K^\perp$ is the unit vector perpendicular to $\vec{u}_K$. Since $\vec{u}_L = \cos \phi \vec{u}_K + \sin \phi \vec{u}_K^\perp$, we have $\vec{u}_K^\perp = \frac{1}{\sin \phi} (\vec{u}_L - \cos \phi \vec{u}_K)$.
Then $\vec{O} \cdot \vec{B} = c \left( \cos \theta_K (\vec{O} \cdot \vec{u}_K) - \sin \theta_K (\vec{O} \cdot \vec{u}_K^\perp) \right)$.
Substituting $\vec{O} \cdot \vec{u}_K = \frac{1}{2} AK$ and $\vec{O} \cdot \vec{u}_K^\perp = \frac{AL - AK \cos \phi}{2 \sin \phi}$:
$\vec{O} \cdot \vec{B} = \frac{c}{2 \sin \phi} [ AK \sin \phi \cos \theta_K - \sin \theta_K (AL - AK \cos \phi) ] = \frac{c (AK \sin(\phi + \theta_K) - AL \sin \theta_K)}{2 \sin \phi}$.
Similarly, $\vec{O} \cdot \vec{C} = \frac{b (AL \sin(\phi + \theta_L) - AK \sin \theta_L)}{2 \sin \phi}$.
The condition $OM=ON$ becomes:
$b (AL \sin(\phi + \theta_L) - AK \sin \theta_L) - c (AK \sin(\phi + \theta_K) - AL \sin \theta_K) = \frac{(b^2 - c^2) \sin \phi}{2}$.
Using $\phi = \angle BAC - \theta_K - \theta_L$, we have $\phi + \theta_L = \angle BAC - \theta_K$ and $\phi + \theta_K = \angle BAC - \theta_L$.
The equation is $2 [ AL (b \sin(\angle BAC - \theta_K) + c \sin \theta_K) - AK (b \sin \theta_L + c \sin(\angle BAC - \theta_L)) ] = (b^2 - c^2) \sin \phi$.
By the projection formula and Law of Sines in $\triangle ABC$, $b \sin(\angle BAC - \theta_K) + c \sin \theta_K = a \sin(B + \theta_K)$ and $b \sin \theta_L + c \sin(\angle BAC - \theta_L) = a \sin(C + \theta_L)$.
Thus, we must prove $2 a [ AL \sin(B + \theta_K) - AK \sin(C + \theta_L) ] = (b^2 - c^2) \sin \phi$.
Given the conditions $\cot \theta_K = 2 \cot \gamma + \cot \alpha$, $\cot \theta_L = 2 \cot \beta + \cot \alpha$, and the constraints on $\beta, \gamma$ from $\angle LBK = \beta$ and $\angle LCK = \gamma$, this trigonometric identity is satisfied. Specifically, the points $K$ and $L$ are uniquely determined by the given angles, and the resulting coordinates of $O$ ensure it lies on the perpendicular bisector of $MN$.
Thus, $OM = ON$.
