Let $A$ be the origin of a coordinate system. Let $\vec{b}$ and $\vec{c}$ denote the position vectors of vertices $B$ and $C$, respectively. Let $b = |\vec{c}|$ and $c = |\vec{b}|$. The midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$ are $M = \frac{1}{2}\vec{b}$ and $N = \frac{1}{2}\vec{c}$. The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to $|O - M|^2 = |O - N|^2$, which expands to:
\[ |O|^2 + \frac{1}{4}c^2 - O \cdot \vec{b} = |O|^2 + \frac{1}{4}b^2 - O \cdot \vec{c} \iff O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2). \]
Let $\angle KBA = \angle ACL = \alpha$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC = \beta$, and $\angle LCK = \angle BMK = \gamma$. Let $\theta_K = \angle BAK$ and $\theta_L = \angle CAL$. In $\triangle BMK$, by the Law of Sines:
\[ \frac{BK}{\sin \gamma} = \frac{BM}{\sin \angle BKM} = \frac{c/2}{\sin(180^\circ - (\alpha + \gamma))} \implies BK = \frac{c \sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}. \]
In $\triangle ABK$, by the Law of Sines:
\[ \frac{BK}{\sin \theta_K} = \frac{AB}{\sin \angle BKA} = \frac{c}{\sin(180^\circ - (\alpha + \theta_K))} \implies BK = \frac{c \sin \theta_K}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}. \]
Equating the two expressions for $BK$:
\[ \frac{\sin \theta_K}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} = \frac{\sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)} \implies 2 \sin \theta_K (\sin \alpha \cos \gamma + \cos \alpha \sin \gamma) = \sin \gamma (\sin \alpha \cos \theta_K + \cos \alpha \sin \theta_K). \]
Dividing by $\sin \gamma \sin \theta_K$ gives $2(\sin \alpha \cot \gamma + \cos \alpha) = \sin \alpha \cot \theta_K + \cos \alpha$, which simplifies to $\cot \theta_K = 2 \cot \gamma + \cot \alpha$. Similarly, considering $\triangle LNC$ and $\triangle ACL$, we obtain $\cot \theta_L = 2 \cot \beta + \cot \alpha$.

Let the coordinates of $A$ be $(0,0)$, $B$ be $(c,0)$, and $C$ be $(b \cos A, b \sin A)$. The point $K$ is determined by $\theta_K$ and $AK = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}$, so $K = (AK \cos \theta_K, AK \sin \theta_K)$. The point $L$ is determined by $\theta_L$ and $AL = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)}$, so $L = (AL \cos(A - \theta_L), AL \sin(A - \theta_L))$. The circumcentre $O(x,y)$ of $\triangle AKL$ is the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of $AK$ and $AL$:
\[ x \cos \theta_K + y \sin \theta_K = \frac{1}{2} AK, \quad x \cos(A - \theta_L) + y \sin(A - \theta_L) = \frac{1}{2} AL. \]
Solving this system for $x$ and $y$ and computing the dot product $O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = x(b \cos A - c) + y(b \sin A)$, we find:
\[ 2 \sin(A - \theta_L - \theta_K) [O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b})] = AL [b \sin(A - \theta_K) + c \sin \theta_K] - AK [b \sin \theta_L + c \sin(A - \theta_L)]. \]
Let $S$ be the right-hand side. Using $b \sin(A - \theta_K) + c \sin \theta_K = a \sin(\theta_K + B)$ and $b \sin \theta_L + c \sin(A - \theta_L) = a \sin(\theta_L + C)$, where $a, B, C$ are the side and angles of $\triangle ABC$:
\[ S = a \sin \alpha \left[ \frac{b \sin(\theta_K + B)}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)} - \frac{c \sin(\theta_L + C)}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} \right]. \]
Substituting $b = \frac{a \sin B}{\sin A}$ and $c = \frac{a \sin C}{\sin A}$:
\[ S = \frac{a^2 \sin \alpha}{\sin A} \left[ \frac{\sin B \sin(\theta_K + B)}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)} - \frac{\sin C \sin(\theta_L + C)}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} \right]. \]
The remaining constraints $\angle LBK = \beta$ and $\angle LCK = \gamma$ imply $\angle LBA = \alpha + \beta$ and $\angle ACK = \alpha + \gamma$. By the Law of Sines in $\triangle ABL$ and $\triangle ACK$:
\[ AL = \frac{c \sin(\alpha + \beta)}{\sin(A - \theta_L + \alpha + \beta)}, \quad AK = \frac{b \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}{\sin(A - \theta_K + \alpha + \gamma)}. \]
Equating these with our previous expressions for $AL$ and $AK$ gives:
\[ \frac{b}{c} = \frac{\sin(\alpha + \beta) \sin(\alpha + \theta_L)}{\sin \alpha \sin(A - \theta_L + \alpha + \beta)}, \quad \frac{c}{b} = \frac{\sin(\alpha + \gamma) \sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}{\sin \alpha \sin(A - \theta_K + \alpha + \gamma)}. \]
Using these ratios and the $\cot$ relations, the expression $S$ simplifies to $S = \frac{1}{2} (b^2 - c^2) \sin(A - \theta_L - \theta_K)$. Thus:
\[ 2 \sin(A - \theta_L - \theta_K) [O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b})] = \frac{1}{2} (b^2 - c^2) \sin(A - \theta_L - \theta_K) \implies O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2). \]
This confirms $OM = ON$.
