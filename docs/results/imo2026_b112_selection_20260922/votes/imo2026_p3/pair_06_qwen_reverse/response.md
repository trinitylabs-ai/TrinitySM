# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: For Liu's strategy of creating $n$ pieces of length $\frac{2}{2n+1}$ and one piece of length $\frac{1}{2n+1}$, if Xiang places exactly one point in each of the $n$ large pieces, then Xiang's total length $S_{Xiang} \le \frac{n}{2n+1}$, implying Liu guarantees at least $\frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
Claim gap: The proof does not rigorously justify why distributing exactly one point per large piece is Xiang's optimal strategy among all possible distributions of $\le n$ points. The upper bound section is incomplete and contains a point-count contradiction that is hand-waved away.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The derivation in lines 16-33 uses somewhat messy indexing, but the core combinatorial inequality $\sum_{j=1}^n p_{2j} \le nx$ for the multiset $\{x\} \cup \bigcup_{i=1}^n \{a_i, b_i\}$ with $a_i+b_i=2x$ is mathematically valid. I verified this by explicitly sorting the multiset into larger halves $a_{(i)}$, the singleton $x$, and smaller halves $b_{(i)}$, and pairing $a_{(2j)}$ with $b_{(2j-1)}$ (or $b_{(2j)}$) to show each pair sums to $\le 2x$. No substantive repairs were needed to verify this inequality; the steps correctly pair large and small elements to bound the sum. The gap regarding Xiang's optimal distribution is a standard extremal principle (equalization minimizes the first player's advantage) that is omitted but does not invalidate the calculated bound for the considered case.
Decisive checks: Verified lines 9-34. The claim $S_{Xiang} \le nx$ holds for the specified piece configuration. Tested boundary cases ($a_i \to 2x, b_i \to 0$ and $a_i=b_i=x$) and confirmed the even-indexed sum never exceeds $nx$. The sorting argument correctly identifies that $p_{2j} + p_{2n-2j+2} \le 2x$ in the aggregated multiset, establishing the bound. The upper bound attempt (lines 36-54) fails to construct a valid general strategy for Xiang and is noted as incomplete.

## Proof B
Established theorem: None fully established. The lower bound argument attempts to show Liu can guarantee $\frac{n+1}{2n+1}$ but relies on a false premise. The upper bound only demonstrates tightness against Liu's specific proposed strategy, not against arbitrary Liu strategies.
Claim gap: Line 10 falsely claims that $V_X = \sum l_{(2j)}$ is the *minimum* possible value of $\sum \min(s, s')$ over all pairings. In reality, $V_X$ is the *maximum* possible sum of minimums over all pairings (achieved by pairing adjacent sorted elements). This reverses the inequality direction needed to bound $V_X$ from above, invalidating the entire lower bound derivation. The subsequent pairing construction and alternating sum hand-waving cannot recover from this fundamental logical error.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The false premise in line 10 is a demonstrated defect. For a set like $\{10, 8, 5, 2\}$, $V_X = 10$, but pairing $(10,2)$ and $(8,5)$ yields a sum of mins equal to $7 < 10$, contradicting the "minimum" claim. Because $V_X$ is actually the maximum, constructing an arbitrary pairing only provides a lower bound on $V_X$, not the upper bound required to prove $V_L \ge c$. The upper bound section (lines 24-27) is incomplete as it only analyzes Xiang's response to one specific Liu strategy.
Decisive checks: Line 10 contains a verified mathematical error regarding the extremal property of sorted even-indexed sums. The inequality direction used in lines 15-20 is therefore invalid. The claim that $V_X \le nL + L/2$ does not follow from the constructed pairing. The upper bound analysis is restricted to a single case and does not address the general obligation.

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Proof A correctly establishes the critical lower bound inequality for the worst-case distribution of Xiang's cuts, with a valid (if notationally cluttered) pairing argument that survives verification. Proof B's lower bound relies on a false claim that the sum of even-indexed sorted pieces is the minimum of pairing minima, which reverses the necessary inequality direction and invalidates the core argument. While both proofs have incomplete upper bound sections, A's substantive progress on the lower bound is mathematically sound, whereas B's central derivation is fundamentally flawed.
