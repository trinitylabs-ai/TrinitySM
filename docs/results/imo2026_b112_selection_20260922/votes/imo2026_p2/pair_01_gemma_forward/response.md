# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to $O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2)$. The relations $\cot \theta_K = 2 \cot \gamma + \cot \alpha$ and $\cot \theta_L = 2 \cot \beta + \cot \alpha$ are derived from the given angle conditions. The dot product $O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b})$ is expressed as $S / (2 \sin(A - \theta_L - \theta_K))$, where $S = a \sin \alpha [ \frac{b \sin(\theta_K + B)}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)} - \frac{c \sin(\theta_L + C)}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} ]$.
Claim gap: The simplification of $S$ to $\frac{1}{2}(b^2 - c^2) \sin(A - \theta_L - \theta_K)$ (Line 23) is not justified. This is the central load-bearing gap of the proof.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE.
Decisive checks: The derivation of the dot product in Line 14 was verified as correct. The $\cot$ relations in Line 9 were verified as correct. The jump in Line 23 is a significant gap.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to $\vec{O} \cdot (\vec{B} - \vec{C}) = \frac{c^2 - b^2}{4}$. The relations $\frac{\sin \theta_K}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} = \frac{\sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}$ and $\frac{\sin \theta_L}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)} = \frac{\sin \beta}{2 \sin(\alpha + \beta)}$ are derived. The dot product $\vec{O} \cdot (\vec{B} - \vec{C})$ is expressed as $\frac{a}{2 \sin \phi} [ AK \sin(C + \theta_L) - AL \sin(B + \theta_K) ]$.
Claim gap: The final simplification of the dot product to $\frac{c^2 - b^2}{4}$ (Line 21) is not justified. This is the central load-bearing gap of the proof.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE.
Decisive checks: The derivation of the circumcenter coordinates (Line 10) and the subsequent dot product simplification (Lines 12-14) were verified as correct. The Law of Sines relations (Lines 4-8) were verified as correct. The jump in Line 21 is a significant gap.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Both proofs follow an almost identical mathematical path and suffer from the same load-bearing gap: the final, complex trigonometric simplification. However, Proof B is more rigorous in its intermediate steps. Specifically, Proof B explicitly derives the coordinates of the circumcenter $O$ and provides the detailed trigonometric identities used to simplify the dot product (Lines 10-14), whereas Proof A simply states the result of these calculations (Lines 13-14). Proof B's more detailed derivation of the dot product expression makes it the stronger submission.
