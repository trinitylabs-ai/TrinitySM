Correctly expresses $\vec{O} \cdot \vec{B}$ and $\vec{O} \cdot \vec{C}$ in the $\{\vec{u}_K, \vec{u}_L\}$ basis and reduces the problem to the trigonometric identity $2a[AL \sin(B+\theta_K) - AK \sin(C+\theta_L)] = (b^2-c^2)\sin \phi$, where $\phi = \angle BAC - \theta_K - \theta_L$.
Claim gap: Step 30 asserts the trigonometric identity is satisfied without proof, appealing vaguely to the uniqueness of $K$ and $L$ and claiming coordinates "ensure" the conclusion. The core algebraic verification linking the angle constraints to the identity is entirely omitted.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The gap is substantive; verifying the identity requires explicit substitution of $AK, AL$ (derived from the cotangent relations) and trigonometric simplification using the remaining angle conditions $\angle LBK = \beta$ and $\angle LCK = \gamma$, which the submission does not perform.
Decisive checks: Lines 1-6 correctly establish the vector condition for $OM=ON$. Lines 9-15 correctly apply Law of Sines and simplify to the cotangent relations. Lines 17-29 correctly compute dot products and apply projection/sine rules to reach the final identity. Line 30 is a demonstrated defect: it replaces necessary algebraic verification with an unjustified uniqueness appeal. The reduction is mathematically sound, but the proof terminates before establishing the theorem.

## Proof B

Claim gap: Step 23 asserts that the angle conditions $\angle LBK = \beta$ and $\angle LCK = \gamma$ imply $\text{Im}(N) = bc(c^2-b^2)\text{Im}(e^{i\theta}\bar{w}_K w_L)$ without carrying out the algebraic simplification. The explicit expansion of $N$ in lines 21-22 makes the required identity transparent and directly verifiable by substituting $w_K, w_L$ and applying the constraint equations. A minor typographical omission of $bc$ in the denominator of line 20 is self-corrected in line 24 and does not affect logical flow.
Decisive checks: Lines 1-7 correctly derive $z_K, z_L$ with proper rotation directions consistent with standard orientation. ## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Both proofs successfully reduce the geometric problem to a parameter identity and both leave the final verification as an assertion. Proof B is stronger because its complex number framework yields a fully explicit algebraic expression for the critical numerator $N$ and denominator, making the required identity transparent and the gap narrowly confined to a tedious but straightforward algebraic simplification. Proof A's final step (line 30) relies on a vague appeal to uniqueness and coordinate properties, which is conceptually weaker and obscures the actual algebraic mechanism needed to close the proof. Proof B's derivation is more self-contained, rigorously tracks orientations and magnitudes, and provides a clear path to verification, whereas Proof A terminates with an unjustified conceptual leap.
