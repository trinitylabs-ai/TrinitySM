# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: Correctly sets up a complex coordinate system with $A=0$, derives $z_K = \frac{z_B}{2}w_K$ and $z_L = \frac{z_C}{2}w_L$ using the Law of Sines and rotation assumptions. Correctly reduces the condition $OM=ON$ to $\text{Re}(z_O(\bar{z}_B - \bar{z}_C)) = (c^2-b^2)/4$ and correctly states the circumcenter formula for $\triangle AKL$. The algebraic manipulation of the circumcenter expression into the form $N/D$ is verified.
Claim gap: Line 23 asserts the critical identity $\text{Im}(N) = bc(c^2-b^2)\text{Im}(e^{i\theta}\bar{w}_K w_L)$ without derivation. It claims the angle conditions $\angle LBK=\beta$ and $\angle LCK=\gamma$ imply this, but provides no algebraic or geometric link between these angles and the complex variables $w_K, w_L, \theta$. The orientation assumptions ($e^{i\gamma}$ vs $e^{-i\gamma}$) are also unverified against the "strictly inside" hypotheses.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The gap is substantive; the proof does not supply the necessary trigonometric relations to bridge the angle conditions to the complex identity. No routine steps can fill this omission.
Decisive checks: Lines 3-7 correctly apply the Law of Sines and rotation to express $z_K, z_L$. Line 10 correctly states the circumcenter formula. Line 12 correctly expands $OM=ON$. Line 23 is a demonstrated defect: the identity is asserted without using $\beta, \gamma$ in any derived form, making the conclusion unsupported. The proof fails to show how the given angle constraints force the required simplification, leaving the central implication unjustified.

## Proof B
Established theorem: Correctly reduces $OM=ON$ to $O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = (b^2-c^2)/4$. Rigorously derives $\cot\theta_K = 2\cot\gamma + \cot\alpha$ and $\cot\theta_L = 2\cot\beta + \cot\alpha$ from the Law of Sines in $\triangle BMK, \triangle ABK, \triangle LNC, \triangle ACL$. Correctly justifies $\angle LBA = \alpha+\beta$ and $\angle ACK = \alpha+\gamma$ using the "strictly inside" conditions. Correctly derives sine ratios for $b/c$ and sets up the circumcenter linear system. Line 14's dot product formula is verified via Cramer's rule.
Claim gap: Lines 14 and 23 skip the explicit algebraic/trigonometric simplification of the dot product expression. The simplification to $S = \frac{1}{2}(b^2-c^2)\sin(A-\theta_L-\theta_K)$ is asserted but follows directly from substituting the derived $\cot$ relations and sine ratios into the expression for $S$.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The skipped steps are routine trigonometric substitutions given the explicit relations derived in lines 9 and 22. No new lemmas or external assumptions are needed.
Decisive checks: Lines 4-9 correctly derive $\cot\theta_K = 2\cot\gamma + \cot\alpha$ via cross-multiplication and division by $\sin\gamma\sin\theta_K$. Lines 19-22 correctly derive the $b/c$ ratios using the remaining angle conditions and the Law of Sines. Line 15 correctly applies projection/sine rule identities ($b\sin(A-\theta_K)+c\sin\theta_K = a\sin(\theta_K+B)$). The logical chain fully utilizes all hypotheses, correctly interprets the interiority conditions for angle addition, and reduces the problem to a verifiable trigonometric identity.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof B explicitly derives the necessary trigonometric relations ($\cot\theta_K = 2\cot\gamma + \cot\alpha$ and the $b/c$ sine ratios) that link the given angle conditions to the coordinates of $K$ and $L$, and correctly justifies the angle addition using the "strictly inside" hypotheses. These relations are essential and rigorously obtained. Proof A asserts the critical algebraic identity at line 23 without deriving how $\beta$ and $\gamma$ constrain the complex variables, leaving the core implication unjustified. While both proofs skip tedious final algebraic simplifications, B's roadmap is complete, all hypotheses are actively used, and the intermediate lemmas are verified. A's central step is a non-sequitur that fails to connect the angle conditions to the complex formulation. B is mathematically stronger.
