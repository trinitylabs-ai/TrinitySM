# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: For sufficiently large $n \ge N$, the sequence satisfies $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ for some positive integers $T, L$. The proof correctly establishes that the family of minimal prime-factor sets $\mathcal{G}_n$ stabilizes, and that the hitting condition becomes periodic modulo $M = \prod_{p \in P} p$, yielding eventual linear recurrence.
Claim gap: Fails to prove the statement for all $n \ge 1$ (stops at $n \ge N$). Contains a demonstrably false claim that $\mathcal{G}_n$ is an intersecting family (Step 5), and relies on an unverified heuristic that "the gap between $P$-smooth numbers grows" to force stabilization of $\mathcal{G}_n$ (Step 13).
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The intersecting family claim is false (the inductive step incorrectly assumes pairwise intersection propagates; e.g., minimal hitting sets need not pairwise intersect). This claim is unused later. The stabilization heuristic in Step 13 requires a rigorous bound on smooth number gaps (e.g., using density estimates or explicit counting) to guarantee gaps exceed $m$ eventually; this justification is absent. The extension to $n < N$ is entirely omitted.
Decisive checks: Step 5's induction fails at the base-to-step transition; the condition that $\mathcal{P}(a_{n+1})$ hits every $S \in \mathcal{G}_n$ does not force existing sets in $\mathcal{G}_n$ to intersect. Step 9's bound $a_{n+1} \le a_n + m$ is verified. Step 13's stabilization argument is conceptually aligned with known number theory but lacks the quantitative gap estimate needed to rule out new minimal hitting sets indefinitely. Step 15 correctly derives eventual periodicity from finite state modulo $M$, but does not address the problem's requirement for all $n \ge 1$.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The sequence satisfies $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ for all $n \ge 1$. The proof rigorously bounds the set of relevant primes, establishes eventual periodicity of the state $(a_n \bmod L_0, \mathcal{F}_n)$, and provides a downward induction framework to extend the recurrence to all initial terms.
Claim gap: Minor logical defect in Step 14 during downward induction: the written claim $\gcd(a_n, a_i) > 1$ for $n \le i < N$ is unjustified and generally false. The argument conflates indices and requires the shift $a_i = a_{i-T} + L$ to correctly reduce to $\gcd(a_n, a_{i-T}) > 1$ (since $i-T < n$).
Qualifications and supplied repairs: Step 5's finite prime set argument is rigorous and correctly uses witness indices and gcd stabilization. Step 7's state machine periodicity is verified. Step 14's defect is localized: for $i \ge N$, the shift correctly reduces the gcd condition to indices $< n$. For $i < N$, the text incorrectly asserts $\gcd(a_n, a_i) > 1$; the intended logic relies on $a_i = a_{i-T} + L$ and $i-T < n$, which holds but is not explicitly written. No substantive new lemmas are needed to fix the index shift, but the written justification is incomplete.
Decisive checks: Step 3's bound $a_{n+1} \le a_n + m_{\max}$ is verified. Step 5's stabilization of $h_n$ and finiteness of $\mathcal{P}_{\text{global}}$ is mathematically sound and avoids heuristics. Step 7's finite state argument correctly implies eventual periodicity. Step 9-15's downward induction structure correctly handles the domain extension; the only defect is the sloppy gcd justification in Step 14 for intermediate indices, which does not break the overall induction framework.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof B provides a complete, rigorous framework that addresses all $n \ge 1$, whereas Proof A only establishes the result for $n \ge N$ and leaves the initial segment entirely unaddressed. B's core argument (finite prime set via gcd stabilization, state machine periodicity) is algebraically rigorous and avoids the heuristic smooth-number gap estimate used in A. While B contains a localized textual defect in Step 14 regarding index shifting in the gcd condition, the underlying induction structure is correct and standard, whereas A's gaps (false intersecting-family lemma, unverified stabilization heuristic, and incomplete domain) are structural and leave the problem statement unfulfilled. B's verified progress and correct extension strategy decisively outweigh A's partial result.
