# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: Under the assumption that prime factors appearing in minimal hitting sets eventually belong to a finite set $\mathcal{P}_{global}$, the sequence satisfies $a_{n+T'} = a_n + L'$ for all $n \ge N$. The submission attempts to extend this to all $n \ge 1$ via downward induction.
Claim gap: The finiteness of $\mathcal{P}_{global}$ (Step 5) is asserted without rigorous verification of the stabilization of the gcd sequences $g_{S',n}$ or handling of cases where $I_{S'}$ is empty. The downward induction (Steps 9-15) contains a logical inconsistency in Step 14: it claims $\gcd(a_n, a_i) > 1$ "since $i > n$", but the index $i$ ranges over $N \le i < n+T$, which includes values $i < n$. The induction also assumes the constraint family $\mathcal{F}_{n+T-1}$ maps perfectly to $\mathcal{F}_{n-1}$ under the shift $x \mapsto x-L$, which is not justified.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The finiteness argument relies on unstated properties of hitting set witnesses and gcd stabilization. The downward induction's minimality check assumes $\gcd(y-L, a_j) = \gcd(y, a_{j+T})$ preserves the exact constraint structure, which requires $\mathcal{P}(a_{j+T}) = \mathcal{P}(a_j)$ or similar, not established.
Decisive checks: Step 3 correctly bounds $a_{n+1} \le a_n + m_{max}$ using $Q_1$ as a universal hitting set. Step 5's claim that $p$ divides $g_{S',n}$ is correct in isolation, but the conclusion that $\mathcal{P}_{global}$ is finite requires proving $g_{S'} > 1$ or that empty intersections don't introduce unbounded primes, which is omitted. Step 14's index claim "$i > n$" is demonstrably false for $i \in [N, n)$, breaking the minimality contradiction argument.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The family of minimal prime-factor sets $\mathcal{G}_n$ stabilizes to a constant intersecting family $\mathcal{G}$ for $n \ge N$. Consequently, $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ for all $n \ge N$, where $T$ and $L$ arise from the periodicity of residues modulo $M = \prod_{p \in P} p$.
Claim gap: The proof only establishes the relation for $n \ge N$, not for every positive integer $n$ as requested. Extending to $n < N$ requires additional work (e.g., adjusting $T, L$ or verifying initial segments), which is omitted.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The argument that gaps between $P$-smooth numbers exceed any fixed bound $m$ for large numbers is a standard analytic number theory fact (density of smooth numbers tends to 0), accepted as routine justification. The modular periodicity derivation in Step 15 is complete for $n \ge N$.
Decisive checks: Step 5 correctly proves $\mathcal{G}_n$ is pairwise intersecting by induction. Step 9 correctly shows $m_n$ is non-decreasing and bounded, hence stabilizes. Step 11 correctly shows $P_n = \bigcup \mathcal{G}_n$ is non-increasing and stabilizes to $P$. Step 13 correctly uses the unbounded gaps between $P$-smooth numbers to force $\mathcal{G}_n$ to stabilize, as $a_{n+1} \le a_n + m$ prevents introducing new minimal prime sets eventually. Step 15 correctly reduces the recurrence to a finite-state machine on residues mod $M$, yielding eventual linear periodicity.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof B provides a rigorous, self-contained derivation of the eventual linear recurrence $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ for $n \ge N$. Its core steps (stabilization of the minimal hitting family $\mathcal{G}_n$, boundedness of the minimal hitting product, and modular periodicity) are mathematically sound and rely on standard, verifiable number-theoretic facts. Proof A attempts to cover the full domain $n \ge 1$ but contains unverified claims in its finiteness argument (Step 5) and a demonstrable index error in its downward induction (Step 14, claiming $i > n$ when $i$ can be $< n$). While B omits the extension to $n < N$, this is a scope limitation rather than a logical defect in its central derivation. B's mathematical core is substantially more rigorous and correctly justified.
