# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The sequence of prime factors involved in minimal hitting sets is finite (Step 5), implying the sequence of states $(a_n \pmod{L_0}, \mathcal{F}_n)$ is eventually periodic. This establishes that there exist $T', L'$ such that $a_{n+T'} = a_n + L'$ for all $n \ge N$ (Step 7).
Claim gap: The extension of the linear relation $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ to all $n \ge 1$ (Steps 9-15) relies on choosing $L$ as a multiple of $\text{rad}(a_1 \dots a_{N+mT'})$. This condition is likely impossible to satisfy if the sequence introduces arbitrarily large prime factors, as the radical grows super-linearly while $L$ grows linearly with the scaling factor $m$. The argument fails to justify why the required compatibility for the "backtracking" step can be achieved with a fixed $L$ derived from the tail periodicity.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The audit assumes the finiteness of primes in minimal hitting sets is correct (a non-trivial claim). The gap in Step 9 is a demonstrated defect in the justification; the proof claims a sufficient condition (multiple of radical) that is likely too strong and unattainable, without proving a weaker necessary condition (multiple of common factors) is sufficient and attainable.
Decisive checks: Step 3 correctly bounds gaps. Step 5's finiteness argument is dense but plausible. Step 9's requirement $L \mid \text{rad}(a_1 \dots a_{N+mT'})$ is the critical failure point. For $a_n = 2n+4$, $\text{rad}(a_1 \dots a_K)$ grows exponentially, making the divisibility condition impossible for large $K$.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The gaps are bounded by $M = \prod_{p \in P(a_1)} p$ (Step 3). The sequence of restricted prime factor sets $S'_n$ stabilizes (Step 7).
Claim gap: The proof asserts that $a_{n+1}$ is always the smallest multiple of a hitting set contained in $\mathcal{P}_M$ (Step 5), which is not rigorously justified. More critically, the extension to $n < N$ (Steps 9-11) assumes that $a_{n+1}$ (for $n < N$) hits the restricted sets $S'_N$ (i.e., shares small prime factors with all future terms $a_k$ for $k \le N$). This is not guaranteed; $a_{n+1}$ only needs to share *some* factor with future terms, which could be a large prime not in $\mathcal{P}_M$. If $a_{n+1}$ connects to $a_N$ via a large prime, it does not hit $P(a_N) \cap \mathcal{P}_M$, breaking the gap comparison argument.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The audit identifies the assumption in Step 9 as a demonstrated defect. The logic that $g_{n+T} \le g_n$ depends on $a_n + g_n$ being a valid candidate for the stricter constraints of $S'_N$, which fails if $a_{n+1}$ lacks small common factors with future terms.
Decisive checks: Step 3 is correct. Step 5's claim about $a_{n+1}$ being a multiple of a small hitting set is unsupported. Step 9's claim that $a_{n+1}$ hits $S'_N$ is false in general (counterexample: connection via large prime).

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Proof A provides a more robust structural analysis by identifying the finiteness of primes in minimal hitting sets and using state periodicity to establish tail linearity. While Proof A's extension to $n=1$ has a gap regarding the size of $L$, the core mechanism (periodicity of the constraint structure) is sound. Proof B's extension argument relies on a false assumption that early terms must share small prime factors with all future terms, which is not required by the problem statement and breaks the logical chain for the gap comparison. Proof A's defect is in the sufficiency of a specific condition for $L$, whereas Proof B's defect is in the validity of the structural premise for the induction step.
