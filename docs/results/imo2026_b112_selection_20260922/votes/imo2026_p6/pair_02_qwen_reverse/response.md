# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The set of sequence terms $S$ coincides with $\{x \in \mathbb{Z}^+ : \forall s \in S, \gcd(x,s)>1\}$ for $x \ge a_1$. The family $\mathcal{M}$ of minimal hitting sets of prime factors is finite, implying $S$ is a finite union of arithmetic progressions, which yields $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ for all $n$.
Claim gap: Step 3 asserts $S = \{x \in \mathbb{Z}^+ : \forall s \in S, \gcd(x,s)>1\}$ without restricting to $x \ge a_1$, which is false for integers smaller than $a_1$ that share factors with $S$. Step 13 claims $\mathcal{M}'_p$ is exactly the collection of minimal hitting sets for $\mathcal{F}_p$; it is only a subset. The induction hypothesis phrasing ("provided the hitting sets have size at most $k-1$") is ambiguous regarding whether it bounds all minimal hitting sets or just those in $\mathcal{M}'_p$.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: Restricted the set equality in Step 3 to $x \ge a_1$ to match the sequence domain. Interpreted the induction in Steps 9-16 as applying to the subset $\mathcal{M}'_p \subseteq \text{MinHS}(\mathcal{F}_p)$, which suffices for finiteness. Noted that the transition from "$S$ is a finite union of APs" to "$a_{n+T} = a_n + L'$" relies on standard enumeration properties of periodic integer sets.
Decisive checks: Step 1 correctly deduces the pairwise GCD condition. Step 3's contradiction argument works for $x > a_1$ but fails for $x < a_1$ (e.g., if $S=\{6,10,15,\dots\}$, $x=2$ satisfies the GCD condition but $2 \notin S$). Step 13's claim that $\mathcal{M}'_p$ equals the full collection of minimal hitting sets for $\mathcal{F}_p$ is demonstrably false in general combinatorial settings (it only captures those extending from $\mathcal{M}_p$), though the finiteness conclusion survives by subset containment. The core structural reduction to hitting sets is valid but technically imprecise.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The sequence of prime factor families $\mathcal{F}_n$ and the values $a_n \bmod L_0$ eventually enter a finite state cycle, yielding eventual linear recurrence $a_{n+T'} = a_n + L'$ for $n \ge N$. A downward induction extends this to all $n \ge 1$ with adjusted period $T$ and shift $L$.
Claim gap: Step 7 compresses the deduction from state periodicity to $a_{n+T'} = a_n + L'$. State periodicity gives $a_{n+T'} \equiv a_n \pmod{L_0}$ and $\mathcal{F}_{n+T'} = \mathcal{F}_n$, which implies the recurrence rule repeats. The conclusion that differences $a_{n+T'} - a_n$ stabilize to a constant $L'$ requires invoking strict monotonicity and the bounded gap $a_{n+1} - a_n \le m_{\max}$ (Step 3), which is omitted but standard.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: Supplied the standard monotonicity argument for Step 7: since $a_n$ is strictly increasing and $a_{n+1}-a_n$ is bounded, the sequence of differences $d_n = a_{n+T'} - a_n$ is non-decreasing and bounded modulo $L_0$, forcing $d_n$ to be constant for large $n$. All other steps are self-contained and verified.
Decisive checks: Step 5's stabilization argument is rigorous: it correctly partitions primes in minimal hitting sets into those dividing early terms $a_i$ ($i \le N$) and those dividing stabilized GCDs $g_{S'}$, proving $\mathcal{P}_{\text{global}}$ is finite. Step 9-15's downward induction is fully verified: the choice of $L$ as a multiple of $\text{rad}(a_1 \dots a_{N+mT'})$ correctly handles the GCD conditions for $i < N$, and the minimality contradiction for $y \in (a_{n-1}+L, a_n+L)$ correctly maps back to the original sequence's minimality condition via $\gcd(y-L, a_j) = \gcd(y, a_{j+T})$. The proof handles the dynamic nature of $\mathcal{F}_n$ and the eventual-to-global transition explicitly.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof B provides a more rigorous and self-contained justification for the critical steps. Its stabilization argument in Step 5 carefully bounds the primes that can appear in minimal hitting sets as $n$ grows, avoiding the ambiguous induction phrasing and false set-equality claim in Proof A's Step 3 and Step 13. Furthermore, Proof B's downward induction (Steps 9-15) rigorously bridges eventual periodicity to global periodicity, a non-trivial obligation that Proof A glosses over. While Proof A's structural reduction to hitting sets is elegant, its technical gaps in domain restriction and induction claims weaken its completeness. Proof B's explicit handling of state stabilization and minimality preservation makes it the stronger justified solution.
