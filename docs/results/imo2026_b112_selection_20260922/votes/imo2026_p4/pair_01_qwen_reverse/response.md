# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The submission claims Mulan wins iff $\theta \in \mathbb{Q} \cdot 180^\circ$ and $\theta < 120^\circ$. It correctly identifies that $\theta \ge 120^\circ$ is a loss for Mulan (via the equilateral triangle strategy) and attempts to argue that irrational $\theta$ is a loss. The rational $\theta < 120^\circ$ case is asserted but not rigorously established.
Claim gap: Major gaps in both the irrational avoidance argument and the rational winning strategy. The irrational argument misapplies density of subgroups to a discrete choice game and fails to prove Mulan cannot force $\theta$. The rational argument lacks a monotonic invariant or reachability proof, hand-waves cycle avoidance, and fails to address cases where all angles are $\ge \theta$, which blocks the proposed cut $\alpha_1 = 180^\circ - \beta - \theta$. The claimed condition is incorrect (e.g., $\theta = 72^\circ$ satisfies A's condition but is a loss for Mulan).
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The argument relies on unverified claims about finite state traversal and incorrect density reasoning. No repairs can salvage the rational strategy without introducing a new descent/invariant mechanism.
Decisive checks: 
- Line 3-7: Claims Shan-Yu avoids $S = \{n\theta + m180^\circ\}$ indefinitely. Density of $S$ is irrelevant; Shan-Yu only needs to avoid the single value $\theta$. The argument does not prove Mulan cannot force $\theta$ through geometric constraints.
- Line 13-16: Claims Mulan can force angles into $G$ and then "force the state to $(m, n_2, n_3)$" because "state space is finite and Mulan can choose from multiple values of $k$ to avoid cycles." This is unjustified. Finite state spaces do not guarantee reachability of a target state without a proven monotonic quantity or strategy theorem. No mechanism is given to handle triangles where all angles $\ge \theta$.
- Falsification check: Take $\theta = 72^\circ$. A claims Mulan wins. B's invariant $W = \{72^\circ, 144^\circ\}$ shows Shan-Yu can maintain a triangle with no angles in $W$ forever (since $180^\circ \notin W$ and initial angles can avoid $W$). Thus A's conclusion is false.

## Proof B
Established theorem: Mulan can guarantee victory iff $\theta = 180^\circ/k$ for some integer $k \ge 2$. The proof establishes a complete necessary and sufficient condition with rigorous descent, forcing, and avoidance arguments.
Claim gap: NONE supported by checks. All steps are algebraically verified and cover all cases.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The argument is self-contained and complete.
Decisive checks:
- Line 3: Descent argument. If $T$ has angle $n\theta$ ($n>1$), cutting it into $\theta$ and $(n-1)\theta$ yields two triangles. If Shan-Yu keeps the one with $\theta$, Mulan wins. If he keeps the other, the new triangle has angle $(n-1)\theta$. The coefficient strictly decreases, guaranteeing termination in $\le n$ steps. Verified.
- Line 5-11: Forcing argument for $\theta = 180^\circ/k$. Mulan targets a cut $\alpha$ such that $B+\alpha = n\theta$. Then $T_2$ has $n\theta$ and $T_1$ has $180^\circ - n\theta = (k-n)\theta$. Both are in $W$. The interval $(B, B+A)$ has length $A$. If $A > \theta$, it contains a multiple of $\theta$. If $A,B,C \le \theta$, then $k\theta = 180^\circ \le 3\theta \implies k \le 3$. Cases $k=2,3$ are explicitly resolved, showing a valid $n$ always exists. Verified.
- Line 13-21: Avoidance argument for $\theta \neq 180^\circ/k$. Shan-Yu maintains the invariant "no angle in $W$". For any cut, $T_1$ has an angle in $W$ iff $\alpha \in W$ or $180^\circ-B-\alpha \in W$. $T_2$ has an angle in $W$ iff $A-\alpha \in W$ or $B+\alpha \in W$. The 4 combinations exhaust all possibilities for both triangles to contain $W$. Each combination algebraically implies $A \in W$, $B \in W$, $C \in W$, or $180^\circ \in W$, all contradicting the initial state and $\theta \neq 180^\circ/k$. Thus at least one triangle avoids $W$, and Shan-Yu picks it. Invariant holds. Verified.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof B provides a complete, rigorous characterization using a clean invariant ($W = \{n\theta\}$) and exhaustively verifies forcing, descent, and avoidance. Its 4-case algebraic check (lines 16-20) definitively proves that Shan-Yu can maintain the invariant whenever $\theta \neq 180^\circ/k$, and the interval/descent arguments correctly establish sufficiency. Proof A's rational strategy lacks a monotonic invariant, hand-waves reachability in a finite state space, and incorrectly concludes all rational $\theta < 120^\circ$ are winning (falsified by $\theta = 72^\circ$ under B's verified invariant). B's mathematical obligations are fully met; A's central derivation contains unverified leaps and leads to an incorrect conclusion.
