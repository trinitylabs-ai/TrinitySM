# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: Mulan can guarantee victory if and only if $\theta = 180^\circ/n$ for some integer $n \geq 2$. The proof establishes that when $\theta$ divides $180^\circ$, the set $W = \{k\theta \mid k \in \mathbb{Z}^+, k\theta < 180^\circ\}$ is closed under the operation $x \mapsto 180^\circ-x$. From any triangle with no angles in $W$, Mulan can force at least one child triangle into $W$ by choosing a cut angle $\psi \in W$. Once in $W$, she can iteratively split the angle in $W$ into $\theta$ and a smaller multiple of $\theta$, guaranteeing that Shan-Yu must eventually keep a triangle containing $\theta$. When $\theta$ does not divide $180^\circ$, the proof shows that no cut can place both children in $W$ simultaneously, allowing Shan-Yu to maintain the invariant that no angle belongs to $W$.
Claim gap: NONE supported by checks. The argument covers all cases, correctly handles the open interval constraints for the cut, and rigorously verifies the invariant and induction steps.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The proof is self-contained. A minor phrasing imprecision in line 17 ("unless $\alpha$ is a multiple of $\theta$") does not affect validity: since $\alpha, \beta \notin W$, the endpoints of the interval $(\alpha, 180^\circ-\beta)$ are not multiples of $\theta$. An open interval of length $\gamma \geq 60^\circ \geq \theta$ with non-multiple endpoints necessarily contains a multiple of $\theta$, so the existence of $\psi \in W$ is guaranteed without exception.
Decisive checks: 
- Lines 3-6: Correctly derives the angle sets of the child triangles and establishes that the cut angle $\psi$ ranges over the open interval $(\alpha, 180^\circ-\beta)$ of length $\gamma$. Verified via continuous variation of $P$ along $AB$.
- Line 8: Induction step correctly notes that splitting $\phi=k\theta$ into $\theta$ and $(k-1)\theta$ places both children in $W$, forcing the multiplier to decrease strictly. Verified.
- Lines 13-18: Case $\theta=180^\circ/n$. Uses symmetry $x \in W \iff 180^\circ-x \in W$. Correctly identifies that picking $\psi \in W$ satisfies the condition for both children to contain angles in $W$. Verified.
- Lines 20-29: Case $\theta \neq 180^\circ/n$. Exhaustively checks the four logical combinations for forcing both children into $W$. Each combination algebraically forces an original angle ($\alpha, \beta,$ or $\gamma$) to be in $W$, contradicting the hypothesis. Verified. The invariant argument correctly concludes Shan-Yu can avoid $W$ indefinitely.

## Proof B
Established theorem: Claims Mulan wins iff $\theta$ is a rational multiple of $180^\circ$ and $\theta < 120^\circ$. This characterization is mathematically incorrect.
Claim gap: Fundamental error in the winning condition. The proof fails to distinguish between rational multiples of $180^\circ$ and exact divisors of $180^\circ$. The finite-state reachability argument (lines 14-16) is heuristic, ignores geometric constraints on valid cuts, and lacks rigorous justification. The irrational case argument (lines 3-7) misapplies density modulo $180^\circ$, which is irrelevant to hitting an exact value $\theta$, and contains flawed algebraic deductions about angle membership in the subgroup $S$.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The proof contains irreparable logical defects. A concrete counterexample falsifies the main claim: let $\theta = 100^\circ$. It satisfies B's condition ($100^\circ = \frac{5}{9} \cdot 180^\circ < 120^\circ$), but Shan-Yu wins. Starting from $T=(50^\circ, 60^\circ, 70^\circ)$, any cut creating $100^\circ$ in one child produces $80^\circ$ in the other. Shan-Yu discards the $100^\circ$ triangle. The game state cycles or stays in a region where no angle reaches $100^\circ$. B's claim that "Mulan can always choose a cut... to force the state to $(m, n_2, n_3)$" is unsubstantiated and false for this case.
Decisive checks:
- Lines 9-11: Correctly shows $\theta \geq 120^\circ$ is a loss for Mulan using the equilateral triangle. Verified.
- Lines 3-7: The dense subgroup argument is misapplied. Density modulo $180^\circ$ does not prevent exact hits, nor does it guarantee avoidance. The deduction that $T_2$ has no angles in $S$ when $\alpha_1 \in S$ is correct, but the subsequent handling of the case where both children land in $S$ is algebraically confused and fails to address the exact hit condition properly.
- Lines 12-17: The reduction to a finite integer game assumes reachability of the target state without proof. The claim that rationality alone suffices is false, as demonstrated by the $\theta=100^\circ$ counterexample. The proof does not establish the necessary condition $\theta = 180^\circ/n$.

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Proof A provides a complete, rigorous, and correct characterization of the winning angles ($\theta = 180^\circ/n$). Its geometric analysis of the cut interval, the invariant set $W$, and the exhaustive case analysis for forcing/avoiding $W$ are all mathematically sound and verified. Proof B incorrectly claims that any rational multiple of $180^\circ$ less than $120^\circ$ is a winning angle, which is falsified by counterexamples like $\theta = 100^\circ$. Furthermore, B's arguments for the rational case rely on unjustified reachability claims in a finite state space, and its irrational case argument misapplies density concepts. Proof A's decisive advantage is its correct necessary-and-sufficient condition backed by a verified invariant and induction, whereas B's central claim is false.
