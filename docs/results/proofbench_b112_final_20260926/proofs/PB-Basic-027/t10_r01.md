To prove that $XO$ is perpendicular to $DE$, we establish a coordinate system with $C$ as the origin $(0,0)$ and the line $CA$ as the $x$-axis. Let $a, b, c$ denote the side lengths $BC, AC, AB$ and $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ denote the angles at vertices $A, B, C$, respectively.

### 1. Coordinates of the Points
Let $r_0$ be the length of the altitude $CE$. In $\triangle BCE$, since $\angle BEC = 90^\circ$ and $\angle CBE = \beta$, we have $r_0 = CE = a \sin \beta$. In $\triangle AEC$, since $\angle AEC = 90^\circ$ and $\angle EAC = \alpha$, we have $\angle ECA = 90^\circ - \alpha$. Let $\theta = 90^\circ - \alpha$.
The coordinates of $E$ are:
\[ E = (r_0 \cos \theta, r_0 \sin \theta) \]
Since $E_1$ is the reflection of $E$ across $AC$ (the $x$-axis), its coordinates are:
\[ E_1 = (r_0 \cos \theta, -r_0 \sin \theta) \]
The line $BC$ is at an angle $\gamma$ with the $x$-axis. The reflection $E_2$ of $E$ across $BC$ will have an angle relative to $C$ given by $2\gamma - \theta$. Thus:
\[ E_2 = (r_0 \cos(2\gamma - \theta), r_0 \sin(2\gamma - \theta)) \]

### 2. The Circumcircle of $\triangle CE_1E_2$
Let the equation of the circumcircle $\omega$ of $\triangle CE_1E_2$ be $x^2 + y^2 - 2x_0 x - 2y_0 y = 0$, where $O = (x_0, y_0)$ is the center. Since $E_1$ and $E_2$ lie on $\omega$:
1) $r_0^2 - 2x_0 r_0 \cos \theta + 2y_0 r_0 \sin \theta = 0 \implies x_0 \cos \theta - y_0 \sin \theta = \frac{r_0}{2}$
2) $r_0^2 - 2x_0 r_0 \cos(2\gamma - \theta) - 2y_0 r_0 \sin(2\gamma - \theta) = 0 \implies x_0 \cos(2\gamma - \theta) + y_0 \sin(2\gamma - \theta) = \frac{r_0}{2}$

Equating the two expressions:
\[ x_0 (\cos \theta - \cos(2\gamma - \theta)) = y_0 (\sin(2\gamma - \theta) + \sin \theta) \]
Using the sum-to-product identities $\cos A - \cos B = -2 \sin \frac{A+B}{2} \sin \frac{A-B}{2}$ and $\sin A + \sin B = 2 \sin \frac{A+B}{2} \cos \frac{A-B}{2}$:
\[ -2x_0 \sin \gamma \sin(\theta - \gamma) = 2y_0 \sin \gamma \cos(\gamma - \theta) \]
Since the triangle is acute, $\sin \gamma \neq 0$, so:
\[ x_0 \sin(\gamma - \theta) = y_0 \cos(\gamma - \theta) \implies y_0 = x_0 \tan(\gamma - \theta) \]
Substituting $y_0 = x_0 \tan(\gamma - \theta)$ into equation (1):
\[ x_0 (\cos \theta - \tan(\gamma - \theta) \sin \theta) = \frac{r_0}{2} \implies x_0 \frac{\cos \theta \cos(\gamma - \theta) - \sin \theta \sin(\gamma - \theta)}{\cos(\gamma - \theta)} = \frac{r_0}{2} \]
Using the identity $\cos(A+B) = \cos A \cos B - \sin A \sin B$:
\[ x_0 \frac{\cos(\theta + \gamma - \theta)}{\cos(\gamma - \theta)} = \frac{r_0}{2} \implies x_0 \frac{\cos \gamma}{\cos(\gamma - \theta)} = \frac{r_0}{2} \]
Thus, the coordinates of $O$ are:
\[ x_0 = \frac{r_0 \cos(\gamma - \theta)}{2 \cos \gamma}, \quad y_0 = \frac{r_0 \sin(\gamma - \theta)}{2 \cos \gamma} \]

### 3. Intersection Point $X$ and Vector $XO$
The point $X$ is the intersection of $\omega$ and the $x$-axis ($AC$). Setting $y=0$ in $x^2 + y^2 - 2x_0 x - 2y_0 y = 0$ gives $x(x - 2x_0) = 0$. Since $X \neq C$, we have $X = (2x_0, 0)$.
The vector $\vec{XO}$ is:
\[ \vec{XO} = O - X = (x_0 - 2x_0, y_0 - 0) = (-x_0, y_0) \]

### 4. Perpendicularity of $XO$ and $DE$
The point $D$ is the foot of the altitude from $B$ to $AC$. Since $B = (a \cos \gamma, a \sin \gamma)$, we have $D = (a \cos \gamma, 0)$.
The vector $\vec{DE}$ is:
\[ \vec{DE} = E - D = (r_0 \cos \theta - a \cos \gamma, r_0 \sin \theta) \]
We compute the dot product $\vec{XO} \cdot \vec{DE}$:
\[ \vec{XO} \cdot \vec{DE} = -x_0(r_0 \cos \theta - a \cos \gamma) + y_0 r_0 \sin \theta = -r_0(x_0 \cos \theta - y_0 \sin \theta) + x_0 a \cos \gamma \]
Substituting $x_0 \cos \theta - y_0 \sin \theta = \frac{r_0}{2}$ and the expression for $x_0$:
\[ \vec{XO} \cdot \vec{DE} = -\frac{r_0^2}{2} + \left( \frac{r_0 \cos(\gamma - \theta)}{2 \cos \gamma} \right) a \cos \gamma = \frac{r_0}{2} (a \cos(\gamma - \theta) - r_0) \]
Substitute $\theta = 90^\circ - \alpha$:
\[ \cos(\gamma - \theta) = \cos(\gamma - (90^\circ - \alpha)) = \cos(\gamma + \alpha - 90^\circ) = \sin(\gamma + \alpha) \]
In any triangle, $\sin(\gamma + \alpha) = \sin(180^\circ - \beta) = \sin \beta$. Given $r_0 = a \sin \beta$, we have:
\[ \vec{XO} \cdot \vec{DE} = \frac{r_0}{2} (a \sin \beta - a \sin \beta) = 0 \]
Thus, $XO$ is perpendicular to $DE$.
