To prove that $XO$ is perpendicular to $DE$ in an acute triangle $ABC$, we establish a coordinate system and calculate the slopes of the lines $DE$ and $XO$.

Let $C$ be the origin $(0, 0)$ and the side $CA$ lie on the $x$-axis. Let $a, b, c$ denote the side lengths $BC, CA, AB$ respectively, and let $A, B, C$ denote the angles of the triangle.

1. **Coordinates of $D$ and $E$**:
   Since $C$ is the origin and $A$ lies on the $x$-axis, the coordinates of $A$ are $(b, 0)$. The vertex $B$ has coordinates $(a \cos C, a \sin C)$.
   $D$ is the foot of the altitude from $B$ to $AC$, so $D$ is the projection of $B$ onto the $x$-axis:
   \[ D = (a \cos C, 0) \]
   $E$ is the foot of the altitude from $C$ to $AB$. In $\triangle ACE$, $\angle AEC = 90^\circ$, so $CE = b \sin A$. The angle $\angle ACE = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - A = 90^\circ - A$. Thus, the coordinates of $E$ are:
   \[ E = (CE \cos(90^\circ - A), CE \sin(90^\circ - A)) = (b \sin A \sin A, b \sin A \cos A) = (b \sin^2 A, b \sin A \cos A) \]

2. **Slope of $DE$**:
   The slope of the line $DE$ is given by:
   \[ m_{DE} = \frac{y_E - y_D}{x_E - x_D} = \frac{b \sin A \cos A - 0}{b \sin^2 A - a \cos C} \]
   Using the Law of Sines, $a = \frac{b \sin A}{\sin B}$. Substituting this into the expression for the denominator:
   \[ x_E - x_D = b \sin^2 A - \frac{b \sin A \cos C}{\sin B} = \frac{b \sin A (\sin A \sin B - \cos C)}{\sin B} \]
   Since $A + B + C = 180^\circ$, we have $\cos C = -\cos(A+B) = \sin A \sin B - \cos A \cos B$. Substituting this:
   \[ x_E - x_D = \frac{b \sin A (\sin A \sin B - (\sin A \sin B - \cos A \cos B))}{\sin B} = \frac{b \sin A \cos A \cos B}{\sin B} \]
   Now, we calculate the slope:
   \[ m_{DE} = \frac{b \sin A \cos A}{\frac{b \sin A \cos A \cos B}{\sin B}} = \frac{\sin B}{\cos B} = \tan B \]

3. **Coordinates of $O$ and $X$**:
   Let $\theta = \angle ACE = 90^\circ - A$. The point $E$ has polar coordinates $(CE, \theta)$.
   $E_1$ is the reflection of $E$ across $AC$ (the $x$-axis), so its polar angle is $-\theta$.
   $E_2$ is the reflection of $E$ across $BC$. The line $BC$ has polar angle $C$, so the polar angle of $E_2$ is $2C - \theta$.
   The points $C, E_1, E_2$ lie on the circumcircle $\Gamma$ with center $O$. Since $CE_1 = CE = CE_2$, the triangle $CE_1 E_2$ is isosceles with $C$ as a vertex on the circle. The circumcenter $O$ must lie on the perpendicular bisectors of $CE_1$ and $CE_2$. The polar angle $\phi$ of the vector $\vec{CO}$ is the average of the polar angles of $E_1$ and $E_2$:
   \[ \phi = \frac{-\theta + (2C - \theta)}{2} = C - \theta \]
   Let $O = (x_O, y_O)$. The equation of $\Gamma$ (which passes through the origin $C$) is $x^2 + y^2 - 2x x_O - 2y y_O = 0$.
   $X$ is the intersection of $\Gamma$ and the $x$-axis ($y=0$) other than $C$:
   \[ x^2 - 2x x_O = 0 \implies x = 2x_O \implies X = (2x_O, 0) \]

4. **Slope of $XO$**:
   The vector $\vec{XO} = (x_O - 2x_O, y_O - 0) = (-x_O, y_O)$. The slope of $XO$ is:
   \[ m_{XO} = \frac{y_O}{-x_O} = -\tan \phi = -\tan(C - \theta) \]
   Substituting $\theta = 90^\circ - A$:
   \[ m_{XO} = -\tan(C - (90^\circ - A)) = -\tan(C + A - 90^\circ) \]
   Using the identity $\tan(\psi - 90^\circ) = -\cot \psi$:
   \[ m_{XO} = -(-\cot(C + A)) = \cot(C + A) \]
   Since $A + B + C = 180^\circ$, $C + A = 180^\circ - B$. Thus:
   \[ m_{XO} = \cot(180^\circ - B) = -\cot B \]

5. **Final Verification**:
   The product of the slopes is:
   \[ m_{DE} \cdot m_{XO} = (\tan B) \cdot (-\cot B) = -1 \]
   Since the product of the slopes is $-1$, the lines $XO$ and $DE$ are perpendicular.
