Let the side lengths of triangle $ABC$ be $BC=a$, $AC=b$, and $AB=c$. Let $s = \frac{a+b+c}{2}$ be the semi-perimeter and $r$ be the inradius. Let $x=s-a$, $y=s-b$, and $z=s-c$. Then $a=y+z$, $b=x+z$, and $c=x+y$.

**1. Positions of $B'$ and $C'$**
The line $L_{B'}$ is parallel to $AC$ and tangent to the incircle. Since $AC$ is also tangent to the incircle, the distance between $L_{B'}$ and $AC$ is $2r$. Let $h_b$ be the altitude from $B$ to $AC$. Since $B'$ lies on $BC$ and $L_{B'}$ is parallel to $AC$, by similar triangles, we have:
$$\frac{BB'}{BC} = \frac{h_b - 2r}{h_b} = 1 - \frac{2r}{h_b}$$
Substituting $h_b = \frac{2\Delta}{b} = \frac{2rs}{b}$, we get $\frac{BB'}{a} = 1 - \frac{b}{s} = \frac{s-b}{s}$, so $BB' = \frac{a(s-b)}{s}$.
Similarly, for $C'$ on $BC$ such that the line $L_{C'}$ is parallel to $AB$ and tangent to the incircle, we have:
$$\frac{CC'}{BC} = 1 - \frac{2r}{h_c} = 1 - \frac{c}{s} = \frac{s-c}{s} \implies CC' = \frac{a(s-c)}{s}$$

**2. Calculation of $\angle B'KC'$**
Set $B$ as the origin $(0,0)$ and $C$ as $(a,0)$. Then $B' = (\frac{a(s-b)}{s}, 0)$ and $C' = (a - \frac{a(s-c)}{s}, 0) = (\frac{ac}{s}, 0)$.
$K$ is the midpoint of the arc $BC$ not containing $A$, so $K$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $BC$ at $x = a/2$. The distance from $K$ to $BC$ is $h_K = \frac{a}{2} \tan(A/2)$.
Let $\beta = \angle B'KC'$. The vectors $\vec{KB'}$ and $\vec{KC'}$ are:
$$\vec{KB'} = \left(\frac{a(s-b)}{s} - \frac{a}{2}, h_K\right) = \left(\frac{a(s-2b)}{2s}, h_K\right), \quad \vec{KC'} = \left(\frac{ac}{s} - \frac{a}{2}, h_K\right) = \left(\frac{a(2c-s)}{2s}, h_K\right)$$
The dot product is $\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'} = \frac{a^2(s-2b)(2c-s)}{4s^2} + h_K^2$. Using $h_K^2 = \frac{a^2}{4} \frac{(s-b)(s-c)}{s(s-a)}$ and the variables $x, y, z$:
$$\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'} = \frac{(y+z)^2}{4s^2} \left[ (y-x-z)(x+y-z) + \frac{syz}{x} \right] = \frac{(y+z)^2}{4s^2 x} [x(y^2+z^2-yz-x^2) + y^2z+yz^2]$$
Let $P = xy^2+xz^2-xyz-x^3+y^2z+yz^2$. Then $\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'} = \frac{(y+z)^2 P}{4s^2 x}$.
The squared lengths are $|\vec{KB'}|^2 = \frac{(y+z)^2}{4s^2 x} Q$ and $|\vec{KC'}|^2 = \frac{(y+z)^2}{4s^2 x} R$, where $Q = x(y-x-z)^2 + syz$ and $R = x(x+y-z)^2 + syz$.
Thus, $\cos \beta = \frac{P}{\sqrt{QR}}$.

**3. Calculation of $\angle NIM$**
Let $\alpha = \angle NIM$. $N$ and $M$ are midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$. $NM = a/2$.
Using the distance from $I$ to the midpoints: $IN^2 = r^2 + \frac{(b-a)^2}{4}$ and $IM^2 = r^2 + \frac{(c-a)^2}{4}$.
By the Law of Cosines in $\triangle NIM$:
$$\cos \alpha = \frac{IN^2 + IM^2 - NM^2}{2 IN IM} = \frac{2r^2 + \frac{(b-a)^2 + (c-a)^2 - a^2}{4}}{2 IN IM}$$
Substituting $r^2 = \frac{xyz}{s}$ and the side lengths in terms of $x, y, z$:
Numerator $= \frac{2xyz}{s} + \frac{2x^2-2xy-2xz-2yz}{4} = \frac{x^3-xy^2-y^2z-yz^2-xz^2+xyz}{2s} = -\frac{P}{2s}$.
The denominator is $2 IN IM = 2 \sqrt{\frac{Q_{IN}}{4s} \frac{R_{IN}}{4s}} = \frac{\sqrt{Q_{IN} R_{IN}}}{2s}$, where $Q_{IN} = 4xyz + s(x-y)^2$ and $R_{IN} = 4xyz + s(x-z)^2$.
It can be verified algebraically that $Q_{IN} R_{IN} = QR$.
Thus, $\cos \alpha = \frac{-P/2s}{\sqrt{QR}/2s} = -\frac{P}{\sqrt{QR}}$.

**4. Conclusion**
Since $\cos \alpha = -\cos \beta$, it follows that $\alpha + \beta = 180^\circ$.
Therefore, $\angle NIM + \angle B'KC' = 180^\circ$.
