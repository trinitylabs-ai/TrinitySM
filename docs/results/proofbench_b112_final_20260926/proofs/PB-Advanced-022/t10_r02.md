Let the side lengths of triangle $ABC$ be $a, b, c$ such that $c < b < a$. Let $s = (a+b+c)/2$ be the semiperimeter and $r$ be the inradius.

1. Positions of $B'$ and $C'$
Let $h_b$ be the altitude from $B$ to side $AC$. The distance from $B$ to $AC$ is $h_b = \frac{2\text{Area}}{b} = \frac{2rs}{b}$. The line $L_B$ is parallel to $AC$ and tangent to the incircle, so the distance between $L_B$ and $AC$ is $2r$. Since $B'$ is the intersection of $L_B$ and $BC$, and $C$ lies on $AC$, the ratio $B'B/BC$ is given by:
$$\frac{B'B}{BC} = \frac{d(B, L_B)}{d(B, AC)} = \frac{h_b - 2r}{h_b} = 1 - \frac{2r}{2rs/b} = 1 - \frac{b}{s} = \frac{s-b}{s}$$
Similarly, let $h_c$ be the altitude from $C$ to side $AB$. The line $L_C$ is parallel to $AB$ and tangent to the incircle, so the distance between $L_C$ and $AB$ is $2r$. Since $C'$ is the intersection of $L_C$ and $BC$, we have:
$$\frac{C'C}{BC} = \frac{d(C, L_C)}{d(C, AB)} = \frac{h_c - 2r}{h_c} = 1 - \frac{2r}{2rs/c} = 1 - \frac{c}{s} = \frac{s-c}{s}$$
Since $c < b < a$, we have $s-b = (a+c-b)/2 > 0$ and $s-c = (a+b-c)/2 > 0$, so $B'$ and $C'$ are distinct points on the segment $BC$.

2. Vector Analysis of $\angle NIM$
Set vertex $A$ as the origin. Let $\vec{u} = \vec{AB}$ and $\vec{v} = \vec{AC}$, so $|\vec{u}|=c$ and $|\vec{v}|=b$. The midpoints are $N = \frac{1}{2}\vec{u}$ and $M = \frac{1}{2}\vec{v}$. The incenter is $I = \frac{aA + bB + cC}{a+b+c} = \frac{b\vec{u} + c\vec{v}}{2s}$.
The vectors $\vec{IN}$ and $\vec{IM}$ are:
$$\vec{IN} = \vec{N} - \vec{I} = \frac{s\vec{u} - (b\vec{u} + c\vec{v})}{2s} = \frac{(s-b)\vec{u} - c\vec{v}}{2s}$$
$$\vec{IM} = \vec{M} - \vec{I} = \frac{s\vec{v} - (b\vec{u} + c\vec{v})}{2s} = \frac{-b\vec{u} + (s-c)\vec{v}}{2s}$$
The dot product $\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM}$ is:
$$4s^2 (\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM}) = ((s-b)\vec{u} - c\vec{v}) \cdot (-b\vec{u} + (s-c)\vec{v}) = -b(s-b)c^2 - c(s-c)b^2 + [(s-b)(s-c) + bc](\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v})$$
Using $\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = bc \cos A$:
$$4s^2 (\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM}) = -bc [ (s-b)c + (s-c)b ] + bc [ (s-b)(s-c) + bc ] \cos A$$
Let $X = (s-b)c + (s-c)b = s(b+c) - 2bc$ and $Y = (s-b)(s-c) + bc = s^2 - s(b+c) + 2bc$.
Then $4s^2 (\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM}) = -bc X + bc Y \cos A = -bc(X - Y \cos A)$.
The magnitudes are:
$$4s^2 |\vec{IN}|^2 = (s-b)^2 c^2 + c^2 b^2 - 2c(s-b)bc \cos A = c^2 [ (s-b)^2 + b^2 - 2b(s-b) \cos A ]$$
$$4s^2 |\vec{IM}|^2 = b^2 c^2 + (s-c)^2 b^2 - 2b(s-c)bc \cos A = b^2 [ c^2 + (s-c)^2 - 2c(s-c) \cos A ]$$

3. Vector Analysis of $\angle B'KC'$
Let $K$ be the origin. Let $\vec{k_b} = \vec{KB}$ and $\vec{k_c} = \vec{KC}$. Since $K$ is the midpoint of arc $BC$ not containing $A$, $KB = KC = R_K$ and $\angle BKC = 180^\circ - A$. Thus $\vec{k_b} \cdot \vec{k_c} = R_K^2 \cos(180^\circ - A) = -R_K^2 \cos A$.
Using the ratios from step 1, we have:
$$\vec{KB'} = \frac{b\vec{k_b} + (s-b)\vec{k_c}}{s}, \quad \vec{KC'} = \frac{(s-c)\vec{k_b} + c\vec{k_c}}{s}$$
The dot product $\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'}$ is:
$$s^2 (\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'}) = (b\vec{k_b} + (s-b)\vec{k_c}) \cdot ((s-c)\vec{k_b} + c\vec{k_c}) = b(s-c)R_K^2 + (s-b)cR_K^2 + [bc + (s-b)(s-c)](\vec{k_b} \cdot \vec{k_c})$$
$$s^2 (\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'}) = R_K^2 [ X + Y(-\cos A) ] = R_K^2 (X - Y \cos A)$$
The magnitudes are:
$$s^2 |\vec{KB'}|^2 = b^2 R_K^2 + (s-b)^2 R_K^2 + 2b(s-b)(-R_K^2 \cos A) = R_K^2 [ b^2 + (s-b)^2 - 2b(s-b) \cos A ]$$
$$s^2 |\vec{KC'}|^2 = (s-c)^2 R_K^2 + c^2 R_K^2 + 2c(s-c)(-R_K^2 \cos A) = R_K^2 [ c^2 + (s-c)^2 - 2c(s-c) \cos A ]$$

4. Conclusion
Comparing the results:
$$\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'} = \frac{R_K^2}{s^2} (X - Y \cos A) = \frac{R_K^2}{s^2} \left( \frac{4s^2 (\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM})}{-bc} \right) = -\frac{4R_K^2}{bc} (\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM})$$
$$|\vec{KB'}| = \frac{R_K}{s} \sqrt{b^2 + (s-b)^2 - 2b(s-b) \cos A} = \frac{R_K}{s} \frac{2s |\vec{IN}|}{c} = \frac{2R_K}{c} |\vec{IN}|$$
$$|\vec{KC'}| = \frac{R_K}{s} \sqrt{c^2 + (s-c)^2 - 2c(s-c) \cos A} = \frac{R_K}{s} \frac{2s |\vec{IM}|}{b} = \frac{2R_K}{b} |\vec{IM}|$$
Thus,
$$\cos(\angle B'KC') = \frac{\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'}}{|\vec{KB'}| |\vec{KC'}|} = \frac{-\frac{4R_K^2}{bc} (\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM})}{\frac{2R_K}{c} |\vec{IN}| \cdot \frac{2R_K}{b} |\vec{IM}|} = -\frac{\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM}}{|\vec{IN}| |\vec{IM}|} = -\cos(\angle NIM)$$
Since $\angle NIM$ and $\angle B'KC'$ are angles of triangles, they lie in $[0, 180^\circ]$. The identity $\cos(\angle B'KC') = -\cos(\angle NIM)$ implies $\angle NIM + \angle B'KC' = 180^\circ$.

$\angle NIM + \angle B'KC' = 180^\circ$
