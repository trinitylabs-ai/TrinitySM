To prove that one specific flag color is held by at least 200 Googlers, we model the problem using the language of hypergraphs.

### 1. Formalization
Let $N = 2024$ be the number of Googlers. For each Googler $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, N\}$, let $S_i$ be the set of colors of the flags that Googler $i$ holds. Based on the problem statement:
- Each Googler holds up to five flags, so $|S_i| \le 5$ for all $i$.
- Any group of three Googlers contains at least two people who share a flag color. This means that for any three indices $i, j, k \in \{1, \dots, N\}$, at least one of the intersections $S_i \cap S_j$, $S_i \cap S_k$, or $S_j \cap S_k$ is non-empty.

In hypergraph theory, a matching is a collection of pairwise disjoint sets. The matching number $\nu(\mathcal{F})$ of a family $\mathcal{F} = \{S_1, \dots, S_N\}$ is the size of the largest matching. The condition that no three sets in $\mathcal{F}$ are pairwise disjoint implies that the matching number $\nu(\mathcal{F})$ is at most 2.

### 2. The Hitting Set Bound
A hitting set (or transversal) of a family $\mathcal{F}$ is a set of elements $T$ such that $T \cap S_i \neq \emptyset$ for every $S_i \in \mathcal{F}$. The minimum size of a hitting set is denoted by $\tau(\mathcal{F})$.

We utilize the following theorem: For any family $\mathcal{F}$ of non-empty sets where the maximum size of any set is $k$, the hitting set size $\tau(\mathcal{F})$ is bounded by $k \cdot \nu(\mathcal{F})$.

**Proof:** Let $\mathcal{M} = \{M_1, M_2, \dots, M_\nu\}$ be a maximum matching in $\mathcal{F}$, where $\nu = \nu(\mathcal{F})$. Let $T$ be the union of all sets in this matching:
\[ T = \bigcup_{j=1}^\nu M_j \]
The size of $T$ is $|T| \le \sum_{j=1}^\nu |M_j| \le \nu \cdot k$. We claim that $T$ is a hitting set. If there existed a set $S \in \mathcal{F}$ such that $S \cap T = \emptyset$, then $S$ would be disjoint from every set $M_j$ in the matching $\mathcal{M}$. This would imply that $\mathcal{M} \cup \{S\}$ is a matching of size $\nu + 1$, contradicting the maximality of $\mathcal{M}$. Thus, $T \cap S \neq \emptyset$ for all $S \in \mathcal{F}$, confirming that $T$ is a hitting set of size at most $k\nu$. $\square$

### 3. Analysis of Googlers with Flags
If a Googler holds no flags ($S_i = \emptyset$), they cannot share a color with anyone. If two Googlers $i$ and $j$ both hold no flags, then for any third Googler $k$, the group $\{i, j, k\}$ would have no two people sharing a color (since $S_i \cap S_j = \emptyset$, $S_i \cap S_k = \emptyset$, and $S_j \cap S_k = \emptyset$), violating the problem condition. Thus, at most one Googler can hold no flags.

**Case 1: All Googlers hold at least one flag.**
In this case, the family $\mathcal{F}$ consists of non-empty sets. With $k = 5$ and $\nu(\mathcal{F}) \le 2$, the theorem implies:
\[ \tau(\mathcal{F}) \le 5 \cdot 2 = 10 \]
There exists a set of colors $T = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_m\}$ with $m \le 10$ such that every Googler holds at least one color from $T$. Let $x_j$ be the number of Googlers who hold a flag of color $c_j$ for $j = 1, \dots, m$. Then:
\[ \sum_{j=1}^m x_j \ge N = 2024 \]
By the Pigeonhole Principle, there exists at least one color $c_j \in T$ such that:
\[ x_j \ge \frac{2024}{m} \ge \frac{2024}{10} = 202.4 \]
Since $x_j$ must be an integer, $x_j \ge 203$.

**Case 2: Exactly one Googler holds no flags.**
Let Googler 1 be the one with $S_1 = \emptyset$. For any two other Googlers $j, k \in \{2, \dots, N\}$, the group $\{1, j, k\}$ must contain at least two people sharing a color. Since $S_1 \cap S_j = \emptyset$ and $S_1 \cap S_k = \emptyset$, it must be that $S_j \cap S_k \neq \emptyset$.
Thus, any two Googlers in the remaining group of $N-1 = 2023$ share a color, meaning the matching number of the subfamily $\mathcal{F}' = \{S_2, \dots, S_N\}$ is $\nu(\mathcal{F}') = 1$. Applying the theorem to $\mathcal{F}'$:
\[ \tau(\mathcal{F}') \le 5 \cdot 1 = 5 \]
There exists a set of colors $T$ with $|T| \le 5$ such that every Googler in $\mathcal{F}'$ holds at least one color from $T$. Let $x_j$ be the number of Googlers who hold color $c_j \in T$. Then:
\[ \sum_{j=1}^m x_j \ge 2023 \]
By the Pigeonhole Principle, there exists at least one color $c_j \in T$ such that:
\[ x_j \ge \frac{2023}{5} = 404.6 \implies x_j \ge 405 \]

In both cases, there exists a specific flag color held by at least 203 Googlers, which is $\ge 200$.
