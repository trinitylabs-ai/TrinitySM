To prove that the circles $(W_3)$ and $(W_4)$ are tangent to each other, we employ a coordinate geometry approach.

### 1. Coordinate Setup
Let the trapezoid $ABCD$ have parallel bases $AD$ and $BC$. Let the height of the trapezoid be $h$. We set up a coordinate system such that the bases lie on the lines $y=h$ and $y=0$. Let the vertices be:
$A = (x_A, h), \quad D = (x_D, h), \quad B = (x_B, 0), \quad C = (x_C, 0)$.
The legs are $AB$ and $CD$. Let $L_1$ and $L_2$ be their lengths:
$L_1^2 = (x_B - x_A)^2 + h^2, \quad L_2^2 = (x_C - x_D)^2 + h^2$.
Let $X = x_B - x_A$ and $Y = x_C - x_D$.

### 2. Centers and Radii of the Circles
For a circle passing through two points $P, Q$ with an inscribed angle $\theta$ corresponding to the arc $PQ$ on the side opposite to a point $R$, the radius is $R = \frac{PQ}{2 \sin \theta}$. The center $O$ is located at $M \pm \frac{\cot \theta}{2} \vec{n}$, where $M$ is the midpoint of $PQ$ and $\vec{n}$ is a vector perpendicular to $\vec{PQ}$ pointing toward the side of $R$.

For $(W_1)$ passing through $A, B$ with inscribed angle $\alpha$:
$R_1 = \frac{L_1}{2 \sin \alpha}, \quad M_1 = \left(\frac{x_A+x_B}{2}, \frac{h}{2}\right), \quad \vec{AB} = (X, -h), \quad \vec{n_1} = (h, X)$.
The center is $O_1 = M_1 + \frac{\cot \alpha}{2} \vec{n_1}$.

For $(W_2)$ passing through $C, D$ with inscribed angle $\beta$:
$R_2 = \frac{L_2}{2 \sin \beta}, \quad M_2 = \left(\frac{x_C+x_D}{2}, \frac{h}{2}\right), \quad \vec{DC} = (Y, -h), \quad \vec{n_2} = (h, Y)$.
The center is $O_2 = M_2 - \frac{\cot \beta}{2} \vec{n_2}$.

### 3. The Tangency Condition
Let $u = \cot \alpha$ and $v = \cot \beta$. Then $R_1 = \frac{L_1}{2}\sqrt{1+u^2}$ and $R_2 = \frac{L_2}{2}\sqrt{1+v^2}$.
Let $\vec{S} = M_2 - M_1 = \left(\frac{x_C+x_D-x_A-x_B}{2}, 0\right)$.
The distance between centers $O_1$ and $O_2$ is:
$O_1 - O_2 = -\vec{S} + \frac{u}{2}\vec{n_1} + \frac{v}{2}\vec{n_2}$.
The circles $(W_1)$ and $(W_2)$ are tangent if $O_1O_2 = R_1 \pm R_2$, which is equivalent to $O_1O_2^2 - (R_1^2 + R_2^2) = \pm 2R_1R_2$.
Expanding the distance:
$4O_1O_2^2 = |-2\vec{S} + u\vec{n_1} + v\vec{n_2}|^2 = 4S^2 + u^2L_1^2 + v^2L_2^2 - 4\vec{S} \cdot (u\vec{n_1} + v\vec{n_2}) + 2uv(\vec{n_1} \cdot \vec{n_2})$.
Since $\vec{S} = (s, 0)$ and $\vec{n_1} = (h, X), \vec{n_2} = (h, Y)$, we have $\vec{S} \cdot \vec{n_1} = \vec{S} \cdot \vec{n_2} = sh$.
$4O_1O_2^2 = 4S^2 + u^2L_1^2 + v^2L_2^2 - 4sh(u+v) + 2uv(\vec{n_1} \cdot \vec{n_2})$.
Subtracting $4(R_1^2 + R_2^2) = L_1^2(1+u^2) + L_2^2(1+v^2)$:
$4(O_1O_2^2 - (R_1^2 + R_2^2)) = 4S^2 - 4sh(u+v) + 2uv(\vec{n_1} \cdot \vec{n_2}) - (L_1^2 + L_2^2)$.
Let $f(u, v) = 4S^2 - 4sh(u+v) + 2uv(\vec{n_1} \cdot \vec{n_2}) - (L_1^2 + L_2^2)$.
The tangency condition for $(W_1)$ and $(W_2)$ is $f(u, v) = \pm 8R_1R_2 = \pm 2L_1L_2\sqrt{1+u^2}\sqrt{1+v^2}$.

### 4. Proof for $(W_3)$ and $(W_4)$
For $(W_3)$ and $(W_4)$, the inscribed angles are swapped: $\alpha \to \beta$ and $\beta \to \alpha$. This means $u \to v$ and $v \to u$.
The radius and center formulas for $(W_3, W_4)$ are:
$R_3 = \frac{L_1}{2}\sqrt{1+v^2}, \quad R_4 = \frac{L_2}{2}\sqrt{1+u^2}, \quad O_3 = M_1 + \frac{v}{2}\vec{n_1}, \quad O_4 = M_2 - \frac{u}{2}\vec{n_2}$.
The distance between centers $O_3$ and $O_4$ is:
$O_3 - O_4 = -\vec{S} + \frac{v}{2}\vec{n_1} + \frac{u}{2}\vec{n_2}$.
Thus, $4(O_3O_4^2 - (R_3^2 + R_4^2)) = f(v, u)$.
Observing the expression for $f(u, v)$:
1. $(u+v)$ is symmetric.
2. $uv(\vec{n_1} \cdot \vec{n_2})$ is symmetric.
Thus, $f(v, u) = f(u, v)$.
The tangency condition for $(W_3)$ and $(W_4)$ is $f(v, u) = \pm 8R_3R_4$.
$8R_3R_4 = 8 \left(\frac{L_1}{2}\sqrt{1+v^2}\right) \left(\frac{L_2}{2}\sqrt{1+u^2}\right) = 2L_1L_2\sqrt{1+v^2}\sqrt{1+u^2}$.
This is exactly the same as $8R_1R_2$. Since $f(u, v) = \pm 8R_1R_2$ from the tangency of $(W_1)$ and $(W_2)$, it follows that $f(v, u) = \pm 8R_3R_4$.
Therefore, $(W_3)$ and $(W_4)$ are tangent to each other.
