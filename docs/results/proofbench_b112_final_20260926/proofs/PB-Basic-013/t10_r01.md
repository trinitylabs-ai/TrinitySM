To prove that there are two colors that occur together in more than one box, we use a combinatorial counting argument based on the intersections of the sets of colors in each box.

**1. Problem Formalization**
Let $B_1, B_2, \dots, B_8$ be the sets of colors contained in the 8 boxes. We are given that each box contains 6 balls of distinct colors, so $|B_i| = 6$ for all $i \in \{1, \dots, 8\}$. The colors are chosen from a set $C$ of 22 available colors, so $B_i \subseteq C$ and $|C| = 22$.

We wish to prove that there exist two distinct colors $c_j, c_k \in C$ and two distinct boxes $B_m, B_n$ such that $\{c_j, c_k\} \subseteq B_m$ and $\{c_j, c_k\} \subseteq B_n$. This is equivalent to proving that there exist $m \neq n$ such that $|B_m \cap B_n| \ge 2$.

**2. Proof by Contradiction**
Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that no two colors occur together in more than one box. This means that for any two distinct boxes $B_m$ and $B_n$, they can share at most one color:
\[ |B_m \cap B_n| \le 1 \quad \text{for all } 1 \le m < n \le 8 \]
Let $S$ be the sum of the sizes of the intersections of all pairs of boxes:
\[ S = \sum_{1 \le m < n \le 8} |B_m \cap B_n| \]
Given our assumption, and since there are $\binom{8}{2} = 28$ pairs of boxes, we have:
\[ S \le \sum_{1 \le m < n \le 8} 1 = \binom{8}{2} = 28 \]

**3. Lower Bound for $S$ via Color Frequencies**
We can also calculate $S$ by counting how many pairs of boxes share each color. Let $n_k$ be the number of boxes that contain color $c_k$ for $k = 1, 2, \dots, 22$.
Each color $c_k$ is contained in $n_k$ boxes, and thus it contributes $\binom{n_k}{2}$ to the sum $S$. Therefore:
\[ S = \sum_{k=1}^{22} \binom{n_k}{2} \]
The total number of balls across all boxes is $8 \times 6 = 48$. Since each ball has exactly one color, the sum of the frequencies $n_k$ is:
\[ \sum_{k=1}^{22} n_k = 48 \]
To find the minimum possible value of $S$, we use the fact that the function $f(n) = \binom{n}{2} = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$ is convex for $n \ge 0$. For a fixed sum $\sum n_k$, the sum $\sum f(n_k)$ is minimized when the values of $n_k$ are as close to each other as possible.
Dividing 48 by 22, we have:
\[ 48 = 22 \times 2 + 4 \]
The minimum occurs when 4 of the $n_k$ values are 3 and the remaining 18 values are 2. Calculating this minimum:
\[ S \ge 4 \binom{3}{2} + 18 \binom{2}{2} = 4(3) + 18(1) = 12 + 18 = 30 \]

**4. Final Contradiction**
We have established two bounds for the sum $S$:
1. From the assumption that no two colors occur together in more than one box: $S \le 28$.
2. From the distribution of colors and the convexity of the binomial coefficient: $S \ge 30$.

This yields the inequality $30 \le S \le 28$, which is a contradiction. Thus, the assumption must be false, and there must be at least two colors that occur together in more than one box.

\(\square\)
