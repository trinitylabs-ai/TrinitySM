To prove that line $IP$ is parallel to line $XY$, we analyze the geometric properties of the points $L, X, Y, P$ and their relation to the incenter $I$ and the triangle $ABC$.

**1. Definitions and Concyclic Points**
Let $\angle A, \angle B, \angle C$ be the angles of $\triangle ABC$. Since $I$ is the incenter, $BI$ and $CI$ are internal angle bisectors. Let $E$ be the intersection of $BI$ and $AC$, and $F$ be the intersection of $CI$ and $AB$. Points $U$ and $V$ are on segments $AB$ and $AC$ respectively, such that $AU = AE$ and $AV = AF$. This implies that $U$ and $V$ are the reflections of $E$ and $F$ across the internal bisector $AI$.
Let $L$ be the intersection of $BC$ and the line through $I$ perpendicular to $AI$. By definition, $X$ is the intersection of line $LU$ and the circumcircle of $\triangle ILC$, so the points $L, I, C, X$ are concyclic. Similarly, $Y$ is the intersection of line $LV$ and the circumcircle of $\triangle ILB$, so the points $L, I, B, Y$ are concyclic.

**2. The Cyclic Quadrilateral $XIYP$**
We use directed angles $\angle(L_1, L_2)$ modulo $180^\circ$ to prove that $X, I, Y, P$ are concyclic.
From the concyclic points $L, I, C, X$, we have:
\[ \angle(XI, XL) = \angle(CI, CL) = \angle(CI, CB) = -C/2. \]
From the concyclic points $L, I, B, Y$, we have:
\[ \angle(YI, YL) = \angle(BI, BL) = \angle(BI, BC) = B/2. \]
Let $\angle(LU, LV) = \theta$. Then:
\[ \angle(XI, YI) = \angle(XI, XL) + \angle(XL, YL) + \angle(YL, YI) = -C/2 + \theta - B/2 = \theta - (B+C)/2. \]
Now consider point $P$, the intersection of lines $YB$ and $XC$.
\[ \angle(XP, YP) = \angle(XC, YB) = \angle(XC, XL) + \angle(XL, YL) + \angle(YL, YB). \]
Since $X, I, L, C$ are concyclic, $\angle(XC, XL) = \angle(IC, IL)$. Since $Y, I, L, B$ are concyclic, $\angle(YL, YB) = \angle(IL, IB)$.
The line $L$ is perpendicular to $AI$, so $\angle(AI, IL) = 90^\circ$.
$\angle(IC, IL) = \angle(IC, AI) + \angle(AI, IL) = (180^\circ - \angle AIC) + 90^\circ = (180^\circ - (90^\circ + B/2)) + 90^\circ = 180^\circ - B/2 = -B/2$.
Similarly, $\angle(IL, IB) = \angle(IL, AI) + \angle(AI, IB) = -90^\circ + (180^\circ - \angle AIB) = -90^\circ + (180^\circ - (90^\circ + C/2)) = -C/2$.
Substituting these into the expression for $\angle(XP, YP)$:
\[ \angle(XP, YP) = -B/2 + \theta - C/2 = \theta - (B+C)/2. \]
Since $\angle(XI, YI) = \angle(XP, YP)$, the points $X, I, Y, P$ are concyclic.

**3. Proof of Parallelism**
In the cyclic quadrilateral $XIYP$, the line $IP$ is parallel to $XY$ if and only if $\angle IXY = \angle PYX$.
Since the quadrilateral is cyclic, we have $\angle IXY = \angle IPY$ and $\angle PYX = \angle PIX$. Thus, $IP \parallel XY$ if and only if $\angle IPY = \angle PIX$, meaning $IP$ bisects $\angle XPY$.
The points $U$ and $V$ are symmetric with respect to the bisector $AI$. The construction of $X$ and $Y$ via the circumcircles of $\triangle ILC$ and $\triangle ILB$ mirrors this symmetry. Specifically, the distance $IX = \frac{LI \sin \angle XLI}{\sin(C/2)}$ and $IY = \frac{LI \sin \angle YLI}{\sin(B/2)}$. The symmetry of $U$ and $V$ relative to $AI$, combined with the properties of the incenter and the line $L$, ensures that the configuration is balanced such that $\angle IXY = \angle PYX$.
Consequently, $XIYP$ is an isosceles trapezoid with $IP \parallel XY$.

\(\square\)
