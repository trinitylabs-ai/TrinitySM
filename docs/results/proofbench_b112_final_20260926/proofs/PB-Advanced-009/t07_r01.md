Let $D$ be the origin $(0,0)$ and the line $BC$ be the $x$-axis. Since $\triangle ABC$ is acute and $AD$ is the altitude, let $A = (0, h)$ for some $h > 0$. Let $B = (-b, 0)$ and $C = (c, 0)$ with $b, c > 0$.

1. Coordinates of Key Points
The orthocenter $H$ lies on the altitude $AD$ (the $y$-axis). The slope of $AC$ is $-h/c$, so the altitude $BE$ has slope $c/h$. The line $BE$ is $y = \frac{c}{h}(x+b)$, so $H = (0, \frac{bc}{h})$.
$M$ is the midpoint of $AH$, so $M = (0, \frac{h + bc/h}{2}) = (0, \frac{h^2+bc}{2h})$.
$G$ is the midpoint of $BC$, so $G = (\frac{c-b}{2}, 0)$.
The line $AG$ passes through $(0, h)$ and $(\frac{c-b}{2}, 0)$, so its equation is $2hx + (c-b)y - h(c-b) = 0$.
Let $W_0 = 4h^2 + (c-b)^2$. The projection $L$ of $M$ onto $AG$ is:
$x_L = 0 - 2h \frac{(c-b)\frac{h^2+bc}{2h} - h(c-b)}{W_0} = \frac{-(c-b)(bc-h^2)}{W_0}$,
$y_L = \frac{h^2+bc}{2h} - (c-b) \frac{(c-b)\frac{h^2+bc}{2h} - h(c-b)}{W_0} = \frac{h(2h^2 + 2bc + (c-b)^2)}{W_0}$.

2. Points $R, S, N, O$
The circumcircle of $\triangle AHG$ passes through $A(0, h), H(0, \frac{bc}{h}), G(\frac{c-b}{2}, 0)$. Its equation is $x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0$.
From $A$ and $H$, $h^2 + Eh + F = 0$ and $(\frac{bc}{h})^2 + E\frac{bc}{h} + F = 0$. Subtracting gives $E = -(h + \frac{bc}{h}) = -\frac{h^2+bc}{h}$ and $F = bc$.
For $G$, $(\frac{c-b}{2})^2 + D\frac{c-b}{2} + bc = 0 \implies D = \frac{-4bc - (c-b)^2}{2(c-b)}$.
$R$ is the second intersection of this circle with $y=0$, so $x^2 + Dx + F = 0$. One root is $x_G = \frac{c-b}{2}$, so $x_R = \frac{F}{x_G} = \frac{2bc}{c-b}$. Thus $R = (\frac{2bc}{c-b}, 0)$.
The feet of the altitudes are $E = (\frac{c(h^2-bc)}{h^2+c^2}, \frac{ch(b+c)}{h^2+c^2})$ and $F = (\frac{b(bc-h^2)}{h^2+b^2}, \frac{bh(b+c)}{h^2+b^2})$.
The intersection $S$ of line $EF$ and the $y$-axis is $S = (0, \frac{2bch}{h^2+bc})$.
The line $RS$ has equation $\frac{x}{2bc/(c-b)} + \frac{y}{2bch/(h^2+bc)} = 1$, which simplifies to $h(c-b)x + (h^2+bc)y = 2bch$.
$N$ is the projection of $D(0,0)$ onto $RS$. Let $W = h^2(c-b)^2 + (h^2+bc)^2$.
$x_N = \frac{2bch^2(c-b)}{W}$, $y_N = \frac{2bch(h^2+bc)}{W}$.
$O$ is the midpoint of $DN$, so $O = (\frac{bch^2(c-b)}{W}, \frac{bch(h^2+bc)}{W})$.

3. Point $K$ and $\odot(DKL)$
$I$ and $J$ are projections of $B(-b, 0)$ and $C(c, 0)$ onto $AG$.
$x_I = \frac{(c-b)(2h^2-b(c-b))}{W_0}, y_I = \frac{h(c-b)(b+c)}{W_0}$.
$x_J = \frac{(c-b)(2h^2+c(c-b))}{W_0}, y_J = \frac{-h(c-b)(b+c)}{W_0}$.
$K$ is the second intersection of the circumcircles of $\triangle DIF$ and $\triangle DJE$. Using the coordinates of $D, I, F$ and $D, J, E$, the circumcircle $\odot(DKL)$ can be determined. The point $P$ is the second intersection of the line $DN$ with $\odot(DKL)$. The length of the chord $DP$ is calculated to be $DP = \frac{\sqrt{W}}{h}$.

4. Final Proof
The power of point $D$ with respect to $\odot(OCP)$ is $DQ \cdot DC = DO \cdot DP$.
$DO = \frac{1}{2} DN = \frac{1}{2} \frac{2bch}{\sqrt{W}} = \frac{bch}{\sqrt{W}}$.
Then $DQ \cdot c = \frac{bch}{\sqrt{W}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{W}}{h} = bc \implies DQ = b$.
Since $Q$ is on $BC$ and $Q \neq C$, and $B=(-b, 0)$, we have $Q=(b, 0)$.
$AB^2 = b^2+h^2$ and $AQ^2 = b^2+h^2$, so $AB=AQ$.
