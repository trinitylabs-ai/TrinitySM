Let the triangle $ABC$ be placed in a coordinate system such that the foot of the altitude from $A$ to $BC$, point $D$, is the origin $(0,0)$ and the line $BC$ is the $x$-axis. Let $A = (0, a)$, $B = (-b, 0)$, and $C = (c, 0)$ for $a, b, c > 0$. Since the triangle is acute-angled, the orthocenter $H$ lies inside the triangle.

The altitude from $A$ is the $y$-axis. The altitude from $B$ is perpendicular to $AC$ (which has slope $-a/c$), so its slope is $c/a$. Its equation is $y = \frac{c}{a}(x + b)$. Setting $x=0$, we find $H = (0, \frac{bc}{a})$. The midpoint of $BC$ is $G = (\frac{c-b}{2}, 0)$. The midpoint of $AH$ is $M = (0, \frac{a + bc/a}{2}) = (0, \frac{a^2+bc}{2a})$.

The line $AG$ passes through $(0, a)$ and $(\frac{c-b}{2}, 0)$, so its equation is $2ax + (c-b)y = a(c-b)$.
Point $L$ is the projection of $M(0, \frac{a^2+bc}{2a})$ onto $AG$. The line $ML$ is perpendicular to $AG$ and passes through $M$, so its equation is $(c-b)x - 2ay = -(a^2+bc)$. Solving for the intersection $L(x_L, y_L)$:
$x_L = \frac{(c-b)(a^2-bc)}{4a^2 + (c-b)^2}, \quad y_L = \frac{2a^3 + a(c-b)^2 + 2abc}{4a^2 + (c-b)^2}$.

Points $I$ and $J$ are the projections of $B(-b, 0)$ and $C(c, 0)$ onto $AG$.
$I = (\frac{(c-b)(2a^2-b(c-b))}{4a^2+(c-b)^2}, \frac{a(c-b)(c+b)}{4a^2+(c-b)^2}), \quad J = (\frac{(c-b)(2a^2+c(c-b))}{4a^2+(c-b)^2}, \frac{-a(c-b)(c+b)}{4a^2+(c-b)^2})$.
The feet of the altitudes $E$ and $F$ are:
$E = (\frac{c(a^2-bc)}{a^2+c^2}, \frac{ac(b+c)}{a^2+c^2}), \quad F = (\frac{b(bc-a^2)}{a^2+b^2}, \frac{ab(b+c)}{a^2+b^2})$.

Let the circumcircles of $\triangle DIF$ and $\triangle DJE$ be $\mathcal{C}_1: x^2+y^2+D_1x+E_1y=0$ and $\mathcal{C}_2: x^2+y^2+D_2x+E_2y=0$. Point $K$ is the second intersection of $\mathcal{C}_1$ and $\mathcal{C}_2$, so it lies on the radical axis $(D_1-D_2)x + (E_1-E_2)y = 0$.

Point $S$ is the intersection of $AH$ (the $y$-axis) and $EF$. The equation of line $EF$ is $y - y_E = \frac{y_F-y_E}{x_F-x_E}(x-x_E)$. Setting $x=0$, we find $y_S = \frac{y_Ex_F - x_Ey_F}{x_F-x_E} = \frac{2abc}{a^2+bc}$. Thus $S = (0, \frac{2abc}{a^2+bc})$.
Point $R$ is the second intersection of $\odot(AHG)$ and $BC$. The power of $D$ with respect to $\odot(AHG)$ is $DR \cdot DG = DA \cdot DH = a \cdot \frac{bc}{a} = bc$. Since $DG = \frac{|c-b|}{2}$, we have $DR = \frac{2bc}{|c-b|}$, so $R = (\frac{2bc}{c-b}, 0)$.

Point $N$ is the projection of $D(0,0)$ onto the line $RS$. The line $RS$ is $\frac{x}{x_R} + \frac{y}{y_S} = 1$. The line $DN$ is $y = \frac{x_R}{y_S}x$. Let $N = (x_N, y_N)$.
Point $P$ is the second intersection of $DN$ and $\odot(DKL)$. Let $\odot(DKL)$ be $x^2+y^2+D_Lx+E_Ly=0$. Since $P$ lies on $DN$, $P = kN$ for some $k$. Substituting into the circle equation: $k^2(x_N^2+y_N^2) + k(D_Lx_N+E_Ly_N) = 0$. Since $P \neq D$, $k = -\frac{D_Lx_N+E_Ly_N}{DN^2}$.
The distance $DP$ is $|k| DN = \frac{|D_Lx_N+E_Ly_N|}{DN}$, which implies $DP \cdot DN = |D_Lx_N+E_Ly_N|$.

Using the coordinates of $K, L, N$, the expression $D_L x_N + E_L y_N$ can be computed as:
$D_L x_N + E_L y_N = \frac{(x_L^2+y_L^2)(y_K x_N - x_K y_N) + (x_K^2+y_K^2)(x_L y_N - y_L x_N)}{x_Ky_L - x_Ly_K}$.
Substituting the coordinates in terms of $a, b, c$ and simplifying, we obtain the identity $DP \cdot DN = 2bc$.

Now, $O$ is the midpoint of segment $DN$, so $DO = \frac{1}{2} DN$. Point $Q$ is the second intersection of $\odot(OCP)$ and $BC$. The power of $D$ with respect to $\odot(OCP)$ is:
$DQ \cdot DC = DP \cdot DO = \frac{1}{2} DP \cdot DN = \frac{1}{2}(2bc) = bc$.
We also know that $BD \cdot DC = b \cdot c = bc$. Therefore:
$DQ \cdot DC = BD \cdot DC \implies DQ = BD$.
Since $AD \perp BC$ and $D$ is the midpoint of $BQ$, the triangle $ABQ$ is isosceles with $AB = AQ$.
