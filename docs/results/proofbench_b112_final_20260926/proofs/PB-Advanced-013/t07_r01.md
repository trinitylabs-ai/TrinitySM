For an integer $n \geq 2$, let $0 < a_{1} \leq a_{2} \leq \cdots \leq a_{n}$ be real numbers satisfying $a_{1} a_{2} \cdots a_{n}=1$. We wish to prove that
\[ \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{b_{k}} \geq \frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}, \quad \text{where } b_{k}=2^{k}\left(1+a_{k}^{2^{k}}\right). \]
First, we observe that
\[ \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{2^{k+1}} = \frac{1}{4} \left( \frac{1 - (1/2)^n}{1 - 1/2} \right) = \frac{1}{2} \left( 1 - \frac{1}{2^n} \right) = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2^{n+1}}. \]
Thus, the inequality is equivalent to showing that
\[ S = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \left( \frac{1}{2^{k}(1+a_{k}^{2^{k}})} - \frac{1}{2^{k+1}} \right) \geq 0. \]
Let $y_{k} = \ln a_{k}$. The conditions $a_{1} \leq \cdots \leq a_{n}$ and $\prod a_{k} = 1$ imply $y_{1} \leq \cdots \leq y_{n}$ and $\sum_{k=1}^{n} y_{k} = 0$. We rewrite the terms of the sum as
\[ f_{k}(y) = \frac{1}{2^{k}(1+e^{2^{k} y})} - \frac{1}{2^{k+1}} = \frac{2 - (1+e^{2^{k} y})}{2^{k+1}(1+e^{2^{k} y})} = \frac{1 - e^{2^{k} y}}{2^{k+1}(1+e^{2^{k} y})}. \]
Using the identity $\tanh(x) = \frac{e^{2x}-1}{e^{2x}+1}$, we have
\[ f_{k}(y) = -\frac{1}{2^{k+1}} \tanh(2^{k-1} y). \]
Let $g(z) = -\frac{1}{4} \tanh(z)$, $w_{k} = \frac{1}{2^{k-1}}$, and $z_{k} = 2^{k-1} y_{k}$. Then
\[ w_{k} g(z_{k}) = \frac{1}{2^{k-1}} \left( -\frac{1}{4} \tanh(2^{k-1} y_{k}) \right) = -\frac{1}{2^{k+1}} \tanh(2^{k-1} y_{k}) = f_{k}(y). \]
The constraint $\sum y_{k} = 0$ is equivalent to $\sum_{k=1}^{n} w_{k} z_{k} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} 2^{-(k-1)} 2^{k-1} y_{k} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} y_{k} = 0$.
The sum becomes $S = \sum_{k=1}^{n} w_{k} g(z_{k})$.
Since $y_{k}$ is non-decreasing, there exists an index $m \in \{1, \dots, n\}$ such that $y_{1} \leq \cdots \leq y_{m} \leq 0 < y_{m+1} \leq \cdots \leq y_{n}$. (If all $y_{k}=0$, then $S=0$. If all $y_{k} \leq 0$, then $\sum y_{k} = 0$ implies all $y_{k} = 0$).
Let $\mathcal{N} = \{1, \dots, m\}$ and $\mathcal{P} = \{m+1, \dots, n\}$.
Let $X = \sum_{k \in \mathcal{P}} w_{k} z_{k}$. Since $z_{k} > 0$ for $k \in \mathcal{P}$, we have $X > 0$.
Then $\sum_{k \in \mathcal{N}} w_{k} z_{k} = -X$.
The second derivative of $g(z)$ is $g''(z) = \frac{1}{2} \text{sech}^2(z) \tanh(z)$. Thus, $g$ is convex on $[0, \infty)$ and concave on $(-\infty, 0]$.
By Jensen's Inequality, for the positive terms:
\[ \sum_{k \in \mathcal{P}} w_{k} g(z_{k}) \geq W_{P} g\left( \frac{\sum_{k \in \mathcal{P}} w_{k} z_{k}}{W_{P}} \right) = W_{P} g(X/W_{P}), \]
where $W_{P} = \sum_{k \in \mathcal{P}} w_{k}$.
For the negative terms, since $g$ is concave on $(-\infty, 0]$, the sum $\sum_{k \in \mathcal{N}} w_{k} g(z_{k})$ subject to $\sum_{k \in \mathcal{N}} w_{k} z_{k} = -X$ and $z_{k} \leq 0$ is minimized at the extreme points of the constraint set. These occur when $z_{j} = -X/w_{j}$ for some $j \in \mathcal{N}$ and $z_{k} = 0$ for $k \neq j$.
The value at such a point is $w_{j} g(-X/w_{j}) + \sum_{k \neq j} w_{k} g(0) = w_{j} g(-X/w_{j})$.
Let $h(w) = w g(-X/w) = \frac{w}{4} \tanh(X/w)$.
The derivative is $h'(w) = \frac{1}{4} \left( \tanh(X/w) - \frac{X}{w} \text{sech}^2(X/w) \right)$.
Let $u = X/w > 0$. The expression $\tanh u - u \text{sech}^2 u$ is positive for $u > 0$ because its derivative is $2u \text{sech}^2 u \tanh u > 0$ and it vanishes at $u=0$. Thus, $h(w)$ is strictly increasing for $w > 0$.
The minimum of $w_{j} g(-X/w_{j})$ for $j \in \mathcal{N}$ occurs at the smallest weight $w_{m} = 2^{-(m-1)}$.
Hence, $\sum_{k \in \mathcal{N}} w_{k} g(z_{k}) \geq h(w_{m})$.
Combining these bounds, we have
\[ S \geq h(w_{m}) + W_{P} g(X/W_{P}) = h(w_{m}) - h(W_{P}). \]
We calculate $W_{P} = \sum_{k=m+1}^{n} \frac{1}{2^{k-1}} = \frac{1}{2^{m}} + \cdots + \frac{1}{2^{n-1}} = \frac{1}{2^{m-1}} - \frac{1}{2^{n-1}}$.
Since $w_{m} = \frac{1}{2^{m-1}}$, we have $W_{P} = w_{m} - \frac{1}{2^{n-1}} < w_{m}$.
Since $h$ is strictly increasing, $h(w_{m}) - h(W_{P}) > 0$.
Thus, $S \geq 0$ for all $n \geq 2$, which proves the inequality.
