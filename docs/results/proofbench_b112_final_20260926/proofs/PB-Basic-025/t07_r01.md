To find the angle $\gamma$ between the lines $MN$ and $OI$ in triangle $XYZ$, we employ a vector-based approach. Let the side lengths of the triangle be $YZ = a$, $XZ = b$, and $XY = c$. Let the circumcenter $O$ be the origin of our coordinate system. Thus, the vectors $\vec{X}, \vec{Y}, \vec{Z}$ from $O$ to the vertices satisfy $|\vec{X}| = |\vec{Y}| = |\vec{Z}| = R$, where $R$ is the circumradius.

The incenter $I$ of a triangle is the weighted average of its vertices:
\[ \vec{I} = \frac{a\vec{X} + b\vec{Y} + c\vec{Z}}{a+b+c} \]
Let $S = a+b+c$. Since $O$ is the origin, the vector $\vec{OI} = \vec{I}$.

Points $M$ and $N$ are on the sides $XY$ and $XZ$ respectively such that $YM = ZN = YZ = a$.
The point $M$ divides the segment $XY$ in the ratio $XM:MY = (c-a):a$. Thus:
\[ \vec{M} = \frac{a\vec{X} + (c-a)\vec{Y}}{c} = \frac{a}{c}\vec{X} + \left(1 - \frac{a}{c}\right)\vec{Y} \]
Similarly, the point $N$ divides the segment $XZ$ in the ratio $XN:NZ = (b-a):a$:
\[ \vec{N} = \frac{a\vec{X} + (b-a)\vec{Z}}{b} = \frac{a}{b}\vec{X} + \left(1 - \frac{a}{b}\right)\vec{Z} \]
The vector $\vec{MN}$ is given by:
\[ \vec{MN} = \vec{N} - \vec{M} = \left(\frac{a}{b} - \frac{a}{c}\right)\vec{X} - \left(1 - \frac{a}{c}\right)\vec{Y} + \left(1 - \frac{a}{b}\right)\vec{Z} \]
To determine the angle $\gamma$, we compute the dot product $\vec{MN} \cdot \vec{OI}$. Let $\vec{S} = a\vec{X} + b\vec{Y} + c\vec{Z} = S\vec{I}$. Then:
\[ \vec{MN} \cdot \vec{OI} = \frac{1}{S} \left[ \left(\frac{a}{b} - \frac{a}{c}\right)\vec{X} \cdot \vec{S} - \left(1 - \frac{a}{c}\right)\vec{Y} \cdot \vec{S} + \left(1 - \frac{a}{b}\right)\vec{Z} \cdot \vec{S} \right] \]
We evaluate the dot products $\vec{X} \cdot \vec{S}$, $\vec{Y} \cdot \vec{S}$, and $\vec{Z} \cdot \vec{S}$ using the identity $2\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = |\vec{A}|^2 + |\vec{B}|^2 - |\vec{A}-\vec{B}|^2$. For example, $2\vec{X} \cdot \vec{Y} = 2R^2 - c^2$.
\[ \vec{X} \cdot \vec{S} = aR^2 + b\left(R^2 - \frac{c^2}{2}\right) + c\left(R^2 - \frac{b^2}{2}\right) = SR^2 - \frac{bc(b+c)}{2} \]
\[ \vec{Y} \cdot \vec{S} = a\left(R^2 - \frac{c^2}{2}\right) + bR^2 + c\left(R^2 - \frac{a^2}{2}\right) = SR^2 - \frac{ac(a+c)}{2} \]
\[ \vec{Z} \cdot \vec{S} = a\left(R^2 - \frac{b^2}{2}\right) + b\left(R^2 - \frac{a^2}{2}\right) + cR^2 = SR^2 - \frac{ab(a+b)}{2} \]
Substituting these back into the expression for $\vec{MN} \cdot \vec{OI}$:
The coefficient of $SR^2$ is $\left(\frac{a}{b} - \frac{a}{c}\right) - \left(1 - \frac{a}{c}\right) + \left(1 - \frac{a}{b}\right) = 0$.
The remaining terms are:
\[ \vec{MN} \cdot \vec{OI} = \frac{1}{2S} \left[ -\left(\frac{a}{b} - \frac{a}{c}\right)bc(b+c) + \left(1 - \frac{a}{c}\right)ac(a+c) - \left(1 - \frac{a}{b}\right)ab(a+b) \right] \]
\[ = \frac{1}{2S} \left[ -a(c-b)(b+c) + a(c-a)(a+c) - a(b-a)(a+b) \right] \]
\[ = \frac{a}{2S} \left[ -(c^2 - b^2) + (c^2 - a^2) - (b^2 - a^2) \right] = \frac{a}{2S} [ -c^2 + b^2 + c^2 - a^2 - b^2 + a^2 ] = 0 \]
Since the dot product is zero, the line $MN$ is perpendicular to the line $OI$. Thus, $\gamma = 90^\circ$.
The value of $\frac{\gamma}{2}$ is therefore:
\[ \frac{90^\circ}{2} = 45^\circ \]

$\frac{\gamma}{2} = 45^\circ$ \(\square\)
