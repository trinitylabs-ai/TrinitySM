To find the angle $\gamma$ between the lines $MN$ and $OI$, we analyze the geometry of triangle $XYZ$ using vector methods. Let the side lengths be $YZ = a$, $XZ = b$, and $XY = c$, and let the internal angles at vertices $X, Y, Z$ be $A, B, C$ respectively.

### 1. Vector Representation of $MN$
Let vertex $X$ be the origin $(0,0)$. Let $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ be unit vectors along the sides $XY$ and $XZ$ respectively. The positions of the vertices are:
- $X = \vec{0}$
- $Y = c\vec{u}$
- $Z = b\vec{v}$

Points $M$ and $N$ lie on the sides $XY$ and $XZ$ such that $YM = ZN = YZ = a$. Their positions are:
- $\vec{XM} = (c-a)\vec{u}$
- $\vec{XN} = (b-a)\vec{v}$

The vector $\vec{MN}$ is given by:
$$\vec{MN} = \vec{XN} - \vec{XM} = (b-a)\vec{v} - (c-a)\vec{u}$$

### 2. Vector Representation of $OI$
The incenter $I$ is given by the formula:
$$\vec{XI} = \frac{a\vec{X} + b\vec{Y} + c\vec{Z}}{a+b+c} = \frac{a(0) + b(c\vec{u}) + c(b\vec{v})}{a+b+c} = \frac{bc}{S}(\vec{u} + \vec{v})$$
where $S = a+b+c$.

The circumcenter $O$ is the point such that its projections onto $XY$ and $XZ$ are the midpoints of those sides. Let $\vec{XO} = p\vec{u} + q\vec{v}$. Then:
- $\vec{XO} \cdot \vec{u} = p + q\cos A = \frac{c}{2}$
- $\vec{XO} \cdot \vec{v} = p\cos A + q = \frac{b}{2}$

Solving for $p$ and $q$:
$$p = \frac{c - b\cos A}{2\sin^2 A}, \quad q = \frac{b - c\cos A}{2\sin^2 A}$$
The vector $\vec{OI}$ is:
$$\vec{OI} = \vec{XI} - \vec{XO} = \left( \frac{bc}{S} - p \right)\vec{u} + \left( \frac{bc}{S} - q \right)\vec{v}$$

### 3. Evaluating the Angle $\gamma$
Let $C_u = \frac{bc}{S} - p$ and $C_v = \frac{bc}{S} - q$. The dot product $\vec{MN} \cdot \vec{OI}$ is:
$$\vec{MN} \cdot \vec{OI} = [(b-a)\vec{v} - (c-a)\vec{u}] \cdot [C_u\vec{u} + C_v\vec{v}] = (b-a)C_v - (c-a)C_u + \cos A [(b-a)C_u - (c-a)C_v]$$
Rearranging the terms:
$$\vec{MN} \cdot \vec{OI} = C_v(b-a - (c-a)\cos A) - C_u(c-a - (b-a)\cos A)$$
Substituting $C_u$ and $C_v$:
$$\vec{MN} \cdot \vec{OI} = \frac{bc}{S} [b-a - (c-a)\cos A - (c-a - (b-a)\cos A)] - [q(b-a - (c-a)\cos A) - p(c-a - (b-a)\cos A)]$$
$$\vec{MN} \cdot \vec{OI} = \frac{bc}{S} (b-c)(1+\cos A) - T$$
where $T = q(b-a - (c-a)\cos A) - p(c-a - (b-a)\cos A)$. Substituting $p$ and $q$:
$$2\sin^2 A \cdot T = (b-c\cos A)(b-a - c\cos A + a\cos A) - (c-b\cos A)(c-a - b\cos A + a\cos A)$$
Expanding the products:
$$2\sin^2 A \cdot T = (b^2 - ab - bc\cos A + ab\cos A - bc\cos A + ac\cos A + c^2\cos^2 A - ac\cos^2 A) - (c^2 - ac - bc\cos A + ac\cos A - bc\cos A + ab\cos A + b^2\cos^2 A - ab\cos^2 A)$$
$$2\sin^2 A \cdot T = b^2 - c^2 - ab + ac + (c^2-b^2)\cos^2 A + (ab-ac)\cos^2 A = (b^2-c^2)\sin^2 A - a(b-c)\sin^2 A$$
Thus, $T = \frac{(b-c)(b+c-a)}{2}$. Substituting $T$ back:
$$\vec{MN} \cdot \vec{OI} = (b-c) \left[ \frac{bc(1+\cos A)}{S} - \frac{b+c-a}{2} \right]$$
By the Law of Cosines, $a^2 = b^2+c^2-2bc\cos A$, so $2bc(1+\cos A) = 2bc + b^2+c^2-a^2 = (b+c)^2-a^2 = (b+c-a)(b+c+a) = (b+c-a)S$.
Therefore, $\frac{bc(1+\cos A)}{S} = \frac{b+c-a}{2}$, which implies $\vec{MN} \cdot \vec{OI} = 0$.

Since the dot product is zero, the lines $MN$ and $OI$ are perpendicular for any non-equilateral triangle. Thus, $\gamma = 90^\circ$.
$$\frac{\gamma}{2} = \frac{90^\circ}{2} = 45^\circ$$
