Let the side lengths of triangle $XYZ$ be $YZ = x$, $XZ = y$, and $XY = z$. Let $O$ be the circumcenter and $I$ be the incenter of the triangle. We set the circumcenter $O$ as the origin of our coordinate system. Let the vectors from $O$ to the vertices $X, Y, Z$ be $\vec{X}, \vec{Y}, \vec{Z}$. Since $O$ is the circumcenter, $|\vec{X}| = |\vec{Y}| = |\vec{Z}| = R$, where $R$ is the circumradius.

The vector to the incenter $I$ is given by the formula:
\[ \vec{OI} = \frac{x\vec{X} + y\vec{Y} + z\vec{Z}}{x+y+z} \]
The points $M$ and $N$ lie on the sides $XY$ and $XZ$, respectively, such that $YM = ZN = YZ = x$. Consequently, the distances from $X$ are $XM = XY - YM = z - x$ and $XN = XZ - ZN = y - x$. We express the vectors $\vec{XM}$ and $\vec{XN}$ as:
\[ \vec{XM} = \frac{z-x}{z} \vec{XY} = \frac{z-x}{z} (\vec{Y} - \vec{X}), \quad \vec{XN} = \frac{y-x}{y} \vec{XZ} = \frac{y-x}{y} (\vec{Z} - \vec{X}) \]
The vector $\vec{MN}$ is then:
\[ \vec{MN} = \vec{XN} - \vec{XM} = \frac{y-x}{y} \vec{Z} - \frac{z-x}{z} \vec{Y} + \left( \frac{z-x}{z} - \frac{y-x}{y} \right) \vec{X} \]
Simplifying the coefficient of $\vec{X}$:
\[ \frac{z-x}{z} - \frac{y-x}{y} = \left(1 - \frac{x}{z}\right) - \left(1 - \frac{x}{y}\right) = \frac{x}{y} - \frac{x}{z} = \frac{x(z-y)}{yz} \]
Thus, we have:
\[ \vec{MN} = \frac{1}{yz} [ x(z-y)\vec{X} - y(z-x)\vec{Y} + z(y-x)\vec{Z} ] \]
To find the angle $\gamma$ between the lines $MN$ and $OI$, we compute the dot product $\vec{MN} \cdot \vec{OI}$:
\[ \vec{MN} \cdot \vec{OI} = \frac{1}{yz(x+y+z)} [ x(z-y)\vec{X} - y(z-x)\vec{Y} + z(y-x)\vec{Z} ] \cdot [ x\vec{X} + y\vec{Y} + z\vec{Z} ] \]
Let $S$ be the value of the dot product in the brackets. Using $\vec{X}^2 = \vec{Y}^2 = \vec{Z}^2 = R^2$:
\[ S = R^2 [ x^2(z-y) - y^2(z-x) + z^2(y-x) ] + [ xy(z-y) - xy(z-x) ] \vec{X} \cdot \vec{Y} + [ xz(z-y) + xz(y-x) ] \vec{X} \cdot \vec{Z} + [ -yz(z-x) + yz(y-x) ] \vec{Y} \cdot \vec{Z} \]
The first term simplifies to $R^2(x-y)(y-z)(z-x)$. The other coefficients simplify as follows:
\[ xy(z-y) - xy(z-x) = xy(x-y), \quad xz(z-y) + xz(y-x) = xz(z-x), \quad -yz(z-x) + yz(y-x) = yz(y-z) \]
Thus,
\[ S = R^2(x-y)(y-z)(z-x) + xy(x-y)\vec{X} \cdot \vec{Y} + xz(z-x)\vec{X} \cdot \vec{Z} + yz(y-z)\vec{Y} \cdot \vec{Z} \]
Using the property $\vec{X} \cdot \vec{Y} = R^2 \cos(2\angle Z)$, $\vec{Y} \cdot \vec{Z} = R^2 \cos(2\angle X)$, and $\vec{Z} \cdot \vec{X} = R^2 \cos(2\angle Y)$:
\[ S = R^2 [ (x-y)(y-z)(z-x) + xy(x-y)\cos(2\angle Z) + yz(y-z)\cos(2\angle X) + zx(z-x)\cos(2\angle Y) ] \]
Using $\cos(2\theta) = 1 - 2\sin^2 \theta$:
\[ \sum_{\text{cyc}} xy(x-y)\cos(2\angle Z) = \sum_{\text{cyc}} xy(x-y) - 2 \sum_{\text{cyc}} xy(x-y)\sin^2(\angle Z) \]
We observe that $\sum_{\text{cyc}} xy(x-y) = x^2y - xy^2 + y^2z - yz^2 + z^2x - zx^2 = -(x-y)(y-z)(z-x)$. This cancels the first term in $S$. Substituting $\sin(\angle Z) = \frac{z}{2R}$:
\[ S = -2R^2 \sum_{\text{cyc}} xy(x-y) \frac{z^2}{4R^2} = -\frac{xyz}{2} \sum_{\text{cyc}} z(x-y) = -\frac{xyz}{2} (zx - zy + xy - xz + yz - yx) = 0 \]
Since $\vec{MN} \cdot \vec{OI} = 0$, the lines $MN$ and $OI$ are perpendicular, so $\gamma = 90^\circ$. Therefore:
\[ \frac{\gamma}{2} = \frac{90^\circ}{2} = 45^\circ \]
