To prove that $\sum_{(a, b) \in S_{AB}} (b - a) \neq \sum_{(a, b) \in S_{BA}} (a - b)$, we define the two sums as $\Sigma_{AB}$ and $\Sigma_{BA}$, respectively.

**1. Decomposition of the Sums**
The set $A \times B$ is the set of all ordered pairs $(a, b)$ where $a \in A$ and $b \in B$. Since $A$ and $B$ form a partition of the set $\{1, 2, \ldots, 2022\}$, we have $A \cap B = \emptyset$, which implies that for any $a \in A$ and $b \in B$, $a \neq b$.
The sets $S_{AB}$ and $S_{BA}$ are defined as:
- $S_{AB} = \{ (a, b) \in A \times B \mid a < b \}$
- $S_{BA} = \{ (a, b) \in A \times B \mid b < a \}$

Because every pair $(a, b) \in A \times B$ satisfies either $a < b$ or $b < a$, the sets $S_{AB}$ and $S_{BA}$ partition the set $A \times B$.

**2. Algebraic Manipulation of the Difference**
We consider the difference $\Delta = \Sigma_{AB} - \Sigma_{BA}$:
\[ \Delta = \sum_{(a, b) \in S_{AB}} (b - a) - \sum_{(a, b) \in S_{BA}} (a - b) \]
For any pair $(a, b) \in S_{BA}$, we have $b < a$, and thus $a - b = -(b - a)$. Substituting this into the expression for $\Delta$:
\[ \Delta = \sum_{(a, b) \in S_{AB}} (b - a) - \sum_{(a, b) \in S_{BA}} (-(b - a)) = \sum_{(a, b) \in S_{AB}} (b - a) + \sum_{(a, b) \in S_{BA}} (b - a) \]
Since $S_{AB} \cup S_{BA} = A \times B$, we can combine these into a single sum over all pairs in $A \times B$:
\[ \Delta = \sum_{(a, b) \in A \times B} (b - a) \]
Expanding this double sum over the sets $A$ and $B$:
\[ \Delta = \sum_{a \in A} \sum_{b \in B} (b - a) = \sum_{a \in A} \left( \sum_{b \in B} b - \sum_{b \in B} a \right) \]
Let $m$ denote the size of sets $A$ and $B$, so $m = 1011$. The inner sum $\sum_{b \in B} a$ is simply $m \cdot a$. Thus:
\[ \Delta = \sum_{a \in A} \left( \sum_{b \in B} b - m a \right) = \sum_{a \in A} \left( \sum_{b \in B} b \right) - m \sum_{a \in A} a \]
Since $\sum_{b \in B} b$ is a constant with respect to $a$, the first term is $m \sum_{b \in B} b$. Therefore:
\[ \Delta = m \sum_{b \in B} b - m \sum_{a \in A} a = m \left( \sum_{b \in B} b - \sum_{a \in A} a \right) \]

**3. Parity Analysis**
To prove $\Sigma_{AB} \neq \Sigma_{BA}$, we must show that $\Delta \neq 0$. Since $m = 1011 \neq 0$, $\Delta = 0$ if and only if $\sum_{b \in B} b = \sum_{a \in A} a$.
Let $S$ be the sum of all integers from 1 to 2022:
\[ S = \sum_{i=1}^{2022} i = \frac{2022 \times 2023}{2} = 1011 \times 2023 \]
We know that $\sum_{a \in A} a + \sum_{b \in B} b = S$. If $\sum_{a \in A} a = \sum_{b \in B} b$, then $S = 2 \sum_{a \in A} a$, which implies that $S$ must be an even number.
However, we calculate the parity of $S$:
\[ S = 1011 \times 2023 \equiv 1 \times 1 = 1 \pmod 2 \]
Since $S$ is odd, it cannot be expressed as $2 \sum_{a \in A} a$ for any integer $\sum a$. Thus, $\sum_{b \in B} b \neq \sum_{a \in A} a$.

**Conclusion**
Since $m \neq 0$ and $\sum_{b \in B} b - \sum_{a \in A} a \neq 0$, the difference $\Delta$ is non-zero. Thus, $\Sigma_{AB} \neq \Sigma_{BA}$, which proves that:
\[ \sum_{(a, b) \in S_{AB}} (b - a) \neq \sum_{(a, b) \in S_{BA}} (a - b) \]
\(\square\)
