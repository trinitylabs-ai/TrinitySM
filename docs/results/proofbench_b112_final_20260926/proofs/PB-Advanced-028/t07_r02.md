To prove that $C$ is the midpoint of the chord $XY$, we use a coordinate geometry approach.

**1. Coordinate Setup**
Let $C$ be the origin $(0,0)$. Let the line $BC$ lie along the $x$-axis. Let the side lengths of $\triangle ABC$ be $a, b, c$ and the angles be $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$. Thus, $B = (a, 0)$ and $A = (b \cos \gamma, b \sin \gamma)$. Since the triangle is acute, $\cos \gamma > 0$.

**2. Coordinates of $H$ and $P$**
The altitude from $C$ to $AB$ is the line $CF$. The slope of $AB$ is $m_{AB} = \frac{b \sin \gamma}{b \cos \gamma - a}$. Since $CF \perp AB$, the slope of $CF$ is $m_{CF} = \frac{a - b \cos \gamma}{b \sin \gamma}$.
The orthocenter $H$ is the intersection of $CF$ and the altitude from $A$ to $BC$. The altitude from $A$ is the vertical line $x = b \cos \gamma$. Thus, the coordinates of $H$ are:
\[ H = \left( b \cos \gamma, m_{CF} \cdot b \cos \gamma \right) = \left( b \cos \gamma, \frac{(a - b \cos \gamma) \cos \gamma}{\sin \gamma} \right) \]
The point $P$ is the reflection of $H$ across the line $BC$ (the $x$-axis), so its coordinates are:
\[ P = \left( b \cos \gamma, \frac{(b \cos \gamma - a) \cos \gamma}{\sin \gamma} \right) \]

**3. The Center of the Circumcircle of $\triangle AFP$**
Let $O' = (x_0, y_0)$ be the center of the circumcircle of $\triangle AFP$. Since $A$ and $P$ share the same $x$-coordinate $b \cos \gamma$, the perpendicular bisector of $AP$ is the horizontal line $y = \frac{y_A + y_P}{2}$:
\[ y_0 = \frac{b \sin \gamma + \frac{b \cos^2 \gamma - a \cos \gamma}{\sin \gamma}}{2} = \frac{b \sin^2 \gamma + b \cos^2 \gamma - a \cos \gamma}{2 \sin \gamma} = \frac{b - a \cos \gamma}{2 \sin \gamma} \]
The center $O'$ must also satisfy $O'A^2 = O'F^2$. Let $F = (x_F, y_F)$.
\[ (x_0 - b \cos \gamma)^2 + (y_0 - b \sin \gamma)^2 = (x_0 - x_F)^2 + (y_0 - y_F)^2 \]
Expanding both sides:
\[ x_0^2 - 2x_0 b \cos \gamma + b^2 \cos^2 \gamma + y_0^2 - 2y_0 b \sin \gamma + b^2 \sin^2 \gamma = x_0^2 - 2x_0 x_F + x_F^2 + y_0^2 - 2y_0 y_F + y_F^2 \]
Rearranging for $x_0$:
\[ 2x_0(x_F - b \cos \gamma) = x_F^2 + y_F^2 - (b^2 \cos^2 \gamma + b^2 \sin^2 \gamma) + 2y_0 b \sin \gamma - 2y_0 y_F \]
Using $x_F^2 + y_F^2 = CF^2$, we have:
\[ 2x_0(x_F - b \cos \gamma) = CF^2 - b^2 + 2y_0(b \sin \gamma - y_F) \]
To show that $x_0 = 0$, we must verify the condition:
\[ b^2 - CF^2 = 2y_0(b \sin \gamma - y_F) \]
From the geometry of $\triangle ABC$, $CF = b \sin A = \frac{ab \sin \gamma}{c}$. Thus:
\[ b^2 - CF^2 = b^2 - \frac{a^2 b^2 \sin^2 \gamma}{c^2} = \frac{b^2(a^2+b^2-2ab \cos \gamma) - a^2 b^2 (1-\cos^2 \gamma)}{c^2} = \frac{(b^2 - ab \cos \gamma)^2}{c^2} \]
For the right side, $y_F$ is the $y$-coordinate of the projection of $C$ onto $AB$, given by $y_F = \frac{ab \sin \gamma (a - b \cos \gamma)}{c^2}$. Then:
\[ b \sin \gamma - y_F = b \sin \gamma \left( 1 - \frac{a(a - b \cos \gamma)}{c^2} \right) = b \sin \gamma \frac{c^2 - a^2 + ab \cos \gamma}{c^2} = b \sin \gamma \frac{b^2 - ab \cos \gamma}{c^2} \]
Substituting $y_0 = \frac{b - a \cos \gamma}{2 \sin \gamma}$:
\[ 2y_0(b \sin \gamma - y_F) = 2 \left( \frac{b - a \cos \gamma}{2 \sin \gamma} \right) \frac{b \sin \gamma (b^2 - ab \cos \gamma)}{c^2} = \frac{b(b - a \cos \gamma)(b^2 - ab \cos \gamma)}{c^2} = \frac{b^2(b - a \cos \gamma)^2}{c^2} \]
Since $\frac{(b^2 - ab \cos \gamma)^2}{c^2} = \frac{b^2(b - a \cos \gamma)^2}{c^2}$, the equation holds, and we conclude $x_0 = 0$.

**4. Final Proof**
The center $O'$ of the circumcircle of $\triangle AFP$ is $(0, y_0)$. The line $BC$ is the $x$-axis. The projection of $O'$ onto the line $BC$ is the point $(0,0)$, which is $C$. In any circle, the midpoint of a chord is the projection of the center of the circle onto the line containing the chord. Since $X$ and $Y$ are the intersections of the circle with the line $BC$, $C$ is the midpoint of $XY$.

(Note: The power of point $C$ is $P(C) = O'C^2 - R^2 = y_0^2 - (b^2 \cos^2 \gamma + (y_0 - b \sin \gamma)^2) = 2y_0 b \sin \gamma - b^2 = -ab \cos \gamma$. Since $\triangle ABC$ is acute, $P(C) < 0$, ensuring $C$ is inside the circle and $X, Y$ are distinct.) \(\square\)
