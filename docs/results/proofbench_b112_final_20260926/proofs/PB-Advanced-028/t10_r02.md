To prove that $C$ is the midpoint of the segment $XY$, we employ a coordinate geometry approach.

**1. Coordinate System Setup**
Let the line $BC$ be the $x$-axis and the vertex $C$ be the origin $(0, 0)$. Let the coordinates of the vertices of the acute triangle $ABC$ be:
- $C = (0, 0)$
- $B = (a, 0)$
- $A = (b, c)$
Since the triangle is acute, we have $a, b, c > 0$ and $a > b$. The condition $a > b$ ensures that the altitude from $A$ to $BC$ falls inside the segment $BC$.

**2. Coordinates of the Orthocenter $H$ and its Reflection $P$**
The orthocenter $H$ is the intersection of the altitudes. The altitude from $A$ to $BC$ is the line $x = b$. The altitude from $B$ to $AC$ is perpendicular to $AC$ (which has slope $c/b$), so it has slope $-b/c$ and passes through $(a, 0)$. Its equation is:
\[ y = -\frac{b}{c}(x - a) \]
Substituting $x = b$, we find the $y$-coordinate of $H$:
\[ y_H = -\frac{b}{c}(b - a) = \frac{b(a - b)}{c} \]
Point $P$ is the reflection of $H$ across the line $BC$ (the $x$-axis), so:
\[ P = \left(b, -\frac{b(a - b)}{c}\right) \]

**3. Coordinates of the Foot of the Altitude $F$**
$F$ is the foot of the altitude from $C$ to $AB$. The slope of $AB$ is $\frac{c}{b - a}$. The line $CF$ is perpendicular to $AB$ and passes through the origin, so its slope is $k = \frac{a - b}{c}$.
The equation of line $CF$ is $y = kx$, and the equation of line $AB$ is $y = -\frac{1}{k}(x - a)$. Solving for their intersection $F$:
\[ kx = -\frac{1}{k}(x - a) \implies k^2 x = -x + a \implies x = \frac{a}{1 + k^2} \]
Thus, $F = \left(\frac{a}{1+k^2}, \frac{ak}{1+k^2}\right)$.

**4. The Circumcircle $\omega$ of $\triangle AFP$**
Let the center of $\omega$ be $O_\omega = (x_0, y_0)$. Since $A = (b, c)$ and $P = (b, -bk)$, the perpendicular bisector of $AP$ is the horizontal line:
\[ y_0 = \frac{c + (-bk)}{2} = \frac{c - bk}{2} \]
Since $O_\omega$ is equidistant from $A$ and $F$, we have $O_\omega A^2 = O_\omega F^2$:
\[ (x_0 - b)^2 + \left(c - \frac{c - bk}{2}\right)^2 = \left(x_0 - \frac{a}{1+k^2}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{ak}{1+k^2} - \frac{c - bk}{2}\right)^2 \]
Simplifying the $y$-term for $A$ as $\frac{c + bk}{2}$ and expanding:
\[ x_0^2 - 2x_0 b + b^2 + \frac{(c+bk)^2}{4} = x_0^2 - \frac{2x_0 a}{1+k^2} + \frac{a^2}{(1+k^2)^2} + \frac{a^2 k^2}{(1+k^2)^2} - \frac{ak(c - bk)}{1+k^2} + \frac{(c - bk)^2}{4} \]
Rearranging terms to isolate $x_0$:
\[ 2x_0 \left(\frac{a}{1+k^2} - b\right) = \frac{a^2(1+k^2)}{(1+k^2)^2} - \frac{ak(c - bk)}{1+k^2} + \frac{(c - bk)^2 - (c+bk)^2}{4} - b^2 \]
Using $\frac{(c-bk)^2 - (c+bk)^2}{4} = -cbk$, and multiplying by $(1+k^2)$:
\[ 2x_0 (a - b - bk^2) = a^2 - ak(c - bk) - (cbk + b^2)(1+k^2) = a^2 - akc + ak^2b - cbk - cbk^3 - b^2 - b^2k^2 \]
Substituting $k = \frac{a - b}{c}$:
\[ \text{RHS} = a^2 - a(a - b) + \frac{ab(a - b)^2}{c^2} - b(a - b) - \frac{b(a - b)^3}{c^2} - b^2 - \frac{b^2(a - b)^2}{c^2} \]
\[ = (a^2 - a^2 + ab - ab + b^2 - b^2) + \frac{(a - b)^2 [ab - b(a - b) - b^2]}{c^2} = 0 + \frac{(a - b)^2 [ab - ab + b^2 - b^2]}{c^2} = 0 \]
Thus, $2x_0(a - b - bk^2) = 0$. For an acute triangle, $a - b - bk^2 = \frac{(a - b)(c^2 - ab + b^2)}{c^2} \neq 0$ (since $c^2 - ab + b^2 = 0$ implies $\angle A = 90^\circ$). Therefore, $x_0 = 0$.

**5. Conclusion**
The $x$-coordinate of the center $O_\omega$ is $0$. The projection of the center of a circle onto a chord is the midpoint of that chord. Since the chord $XY$ lies on the $x$-axis (the line $BC$), its midpoint has the same $x$-coordinate as $O_\omega$, which is $0$. Thus, the midpoint of $XY$ is $(0, 0)$, which is vertex $C$.

\(\square\)
