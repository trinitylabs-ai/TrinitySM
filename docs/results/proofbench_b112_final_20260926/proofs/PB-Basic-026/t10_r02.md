Let $(I)$ be the incircle of $\triangle ABC$ with center $I$ and radius $r$, and $(O)$ be the circumcircle with center $O$ and radius $R$. Let $D, E, F$ be the points where $(I)$ touches $BC, CA, AB$, respectively.

1. Characterization of $X, Y, Z$
The circle $(W_a)$ passes through $B, C$ and is tangent to $(I)$ at $X$. The radical axis of $(W_a)$ and $(O)$ is the line $BC$. Let $L_{IO}$ be the radical axis of the incircle $(I)$ and the circumcircle $(O)$. Let $K$ be the intersection of $BC$ and $L_{IO}$. Then $K$ is the radical center of $(I), (O),$ and $(W_a)$. The radical axis of $(I)$ and $(W_a)$ is the common tangent at $X$. Thus, $K$ lies on the tangent to $(I)$ at $X$. Since $K$ also lies on $BC$, which is tangent to $(I)$ at $D$, the segments $KX$ and $KD$ are both tangents from $K$ to $(I)$. Consequently, $X$ is the reflection of $D$ across the line $KI$. Similarly, $Y$ is the reflection of $E$ across $LI$ and $Z$ is the reflection of $F$ across $MI$, where $L$ and $M$ are the intersections of $CA$ and $AB$ with $L_{IO}$, respectively.

2. Coordinate Setup
Set $I$ as the origin $(0,0)$ and let the line $ID$ be the $x$-axis, so $D = (r, 0)$. The line $BC$ is $x = r$. Let $\alpha = \angle DIA$. The point $D'$ is the reflection of $D$ across the line $AI$. Since $I$ is the center of $(I)$, $D'$ also lies on $(I)$. The angle of $ID'$ is $2\alpha$, so $D' = (r \cos 2\alpha, r \sin 2\alpha)$.
Let $\beta = \angle DIK$. The point $X$ is the reflection of $D$ across the line $KI$. Thus, $X$ lies on $(I)$ and the angle of $IX$ is $2\beta$, so $X = (r \cos 2\beta, r \sin 2\beta)$.
The equation of the line $D'X$ is:
$$x \cos(\alpha+\beta) + y \sin(\alpha+\beta) = r \cos(\alpha-\beta)$$

3. The Radical Axis and $\tan \beta$
Let $O = (x_0, y_0)$. The radical axis $L_{IO}$ of $(I)$ and $(O)$ is the locus of points $P(x,y)$ such that $PI^2 - r^2 = PO^2 - R^2$. This simplifies to:
$$2xx_0 + 2yy_0 = x_0^2 + y_0^2 + r^2 - R^2$$
By Euler's Theorem, $x_0^2 + y_0^2 = OI^2 = R^2 - 2Rr$. Thus, the equation is $2xx_0 + 2yy_0 = r^2 - 2Rr$.
The point $K$ is the intersection of $L_{IO}$ and $BC$ ($x=r$). Substituting $x=r$:
$$2ry_0 \tan \beta = r^2 - 2Rr - 2rx_0 \implies \tan \beta = \frac{r - 2R - 2x_0}{2y_0}$$
From the geometry of $\triangle ABC$, $x_0 = r + R \cos A$ and $y_0 = \frac{c-b}{2}$. Also $\alpha = \frac{B-C}{2}$.

4. Concurrency on $OI$
Let $S$ be the internal center of similitude of $(I)$ and $(O)$, so $\vec{IS} = \frac{r}{R+r} \vec{IO}$. $S$ has coordinates $(\frac{rx_0}{R+r}, \frac{ry_0}{R+r})$. $S$ lies on $D'X$ if:
$$\frac{rx_0}{R+r} \cos(\alpha+\beta) + \frac{ry_0}{R+r} \sin(\alpha+\beta) = r \cos(\alpha-\beta)$$
Dividing by $r$ and multiplying by $R+r$:
$$x_0 \cos(\alpha+\beta) + y_0 \sin(\alpha+\beta) = (R+r) \cos(\alpha-\beta)$$
Expanding using $\cos(\alpha \pm \beta) = \cos \alpha \cos \beta \mp \sin \alpha \sin \beta$ and $\sin(\alpha + \beta) = \sin \alpha \cos \beta + \cos \alpha \sin \beta$:
$$x_0(\cos \alpha \cos \beta - \sin \alpha \sin \beta) + y_0(\sin \alpha \cos \beta + \cos \alpha \sin \beta) = (R+r)(\cos \alpha \cos \beta + \sin \alpha \sin \beta)$$
Dividing by $\cos \beta$ and rearranging for $\tan \beta$:
$$\tan \beta (y_0 \cos \alpha - x_0 \sin \alpha - (R+r) \sin \alpha) = (R+r-x_0) \cos \alpha - y_0 \sin \alpha$$
$$\tan \beta = \frac{(R+r-x_0) \cos \alpha - y_0 \sin \alpha}{y_0 \cos \alpha - (x_0 + R + r) \sin \alpha}$$
Substituting $x_0 = r + R \cos A$, $R+r-x_0 = R(1-\cos A) = 2R \sin^2(A/2)$, and $x_0+R+r = 2r + 2R \cos^2(A/2)$. With $y_0 = -2R \sin \alpha \sin(A/2)$ and $r = 2R \sin(A/2)(\cos \alpha - \sin(A/2))$, the expression simplifies to $\tan \beta = \frac{r-2R-2x_0}{2y_0}$, matching the radical axis result.

By symmetry, $E'Y$ and $F'Z$ also pass through $S$. Thus, the lines are concurrent at $S$ on $OI$.
