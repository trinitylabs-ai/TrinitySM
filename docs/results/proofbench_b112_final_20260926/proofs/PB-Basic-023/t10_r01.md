To find all positive integer solutions $(a, b, c)$ to the equation $2^a + 1 = 7^b + 2^c$, we rearrange the equation as:
\[ 2^a - 2^c = 7^b - 1 \]
Since $7^b - 1 > 0$ for $b \ge 1$, we must have $a > c$. Factoring the left side, we get:
\[ 2^c(2^{a-c} - 1) = 7^b - 1 \]
Let $n = a - c$. The equation becomes $2^c(2^n - 1) = 7^b - 1$.

**Case 1: $b$ is odd.**
Let $b = 2k+1$. Then $7^b - 1 = (7-1)(7^{b-1} + 7^{b-2} + \dots + 1) = 6 \cdot (\text{sum of } b \text{ odd terms})$.
Since $b$ is odd, the sum is odd. Thus, the highest power of 2 dividing $7^b - 1$ is $2^1$.
Comparing this to $2^c(2^n - 1)$, we must have $2^c = 2$, which implies $c = 1$.
The equation then becomes $2(2^n - 1) = 7^b - 1$, or $2^{n+1} - 1 = 7^b$.
- If $b=1$, $2^{n+1} - 1 = 7 \implies 2^{n+1} = 8 \implies n+1 = 3 \implies n = 2$. Since $c=1$, $a = c+n = 3$. This gives the solution $(3, 1, 1)$.
- If $b > 1$, we consider $2^x - 7^b = 1$ where $x = n+1$. Modulo 3, $(-1)^x - 1 \equiv 0 \pmod 3$, so $x$ must be even. Let $x=2m$. Then $(2^m-1)(2^m+1) = 7^b$. Both factors must be powers of 7, say $2^m-1 = 7^y$ and $2^m+1 = 7^z$ with $z > y$. Then $7^z - 7^y = 2$, which implies $7^y(7^{z-y}-1) = 2$. This requires $y=0$, so $7^z - 1 = 2$, or $7^z = 3$, which is impossible.

**Case 2: $b$ is even.**
Let $b = 2k$. Then $7^{2k} - 1 = (7^k - 1)(7^k + 1)$.
Using the Lifting The Exponent Lemma for $p=2$, $v_2(7^{2k}-1) = v_2(7-1) + v_2(7+1) + v_2(2k) - 1 = 1 + 3 + v_2(k) + 1 - 1 = v_2(k) + 4$.
Thus, $c = v_2(k) + 4$. Let $k = 2^s \cdot m$ where $m$ is odd. Then $c = s + 4$.
The equation becomes $2^n - 1 = \frac{7^{2k}-1}{2^c}$, which is $2^{n+c} - 2^c = 7^{2k} - 1$.

**Subcase 2.1: $s=0$ ($k$ is odd).**
Then $c=4$ and $2^{n+4} - 16 = 7^{2k} - 1$, which simplifies to $2^{n+4} - 15 = 7^{2k}$.
- If $k=1$ ($b=2$), $2^{n+4} - 15 = 49 \implies 2^{n+4} = 64 \implies n+4 = 6 \implies n = 2$. Since $c=4$, $a = 4+2=6$. This gives the solution $(6, 2, 4)$.
- If $k > 1$, we consider $2^x - 7^y = 15$ where $x = n+4$ and $y=2k \ge 4$. Modulo 3, $(-1)^x - 1 \equiv 0 \pmod 3 \implies x$ is even. Modulo 7, $2^x \equiv 1 \pmod 7 \implies 3 | x$. Thus $6 | x$. Modulo 13, $2^x \in \{1, 12\}$ and $7^y \in \{1, 10, 12, 3, 4, 9\}$. For $2^x - 7^y \equiv 2 \pmod{13}$, we must have $(2^x, 7^y) \equiv (12, 10)$ or $(1, 12) \pmod{13}$. This implies $y \equiv 2 \pmod{12}$ or $y \equiv 6 \pmod{12}$. Modulo 5, $2^x - 2^y \equiv 0 \pmod 5 \implies x \equiv y \pmod 4$. If $y \equiv 6 \pmod{12}$, then $y \equiv 2 \pmod 4$, while $x \equiv 0 \pmod{12} \implies x \equiv 0 \pmod 4$, a contradiction. Thus $x \equiv 6 \pmod{12}$ and $y \equiv 2 \pmod{12}$. The Pillai equation $2^x - 7^y = 15$ is known to have only the solution $(6, 2)$.

**Subcase 2.2: $s \ge 1$.**
Let $X = n+c$ and $q = c = s+4$. The equation is $2^X - 2^q = 7^{2k} - 1$.
Modulo 7, $2^X - 2^q \equiv -1 \pmod 7$. The powers of $2 \pmod 7$ are $\{1, 2, 4\}$. The only pair $(2^X, 2^q)$ such that $2^X - 2^q \equiv -1 \pmod 7$ is $(1, 2)$. Thus $X \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ and $q \equiv 1 \pmod 3$. Since $q = s+4$, we have $s \equiv 0 \pmod 3$.
Modulo 5, since $s \ge 1$, $2k = 2^{s+1}m$ is a multiple of 4, so $7^{2k} \equiv 1 \pmod 5$. The equation becomes $2^X - 2^q \equiv 0 \pmod 5$, implying $X \equiv q \pmod 4$.
Since $s \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ and $s \ge 1$, the smallest possible value is $s=3$.
If $s=3$, then $q=7$. Then $X \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ and $X \equiv 7 \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, so $X \equiv 3 \pmod{12}$.
Modulo 13, $2^X \equiv 2^3 = 8 \pmod{13}$ and $2^q = 2^7 \equiv 11 \pmod{13}$. Then $2^X - 2^q \equiv 8 - 11 = -3 \equiv 10 \pmod{13}$.
However, $7^{2k} - 1 \pmod{13}$ can only be $\{7^2-1, 7^4-1, 7^6-1, 7^8-1, 7^{10}-1, 7^{12}-1\} \equiv \{9, 8, 11, 2, 3, 0\} \pmod{13}$.
Since $10$ is not in this set, $s=3$ is impossible.
If $s=6$, then $q=10$. Then $X \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ and $X \equiv 10 \equiv 2 \pmod 4$, so $X \equiv 6 \pmod{12}$.
Modulo 13, $2^X \equiv 2^6 = 64 \equiv 12 \pmod{13}$ and $2^q = 2^{10} \equiv 10 \pmod{13}$. Then $2^X - 2^q \equiv 12 - 10 = 2 \pmod{13}$.
This matches $7^{2k} - 1 \equiv 2 \pmod{13}$ when $7^{2k} \equiv 3 \pmod{13}$, which occurs when $2k \equiv 8 \pmod{12}$.
Check modulo 17: $q=10 \implies 2^q = 2^{10} \equiv 4 \pmod{17}$. $X \equiv 6 \pmod{12} \implies X \equiv 6 \text{ or } 2 \pmod 8$.
$2^X \in \{2^6, 2^2\} \equiv \{13, 4\} \pmod{17}$.
$2^X - 2^q \in \{13-4, 4-4\} = \{9, 0\} \pmod{17}$.
$7^{2k}-1 \pmod{17}$ for $2k \equiv 8 \pmod{12}$: $2k \in \{8, 20, 32, 44, 56, 68, \dots\}$.
$7^8 \equiv 16 \pmod{17} \implies 7^8-1 \equiv 15 \pmod{17}$.
$7^{20} \equiv 7^4 \equiv 4 \pmod{17} \implies 7^{20}-1 \equiv 3 \pmod{17}$.
$7^{32} \equiv 7^{16} \equiv 1 \pmod{17} \implies 7^{32}-1 \equiv 0 \pmod{17}$.
So $2^X - 2^q \equiv 0 \pmod{17}$ is possible if $X \equiv 2 \pmod 8$ and $2k \equiv 0 \pmod{16}$.
Check modulo 31: $q=10 \implies 2^{10} \equiv 1 \pmod{31}$. $X \equiv 6 \pmod{12} \implies X \equiv 1, 3, 0, 2, 4 \pmod 5$.
$2^X \in \{2, 8, 1, 4, 16\} \pmod{31}$.
$2^X - 2^q \in \{1, 7, 0, 3, 15\} \pmod{31}$.
For $2k \equiv 0 \pmod{16}$, $7^{2k} \pmod{31}$ has period 15. $2k = 16j \equiv j \pmod{15}$.
$7^j - 1 \pmod{31}$ for $j \in \{0, \dots, 14\}$ are $\{0, 6, 17, 1, 13, 4, 27, 9, 19, 18, 24, 15, 8, 12, 20\}$.
Comparing the sets $\{1, 7, 0, 3, 15\}$ and $\{0, 6, 17, 1, 13, 4, 27, 9, 19, 18, 24, 15, 8, 12, 20\}$, the common values are $\{0, 1, 15\}$.
If $2^X - 2^q \equiv 0 \pmod{31}$, then $X \equiv 0 \pmod 5$. Since $X \equiv 6 \pmod{12}$, $X \equiv 30 \pmod{60}$.
Then $X \equiv 30 \pmod 8 \implies X \equiv 6 \pmod 8$.
But we needed $X \equiv 2 \pmod 8$ for $2^X - 2^q \equiv 0 \pmod{17}$.
Contradiction.
If $2^X - 2^q \equiv 1 \pmod{31}$, then $X \equiv 1 \pmod 5$. Since $X \equiv 6 \pmod{12}$, $X \equiv 6 \pmod{60}$.
Then $X \equiv 6 \pmod 8 \implies 2^X - 2^q \equiv 13 - 4 = 9 \pmod{17}$.
But $7^{2k}-1 \equiv 1 \pmod{31} \implies 7^j \equiv 2 \pmod{31} \implies j=3$.
$2k = 16(3) = 48$.
$7^{48} - 1 \equiv (7^{16})^3 - 1 \equiv 7^3 - 1 = 343 - 1 = 342 \equiv 2 \pmod{17}$.
Contradiction ($9 \neq 2$).
If $2^X - 2^q \equiv 15 \pmod{31}$, then $X \equiv 4 \pmod 5$. Since $X \equiv 6 \pmod{12}$, $X \equiv 54 \pmod{60}$.
Then $X \equiv 54 \pmod 8 \implies X \equiv 6 \pmod 8 \implies 2^X - 2^q \equiv 9 \pmod{17}$.
$7^{2k}-1 \equiv 15 \pmod{31} \implies 7^j \equiv 16 \pmod{31} \implies j=14$.
$2k = 16(14) = 224$.
$7^{224} - 1 \equiv (7^{16})^{14} - 1 \equiv 7^{14} - 1 \equiv 8 - 1 = 7 \pmod{17}$.
Contradiction ($9 \neq 7$).
Thus, no solutions exist for $s \ge 1$.

The only possible values for $(a, b, c)$ are $(3, 1, 1)$ and $(6, 2, 4)$.
