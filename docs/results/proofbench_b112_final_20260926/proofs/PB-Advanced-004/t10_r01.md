Let the $18n$ triangles of the triangulation be the vertices of a dual graph $G$. Two vertices are connected by an edge if the corresponding triangles share a diagonal. Since the triangulation is of a convex polygon, $G$ is a tree with $N = 18n$ vertices and a maximum degree of 3. Removing two edges (diagonals) from $G$ splits the tree into three components of sizes $T_1, T_2, T_3$. We must show there exist two edges such that $3n \le |T_i| \le 9n$ for $i=1, 2, 3$.

Let $v$ be a centroid of $G$. By the properties of a centroid, every component of $G - v$ has size at most $N/2 = 9n$. Let the components be $C_1, C_2, C_3$ with sizes $s_1 \le s_2 \le s_3 \le 9n$. Since $v$ is a vertex, the sum of the sizes of the components is $s_1 + s_2 + s_3 = 18n - 1$ (where some $s_i$ may be 0 if the degree of $v$ is less than 3).

Case 1: $s_1 \ge 3n$.
If $s_3 \le 9n - 1$, we remove the edges incident to $v$ that lead to $C_1$ and $C_2$. The resulting components are $C_1$, $C_2$, and $C_3 \cup \{v\}$. Their sizes are $s_1, s_2, s_3 + 1$. Since $s_1 \ge 3n$, $s_2 \ge 3n$, and $s_3 + 1 \le 9n$, all three components satisfy the condition.
If $s_3 = 9n$, we remove the edges incident to $v$ that lead to $C_1$ and $C_3$. The resulting components are $C_1$, $C_3$, and $C_2 \cup \{v\}$. Their sizes are $s_1, 9n, s_2 + 1$. We have $s_1 \ge 3n$ and $s_2 + 1 \ge s_1 + 1 \ge 3n + 1$. Also, $s_2 + 1 = (18n - 1 - 9n - s_1) + 1 = 9n - s_1 \le 9n - 3n = 6n$. Thus, all three components are in the range $[3n, 9n]$.

Case 2: $s_1 < 3n$.
Since $s_1 \le 3n - 1$ and $s_1 + s_2 + s_3 = 18n - 1$, we have $s_2 + s_3 \ge 15n$. Given $s_3 \ge s_2$, it follows that $2s_3 \ge 15n$, so $s_3 \ge 7.5n$, which implies $s_3 \ge 8n$ for any positive integer $n$. We also know $s_3 \le 9n$.
We choose the first diagonal to be the edge $e_1$ incident to $v$ that leads to $C_3$. This splits $G$ into $T_1 = C_3$ and $T' = G \setminus C_3$. The size of $T_1$ is $s_3 \in [8n, 9n]$, which is within $[3n, 9n]$.
The remaining part $T'$ is a tree of size $M = 18n - s_3$. Since $s_3 \in [8n, 9n]$, we have $M \in [9n, 10n]$.
We now seek a second diagonal $e_2$ in $T'$ that splits $T'$ into $T_2$ and $T_3$ such that $3n \le |T_2| \le 9n$ and $3n \le |T_3| \le 9n$. This is equivalent to finding $e_2$ such that $|T_2| \in [M - 9n, 9n]$.
Let $v'$ be a centroid of $T'$. The components of $T' - v'$ each have size at most $M/2 \le 10n/2 = 5n$. Let $S'_{max}$ be the largest component of $T' - v'$. The sum of the sizes of the components is $M - 1$. Since the maximum degree of $v'$ is 3, we have $S'_{max} \ge (M - 1)/3 \ge (9n - 1)/3 = 3n - 1/3$, so $S'_{max} \ge 3n$.
Thus, $S'_{max} \in [3n, 5n]$. We choose $e_2$ to be the edge incident to $v'$ that leads to $S'_{max}$.
Then $|T_2| = S'_{max} \in [3n, 5n] \subseteq [3n, 9n]$.
The size of the third component is $|T_3| = M - S'_{max}$. Since $S'_{max} \le 5n$ and $M \ge 9n$, we have $|T_3| \ge 9n - 5n = 4n \ge 3n$. Since $S'_{max} \ge 3n$ and $M \le 10n$, we have $|T_3| \le 10n - 3n = 7n \le 9n$.
Thus, $|T_2|$ and $|T_3|$ both lie in $[3n, 9n]$.

In all cases, we have found two diagonals that divide $P$ into three parts each containing between $3n$ and $9n$ triangles.
