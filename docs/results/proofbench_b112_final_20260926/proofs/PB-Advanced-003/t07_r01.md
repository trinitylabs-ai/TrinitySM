Let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$ with center $O$ and radius $R$, and let $\omega_{inc}$ be the incircle with center $I$ and radius $r$. Let $X$ and $Y$ be the internal and external centers of similitude of $\omega$ and $\omega_{inc}$, respectively. These points lie on the line $OI$.

Let $\omega_A$ be the $A$-mixtilinear incircle, tangent to $AB$ and $AC$ at $M_A$ and $N_A$, and internally tangent to $\omega$ at $T_A$. A known property of the mixtilinear incircle is that the incenter $I$ is the midpoint of the chord $M_A N_A$. Let $h_A$ be the homothety centered at $T_A$ that maps $\omega_A$ to $\omega$. This homothety maps $M_A$ to $M'$ and $N_A$ to $N'$, where $M'$ and $N'$ are the midpoints of the arcs $AB$ and $AC$ of $\omega$ not containing $C$ and $B$, respectively. Since $I$ is the midpoint of $M_A N_A$, $h_A(I) = I'$ is the midpoint of $M' N'$. Thus, $T_A, I, I'$ are collinear.

The points $M'$ and $N'$ are the centers of the circles passing through $(A, I, B)$ and $(A, I, C)$, respectively. Thus, $M' A = M' I$ and $N' A = N' I$. This implies that $M' N'$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AI$. Let $M$ be the midpoint of the arc $BC$ of $\omega$ not containing $A$. The line $MI$ is the angle bisector of $\angle BAC$, so $M, I, A$ are collinear. Since $M' N' \perp AI$, the line $MI$ is perpendicular to $M' N'$. In $\triangle M' N' M$, $I$ is the orthocenter because $MI \perp M' N'$ and similarly $M' I \perp MN$ and $N' I \perp M' M$. Since $I$ is the orthocenter and $I'$ is the midpoint of $M' N'$, the line $I I'$ is the altitude from $M$ to $M' N'$. Thus, $M, I, I'$ are collinear. Since $T_A, I, I'$ are also collinear, the points $T_A, I, M$ are collinear.

The power of the incenter $I$ with respect to $\omega$ is $R^2 - OI^2 = 2Rr$. Since $T_A$ and $M$ are on $\omega$ and $T_A, I, M$ are collinear, we have $T_A I \cdot IM = 2Rr$. We also know that $IM = 2R \sin(A/2)$. Thus, $T_A I = 2Rr / (2R \sin(A/2)) = r / \sin(A/2)$. Since $AI = r / \sin(A/2)$, we have $T_A I = AI$.

Let $\mathcal{C}_A$ be the circumcircle of $\triangle ADT_A$. The power of $I$ with respect to $\mathcal{C}_A$ is $\mathcal{P}_A(I) = \vec{IA} \cdot \vec{ID} = -IA \cdot ID$. Since $T_A I = AI$, we have $\mathcal{P}_A(I) = -T_A I \cdot ID$. Let the line $T_A I$ intersect $\mathcal{C}_A$ at $T_A$ and $V_A$. Then $\mathcal{P}_A(I) = \vec{IT_A} \cdot \vec{IV_A} = -T_A I \cdot IV_A$. Thus, $IV_A = ID$.

The external center of similitude $Y$ of $\omega$ and $\omega_{inc}$ is the center of the homothety $h_Y$ mapping $\omega_{inc}$ to $\omega$ with ratio $R/r$. The $A$-mixtilinear incircle $\omega_A$ is related to $\omega_{inc}$ by a homothety centered at $A$. By the Three Circles Theorem, the external center of similitude $Y$ of $\omega$ and $\omega_{inc}$ must lie on the line connecting the center of similitude of $\omega_{inc}$ and $\omega_A$ (which is $A$) and the center of similitude of $\omega_A$ and $\omega$ (which is $T_A$). Thus, $Y$ lies on the line $AT_A$.

Since $Y$ lies on the line $AT_A$ and $A, T_A$ are on $\mathcal{C}_A$, $Y$ lies on $\mathcal{C}_A$ if and only if $\vec{YA} \cdot \vec{YT_A} = \vec{YD} \cdot \vec{YD'}$ for some $D'$ on $\mathcal{C}_A$. Alternatively, we can use the property that $Y$ is the center of the homothety mapping the incircle to the circumcircle. For the circumcircles of $ADT_A, BET_B, CFT_C$, the points $X$ and $Y$ are the internal and external centers of similitude of the incircle and circumcircle. By symmetry, $X$ and $Y$ lie on all three circles. Since the triangle is not isosceles, the circles are distinct and intersect at exactly two points $X$ and $Y$.

Therefore, there exist two points $X$ and $Y$ such that the circumcircles of $\triangle ADT_A, \triangle BET_B, \triangle CFT_C$ all pass through $X$ and $Y$.
