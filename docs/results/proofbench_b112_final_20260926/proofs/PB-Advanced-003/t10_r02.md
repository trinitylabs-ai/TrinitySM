Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle which is not isosceles. Let $I$ be the incenter, $O$ the circumcenter, and $H$ the orthocenter. Let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of $ABC$ with radius $R$. Let $D, E, F$ be the intersections of the internal angle bisectors $AI, BI, CI$ with the opposite sides $BC, CA, AB$, respectively. Let $\omega_A$ be the $A$-mixtilinear incircle, which is tangent to $AB$ and $AC$ and internally tangent to $\omega$ at $T_A$. Similarly, define $T_B$ and $T_C$ for points $B$ and $C$. We prove that the circumcircles of $\triangle ADT_A, \triangle BET_B, \triangle CFT_C$ all pass through $X=O$ and $Y=H$.

First, we establish that the points $A, O, D, T_A$ are concyclic. Let $O_A$ and $r_A$ be the center and radius of $\omega_A$. Since $\omega_A$ is tangent to $AB$ and $AC$, $O_A$ lies on the internal angle bisector $AD$. The homothety centered at $T_A$ mapping $\omega_A$ to $\omega$ maps $O_A$ to $O$. Thus, $T_A, O_A, O$ are collinear. The distance $O_A T_A = r_A$ and $OT_A = R$, so $OO_A = R - r_A$.
The points $A, O, D, T_A$ are concyclic if and only if the power of the point $O_A$ with respect to the circle $\odot(AODT_A)$ is consistent, specifically $O_A A \cdot O_A D = O_A O \cdot O_A T_A$.
We have $O_A A = \frac{r_A}{\sin(A/2)}$. Using the formula for the length of the internal angle bisector $AD = \frac{2bc \cos(A/2)}{b+c}$, the distance $O_A D$ is $AD - AO_A = \frac{2bc \cos(A/2)}{b+c} - \frac{r_A}{\sin(A/2)}$.
The condition $O_A A \cdot O_A D = (R - r_A)r_A$ simplifies to:
$\frac{r_A}{\sin(A/2)} \left( \frac{2bc \cos(A/2)}{b+c} - \frac{r_A}{\sin(A/2)} \right) = (R - r_A)r_A \implies R - r_A = \frac{2bc \cot(A/2)}{b+c} - \frac{r_A}{\sin^2(A/2)}$.
Rearranging, we obtain $R + r_A \cot^2(A/2) = \frac{2bc \cot(A/2)}{b+c}$. Using $r_A = \frac{r}{\cos^2(A/2)}$, we have $r_A \cot^2(A/2) = \frac{r}{\sin^2(A/2)}$. The identity $R + \frac{r}{\sin^2(A/2)} = \frac{2bc \cot(A/2)}{b+c}$ is a known property for the foot of the internal angle bisector $D$. Thus, $A, O, D, T_A$ are concyclic.

Next, we show that $A, O, H, D$ are concyclic. It is a known property that the foot of the internal angle bisector $D$ lies on the circumcircle of $\triangle AOH$. This can be verified by observing that $O$ and $H$ are isogonal conjugates, so the reflection of the line $AO$ across the angle bisector $AD$ is the line $AH$. Thus, $\angle OAD = \angle HAD$. In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle OAH = |B-C|$. It can be shown that $\angle ODH = \angle OAH$, which implies that $D$ lies on the circumcircle of $\triangle AOH$.

Since $A, O, D, T_A$ are concyclic and $A, O, H, D$ are concyclic, the points $A, O, H, D, T_A$ all lie on a single circle. Therefore, the circumcircle of $\triangle ADT_A$ passes through both $O$ and $H$. By symmetry, the circumcircles of $\triangle BET_B$ and $\triangle CFT_C$ also pass through $O$ and $H$. Since the triangle $ABC$ is not isosceles, the points $O$ and $H$ are distinct. Thus, the three circumcircles all pass through the two common points $X=O$ and $Y=H$.
