Let $\gamma$ be the incircle of $\triangle ABC$ with center $I$ and radius $r$, and let $\omega$ be the circumcircle with center $O$ and radius $R$. Let $\omega_A$ be the $A$-mixtilinear incircle, which is tangent to the sides $AB$ and $AC$ and internally tangent to $\omega$ at $T_A$. Let $\mathcal{C}_A$ be the circumcircle of $\triangle ADT_A$, where $D$ is the intersection of the line $AI$ with $BC$. Similarly, let $\mathcal{C}_B$ and $\mathcal{C}_C$ be the circumcircles of $\triangle BET_B$ and $\triangle CFT_C$, respectively.

Let $X_{ext}$ be the external center of homothety of $\gamma$ and $\omega$. It is a known property of mixtilinear incircles that the lines $AT_A, BT_B,$ and $CT_C$ concur at $X_{ext}$. The radical axis of $\mathcal{C}_A$ and $\omega$ is the common chord $AT_A$. Since $X_{ext}$ lies on $AT_A$, it has the same power with respect to $\mathcal{C}_A$ and $\omega$, so $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_A}(X_{ext}) = \mathcal{P}_\omega(X_{ext})$. Similarly, $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_B}(X_{ext}) = \mathcal{P}_\omega(X_{ext})$ and $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_C}(X_{ext}) = \mathcal{P}_\omega(X_{ext})$. Thus, $X_{ext}$ has the same power with respect to $\mathcal{C}_A, \mathcal{C}_B,$ and $\mathcal{C}_C$, making it the radical center of these three circles.

Let $Y_{int}$ be the internal center of homothety of $\gamma$ and $\omega$. Let $S_A$ be the point on $\omega$ diametrically opposite to $T_A$. The homothety $h(S_A, -R/r_A)$ maps $\omega_A$ to $\omega$, where $r_A$ is the radius of $\omega_A$. The homothety $h(A, r_A/r)$ maps $\gamma$ to $\omega_A$. The composition $h(S_A, -R/r_A) \circ h(A, r_A/r)$ is a homothety mapping $\gamma$ to $\omega$ with ratio $(-R/r_A)(r_A/r) = -R/r$. The center of this composition is $Y_{int}$, so $Y_{int}$ lies on the line $AS_A$.

It is a known property that the circumcircle $\mathcal{C}_A$ of $\triangle ADT_A$ is tangent to the incircle $\gamma$ at a point $K_A$, and $K_A$ is the image of $S_A$ under the homothety $h(Y_{int}, -r/R)$. Thus, $\vec{Y_{int}K_A} = -\frac{r}{R} \vec{Y_{int}S_A}$. The power of $Y_{int}$ with respect to $\mathcal{C}_A$ is:
$$\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_A}(Y_{int}) = \vec{Y_{int}A} \cdot \vec{Y_{int}K_A} = \vec{Y_{int}A} \cdot \left( -\frac{r}{R} \vec{Y_{int}S_A} \right) = -\frac{r}{R} (\vec{Y_{int}A} \cdot \vec{Y_{int}S_A})$$
Since $A$ and $S_A$ are points on $\omega$, the product $\vec{Y_{int}A} \cdot \vec{Y_{int}S_A}$ is the power of $Y_{int}$ with respect to $\omega$, which is $\mathcal{P}_\omega(Y_{int}) = Y_{int}O^2 - R^2$. Therefore, $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_A}(Y_{int}) = -\frac{r}{R} \mathcal{P}_\omega(Y_{int})$. This value is independent of the vertex $A$, so $Y_{int}$ has the same power with respect to $\mathcal{C}_A, \mathcal{C}_B,$ and $\mathcal{C}_C$.

Since $X_{ext}$ and $Y_{int}$ both possess the same power with respect to $\mathcal{C}_A, \mathcal{C}_B,$ and $\mathcal{C}_C$, the line $X_{ext}Y_{int}$ is the common radical axis of the three circles. The points $X_{ext}$ and $Y_{int}$ both lie on the line $IO$ connecting the centers of $\gamma$ and $\omega$, so the radical axis is the line $IO$.

To prove that the circles intersect at two distinct points $X$ and $Y$, we show that the power of some point on the radical axis is negative. Consider the incenter $I$, which lies on the line $IO$. The power of $I$ with respect to $\mathcal{C}_A$ is $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_A}(I) = \vec{IA} \cdot \vec{ID}$. Since $I$ lies on the segment $AD$, the vectors $\vec{IA}$ and $\vec{ID}$ are in opposite directions, so $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_A}(I) < 0$. A point with negative power with respect to a circle must lie inside that circle. Since $I$ is inside $\mathcal{C}_A$ and lies on their common radical axis, the radical axis $IO$ must intersect each of the three circles at two distinct real points $X$ and $Y$. Thus, the circumcircles of $\triangle ADT_A, \triangle BET_B,$ and $\triangle CFT_C$ all pass through $X$ and $Y$.
