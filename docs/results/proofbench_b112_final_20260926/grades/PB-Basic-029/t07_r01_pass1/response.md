The claimed lower bound is incomplete. The cited level theorem is unproved and only applies for \(k<n/2\); the argument does not cover the intermediate cases
\[
\frac n2\le p<n-2.
\]
The observation for \(p=n-2\) does not fill this gap.

Moreover, the proposed extremal construction is generally impossible. For example, take \(p=1,n=4\) and three tangents at angular parameters \(-a,0,a\) to the unit circle. Their equations include
\[
y=1,\qquad y=\sec a\pm x\tan a.
\]
No point on \(y=1\) lies on the opposite side from \(O\) of both other tangents: this would require simultaneously
\[
x>\tan(a/2)\quad\text{and}\quad x<-\tan(a/2).
\]
Thus the intersection of any added fourth line with the middle tangent crosses at most one of the original three lines and is also red, contradicting the claim that only the original three intersections are red.

Hence neither a complete lower-bound proof nor a valid optimal construction is provided. The correct numerical answer alone does not meet the stated partial-credit criterion.
