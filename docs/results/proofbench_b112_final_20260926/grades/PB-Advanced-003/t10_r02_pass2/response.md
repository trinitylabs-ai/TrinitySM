The central claim is false. Consider the acute scalene triangle with
\[
a=13,\qquad b=14,\qquad c=15.
\]
Here
\[
R=\frac{65}{8},\quad r=4,\quad \sin^2\frac A2=\frac15,\quad \cot\frac A2=2.
\]
The purported “known identity” becomes
\[
R+\frac r{\sin^2(A/2)}=\frac{225}{8},
\]
whereas
\[
\frac{2bc\cot(A/2)}{b+c}=\frac{840}{29}.
\]
These are unequal. Hence the power equality used to prove \(A,O,D,T_A\) cyclic does not hold.

The second assertion that \(A,O,H,D\) are cyclic is also not a standard property and is generally false. Although \(O\) and \(H\) are isogonal conjugates, this only establishes the symmetry of the directions \(AO\) and \(AH\); it does not imply that the angle-bisector foot \(D\) lies on \(\odot(AOH)\).

Thus neither claimed cyclicity is established, and the asserted common points \(O,H\) are incorrect. The submission also does not prove the concurrency of \(AT_A,BT_B,CT_C\), the specified partial-credit result.
