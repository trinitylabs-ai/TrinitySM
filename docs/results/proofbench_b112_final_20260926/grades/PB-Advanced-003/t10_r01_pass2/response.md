The first part gives valid substantial progress. The concurrency of \(AT_A, BT_B, CT_C\) at the external homothety center of the incircle and circumcircle is correct. Consequently, the argument that this point has equal power with respect to \(\mathcal C_A,\mathcal C_B,\mathcal C_C\) is also correct.

However, the proposed second common-power point is based on a false homothety. If \(Q_A\) and \(r_A\) are the center and radius of \(\omega_A\), then the homothety mapping \(\omega_A\) to \(\omega\) must map \(Q_A\) to \(O\). The antipode \(S_A\) of \(T_A\) is not such a homothety center, so
\[
h(S_A,-R/r_A)
\]
does not map \(\omega_A\) to \(\omega\). Therefore the claimed collinearity \(Y_{\mathrm{int}},A,S_A\) is unsupported.

The subsequent assertion that \(\mathcal C_A\) is tangent to the incircle at a point obtained as the stated image of \(S_A\) is likewise unproved and generally false. Hence the power computation for \(Y_{\mathrm{int}}\), and thus the assertion that \(IO\) is the common radical axis, do not follow. Although the final observation that \(I\) lies inside each relevant circle is valid, it cannot establish two common points without first proving a common radical axis.

Thus the solution satisfies precisely the stated partial-credit criterion of establishing the concurrency, but the remaining argument has major, not minor, errors.
