The main argument is sound:

- The cyclicity of \(B,C,E,F\) gives \(\triangle AEF\sim\triangle ABC\), hence \(r_{AEF}=r\cos\alpha\).
- The rhombus condition is correctly reduced to the required radius
  \[
  r_W=\frac{r\cos\alpha}{1+\cos\alpha}.
  \]
- The coordinate computation for the Euler-circle center and the use of Feuerbach’s theorem correctly verify that a circle of this radius is externally tangent to the Euler circle.

However, the final identification is not fully justified. Verifying that the proposed radius is one root of the tangency quadratic does not by itself show that it is the root corresponding to the specified circle. The other root should be computed or compared. In fact it is
\[
r+4R\sin^2\frac{\alpha}{2},
\]
which is larger than \(r\), whereas the proposed root is less than \(r\) for acute \(\alpha\). This would justify selecting the proposed root. The connection between the problem’s “closer to \(A\)” condition and the smaller root is also asserted rather than established. The acute-angle assumption likewise deserves explicit justification from the configuration.

These are minor, readily repairable gaps in an otherwise correct proof.
