The similarity \(\triangle AEF\sim\triangle ABC\), the resulting formula for \(AI'\), and the reduction of the rhombus condition to
\[
r_W=\frac{r\cos A}{1+\cos A}
\]
are correct. The coordinate calculation using Feuerbach’s theorem is also algebraically consistent and proves that a circle of this radius is externally tangent to the Euler circle.

There is, however, a small logical gap at the end: the solution merely asserts that this value is the smaller root and hence corresponds to the specified circle. It also replaces the stated “closer to \(A\) than the Euler circle” condition by “closer than the other tangent circle” without justification. This can be repaired routinely: the other root is the \(A\)-exradius
\[
r_a=s\tan\frac A2,
\]
corresponding to the \(A\)-excircle by Feuerbach, and
\[
\frac{r\cos A}{1+\cos A}<r<r_a.
\]
Thus the calculated circle is indeed the nearer one. The converse in the initial rhombus reduction is likewise only implicit but follows immediately from the cosine rule.

These are minor omissions rather than defects in the central argument.
