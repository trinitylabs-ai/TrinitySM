The analytic approach is essentially correct:

- The computation of \(AO_E^2\) yields the valid identity
  \[
  AO_E^2-\frac{R^2}{4}=2R^2\cos A\sin B\sin C.
  \]
- Once \(A\) is known to be acute, \(\triangle AEF\sim\triangle ABC\) with ratio \(\cos A\), so \(r'=r\cos A\) and
  \[
  AI'=\frac{r\cos A}{\sin(A/2)}.
  \]
- The external-tangency condition is correctly converted into a quadratic for \(r_W\). The verification that
  \[
  r_W=\frac{r\cos A}{1+\cos A}
  \]
  is a root is algebraically correct, and the root-product comparison correctly identifies it as the smaller, hence nearer, circle.
- Consequently,
  \[
  AX=r_W\cot\frac A2=r\cot A,
  \]
  and the final cosine-law computation correctly proves \(XI'=AX\). Symmetry about the angle bisector gives \(AY=AX\) and \(YI'=XI'\), establishing the rhombus.

There is, however, a minor rigor issue at the beginning: before establishing that \(A\) is acute, the solution calls \(AH=2R\cos A\) a distance and describes the direction of \(AH\) accordingly. For obtuse \(A\), the unsigned distance is \(-2R\cos A\), and the direction is reversed. The displayed coordinate formula remains correct if \(AH\) is interpreted as a directed quantity, but that interpretation is not stated. Likewise, the assertion that the “closer” condition forces \(A\) outside the Euler circle deserves a brief justification. Both issues are readily repairable and do not affect the sound core argument.
