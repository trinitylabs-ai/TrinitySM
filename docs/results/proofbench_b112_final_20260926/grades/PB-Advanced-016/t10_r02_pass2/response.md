The side-length formulas
\[
AE=\frac{bc}{a+c},\qquad AF=\frac{bc}{a+b}
\]
are correct. However, the subsequent ratios are not:
\[
\frac{AU}{UB}=\frac{b}{a+c-b},\qquad
\frac{AV}{VC}=\frac{c}{a+b-c},
\]
not \(b/a\) and \(c/a\). Thus the Menelaus argument is invalid.

Moreover, although \(LI\) is parallel to the external angle bisector through \(A\), this does not imply that its intersection \(L\) with \(BC\) has the same division ratio as the external-bisector intersection \(L'\). Hence \(L,U,V\) have not been proved collinear, so the stated partial-credit criterion is not met.

Finally, the claim that the circumcircle definitions and “geometric symmetry” position \(P\) so that \(IP\parallel XY\) is merely asserted; the vector equations are never solved and provide no proof of this conclusion.
