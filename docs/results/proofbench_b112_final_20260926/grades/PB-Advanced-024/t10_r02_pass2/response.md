The surjectivity argument is correct. However, the proof of injectivity contains a major gap: from “at most one of \(X(x,b),X(y,b)\) is zero,” it does not follow that both are nonzero. Hence one cannot conclude that both corresponding \(Y\)-terms vanish or that
\[
P(x+z)=P(y+z)
\]
for every \(z\). Thus bijectivity is not established.

The claimed involution \(P(P(x))=x\) is also unsupported. In particular, when \(P(P(b_0))\ne b_0\), the fact that \(X(a,b_0)=0\) gives \(Y(a,b_0)\ne0\) is fully compatible with \(XY=0\); it does not force \(Y(a,b_0)=0\). The later assertion that a displayed relation “implies \(P\) is linear” is unproved.

Consequently, the derivation of \(P(-P(x))=-x\), additivity, and the classification \(P(x)=\pm x\) all depend on invalid steps. Indeed, the reference example
\[
P(x)=2\lfloor x\rfloor-x
\]
satisfies the equation and produces two values, \(0\) and \(-2\), directly contradicting the submitted conclusion that the maximum is \(1\).

Although surjectivity is valid progress, none of the specific partial-credit milestones—bijectivity, the identity \(P(-P(x))=-x\), or a correct classification—is rigorously achieved.
