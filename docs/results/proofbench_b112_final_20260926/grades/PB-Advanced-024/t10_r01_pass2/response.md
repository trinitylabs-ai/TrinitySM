The surjectivity argument is correct and constitutes meaningful progress.

There are, however, two erroneous substitutions. From
\[
P(z+P(b))=b,
\]
replacing \(b\) by \(z+P(b)\) gives
\[
P(z+P(z+P(b)))=z+P(b),
\]
not \(P(b)\). The same mistake recurs with \(x\). These gaps are locally repairable: setting \(b=z\) directly gives \(P(z)=z\), hence \(z=0\), and similarly establishes that \(0\) is the unique zero. This then justifies
\[
P(-P(a))=-a,
\]
and consequently bijectivity, matching key partial-credit milestones.

The remainder is fundamentally incorrect. The claim that a bijection satisfying
\[
P(b+y)-P(y)\in\{P(b),P^{-1}(b)\}
\]
must be linear is neither proved nor true. In particular,
\[
P(x)=2\lfloor x\rfloor-x
\]
is a non-linear solution, for which
\[
\{P(a)+P(-a):a\in\mathbb Q\}=\{0,-2\}.
\]
Thus the claimed classification and maximum \(1\) are false; the actual maximum is \(2\). The valid preliminary progress merits only partial credit.
