The radical-center argument is valid: \(K\) lies on the tangents to the incircle at \(D\) and \(X\), so \(X\) is the reflection of \(D\) across \(IK\). The resulting generic equations for \(D'X\) and for \(\tan\beta\) are meaningful partial progress.

However, the claimed concurrency point \(S\) is incorrect. For example, take
\[
A=(0,0),\quad B=(4,0),\quad C=(0,3).
\]
Then \(I=(1,1), r=1, O=(2,\tfrac32), R=\tfrac52\). In orthonormal coordinates centered at \(I\), with the \(x\)-axis along \(ID\), one obtains
\[
D'=\left(\frac{24}{25},\frac7{25}\right),\qquad
X=\left(-\frac{35}{37},-\frac{12}{37}\right).
\]
Hence
\[
D'X:\quad 559x-1763y=43.
\]
The proposed internal similitude center is
\[
S=\frac{r}{R+r}O=\left(\frac27,\frac17\right)
\]
in these relative coordinates, but
\[
559\cdot\frac27-1763\cdot\frac17=-\frac{645}{7}\ne43.
\]
Thus \(S\notin D'X\).

There is also a sign error in the claimed formula \(x_0=r+R\cos A\): with the positive \(x\)-direction from \(I\) toward \(D\), the correct signed relation is
\[
x_0=r-R\cos A.
\]
Consequently, the asserted final trigonometric simplification is false. The correct common point is the centroid of the contact triangle, not the internal similitude center, so this is not a minor verification error. The valid radical-axis characterization merits only the specified partial credit.
