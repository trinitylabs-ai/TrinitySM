The coordinate setup correctly models the trapezoid, and the formulas for the centers and radii are consistent with the prescribed arcs. The algebraic expansion is correct, and after cancellation the expression \(f(u,v)\) is indeed symmetric in \(u,v\). Likewise, \(R_1R_2=R_3R_4\), so swapping \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) preserves the derived tangency equation.

There is one minor logical gap:  
\[
d^2-(r^2+R^2)=\pm2rR
\]
does not by itself always imply tangency. For the negative sign it also permits \(d=0\) and \(r=R\), meaning that the two circles coincide. The proof does not explicitly exclude \(W_3=W_4\).

This is readily repairable: if \(W_3=W_4\), then \(ABCD\) is a cyclic trapezoid, hence isosceles, so \(AB=CD\). The corresponding selected arcs, and therefore their inscribed angles, are equal, giving \(\alpha=\beta\). By uniqueness of the specified circles, this would imply \(W_1=W_3=W_4=W_2\), contradicting that \(W_1,W_2\) are two tangent circles rather than coincident ones.

Thus the central argument is sound, but a small degeneracy must be excluded.
