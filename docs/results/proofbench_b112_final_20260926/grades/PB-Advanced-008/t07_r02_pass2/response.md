The initial periodicity argument gives meaningful progress: it essentially establishes that \(F_n \bmod \phi(c)\) is eventually periodic.

However, the main existence argument fails:

- \(V(R)\) depends on \(R\pmod M\), not merely on \(r=R\pmod{\phi(c)}\). The deeper residues modulo \(\phi^{(i)}(c)\) are not generally determined by \(r\).
- Consequently, \(V(r)\) and hence the map \(f(r)\) are not well-defined as claimed.
- The asserted surjectivity for \(h>2\) is unsupported; saying that the linear and exponential terms “ensure” all residues are attained is not a proof.
- Moreover, the chosen period \(M\) is a multiple of \(c\), whereas the crucial reference argument needs a period whose gcd with \(c\) is strictly smaller than \(c\).

Thus the solution proves a useful periodicity result but leaves the central step unproved, so it is not eligible for 6 or 7 points.
