The reformulation as a rainbow matching problem is reasonable, but the decisive claimed theorem for circular intervals is false in the stated form.

For example, on a circle with vertices \(1,\dots,8\), let
\[
A=\{8,1,2\},\quad B=\{4,5,6\},\quad
C=\{2,3,4\},\quad D=\{6,7,8\}.
\]
The families \(\mathcal F_1=\{A,B\}\) and \(\mathcal F_2=\{C,D\}\) each have matching number \(2\), yet every member of \(\mathcal F_1\) intersects every member of \(\mathcal F_2\). Thus no rainbow matching exists. Hence the asserted inequality
\[
\nu_{\rm rainbow}(\mathcal F_1,\dots,\mathcal F_n)\ge \min_i\nu(\mathcal F_i)
\]
does not hold for arbitrary circular-interval families.

The submission supplies no argument exploiting the stronger partition/score structure that makes the original problem true.
