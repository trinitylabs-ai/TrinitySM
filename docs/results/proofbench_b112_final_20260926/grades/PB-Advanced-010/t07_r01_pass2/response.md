The central claim that \(H,K,P,Q\) are concyclic whenever \(X\in OG\) is false.

For example, take
\[
A=(0,0),\quad B=(4,0),\quad C=(1,3).
\]
Then \(O=(2,1)\), \(G=(5/3,1)\), so choose \(X=O\in OG\). Direct calculation gives
\[
H=(2,2),\quad P=(4/7,12/7),\quad Q=(5/2,0),\quad K=(8/7,0).
\]
The circle through \(K,P,Q\) has equation
\[
x^2+y^2-\frac{51}{14}x-\frac{33}{14}y+\frac{20}{7}=0.
\]
Substitution of \(H=(2,2)\) gives \(-8/7\ne0\), so \(H\) does not lie on this circle. Therefore the conclusion \(T=H\) is invalid.

Moreover, when \(X=G\), the two circles coincide, so the stated definition of \(T\) becomes degenerate; one cannot simply declare \(T\) to be any point on the circle. The submission also proves neither of the two key partial-credit results: it does not prove \(B,T,P,C\) concyclic and does not establish the required equality of power ratios.
