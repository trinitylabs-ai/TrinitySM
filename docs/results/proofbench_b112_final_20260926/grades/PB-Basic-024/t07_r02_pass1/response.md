The boundary cases are handled correctly, and Cases B and C are essentially valid. Case A, however, contains a major unjustified inference:

From
\[
b^4=2^{2a}(253^c-5^a)
\]
one cannot conclude that \(2a\) is divisible by \(4\), because \(253^c-5^a\) is even and may contribute additional powers of \(2\). Indeed, \(2a=3c\) only forces \(c\) to be even; it does not force \(a\) to be even. Consequently, the assertion that \(253^c-5^a\) is a fourth power is also unjustified.

Thus Case A with \(a\) odd is not addressed. This is an entire nontrivial parity subcase, rather than a minor computational error. The argument does successfully exclude the even-\(a\) cases, so it meets the stated partial-credit criterion.
