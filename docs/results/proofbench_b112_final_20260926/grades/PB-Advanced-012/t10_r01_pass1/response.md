The Gaussian-integer factorization is essentially the correct approach and leads to
\[
a^2=u(u^2-3v^2),\qquad b^2=v(3u^2-v^2).
\]
The divisibility analysis also reaches
\[
x^4-3w^4=z^2,
\]
which is equivalent to the required auxiliary equation \(z^2+3w^4=x^4\).

However, the claimed infinite descent is incomplete. In the \(g=2\) case, branches such as
\[
(x/2)^2=n^4+12M^4
\]
are derived but neither contradicted nor converted rigorously into a smaller solution. Another branch is likewise left unresolved. There are also unaddressed sign issues when separating the Gaussian-integer factors into squares. Thus the impossibility of the auxiliary equation—and hence the \(n=3\) case—is not proved.

This matches the specific “Almost” criterion: the solution reaches the appropriate auxiliary equation but fails to establish that it has no positive solutions.
