The contradiction setup and deduction \(c_k\neq 0\) are correct. The stated Newton inequality is also a legitimate coefficient constraint for real-rooted polynomials, and the subsequent ratio argument would work—up to a minor indexing adjustment—if the required sign condition were established.

However, the central claim that real-rootedness of all partial sums implies the coefficient sequence is a PF sequence, up to a global or alternating sign change, is false. Real-rootedness alone does not imply total positivity or that all roots have the same sign. Indeed, a polynomial with distinct real roots can be extended by adding a sufficiently small leading coefficient while retaining distinct real roots, allowing coefficient sign patterns other than constant or alternating. Thus the assertion that \(c_{n-1}c_{n+1}>0\) eventually is unsupported, and without it the Newton inequality does not yield the ratio decay.

This is a major gap, not a minor error. Nevertheless, the correct contradiction framework and useful Newton coefficient inequality constitute relevant partial progress.
