The logarithmic reformulation and the hierarchy \(f_1(z)\ge f_2(z)\ge\cdots\) for \(z\ge0\) are correct. However, the central argument is invalid.

In particular, the claim that every odd function concave on \([0,\infty)\) satisfies
\[
\sum g(z_k)\le0\qquad\text{whenever }\sum z_k=0
\]
is false. For example, with \(g(z)=\tfrac14\tanh z\) and \((z_1,z_2,z_3)=(-2,1,1)\),
\[
g(-2)+2g(1)=\frac{-\tanh 2+2\tanh 1}{4}>0.
\]
The tangent-line argument also has the inequality reversed for \(z<0\): oddness gives \(g(z)\ge g'(0)z\), not \(g(z)\le g'(0)z\).

Finally, the assertion that the positive and negative contributions are “balanced” and that \(S\) is maximized at zero is unsupported and essentially restates what must be proved. The subsequent comparison uses two different functions, \(f_n\) and \(g\), so the cited odd-concavity claim would not apply even if it were true.

Thus the proof has a major gap and does not establish a suitable localization inequality or an induction toward the desired result. The correct preliminary calculations alone do not meet the stated partial-credit criteria.
