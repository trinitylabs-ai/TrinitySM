The proposed solution is fundamentally incorrect.

- The claim that \(\angle BAC=\angle BYC\) forces \(A,B,C,Y\) to be concyclic is false for ordinary, non-directed angles.
- The proposed point \(D=2A-Y\) does not make \(\angle BDC\) constant.
- The dot-product computation is never completed; instead, the crucial conclusion is asserted via an inapplicable “known” property.
- Neither the correct construction nor the quadrilateral isogonal-conjugacy criterion from the grading guidelines is obtained.

A concrete counterexample is enough. Take
\[
Y=(0,0),\qquad \overrightarrow{YX}=\{(t,0):t\ge0\},\qquad
\overrightarrow{YZ}=\{(t,t):t\ge0\},
\]
so \(\alpha=45^\circ\), and let \(A=(1,\tfrac12)\). The proposed construction gives \(D=(2,1)\).

For
\[
B_1=\left(\tfrac12,0\right),\qquad C_1=\left(\tfrac12,\tfrac12\right),
\]
one has \(\angle B_1AC_1=45^\circ\), while
\[
\tan\angle B_1DC_1=\frac{3}{11}.
\]
For
\[
B_2=\left(\tfrac14,0\right),\qquad C_2=\left(\tfrac7{12},\tfrac7{12}\right),
\]
the dot product and determinant of \(\overrightarrow{AB_2}\) and \(\overrightarrow{AC_2}\) have equal positive absolute values, so again
\(\angle B_2AC_2=45^\circ\). However,
\[
\tan\angle B_2DC_2=\frac{33}{139}\ne\frac3{11}.
\]
Thus \(\angle BDC\) is not constant. In particular, it is not the claimed \(\pi-\alpha=135^\circ\).
