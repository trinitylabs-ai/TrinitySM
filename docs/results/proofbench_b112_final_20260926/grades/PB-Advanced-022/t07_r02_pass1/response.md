The coordinate work in Steps 1–3 is largely correct:

- The lengths \(B'C=\frac{ab}{s}\) and \(C'B=\frac{ac}{s}\) are correctly derived.
- The coordinates of \(K,B',C'\) and the resulting vectors are valid.
- The coordinates of \(I,M,N\) and the formula for \(\cos\angle NIM\) are also correctly obtained.

However, the essential identity is never proved. Verifying
\[
\cos\angle NIM=-\cos\angle B'KC'
\]
for finitely many triangles does not imply it for all triangles merely because the expressions are continuous. A continuous function may vanish at several points without vanishing identically. Moreover, both numerical examples given are right triangles, so they do not even test genuinely different angle configurations.

Thus the central general argument is missing, which rules out 6 or 7 points. The correct coordinate reductions constitute substantial partial progress toward an algebraic proof.
