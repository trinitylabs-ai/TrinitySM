The initial observations that \(f\) preserves unions and that its iterates describe exact-length reachability are correct. The solution also identifies the relevant reduction to bounding the number of distinct sets in one orbit, which constitutes partial progress.

However, the main orbit bound is not proved:

- The preperiod need not be at most the distance to a cycle, nor at most \(120\). Exact-length reachability can have a much longer transient. For example, two directed cycles of lengths \(5\) and \(6\) sharing one vertex produce return lengths governed by the semigroup generated by \(5\) and \(6\), not merely by distance to a cycle.
- Reachable directed cycles need not be vertex-disjoint, so their lengths do not form a partition of \(120\). Consequently, the claimed reduction of their least common multiple to Landau’s function is unjustified. A valid proof requires an additional disjoint-cycle or strongly-connected-component argument.
- The stated numerical value is false: the partition

  16+9+5+7+11+13+17+19+23=120

  gives
  \[
  g(120)\ge 16\cdot9\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11\cdot13\cdot17\cdot19\cdot23

  contradicting the claimed \(g(120)\approx2.23\times10^8\).
