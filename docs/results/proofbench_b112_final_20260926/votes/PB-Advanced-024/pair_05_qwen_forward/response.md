# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The proof correctly establishes that if $S = \{b \in \mathbb{Q} : P(P(b)) \neq b\}$ is non-empty, then $P(P(x)) = x + \delta$ for all $x \in S$ (where $\delta = P(0)$), and that $S$ is closed under subtraction of $\delta$. It also correctly derives $P(b - P(a)) = P(b) - a - \delta$ for $b \in S$ and $a \in \mathbb{Q}$.
Claim gap: The proof contains a load-bearing circular argument in Step 15 regarding the invariance of $S$ under $P$. It asserts that if $b \in S$, then $P(b) \in S$ because $P(P(P(b))) = P(b) + \delta \neq P(b)$. However, the equality $P(P(P(b))) = P(b) + \delta$ relies on applying the rule $P(P(y)) = y + \delta$ to $y = P(b)$, which is only valid if $P(b) \in S$. Without independently proving $P(S) \subseteq S$, the subsequent substitution $b = P(a)$ in Step 17 (which requires $P(a) \in S$) is unjustified, and the contradiction $\delta = 0$ does not follow.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The derivation of $P(P(x)) = x + \delta$ for $x \in S$ and the relation $P(b - P(a)) = P(b) - a - \delta$ are verified. The transition to Cauchy's equation and the final solutions $P(x) = \pm x$ are correct conditional on $P(P(x)) = x$. No substantive repair for the $S$-invariance gap is present in the submission.
Decisive checks: Step 15's claim "$P(b) \in S$ because $P(P(P(b))) = P(b) + \delta$" is a demonstrated defect (circularity). The contradiction $\delta = 0$ in Step 17 depends on $P(a) \in S$ for $a \in S$, which remains unresolved. The quantifier handling for $a, b \in \mathbb{Q}$ is otherwise correct.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The proof rigorously establishes that $P$ is surjective (Step 5). It proves $P(0) = 0$ (Step 12). Assuming bijectivity, it correctly derives $P(P(x)) = x$ (Steps 14-20) via the inverse function $Q = P^{-1}$, and concludes $P(x) = \pm x$ with set size 1.
Claim gap: The injectivity proof (Steps 7-8) omits a case analysis. It assumes that if $P(x) = P(y)$ with $x \neq y$, there exists some $b$ where both $X(x, b) \neq 0$ and $X(y, b) \neq 0$ to derive periodicity. It fails to address the case where for all $b$, exactly one of $X(x, b)$ or $X(y, b)$ is zero. However, this omitted case implies $P(b) - P(b-c) \in \{x, y\}$ for all $b$, which restricts the image of $P$ to a coset of a discrete subgroup of $\mathbb{Q}$, directly contradicting the already established surjectivity of $P$. Thus, the gap is repairable using the proof's own verified results.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The injectivity argument requires the explicit repair that the "one is always zero" case contradicts surjectivity (verified in Step 5). Once injectivity is secured, the use of $P^{-1}$ in Step 15 is valid, and the derivation of $P(P(x)) = x$ is rigorous. The example in Step 19 is illustrative and does not affect the main chain.
Decisive checks: Step 5's surjectivity proof is verified (RHS $P(b_0)-a$ covers $\mathbb{Q}$ as $a$ varies). Step 14-20's derivation of $P(P(x)) = x$ is verified conditional on bijectivity. The gap in Step 7 is a missing case analysis, but the contradiction with surjectivity resolves it without external assumptions. Quantifier and domain handling is precise throughout.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof B is mathematically stronger because its only gap (injectivity case analysis) is directly resolvable using the surjectivity result already rigorously established within the same proof. Proof A contains a circular argument in its core contradiction step (Step 15), asserting the invariance of the set $S$ under $P$ without justification, which undermines the derivation of $\delta = 0$ and leaves the main obligation unmet. Proof B's modular structure allows for a clear, self-contained repair, whereas Proof A's gap is embedded in the central logical chain and lacks a verified path to resolution.
