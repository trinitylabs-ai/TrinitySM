# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: None. The proof relies on the claim that $H, K, P, Q$ are concyclic for any $X$ on the Euler line $OG$, which is not generally true.
Claim gap: The central claim that $H$ lies on the circumcircle $\mathcal{C}_1$ of $\triangle KPQ$ for all $X \in OG$ is false. Consequently, the conclusion that $T=H$ is not justified.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: None.
Decisive checks: A counterexample with $A(0,0), B(4,0), C(1,2)$ and $X(1, 1.5)$ (which lies on $OG$) shows that $H(16/13, 24/13)$ does not lie on the circumcircle of $P(0.8, 1.6), Q(1, 0), K(1.6, 0)$. The distance from $H$ to the center $(1.3, 0.85)$ is $\approx \sqrt{0.997}$, while the radius is $\sqrt{0.8125}$.

## Proof B
Established theorem: None. The proof relies on the claim that $M$ lies on the circumcircle $C_1$ of $\triangle KPQ$ for any $X$ on the Euler line $OG$, which is not generally true.
Claim gap: The central claim that $M \in C_1$ for all $X \in OG$ is false. Furthermore, the formula used to calculate the power of $T$ with respect to $N$ in Line 21 is mathematically unfounded and fabricated.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: None.
Decisive checks: The same counterexample as in Proof A shows that $M(2.5, 1)$ does not lie on the circumcircle of $P(0.8, 1.6), Q(1, 0), K(1.6, 0)$. The distance from $M$ to the center $(1.3, 0.85)$ is $\approx \sqrt{1.4625}$, while the radius is $\sqrt{0.8125}$.

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Both proofs are fundamentally flawed, as they both rely on a false claim that a specific point ($H$ in Proof A, $M$ in Proof B) is always on the circumcircle $C_1$ for $X \in OG$, leading to the incorrect conclusion that $T$ is a fixed point. However, Proof A is more coherent in its structure, whereas Proof B includes a series of unsubstantiated coordinate expressions and a completely fabricated formula for the power of a point (Line 21). Proof A's failure is a single incorrect "known property," while Proof B's failure is a combination of an incorrect property and nonsensical mathematical derivations.
