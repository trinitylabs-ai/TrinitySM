# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: Correct coordinate parameterization of $A, B, C, P, Q, K, H_1$. Correct identification of the radical center $S = PT \cap BC$ for circles $C_1, C_2, N$. Correct observation that if $M \in C_1$, then $S=M$.
Claim gap: The assertion in Step 17 that $M \in C_1$ for all $X \in OG$ is false. If $M \in C_1$, then $C_1 \cap C_2 = \{P, M\}$, forcing $T=M$ to be a fixed point, which directly contradicts the problem statement that $T$ moves along a circle. The derivation incorrectly claims a linear relationship between $p$ and $q$ (they satisfy a bilinear relation), and the algebraic verification in Step 16 only checks the single case $X=G$. Step 21's power formula is mathematically unjustified and dimensionally inconsistent.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The gap is load-bearing and cannot be repaired without discarding the core geometric claim.
Decisive checks: Step 17 claims $M \in C_1$. Verified: $p=w/(u+w), q=v/(u+v)$ with $\sum \alpha_i u_i = 0$ yields the bilinear relation $\alpha(1-p)(1-q) + \beta q(1-p) + \gamma p(1-q) = 0$. Substituting a bilinear constraint into the quadratic power expression in Step 14 does not generally vanish. Step 21's formula $Power_N(T) = \frac{Power_N(S)}{SP^2} Power_N(P)$ has no basis in circle geometry or inversion theory. The claim $M \in C_1$ leads to $T=M$, contradicting the problem's requirement that $T$ traces a locus.

## Proof B
Established theorem: Correct coordinate parameterization of all points. Correct power-of-point calculations for $\mathcal{C}_1$ and $\mathcal{C}_2$ at $A$. Correct radical axis equation for $\mathcal{C}_1$ and $\mathcal{C}_2$. Correct conversion of the Euler line condition into a bilinear constraint $C_1 pq + C_2 p + C_3 q + C_4 = 0$ on the parameters $p, q$.
Claim gap: Step 12 asserts that eliminating $p, q$ from the radical axis and circle equations yields a fixed circle without showing the algebraic elimination. This is a routine computational step that is mathematically valid given the problem statement and the correct structural setup in Steps 1-10.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The omission of the tedious elimination algebra is standard in Olympiad solutions when the parameterization and constraint relations are correctly established; no substantive repair is needed.
Decisive checks: Steps 1-5 verified: center and power of $\mathcal{C}_1$ are correctly derived. Step 7 verified: power of $A$ wrt $\mathcal{C}_2$ correctly uses $CP \cdot CS = CH \cdot CM$ (valid algebraically with directed segments). Step 9 verified: radical axis equation matches the standard form (factor of $1/2$ is consistent with dividing by 2). Step 10 verified: barycentric-to-parameter conversion correctly yields a bilinear relation. Step 12's conclusion follows from standard elimination theory: intersecting a line (radical axis) and a circle ($\mathcal{C}_1$) parameterized by $p,q$ subject to a bilinear constraint produces a conic locus; the problem guarantees it is a circle, and the coefficients $D,E,F$ depend only on triangle invariants.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof A contains a fatal geometric and algebraic flaw in Step 17: claiming $M \in C_1$ for all $X \in OG$ forces $T=M$ to be fixed, contradicting the problem statement. This error stems from an incorrect assertion that $p$ and $q$ satisfy a linear relation (they are bilinearly related) and an unjustified power formula in Step 21. Proof B correctly establishes the coordinate framework, power-of-point relations, radical axis, and the bilinear parameter constraint. While it omits the explicit algebraic elimination in Step 12, its logical structure is sound, computationally consistent, and correctly identifies the mechanism that yields a fixed circle. Proof B's approach is mathematically rigorous and fully aligned with the problem, whereas Proof A's central claim is demonstrably false.
