## Proof A
Established theorem: Correctly establishes a Cartesian coordinate system with $A$ at the origin, derives coordinates for $M, H, P, Q, K$, and obtains the explicit equation of $\mathcal{C}_1$ (circumcircle of $KPQ$). Verifies that for the specific case $X=G$, $\mathcal{C}_1$ coincides with the nine-point circle.
Claim gap: Step 11 asserts without proof that $H, K, P, Q$ are concyclic if and only if $X$ lies on the Euler line $OG$. This claim is false for general $X \in OG$. Consequently, Steps 14-15 incorrectly deduce that $T=H$ for all $X \neq G$, reducing the locus to a single fixed point. This contradicts the problem statement that $T$ moves along a fixed circle (implying a non-degenerate locus). The proof fails to establish the requested theorem.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The coordinate derivations in Steps 1-8 are routine and correct. The fatal defect in Step 11 cannot be repaired without replacing the central geometric assertion. No silent repairs were applied.
Decisive checks: 
- Lines 5-8: Coordinate parameterization ($p,q \in (0,1)$) and circle equation for $\mathcal{C}_1$ are verified correct. The constant term $2pqbc\cos A$ correctly matches the power of $A$ with respect to $\mathcal{C}_1$.
- Line 11: Demonstrated defect. The condition for $H \in \mathcal{C}_1$ requires a specific non-linear relation between $p$ and $q$ derived from substituting $H$'s coordinates into $\mathcal{C}_1$'s equation. The Euler line condition imposes a linear relation between the barycentric coordinates of $X$, which translates to a different constraint on $p,q$. Testing with $X=O$ (circumcenter) shows $H \notin \mathcal{C}_1$, falsifying the claim.
- Lines 14-15: The deduction $T=H$ follows logically from the false premise but yields a degenerate locus, violating the problem's quantifier scope ("as $X$ moves... $T$ moves along a fixed circle").
