# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The functions $f(x)=0$, $f(x)=1$, $f(x)=x$, $f(x)=x \pmod 2$ (range $\{0,1\}$), and $f(x)=x \pmod 3$ (range $\{0,1,-1\}$) satisfy the equation. The proof rigorously establishes $f(0)=0$, $f(1)=1$, and the identities $f(x-f(x))=0$ and $f(1-f(y))=f(1-y)$ for any non-constant solution. It demonstrates that if the zero set $S$ is non-trivial and $f$ is bounded, the period $m$ must be $2$ or $3$.
Claim gap: The proof contains two load-bearing gaps. First, it asserts $f(x) \equiv x \pmod m$ solely because $x-f(x) \in S$ and $m$ is the smallest positive element of $S$, without proving that $S$ is an ideal (i.e., $S \subseteq m\mathbb{Z}$). Second, the claim that non-identity solutions must be bounded relies on heuristic growth comparisons rather than a rigorous derivation.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: To validate the contradiction for $m \ge 4$, I supplied the assumption that $S = m\mathbb{Z}$ and that $f$ is bounded by $1$. Under these conditions, the check for $x=2$ correctly forces $f(2)=2$, contradicting the bounded range. The boundedness claim is treated as a plausible but unproven lemma.
Decisive checks: Lines 12-15 correctly derive $f(0)=0$, $f(1)=1$, and $f(x-f(x))=0$. Lines 31-35 correctly rule out $m \ge 4$ assuming $S \subseteq m\mathbb{Z}$ and $|f(x)| \le 1$. The verification of the modular solutions (Lines 37-53) is arithmetically sound. The deduction $f(x) \equiv x \pmod m$ (Line 22) is an UNRESOLVED check requiring proof that $S$ is closed under subtraction.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The functions $f(x)=0$, $f(x)=1$, $f(x)=x$, $f(x)=x \pmod 2$, and $f(x)=x \pmod 3$ satisfy the equation. The proof rigorously derives $f(0)=0$, $f(1)=1$, and the identity $f(1-f(y))=f(1-y)$. It establishes algebraic relations linking $f(2)$ and $f(-1)$.
Claim gap: The proof fails to justify the exhaustiveness of the solutions. It arbitrarily selects cases for $f(2) \in \{0, 2, -1\}$ without deriving why other integer values are impossible. The "Exhaustiveness" section (Line 60) relies on a heuristic bound $M^2 \le M$ for modular functions, which neither proves that $f$ must be modular nor rules out unbounded non-linear functions.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: No substantive repairs were supplied. The derivation of constraints on $f(2)$ and $f(-1)$ is correct, but the transition to specific cases is a missing logical step. The proof essentially verifies candidate solutions without establishing uniqueness.
Decisive checks: Lines 12-20 correctly derive $f(0)=0$, $f(1)=1$, and $f(1-f(y))=f(1-y)$. Lines 23-28 correctly establish the system $f(2-k)=0$, $f(1-k)=a$, $f(1-a)=k$. The case verifications (Lines 33-57) are arithmetically correct. The case selection for $k=f(2)$ is a DEMONSTRATED defect in completeness, as no derivation restricts $k$ to $\{0, 2, -1\}$.

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Proof A provides a stronger mathematical justification for the classification of solutions. While both proofs contain gaps, Proof A attempts a structural classification based on the zero set $S$ and derives a contradiction for periods $m \ge 4$, actively ruling out other modular candidates. Proof B, conversely, identifies solutions by checking arbitrary values of $f(2)$ without deriving why other values are impossible, leaving the "find all" obligation largely unaddressed. Proof A's heuristic arguments, while not fully rigorous, outline a valid classification path, whereas Proof B's approach is incomplete in its search space and lacks a derivation for case selection.
