# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: Correct coordinate parametrization of $L, I, B, C$ with $L$ as origin and $BC$ as the $x$-axis; correct general equations for circumcircles $\odot(ILC)$ and $\odot(ILB)$; correct algebraic formulas for the intersections $X, Y$ with lines $LU, LV$; correct expressions for slopes $k_X, k_Y$ and intersection $P$ of lines $XC, YB$.
Claim gap: Fails to compute the slopes $m_U, m_V$ from the problem's definitions $AU=AE$ and $AV=AF$. The proof never introduces coordinates for $A$ or the lines $AB, AC$, making it impossible to determine $m_U, m_V$. Step 21 asserts that substituting coordinates makes $m_{IP} = m_{XY}$, but this identity critically depends on the specific positions of $U$ and $V$. Without linking $m_U, m_V$ to the triangle's side lengths/angles, the proof treats them as free parameters, which would make the conclusion false. This is a load-bearing structural omission.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The gap requires introducing $A$'s coordinates, deriving $m_U, m_V$ from $AU=AE, AV=AF$, and performing the non-trivial algebraic verification claimed in Step 21.
Decisive checks: Steps 1-13 are algebraically verified correct. Step 21 is an unsupported assertion. The omission of $m_U, m_V$ computation (Steps 1-21) is a demonstrated defect: the proof does not use the hypotheses $AU=AE, AV=AF$ anywhere after Step 1, breaking the logical chain to the conclusion.

## Proof B
Established theorem: Correct vector framework with origin $L$; correct application of the inscribed angle theorem yielding $\angle LXI = C/2$ and $\angle LYI = B/2$ (Step 5); correct derivation of the intersection $P$ via vector decomposition (Steps 7-12); correct necessary and sufficient linear condition for $IP \parallel XY$ (Equation 21); correct Law of Sines relations for lengths $LX, LY$ in terms of $LI$ and angles (Step 23).
Claim gap: Asserts (Step 25) that substituting the geometric expressions into Equation 21 verifies the identity, without performing the trigonometric simplification. However, all required quantities ($I_u, I_v, \alpha, \beta, s, LX, LY$) are correctly identified in terms of the triangle's parameters, and Equation 21 precisely isolates the condition needed for parallelism.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The gap is a routine trigonometric identity verification. The logical structure and geometric setup are complete up to this calculation step.
Decisive checks: Steps 1-22 are logically and algebraically verified. Equation 21 is correctly derived from the vector parallelism condition (verified by expanding the determinant condition $A_u B_v - A_v B_u = 0$ for $\vec{P}-\vec{I}$ and $\vec{Y}-\vec{X}$). Step 5's angle chasing is verified correct ($\angle LCI = \angle ICB = C/2$ since $L \in BC$). The gap in Step 25 is a local missing justification, not a missing central argument.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof B correctly isolates the exact necessary and sufficient condition for $IP \parallel XY$ (Eq. 21) and properly applies circle geometry ($\angle LXI = C/2$) to express all relevant lengths and directions. Its only gap is a standard trigonometric verification. Proof A, while algebraically correct in its coordinate setup, structurally fails to link the slopes $m_U, m_V$ to the problem's definitions $AU=AE, AV=AF$. It never computes these slopes or introduces coordinates for $A$, rendering the final algebraic claim in Step 21 baseless and disconnected from the problem's hypotheses. B's framework is complete up to a calculation step, whereas A's framework misses a central dependency required to even formulate the verification.
