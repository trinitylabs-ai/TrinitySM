## Proof B
Established theorem: $U$ and $V$ are correctly identified as reflections of $E$ and $F$ across the internal bisector $AI$. The concyclic definitions of $X$ and $Y$ are correctly stated.
Claim gap: The directed angle calculation in steps 19–20 is incorrect, breaking the proof that $X, I, Y, P$ are concyclic. The final parallelism argument (steps 26–29) relies on vague symmetry claims without rigorous justification. The concyclicity claim is central to the synthetic approach, and its failure invalidates the subsequent parallelism deduction.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: Verified step 4 (reflection property). Falsified step 19 by direct computation. No repairs supplied.
Decisive checks: Computed $\angle(IC, IL)$ using directed angles mod $180^\circ$: $\angle(IC, IL) = \angle(IC, AI) + \angle(AI, IL) = (A/2 - C/2) + 90^\circ$. The proof claims $-B/2$. Since $-B/2 = (A+C)/2 - 90^\circ$, the difference is $180^\circ - C \not\equiv 0 \pmod{180^\circ}$ for non-degenerate triangles. This demonstrates a fundamental arithmetic error in the angle-chasing chain. The symmetry argument in step 28 is unsubstantiated and does not replace the missing rigorous deduction.

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Proof A establishes a rigorous coordinate framework with correctly derived circle equations, intersection formulas, and slope expressions. Its gap is an omitted algebraic substitution for $U$ and $V$ and an unshown simplification, which is a local missing justification that does not break the logical structure. Proof B contains a demonstrable arithmetic error in its core directed-angle calculation (step 19), which falsifies the concyclicity claim, and relies on hand-waving symmetry for the conclusion. A's verified progress is mathematically complete up to routine computation, whereas B's central synthetic argument is fundamentally flawed. A is strictly stronger.
