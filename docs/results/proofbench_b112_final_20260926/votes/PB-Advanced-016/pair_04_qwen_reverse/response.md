## Proof A
Established theorem: The points $X, I, Y, P$ are concyclic. The directed angle chasing in lines 9–23 correctly derives $\angle(XI, YI) \equiv \angle(XP, YP) \pmod{180^\circ}$ using the cyclic properties of $\odot(ILC)$ and $\odot(ILB)$ and the perpendicularity $IL \perp AI$.
Claim gap: The parallelism $IP \parallel XY$ is not established. Lines 26–29 assert that reflection symmetry across $AI$ forces $\angle IXY = \angle PYX$, making $XIYP$ an isosceles trapezoid. This is a verified defect: the problem explicitly restricts to non-isosceles triangles, so $AB \neq AC$, $L$ is not the midpoint of $BC$, and $AU \neq AV$. The configuration lacks the required reflection symmetry, invalidating the angle equality claim.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The symmetry argument implicitly changes the domain from non-isosceles to isosceles triangles. No routine repair can salvage the parallelism step without introducing a new geometric or computational argument.
Decisive checks: Lines 9–23 correctly establish concyclicity modulo standard directed angle conventions. Line 28's symmetry claim fails for any scalene triangle (e.g., $B \neq C$ breaks the mapping $U \leftrightarrow V$ and $L \leftrightarrow L$). The gap leaves the core obligation ($IP \parallel XY$) entirely unsupported by the submitted text.

## Proof B

Intersection coordinates for $X, Y$ and slopes $k_X, k_Y$ are correctly computed (lines 7–10). Crucially, the proof never substitutes the specific slopes $m_U, m_V$ determined by $AU=AE$ and $AV=AF$. If the identity holds for arbitrary $m_U, m_V$, the problem's hypothesis is unused; if it requires a specific relation, that relation is missing. This is an unresolved computational check rather than a logical flaw.

The verification that the rational expression for $m_{IP} - m_{XY}$ vanishes (potentially using the specific values of $m_U, m_V$ from the angle bisector theorem) is left unstated. Decisive checks: Lines 5–10 correctly derive circle equations and intersection formulas using standard analytic geometry. Unlike Proof A, there is no false geometric assumption; the method correctly handles the asymmetric case and leaves a verifiable algebraic identity.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof A contains a fatal logical defect: it relies on reflection symmetry across $AI$ to prove parallelism, but the problem explicitly assumes a non-isosceles triangle, breaking that symmetry and invalidating the angle equality claim. Proof B establishes a rigorous coordinate framework, correctly derives all intermediate equations, and provides a direct computational pathway to the slope equality. Its only gap is the omission of the final algebraic verification and the explicit substitution of $m_U, m_V$, which is a local computational omission rather than a conceptual error. B's method correctly accommodates the asymmetric configuration and leaves a verifiable algebraic identity, whereas A's argument collapses on a false geometric premise.
