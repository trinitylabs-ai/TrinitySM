# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: Derives a necessary and sufficient vector condition for $IP \parallel XY$ in the form $I_u LY + I_v LX = (1-s) LX LY + s LC (\alpha LY + \beta LX)$, where coefficients are defined relative to a basis along $LU, LV, BC$. Correctly identifies $\angle LXI = C/2$ and $\angle LYI = B/2$ via cyclic quadrilateral properties. The vector algebra in steps 7–21 is verified correct.
Claim gap: Fails to verify that the geometric data ($AU=AE, AV=AF$, incenter properties, and circle intersections) satisfies the derived vector condition. Step 25 asserts the identity holds after substitution without performing the substitution or showing how the specific lengths $AU, AV$ influence $LX, LY, s, \alpha, \beta$. This is a load-bearing defect: the proof reduces the problem to an algebraic identity but does not prove the identity.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The vector algebra was independently verified, but no geometric verification was supplied. The gap remains as stated; the proof stops short of connecting the hypothesis to the derived condition.
Decisive checks: Steps 7–12 correctly parameterize $P$ as the intersection of $XC$ and $YB$. Steps 14–21 correctly translate $IP \parallel XY$ into the scalar condition in step 21. Step 5's angle equalities hold under standard configuration assumptions. Step 25 is a demonstrated defect: substituting Law of Sines expressions for $LX, LY$ does not automatically satisfy the condition without explicit computation of $I_u, I_v, \alpha, \beta$ and the parameter $s$ in terms of $a,b,c$. The proof provides no mechanism to verify the condition.

## Proof B
Established theorem: Establishes a complete coordinate framework with $L$ at the origin and $BC$ on the $x$-axis. Correctly computes $x_I = \pm r \tan((\beta-\gamma)/2)$, derives explicit circle equations for $\odot(ILC)$ and $\odot(ILB)$, finds closed-form coordinates for $X$ and $Y$, computes slopes $k_X, k_Y$ of lines $XC, YB$, and derives explicit formulas for the intersection $P(x_P, y_P)$. The algebraic manipulations in steps 3–17 are verified correct.
Claim gap: Omits the coordinates of vertex $A$ and the explicit computation of slopes $m_U, m_V$ from the hypothesis $AU=AE, AV=AF$. Step 21 asserts that substituting these into the slope expressions yields $m_{IP} = m_{XY}$, but does not show the substitution. This is a local defect: the computational pathway is fully laid out and correct, but the final parameter substitution is left unstated.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The coordinate geometry setup, circle intersections, slope formulas, and intersection $P$ are all independently verified. The gap is strictly the omission of the final algebraic substitution step, which is routine given the explicit formulas provided. No repairs were supplied.
Decisive checks: Step 3's angle relation $\angle LID = |\beta-\gamma|/2$ is verified via perpendicular rotation invariance. Step 5's circle equations correctly pass through $L, C, I$ and $L, B, I$. Step 7's intersection $x_X$ correctly solves the quadratic. Step 9's slope $k_X$ simplification is algebraically verified. Step 13's intersection $P$ uses standard line intersection formulas correctly. Step 18's substitutions $k_X = x_X/(h_C-y_X)$ and $x_C-x_I = r(r-h_C)/x_I$ are verified identities from the circle definitions. The proof provides a concrete, checkable computational chain that only lacks the final parameter plug-in.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Both proofs correctly derive intermediate algebraic conditions but stop short of full verification. Proof B is mathematically stronger because it constructs a complete, explicit coordinate framework and correctly derives all intermediate geometric quantities (circle equations, intersection points $X,Y$, slopes $k_X,k_Y$, and coordinates of $P$). These steps are independently verified and form a solid computational pathway to the conclusion. Proof A's vector approach derives a parallelism condition but provides no explicit formulas for $X, Y,$ or $P$, and asserts the condition's satisfaction without showing how the hypothesis $AU=AE, AV=AF$ enters the calculation. B's defect is local (omitting the final substitution of $m_U, m_V$), while A's is global (lacking the mechanism to verify the condition). B's explicit intermediate results constitute substantially more verified progress and a clearer route to completion.
