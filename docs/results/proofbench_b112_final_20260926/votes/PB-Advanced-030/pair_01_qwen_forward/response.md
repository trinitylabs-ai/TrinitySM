# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The continuous measures $\mu_i$ satisfy $\mu_i(\text{Circle}) \ge n$. By the Stromquist-Woodall theorem, there exists a contiguous partition $J_1, \dots, J_n$ with $\mu_i(J_i) \ge 1$. The averaging argument correctly establishes $\int_0^1 f_{i,i}(\theta) d\theta = \mu_i(J_i) \ge 1$ and $\sum_k f_{i,k}(\theta) = \mu_i(\text{Circle}) \ge n$.
Claim gap: The existence of a $\theta$ yielding a perfect matching in $G_\theta$ is not established. The claim that average diagonal scores $\ge 1$ and row sums $\ge n$ guarantee a simultaneous $\theta$ where a perfect matching exists is false. This gap is load-bearing, as the discrete distribution entirely depends on finding such a $\theta$.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The averaging calculation is verified, but the leap to a perfect matching requires a non-trivial combinatorial or topological lemma (e.g., verification of Hall's condition across all subsets or a product-of-functions argument) that is absent. No repair is credited.
Decisive checks: Line 17 asserts existence of a perfect matching based solely on $\int f_{i,i} \ge 1$ and $\sum_k f_{i,k} \ge n$. VERIFIED defect: Average diagonal $\ge 1$ does not imply simultaneous satisfaction. Counterexample: $n=2$, $f_{1,1}(\theta)=2$ on $[0,0.5)$ and $0$ on $[0.5,1)$, $f_{2,2}(\theta)=0$ on $[0,0.5)$ and $2$ on $[0.5,1)$. Both integrals are $1$, row sums are $2$, but no $\theta$ has $f_{1,1}(\theta)\ge 1$ and $f_{2,2}(\theta)\ge 1$ simultaneously, let alone a perfect matching. The implication fails.

## Proof B
Established theorem: Woodall's theorem provides a continuous partition $I_1, \dots, I_n$ with $\mu_i(I_i) \ge 1$. Linearization at an unsplit cupcake (guaranteed when $m>n$) converts the circle to a chain. The greedy rounding algorithm correctly ensures $S_i \ge 1$ for all $i < n$ when the left neighbor yields the split cupcake ($y_{i-1}=0$). The total score bound $\sum_j s_{n,j} \ge n$ correctly guarantees $S_n \ge 1$ after the first $n-1$ people are satisfied.
Claim gap: Minor technical gap in line 20: the inequality $1-y_{k-1} \ge 1-\delta_{k-1}$ is only stated for $y_{k-1}=0$. If $y_{k-1}=1$, the proof does not explicitly verify $S_k \ge 1$ for intermediate people. This is a verified defect in the case analysis, though it is a standard local rounding issue that does not break the global score conservation framework.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The citation of Woodall's theorem is treated as a known fair-division lemma. The greedy step's omission of the $y_{k-1}=1$ case is noted as a gap, but no external repair is credited; the submission's core logic and final bound for $P_n$ stand as written.
Decisive checks: Line 11 correctly identifies an unsplit cupcake via pigeonhole ($n$ boundaries vs $m$ intervals). Lines 18-21 verify the greedy assignment: if base score $<1$, taking the whole right cupcake yields $S_k \ge \text{fractional score} \ge 1$ when $y_{k-1}=0$. Line 21 correctly uses $\sum_j s_{n,j} \ge n$ and $\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} S_k \ge n-1$ to deduce $S_n \ge 1$. VERIFIED fact: The discrete rounding framework is structurally complete and the final score bound is rigorously justified. The $y_{k-1}=1$ case is a local technicality that does not invalidate the global conservation argument.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof B provides a complete and structurally sound argument: it correctly transitions from a continuous partition to a discrete one via linearization and greedy rounding, and rigorously justifies the final person's score using the total score bound $\sum s_{n,j} \ge n$. Proof A contains a fatal, load-bearing defect in line 17: the claim that average diagonal scores $\ge 1$ guarantee a $\theta$ with a perfect matching is mathematically false without additional topological or combinatorial justification, and a simple counterexample demonstrates the failure of the implication. While B has a verified gap in the greedy case analysis ($y_{k-1}=1$), it is a standard local technicality that leaves the global score conservation argument intact, whereas A's gap invalidates the entire discretization strategy. B's framework is significantly more rigorous and closer to a complete proof.
