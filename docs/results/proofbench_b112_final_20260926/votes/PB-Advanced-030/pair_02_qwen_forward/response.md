# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The continuous relaxation correctly yields a partition of the circle into $n$ contiguous intervals $J_1, \dots, J_n$ satisfying $\mu_i(J_i) \ge 1$ for all $i$ (Lines 1-5). The shifting construction and averaging identity $\int_0^1 f_{i,k}(\theta) d\theta = \mu_i(J_k)$ are correctly derived (Lines 7-11).
Claim gap: Line 17 asserts that the conditions $\int_0^1 f_{i,i}(\theta) d\theta \ge 1$ and $\sum_{k=1}^n f_{i,k}(\theta) \ge n$ guarantee the existence of a shift $\theta \in [0, 1)$ for which the bipartite graph $G_\theta$ admits a perfect matching. This implication is false. The averaging conditions control marginal integrals and pointwise sums, but they do not enforce the simultaneous combinatorial structure required for a perfect matching across all $n$ people for any single $\theta$.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The gap is substantive. Bridging it would require a topological fixed-point argument (e.g., Borsuk-Ulam or Lyusternik-Schnirelmann theory) to guarantee a simultaneous matching, which is entirely absent from the submission.
Decisive checks: Lines 15-17 contain a verified defect. For $n=2$, define $f_{1,1}(\theta) = 2$ on $[0, 0.5)$ and $0$ on $[0.5, 1)$, with $f_{1,2}(\theta) \equiv 0$. Define $f_{2,2}(\theta) = 0$ on $[0, 0.5)$ and $2$ on $[0.5, 1)$, with $f_{2,1}(\theta) \equiv 0$. These satisfy $\int f_{i,i} = 1$ and $\sum_k f_{i,k} = 2 \ge 2$ for all $\theta$, meeting all premises in Lines 11 and 15. However, for every $\theta$, either $f_{1,1}(\theta)=0$ or $f_{2,2}(\theta)=0$, and all off-diagonal entries vanish. Thus $G_\theta$ has at most one edge for every $\theta$, making a perfect matching impossible. This counterexample definitively falsifies the central claim in Line 17.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The hypothesis is correctly translated into the combinatorial condition $\nu(\mathcal{F}_i) \ge n$ for each family $\mathcal{F}_i$ of consecutive blocks with score $\ge 1$ (Lines 3-5). The Aharoni-Holzman theorem is correctly applied to extract pairwise disjoint blocks $A_i \in \mathcal{F}_i$ (Lines 7-9). The assignment of $A_i$ to $P_i$ and arbitrary distribution of leftovers yields a valid partition where each person receives score $\ge 1$ (Lines 11-16).
Claim gap: NONE. The argument is complete and logically sound.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The cited theorem is a standard result in combinatorial fair division. The transition from continuous arcs to discrete consecutive blocks is trivially valid since consecutive cupcake sets correspond exactly to integer-aligned arcs, and the theorem's conclusion preserves disjointness and the score threshold.
Decisive checks: Lines 5-9 correctly identify the matching number condition and invoke the appropriate system-of-distinct-representatives theorem for circular arc families. Line 14 correctly notes that non-negative scores allow arbitrary distribution of leftovers without violating the $\ge 1$ threshold. The quantifier order is preserved: the theorem guarantees simultaneous disjoint representatives for all $i$, directly satisfying the problem's requirement for a single global distribution.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof A contains a verified fatal defect at Line 17: the averaging argument does not imply the existence of a shift $\theta$ yielding a perfect matching, as demonstrated by a counterexample where diagonal edges are mutually exclusive across $\theta$ while satisfying all integral and sum constraints. Proof B correctly identifies the combinatorial structure, applies a precise and established theorem that directly guarantees disjoint representatives, and cleanly handles the discrete distribution. B's argument is complete and rigorously justified, while A's central discretization step is mathematically unsupported.
