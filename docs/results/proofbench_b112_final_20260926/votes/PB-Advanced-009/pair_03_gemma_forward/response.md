Established theorem: $AB = AQ$ for an acute-angled triangle $ABC$.
Claim gap: The simplification of the coordinate expression $D_L x_N + E_L y_N = 2bc$ (Step 25) is not shown.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE.
Decisive checks: 
- Verified the coordinates of $I$ and $J$ (Step 10) by projecting $B(-b, 0)$ and $C(c, 0)$ onto the line $AG: 2ax + (c-b)y = a(c-b)$.
- Verified the $y$-intercept $S$ of line $EF$ (Step 16) as $y_S = \frac{2abc}{a^2+bc}$.
- Verified the coordinate of $R$ (Step 17) using the power of point $D$ with respect to $\odot(AHG)$.
- Verified the formula for $D_L x_N + E_L y_N$ (Step 24) as the power of point $D$ with respect to $\odot(DKL)$ along the line $DN$.

## Proof B
Established theorem: $AB = AQ$ for an acute-angled triangle $ABC$.
Claim gap: The simplification of the coordinate expression $v_{1P} x_N + v_{2P} y_N = -2bc$ (Step 15) is not shown.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE.
Decisive checks:
- Verified the coordinates of $I$ and $J$ (Step 3) and the $y$-intercept $S$ (Step 9).
- Verified the coordinate of $R$ (Step 9) using the power of point $D$ with respect to $\odot(AHG)$.
- Verified the final implication $DQ = b \implies AB = AQ$ (Step 16-18).

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Both proofs use an identical coordinate geometry approach and both omit the most computationally intensive part of the problem (the simplification of the expression for $DP \cdot DN$). However, Proof A is more detailed, providing explicit coordinates for the points $I$ and $J$ and, most importantly, deriving the general formula for the power of point $D$ with respect to $\odot(DKL)$ (Step 24), whereas Proof B simply states the result of the computation.
