## Proof A
Established theorem: For all positive integers $x, y$ satisfying $2x^2 + x = 3y^2 + y$, the quantity $2x + 2y + 1$ equals $k_m^2$ for some integer $m \ge 1$, where $k_m$ is generated by the recurrence $k_{n+1} = 5k_n + 4z_n$, hence is a perfect square.
Claim gap: NONE. The algebraic identity is fully verified. The closed-form expressions for $k_n$ and $z_n$ are stated without derivation but are standard and easily verified by substitution into the recurrence and initial conditions.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. Verified the closed forms by checking $n=0,1$ against the recurrence. Confirmed that $m \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ correctly enforces $x,y \ge 1$. All modular arithmetic and Pell-equation theory applications are standard and correctly applied.
Decisive checks: 
- Lines 4-11: Transformation to $3k^2 - 2z^2 = 1$ is arithmetically verified.
- Lines 14-21: Recurrence correctly follows from multiplication by $5+2\sqrt{6}$. Modulo analysis correctly restricts valid indices to even $n=2m$.
- Lines 26-35: Closed forms verified. Algebraic expansion of $3k_{2m} + 2z_{2m}$ and $k_m^2$ correctly yields identical expressions $\frac{\alpha^{2m+1} + \beta^{2m+1} + 2}{12}$, establishing $S = k_m^2$.

## Proof B
Established theorem: For integers $x, y$ satisfying the equation, $2x + 2y + 1 = k_m^2$. The proof covers $m \ge 0$, which includes the trivial $(0,0)$ case outside the problem's positive integer constraint, but the identity holds for all valid inputs.
Claim gap: NONE for the algebraic identity. Minor domain imprecision ($m \ge 0$ vs $m \ge 1$) does not affect validity for positive integers.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. Verified closed forms identically to Proof A. The Lagrange bound calculation (Lines 14-16) is correct and rigorously isolates the fundamental solution class, though not strictly necessary. Notation $\beta, \bar{\beta}$ is handled consistently.
Decisive checks:

- Lines 14-16: Bound $0 \le Y_0 \le 1$ correctly isolates $(3,1)$ as the unique fundamental solution for $X^2 - 6Y^2 = 3$.

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Both proofs are mathematically correct, use identical core strategies (Pell-like transformation, recurrence, closed-form verification), and successfully establish the required identity. Proof A is preferred for its precise handling of the domain: it correctly restricts $m \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ to satisfy the problem's condition that $x$ and $y$ are positive integers, whereas Proof B allows $m=0$ (yielding $x=y=0$). Additionally, Proof A's notation ($\alpha, \beta$) is standard for Pell equations, making the algebraic flow slightly clearer. The minor domain imprecision in B does not invalidate the proof but gives A a slight edge in rigor and alignment with the problem statement.
