# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: Under the condition that segment $EF$ is tangent to the incircle, the inradius satisfies $r = 2R \cos A$. Consequently, the intersection $K$ of line $IO$ and side $BC$ coincides with the point of tangency $D_A$ of the $A$-excircle with $BC$.
Claim gap: The proof relies on the unverified claim that the point of tangency $Y$ of the $A$-mixtilinear excircle with the circumcircle is the center of a homothety mapping the $A$-excircle to the circumcircle, and specifically that $Y, D_A, M_{top}$ are collinear. While this property is consistent with advanced geometry theorems, it is stated without justification. The final step asserting the collinearity of $X, Y, K$ based on $X=Y$ or $X=M_{top}$ is also heuristic and lacks rigorous verification of the intersection points.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The derivation of the condition $r = 2R \cos A$ from the tangency of $EF$ to the incircle is geometrically flawed (treating distance between non-parallel lines as constant), but the resulting algebraic condition is correct. The coordinate geometry for $K$ is correct. The properties of $Y$ are assumed.
Decisive checks: The condition $r = 2R \cos A$ is verified to be the correct necessary and sufficient condition for $EF$ to be tangent to the incircle. The identification $K = D_A$ is verified. The claim about $Y$ is a known property (the touch point of the mixtilinear excircle lies on the line connecting the excircle touch point on $BC$ and the midpoint of the arc $BAC$), though not proven in the text.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The condition for $EF$ to be tangent to the incircle is derived correctly as $r = 2R \cos A$. The coordinates of $K$ are correctly identified as $(-x_D, 0)$, which corresponds to the $A$-excircle touch point $D_A$.
Claim gap: The proof contains a fatal geometric error in the definition of point $Y$. It claims that $I$ is the midpoint of the segment $Y M_A$, where $M_A$ is the midpoint of the arc $BC$ not containing $A$. Since $I$ and $M_A$ both lie on the angle bisector of $A$, this implies $Y$ must also lie on the angle bisector. The only points on the circumcircle lying on the angle bisector are $A$ and $M_A$. However, $Y$ is the touch point of the mixtilinear excircle, which is distinct from $A$ and $M_A$ (unless the triangle is isosceles, which is excluded). Thus, $Y$ cannot be defined as $2I - M_A$, and the subsequent coordinate calculations for $Y$ and the collinearity check are based on a false premise.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The derivation of the condition $r = 2R \cos A$ is rigorous. The coordinate setup for $K$ is correct. The definition of $Y$ is fundamentally incorrect.
Decisive checks: The claim "$I$ is the midpoint of $Y M_A$" is demonstrated to be false because it forces $Y$ to lie on the angle bisector, which contradicts the geometric definition of $Y$ for a non-isosceles triangle.

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Proof A correctly identifies the key geometric condition ($r = 2R \cos A$) and the location of $K$ ($K=D_A$). Although its derivation of the condition is geometrically loose, it invokes a valid (though unproven) property regarding the collinearity of $Y, D_A$, and the arc midpoint to conclude the proof. Proof B, while rigorous in deriving the condition, fails catastrophically in the second half by defining $Y$ incorrectly (claiming $I$ is the midpoint of $Y M_A$), which leads to a geometrically impossible configuration for $Y$ in a non-isosceles triangle. Therefore, Proof A is mathematically superior as it relies on correct geometric facts, whereas Proof B relies on a false lemma.
