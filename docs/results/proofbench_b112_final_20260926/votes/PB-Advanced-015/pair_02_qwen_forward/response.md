# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The submission derives the condition $\cos A = \cos B + \cos C$ (equivalent to $r = R(2\cos A - 1)$) for the tangency of $EF$ to the incircle. It identifies $X$ and $Y$ as the tangency points of the $A$-mixtilinear incircle and excircle with the circumcircle, respectively, and asserts they lie on lines $AT$ and $AS$ (where $T, S$ are centers of similitude of incircle/circumcircle).
Claim gap: The derivation of the tangency condition relies on an invalid distance formula ($d(I, EF) = |h_a \cos A - AI \cos(\angle IAH_0)|$) which assumes $EF \perp AH_0$ or $I \in AH_0$, neither of which is true for a general non-isosceles triangle. Furthermore, the final collinearity argument (Step 25) claims $K$ is the center of homothety mapping the mixtilinear incircle to the mixtilinear excircle. This is geometrically impossible because the centers of these circles lie on the angle bisector $AI$, so their homothety center must lie on $AI$, but $K$ (intersection of $IO$ and $BC$) does not generally lie on $AI$.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The geometric premises in the distance calculation and the homothety center identification are fundamentally flawed.
Decisive checks: Step 4's distance formula is invalid for non-parallel/non-perpendicular configurations. Step 25's claim that $K$ is a homothety center for circles centered on $AI$ is false as $K \notin AI$.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The submission derives the condition $r = 2R \cos A$ for the tangency of $EF$ to the incircle. It establishes that under this condition, $K$ coincides with $D_A$ (the touch point of the $A$-excircle on $BC$). It attempts to prove collinearity by relating $Y$ to the $A$-excircle via homothety.
Claim gap: The derivation of the condition $r = 2R \cos A$ relies on the false premise that the distance between the non-parallel lines $EF$ and $BC$ is constant and equal to $2r$ (Step 1). While the resulting condition happens to match the correct limit for isosceles triangles (where $EF \parallel BC$), the derivation is invalid for the general non-isosceles case. Additionally, Step 8 incorrectly identifies $Y$ (tangency of mixtilinear excircle) as the center of homothety mapping the $A$-excircle to the circumcircle; these are distinct circles with no such direct homothety centered at $Y$.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The distance argument in Step 1 is geometrically unsound for non-parallel lines. The identification of $Y$ in Step 8 is conceptually confused.
Decisive checks: Step 1 assumes $EF \parallel BC$ to define distance, which contradicts the "not isosceles" hypothesis. Step 8's homothety claim is unjustified and likely false.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Both proofs contain fatal flaws in their derivations and geometric justifications. Proof A's condition ($\cos A = \cos B + \cos C$) is demonstrably incorrect for the isosceles limit case (where the geometry is simplest and verifiable), yielding a value for $\sin(A/2)$ that does not satisfy the tangency requirement. Proof B's condition ($r = 2R \cos A$), while derived via a flawed parallel-line assumption, yields the correct value for the isosceles limit case. Although Proof B's subsequent geometric arguments regarding $Y$ and homothety are also flawed, its initial condition is mathematically consistent with the boundary case, whereas Proof A's is not. Thus, Proof B is preferred as it contains a "correct" intermediate result (the condition) despite the faulty derivation, whereas Proof A's core condition is wrong.
