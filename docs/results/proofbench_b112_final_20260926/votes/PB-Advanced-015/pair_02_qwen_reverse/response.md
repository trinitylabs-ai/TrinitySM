# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The proof establishes that if the condition $r = 2R \cos A$ holds, then $K$ coincides with the $A$-excircle touch point $D_A$, and under specific (and largely unjustified) homothety claims, $X, Y, K$ are collinear.
Claim gap: The derivation of the condition $r = 2R \cos A$ from the tangency of $EF$ to the incircle is fundamentally flawed. It assumes $EF \parallel BC$ or that the distance between the non-parallel lines $EF$ and $BC$ is constant and equal to $2r$, which is geometrically false for non-isosceles triangles. Furthermore, the geometric properties cited for point $Y$ (center of homothety between $A$-excircle and circumcircle) are incorrect or unsubstantiated.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The audit identified that the condition $r = 2R \cos A$ is actually the condition for $I, O, D_A$ to be collinear ($K=D_A$), which is a significant geometric insight, but the link to the problem's premise ($EF$ tangent to incircle) is broken. The rest of the proof relies on unverified properties of mixtilinear excircles.
Decisive checks: Step 1 claims distance between $EF$ and $BC$ is $2r$. Since $EF$ is antiparallel to $BC$, they are not parallel (unless isosceles), so the distance is not constant. The incircle cannot be tangent to two intersecting lines with diameter equal to the distance between them measured along an arbitrary transversal. This invalidates the premise derivation.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The proof derives the condition $\cos A = \cos B + \cos C$ (equivalent to $r = R(2 \cos A - 1)$) from the tangency of $EF$ to the incircle. It then uses homothety arguments to claim $X, Y, K$ are collinear.
Claim gap: The derivation of the condition $\cos A = \cos B + \cos C$ relies on an incorrect distance formula $d(I, EF) = |h_a \cos A - AI \cos(\angle IAH_0)|$, which assumes the normal to $EF$ is parallel to the altitude $AH_0$ (i.e., $EF \parallel BC$), which is false. Additionally, the identification of $X$ as the tangency point of the $A$-mixtilinear *incircle* is likely incorrect (standard results place $X$ differently or relate it to other points), and the final collinearity argument relies on a "known property" that is not standard and appears fabricated for the specific condition derived.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The audit found that the condition $\cos A = \cos B + \cos C$ is geometrically possible for acute triangles, unlike the contradiction found in a rigorous trilinear distance check (which suggested no solution, implying potential errors in the rigorous check or the problem statement's existence). However, Proof B's derivation is still geometrically unjustified.
Decisive checks: Step 4 uses a projection formula along the altitude to find the distance to $EF$. Since $EF$ is not perpendicular to the altitude, this projection does not yield the perpendicular distance. This invalidates the condition derivation.

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Both proofs fail to correctly derive the condition for $EF$ being tangent to the incircle due to the false assumption that $EF$ is parallel to $BC$ (or that distances can be measured along the altitude). However, Proof A's derived condition ($r = 2R \cos A$) leads to the geometrically significant and verifiable result that $K$ coincides with the $A$-excircle touch point $D_A$ ($K=D_A$). This is a strong structural insight into the configuration. Proof B's derived condition ($\cos A = \cos B + \cos C$) does not yield such a clean geometric identification for $K$, and its subsequent homothety arguments are more opaque and rely on less standard "known properties." Proof A's error is in the premise derivation, but the subsequent geometric consequences of its (incorrectly derived) condition are more coherent and verifiable than Proof B's.
