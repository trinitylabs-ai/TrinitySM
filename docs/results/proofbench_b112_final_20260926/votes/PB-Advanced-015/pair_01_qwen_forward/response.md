# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: Derives the condition $r = R(2\cos A - 1)$ for $EF$ to be tangent to the incircle, identifies $X$ and $Y$ as the tangency points of the $A$-mixtilinear incircle and excircle with the circumcircle, and asserts collinearity of $X, Y, K$ via homothety centers.
Claim gap: The derived condition is incorrect for the stated configuration. The homothety argument in lines 14–25 is geometrically invalid: the external center of homothety between the two mixtilinear circles must lie on the angle bisector $AI$ (since both circles are tangent to rays $AB$ and $AC$), but $K$ lies on $IO \cap BC$. In a non-isosceles triangle, $AI$ and $IO$ are distinct lines, so $K$ cannot be that center. The coordinate setup in lines 18–23 is inconsistent and does not substantiate the collinearity claim.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The defects are structural and load-bearing; no routine repair bridges the gap between the false homothety claim and the required collinearity. The condition derivation skips critical trigonometric steps and arrives at a known result for a different configuration ($EF$ tangent to the $A$-excircle).
Decisive checks: Line 6 claims the tangency condition simplifies to $\cos A = \cos B + \cos C$. Direct projection of $I$ onto the line perpendicular to $AO$ (the direction normal to $EF$) yields $|h_a \cos A - AI \cos((B-C)/2)| = r$, which correctly reduces to $r = 2R \cos A$, not $R(2\cos A - 1)$. Line 25 asserts $K$ is the external center of homothety mapping the mixtilinear incircle to the excircle. Since both mixtilinear centers lie on $AI$, their homothety centers must lie on $AI$. $K \in IO \cap BC$ is provably distinct from $AI$ under the non-isosceles hypothesis. This is a demonstrated geometric defect that invalidates the central implication.

## Proof B
Established theorem: Correctly derives the necessary and sufficient condition $s\cos A = s-a \iff r = 2R\cos A$ for $EF$ to be tangent to the incircle. Establishes a coordinate framework with $M$ (midpoint of $BC$) as origin, computes coordinates for $K$, $Y$, and $X$, and formulates the exact algebraic condition for collinearity of $X, Y, K$.
Claim gap: The final algebraic simplification in lines 48–50 is asserted rather than fully expanded ("this identity holds given the properties..."). Additionally, line 28 uses the lemma that $I$ is the midpoint of $YM_A$ without proof.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: Accepted the lemma in line 28 as standard mixtilinear geometry knowledge (the tangency point of the $A$-mixtilinear excircle, the incenter, and the arc midpoint $M_A$ are collinear with $I$ as the midpoint). Verified the trigonometric derivation in lines 4–20 step-by-step; the case analysis correctly handles the absolute value, respects the acute domain ($\cos A > 0$), and yields $r = 2R\cos A$. The coordinate setup and collinearity determinant formulation in lines 23–47 are algebraically sound and directly verifiable. The gap in the final expansion is a local omission of routine polynomial simplification, not a missing central argument.
Decisive checks: Lines 4–14 correctly compute the distance from $I$ to $EF$ along the direction perpendicular to $AO$, handle the absolute value cases properly, and reduce to $s\cos A = s-a$. Lines 23–27 correctly place $O=(0, r/2)$ and $I=(x_D, r)$ under the derived condition, yielding $K=(-x_D, 0)$ via intersection. Lines 31–39 correctly apply the power-of-a-point/root-product method to find $X$'s coordinates. The collinearity condition in line 45 is the exact determinant test $\vec{KY} \parallel \vec{KX}$. All steps are verified and internally consistent under the problem's quantifiers and domain constraints.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof B correctly derives the tangency condition ($r = 2R\cos A$) through rigorous case analysis and trigonometric identities, whereas Proof A cites an incorrect condition that applies to a different configuration. More critically, Proof A's central collinearity argument relies on a geometrically false claim that $K$ is the external homothety center of the mixtilinear circles (line 25), which is impossible since that center must lie on $AI$, not $IO$, violating the non-isosceles hypothesis. Proof B avoids this by constructing a complete coordinate framework, explicitly computing coordinates for $K, Y, X$, and setting up the exact algebraic collinearity condition. While B omits the final polynomial simplification steps, the path is fully specified and verifiable, making it mathematically complete in structure. A's proof contains a load-bearing geometric contradiction and an incorrect premise, leaving it fundamentally unsound.
