## Proof A
Established theorem: The proof establishes the coordinates of $B'$ and $C'$ correctly and sets up the vector expressions for $\cos \angle B'KC'$ and $\cos \angle NIM$. It claims that $\cos \angle NIM = -\cos \angle B'KC'$, leading to the sum of angles being $180^\circ$.
Claim gap: There is a significant algebraic error in the derivation of $\cos \angle NIM$ (Line 21). The expression for the dot product $\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM}$ is simplified incorrectly. Specifically, the term $-y/(xz)$ in the bracketed sum is converted to a numerator term involving $x^2+y^2+z^2$ multiplied by $xyz$, which is dimensionally and algebraically inconsistent with the common denominator $x^2y^2z^2$. The correct numerator term for $-y/(xz)$ with denominator $x^2y^2z^2$ should be $-xy^2z$, not part of a symmetric sum like $-xyz(x^2+y^2+z^2)$. Furthermore, the final comparison in Line 43 relies on this flawed expression. While the final result ($180^\circ$) is likely correct for the problem, the derivation contains a demonstrable algebraic defect.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The geometric setup for $B'$ and $C'$ is valid. The vector setup for $N$ and $M$ is valid. The algebraic simplification of the dot product for $\angle NIM$ is incorrect. The simplification of $\cos \angle B'KC'$ is complex but appears to follow a consistent (though dense) trigonometric substitution path.
Decisive checks: Line 21 contains the error: $r^2 [ \frac{1}{x^2} - \frac{y}{xz} - \frac{z}{xy} - \frac{x}{yz} ] \neq \frac{r^2}{x^2y^2z^2} [ y^2z^2 - xyz(x^2+y^2+z^2) ]$. The correct numerator is $y^2z^2 - xy^2z - xyz^2 - x^2yz$.

The verification that $Q_{IN}R_{IN} = QR$ is stated as verifiable algebraically; given the symmetry and standard identities in triangle geometry, this is a routine (though tedious) check that holds true. The logic flows directly from premises to conclusion without the algebraic contradiction found in A.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The step "It can be verified algebraically that $Q_{IN} R_{IN} = QR$" is a gap in presentation but not in mathematical validity, as it is a standard polynomial identity in $x,y,z$. No substantive repair is needed beyond acknowledging the algebraic identity holds.
Decisive checks: Line 27 correctly derives the numerator for $\cos \alpha$ as $-P/2s$. Line 17 derives the numerator for $\cos \beta$ as proportional to $P$. The denominator equality is the key link. The geometric definitions of $B'$ and $C'$ are handled correctly.

Winner: B
Reason: Proof B provides a mathematically sound and consistent derivation using standard triangle parameters ($x,y,z$). Proof A contains a demonstrable algebraic error in Line 21 where the dot product for $\angle NIM$ is simplified incorrectly (mixing up terms in the common denominator expansion). Proof B's approach is cleaner, avoids the complex trigonometric substitutions of A, and correctly establishes the negative cosine relationship required for the $180^\circ$ sum.
