# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The proof correctly transforms the inequality into showing $\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{1}{2^k} \tanh(2^{k-1} x_k) \le 0$ subject to $x_1 \le \dots \le x_n$ and $\sum x_k = 0$. It correctly identifies that the positive part $V_{pos}$ is bounded by its unconstrained maximum (which is a valid upper bound for the constrained maximum) and derives the form $\gamma \tanh \alpha$. It correctly identifies that the negative part $V_{neg}$ is maximized at extreme points of the feasible region defined by the ordering constraints.
Claim gap: The proof claims that $h(j, S) = \sum_{k=1}^j \frac{1}{2^k} \tanh(2^{k-1} S/j) \ge \frac{1}{2} \tanh S$ for all $j \ge 1$ and $S > 0$. The justification provided ("sum of concave functions... limits") is heuristic and insufficient to rigorously establish this inequality for all $j$. While numerical checks and asymptotic analysis suggest the inequality is likely true, the gap remains a missing rigorous proof of this specific bound. Additionally, the proof relies on the assumption that the maximum of $V_{neg}$ occurs at the specific symmetric extreme points $P_j$, which requires verification against the ordering constraints $x_1 \le \dots \le x_m \le 0$.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The audit accepts the Lagrange multiplier derivation for the unconstrained bound as valid for an upper bound. The audit notes the potential issue with the ordering constraint for $V_{pos}$ but concludes that using the global maximum is a valid (though loose) upper bound strategy.
Decisive checks: Verified the transformation to tanh sum. Verified the geometric series sum. Verified the concavity of $g_k(x)$ for $x>0$. Verified that the unconstrained maximum provides a valid upper bound for $V_{pos}$. Identified the gap in the justification for $h(j, S) \ge \frac{1}{2} \tanh S$.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The proof correctly transforms the problem and establishes the hierarchy $f_1(z) \ge f_2(z) \ge \dots \ge f_n(z)$ for $z \ge 0$.
Claim gap: The proof relies on the lemma that for an odd function $g$ concave on $[0, \infty)$, $\sum_{k=1}^n g(z_k) \le 0$ whenever $\sum z_k = 0$. This lemma is **false**. A counterexample is $g(z) = \tanh z$ (which is odd and concave on $[0, \infty)$) with $z = (-2, 1, 1)$. Here $\sum z_k = 0$, but $\sum g(z_k) = -\tanh 2 + 2\tanh 1 \approx -0.964 + 1.081 > 0$. The proof's justification that $g(z) \le g'(0)z$ for all $z$ is also false for $z < 0$ (where $\tanh z > z$). Consequently, the conclusion that $S \le 0$ is not supported by the argument.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The defect is fundamental to the core inequality used to bound the sum.
Decisive checks: Verified the hierarchy of $f_k$. Disproved the central lemma $\sum g(z_k) \le 0$ using the counterexample $z=(-2, 1, 1)$ for $g(z)=\tanh z$. Verified that the justification $g(z) \le g'(0)z$ fails for negative $z$.

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Proof A provides a structurally sound approach using optimization techniques (Lagrange multipliers and convexity) to bound the positive and negative parts of the sum. Although it contains a gap in the rigorous justification of a specific inequality ($h(j, S) \ge \frac{1}{2} \tanh S$), the claim itself appears true and the method is valid. In contrast, Proof B relies on a demonstrably false lemma ($\sum g(z_k) \le 0$ for odd concave $g$) and a false inequality ($g(z) \le g'(0)z$ for $z<0$), rendering the entire argument mathematically incorrect. Proof A is significantly stronger as it does not contain fatal logical errors.
