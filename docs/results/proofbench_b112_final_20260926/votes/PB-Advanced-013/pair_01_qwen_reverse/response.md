# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The proof correctly transforms the inequality into $T_n = \sum_{k=1}^n \frac{1}{2^{k+1}} g(a_k^{2^k}) \leq 0$ with $g(x) = \frac{x-1}{x+1}$. It establishes three correct lemmas regarding the properties of $g(x)$ (Lemma 1: $g(u) \leq \frac{1}{2}g(u^2)$ for $u \le 1$; Lemma 2: $g(x)+g(y) \le g(xy)$ for $x,y \le 1$; Lemma 3: $g(x^2) \le 2g(x)$ for $x \ge 1$). It correctly bounds the partial sum $S_{\le m}$ using induction and Lemma 1 & 2. It correctly bounds $S_{> m}$ using Lemma 3 and Jensen's inequality on the concave function $h(y) = g(e^y)$.
Claim gap: The proof fails to establish the final inequality $T_n \le 0$. It derives an upper bound $T_n \le \frac{1}{2^{m+1}} [g(u) + \frac{1}{q} g(u^{-q})]$ where $u = P_m^{2^m} \in (0,1]$ and $q = \frac{2}{n-m} \in (0,2]$. It then claims this bound is non-positive by asserting $g(u^q) \ge q g(u)$. This assertion is false for $q \in (0,1)$ (which occurs when $n-m \ge 3$). Specifically, $h(q) = g(u^q)$ is convex for $u \in (0,1)$, implying $g(u^q) \le q g(u)$, which leads to the bound being non-negative (useless) rather than non-positive. Thus, the proof does not verify the target inequality for cases where $n-m \ge 3$.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The lemmas and bounds are verified as correct, but the final combination step contains a mathematical error in the inequality direction.
Decisive checks: Lemma 1, 2, 3 are verified. The induction for $S_{\le m}$ is verified. The Jensen application for $S_{> m}$ is verified. The final inequality check $g(u^q) \ge q g(u)$ is demonstrated to be false for $q < 1$ via convexity of $g(u^q)$ in $q$.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The proof correctly transforms the problem into maximizing $f_n = \sum \frac{1}{2^k} \tanh(2^{k-1} x_k)$ subject to $\sum x_k = 0$ and ordering. It correctly identifies that $V_{pos}$ (positive $x_k$) is maximized when $x_k$ are proportional to $1/2^{k-1}$ due to concavity. It correctly identifies that $V_{neg}$ (negative $x_k$) is maximized at the vertices of the ordered simplex, which are points $P_j$ where $j$ variables are equal to $-S/j$.
Claim gap: The proof claims that the maximum of $V_{neg}$ is bounded by $-\frac{1}{2} \tanh S$, which corresponds to the case $j=1$. It asserts that $h(j, S) = \sum_{k=1}^j \frac{1}{2^k} \tanh(2^{k-1} S/j) \ge \frac{1}{2} \tanh S$ for all $j$. This claim is false for $j \ge 3$ and small $S$. For example, for $j=3$ and small $S$, $h(3, S) \approx \frac{11}{24} S < \frac{1}{2} S = h(1, S)$. Consequently, $V_{neg}$ can be larger (less negative) than $-\frac{1}{2} \tanh S$, invalidating the bound used to prove $f_n \le 0$.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The optimization setup is correct, but the evaluation of the objective function at the vertices contains a false inequality.
Decisive checks: The concavity/convexity arguments for $V_{pos}$ and $V_{neg}$ are verified. The identification of vertices $P_j$ is verified. The claim $h(j, S) \ge h(1, S)$ is demonstrated to be false via Taylor expansion for small $S$ and $j=3$.

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Proof A is preferred because its intermediate steps (Lemmas 1-3, induction, Jensen's inequality) are mathematically verified and correct. The failure occurs only in the final inequality check, where the derived upper bound is shown to be non-negative (insufficient) rather than non-positive, due to a sign error in analyzing the convexity of $g(u^q)$. In contrast, Proof B contains a false intermediate claim regarding the bound of the negative part ($V_{neg} \le -\frac{1}{2} \tanh S$), which is demonstrably false for $j \ge 3$. Proof A provides a valid (though insufficient) upper bound derived from correct principles, whereas Proof B relies on an incorrect inequality to establish its bound.
