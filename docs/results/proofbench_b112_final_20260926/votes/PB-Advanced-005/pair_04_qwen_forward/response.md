## Proof A
Established theorem: The condition $\angle BAC = \alpha$ restricts the parameters $(b,c)$ of points $B$ and $C$ to a linear relation $c = mb + n$. The tangent of $\angle BDC$ is expressed as a rational function $N(b)/D_{en}(b)$ where both numerator and denominator are quadratic in $b$. If $\tan \angle BDC$ is constant for all valid $b$, the polynomials must be proportional, which forces the constant to be $\tan \alpha$ (from the ratio of $b^2$ coefficients). This proportionality further forces $D$ to lie on ray $\overrightarrow{YA}$ and satisfy $x^2+y^2 = \frac{x_A^2+y_A^2}{y_A}y$, yielding only $D=A$ or $D=Y$.
Claim gap: The derivation proves that $\theta = \alpha$ implies $D=A$ or $D=Y$, directly contradicting the requirement $D \neq A$ inside the angle. The final paragraph abruptly asserts that $D$ is the reflection of $A$ across the angle bisector and $\theta = \alpha$, without any derivation, algebraic verification, or geometric justification. This claim contradicts the preceding algebraic result and leaves the existence of a valid $D$ completely unproven.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The hand-waved reflection claim cannot be repaired without introducing a new geometric argument (e.g., isogonal conjugacy or inversion) not present in the submission.
Decisive checks: Lines 13-18 correctly show that constant $\tan \angle BDC$ forces $\tan \theta = \tan \alpha$ and $D \in \{A, Y\}$. Line 20 abandons this result and asserts an unjustified construction. The condition $\alpha \neq 60^\circ$ is mentioned but never used mathematically. The proof fails to establish the existence of $D \neq A$.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The condition $\angle BAC = \alpha$ yields a linear constraint $L_A(b,c)=0$. The proof correctly observes that assuming $\theta = \alpha$ forces $D=A$, so $\theta \neq \alpha$ is necessary. For $\theta \neq \alpha$, the condition $\angle BDC = \theta$ yields a quadratic equation $Q_D(b,c)=0$ of the form $Abc+Bb+Cc+E=0$. For $L_A(b,c)=0 \implies Q_D(b,c)=0$, $Q_D$ must factor with $L_A$ as a linear factor (degenerate conic). This imposes algebraic conditions on $D$, leading to a system of equations (22)-(23) defining $D$ as the intersection of a line through the origin and a circle through the origin.
Claim gap: The explicit algebraic derivation from the degeneracy/divisibility condition to equations (22)-(23) is omitted ("As derived in the analysis"). The verification that the intersection point lies strictly inside $\angle XYZ$ and satisfies $D \neq A$ using $\alpha \neq 60^\circ$ is stated but not computed. However, these are computational/verification gaps, not conceptual flaws.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The logical architecture (linear constraint $\to$ necessity of $\theta \neq \alpha$ $\to$ quadratic factorization condition $\to$ locus intersection) is complete and mathematically sound. Routine algebraic expansion would fill the omitted steps.
Decisive checks: Lines 14-16 correctly identify that $\theta=\alpha$ forces $D=A$. Lines 20-21 correctly apply the algebraic principle that a quadratic vanishing on a line must be divisible by that line's equation. The resulting system (22)-(23) correctly describes the locus of $D$ as a line-circle intersection, which generically yields a unique point $D \neq Y$. The invocation of $\alpha \neq 60^\circ$ appropriately addresses the exceptional case where the intersection might coincide with $A$ or exit the angular region.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof B correctly identifies the critical insight that $\theta$ cannot equal $\alpha$, and properly sets up the algebraic condition (quadratic factorization) required for $\angle BDC$ to be constant on the linear locus of $(B,C)$. This leads to a well-defined geometric construction for $D$. Proof A, by contrast, derives that $\theta=\alpha$ forces $D=A$ or $D=Y$, contradicting the problem statement, and then abandons rigor to assert an unjustified reflection claim. Proof B's logical framework is complete and addresses the core difficulty of the problem, while Proof A's central derivation contradicts its conclusion and leaves the main obligation unmet.
