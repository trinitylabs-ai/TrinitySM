# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: For a point $A$ inside an acute angle $\angle XYZ = \alpha$, the point $D=A$ satisfies the condition that $\angle BDC = \alpha$ whenever $\angle BAC = \alpha$.
Claim gap: The proof fails to demonstrate the existence of a point $D \neq A$ satisfying the condition. The claim that the reflection of $A$ across the angle bisector of $\angle XYZ$ is a solution is an unsupported assertion and is mathematically incorrect for a general point $A$.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: None.
Decisive checks: The derivation of the linear relationship $c = mb + n$ (line 4) and the expressions for the numerator $N$ and denominator $D_{en}$ of $\tan \angle BDC$ (lines 8-9) are correct. The conclusion that $D=A$ or $D=Y$ are the only points where $\tan \angle BDC$ is constant (lines 13-18) is verified. The final claim (line 20) is an unsupported assertion.

## Proof B
Established theorem: For a point $A$ inside an acute angle $\angle XYZ = \alpha$, the point $D=A$ satisfies the condition that $\angle BDC = \theta$ whenever $\angle BAC = \alpha$.
Claim gap: The proof fails to demonstrate the existence of a point $D \neq A$ satisfying the condition. The claim that the reflection of $A$ across the angle bisector is a solution is an unsupported assertion and is mathematically incorrect for a general point $A$.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: None.
Decisive checks: The derivation of the linear relationship $b = mu + n$ (line 6) and the expressions for the cross product $N(u)$ and dot product $M(u)$ (lines 13, 17) are correct. The conclusion that $D=A$ is the only solution for the proportionality of $N(u)$ and $M(u)$ (lines 18-25) is verified. The final claim (line 27) is an unsupported assertion.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Both proofs are fundamentally incomplete and rely on the same false claim that the reflection of $A$ across the angle bisector provides a solution $D \neq A$. Both proofs correctly derive that $D=A$ is the only solution resulting from the proportionality of the tangent formula. Proof B is preferred because its technical execution of the first part is more rigorous, utilizing vector cross and dot products to define the angle, whereas Proof A's final justification is nonsensical.
