# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: Correctly identifies that $\angle BAC = \angle XYZ = \alpha$ implies $Y, B, A, C$ are concyclic. Correctly sets up vector relations for the circle center $O$ and the intersections $B, C$ with the rays.
Claim gap: The central construction $D = 2A$ (reflection of $Y$ across $A$) fails to yield a constant $\angle BDC$. The proof does not establish the required implication for any $D$.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: None supplied; the geometric claim is demonstrably false and cannot be repaired without changing the construction.
Decisive checks: Line 21 claims $\angle BDC = \pi - \alpha$ for $D=2A$ based on a "known property". Counterexample: Let $\alpha=45^\circ$, $Y=(0,0)$, rays along $x$-axis and $y=x$, $A=(1, 0.5)$, so $D=(2,1)$. For a circle through $Y,A$ yielding $B=(1,0), C=(0.75,0.75)$, $\cos\angle BDC \approx 0.832 \implies \angle BDC \approx 33.7^\circ$. For another valid circle yielding $B=(2,0), C=(0.25,0.25)$, $\cos\angle BDC \approx 0.394 \implies \angle BDC \approx 66.8^\circ$. The angle varies, falsifying the claim. The proof collapses at Line 21.

## Proof B
Established theorem: Correctly reduces $\angle BAC = \alpha$ to a linear constraint $L_A(b,c)=0$ on the ray parameters. Correctly identifies that $\angle BDC = \theta$ yields a quadratic constraint $Q_D(b,c)=0$ (for $\theta \neq \alpha$). Establishes that the implication holds iff $L_A$ divides $Q_D$, reducing the problem to solving a system for $D$. Shows $\theta=\alpha$ forces $D=A$, so $\theta \neq \alpha$. Derives that the factorization conditions define a line and a circle through the origin, guaranteeing an intersection point $D \neq Y$.
Claim gap: Skips the explicit coefficient matching to derive equations (22)-(23). Does not explicitly verify that the resulting $D$ lies strictly inside the angle or that $\theta \in (0, 2\pi)$, though these follow from the intersection geometry and the $\alpha \neq 60^\circ$ condition.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The algebraic derivation of (22)-(23) from the factorization condition is omitted but is a routine polynomial coefficient comparison. The logical framework is complete and correct; no substantive mathematical ideas are missing.
Decisive checks: Lines 3-11 correctly derive the linear relation (verified by direct expansion of cross/dot products). Lines 14-20 correctly handle the $\theta=\alpha$ case. Lines 20-24 correctly apply the algebraic geometry principle that a line contained in a conic must be a factor, leading to a solvable system for $D$. The intersection of a line and circle through the origin yields a unique non-origin point $D$, satisfying existence. The $\alpha \neq 60^\circ$ condition correctly prevents degeneracy ($D=A$ or boundary cases).

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof A contains a fatal, demonstrably false geometric claim (Line 21) that its constructed point $D=2A$ yields a constant angle $\angle BDC$. A direct numerical counterexample shows $\angle BDC$ varies with the choice of $B,C$, invalidating the entire argument. Proof B employs a sound algebraic strategy: it correctly identifies that the hypothesis $\angle BAC=\alpha$ imposes a linear constraint on the ray parameters, while the conclusion $\angle BDC=\theta$ imposes a quadratic one. The requirement that the linear constraint implies the quadratic one correctly reduces to a factorization condition, which yields a well-defined system for $D$. Although Proof B omits the tedious coefficient matching, its logical structure is rigorous and correctly establishes existence. Proof B's method is mathematically valid where Proof A's is fundamentally flawed.
