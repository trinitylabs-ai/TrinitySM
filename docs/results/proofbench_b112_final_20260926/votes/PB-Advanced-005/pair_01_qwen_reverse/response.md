# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: For fixed acute $\alpha$ and interior point $A=(x_A,y_A)$, the condition $\angle BAC=\alpha$ forces $Y,A,B,C$ to be concyclic, yielding a linear relation $c=mb+n$ between the ray parameters of $B$ and $C$. If $\tan\angle BDC$ is constant for all such configurations, the coefficients of the resulting rational function in $b$ must be proportional. Solving this proportionality system rigorously establishes that $D$ must lie on the ray $\overrightarrow{YA}$ and satisfy $x^2+y^2=\frac{x_A^2+y_A^2}{y_A}y$, which uniquely forces $D=A$ or $D=Y$.
Claim gap: The proof fails to establish the existence of $D\neq A$. The algebraic derivation conclusively proves that constant $\tan\angle BDC$ implies $D=A$ or $D=Y$. The subsequent assertion that $D$ is the reflection of $A$ across the angle bisector and yields constant $\angle BDC=\alpha$ is unsupported, contradicts the derived uniqueness, and introduces a logical non-sequitur ("considering the case where $N$ and $D_{en}$ are proportional to a different constant"). The condition $\alpha\neq 60^\circ$ is mentioned but not utilized to resolve the contradiction.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The algebraic steps (lines 3–18) are verified as correct under the stated coordinate system. The leap in line 20 is a substantive missing argument; no routine justification can repair the contradiction between the derived uniqueness of $D=A$ and the claimed existence of a reflection point.
Decisive checks: Lines 3–18 correctly derive the proportionality conditions for constant $\tan\angle BDC$ and solve them to $D=A$ or $D=Y$. A falsification check using $\alpha=45^\circ$, $A=(2,1)$, and $D=(1,2)$ (reflection) shows $\angle BDC$ varies with the circle parameter, confirming the reflection claim is false as stated. The gap is load-bearing: the entire existence claim rests on an unverified and algebraically contradicted assertion.

## Proof B
Established theorem: Identical to Proof A. Under the swapped coordinate system, the concyclic condition yields $b=mu+n$. Computing $\tan\angle BDC$ via cross and dot products leads to quadratic expressions in $u$. Enforcing constant $\tan\theta$ forces proportionality of coefficients, which algebraically resolves to $D=A$.
Claim gap: Same as Proof A. The proof correctly derives $D=A$ but then abandons the algebraic framework to assert that the reflection of $A$ across the bisector works, citing a "known property" and vague symmetry arguments. No verification is provided, and the claim conflicts with the derived uniqueness.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The algebraic derivation (lines 5–25) is verified. The final paragraph (lines 26–27) contains a substantive gap identical to Proof A's. The appeal to a "known property" does not constitute mathematical justification within the submission.
Decisive checks: Lines 9–25 correctly compute $N(u)$ and $M(u)$ and solve the proportionality system to $D=A$. The transition to the reflection claim in line 27 is unsupported. A counterexample check (same as for A) confirms the reflection does not yield a constant angle, validating the defect.

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Both proofs share the same central structure and identical load-bearing gap: they correctly derive that constant $\tan\angle BDC$ forces $D=A$ (or $D=Y$), then unjustifiably assert that the reflection of $A$ across the angle bisector satisfies the condition. Neither proof verifies this claim, and direct calculation shows it is false as stated. However, Proof A is mathematically stronger in its presentation: it explicitly tracks the proportionality of polynomial coefficients, clearly isolates the conditions on $D$, and acknowledges the tension with the problem's $D\neq A$ requirement before attempting a resolution. Proof B skips intermediate algebraic simplifications and relies on an unverified "known property" and vague symmetry language, which provides less mathematical substance to audit or repair. Given that both fail to complete the proof, A's more rigorous algebraic exposition up to the gap constitutes a concrete advantage in mathematical transparency and traceability.
