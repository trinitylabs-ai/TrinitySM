# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: James can guarantee reaching the last row with at most $3000$ penalties ($n \le 3001$) via sequential column testing. The lower bound correctly establishes the confinement lemma: in any unhit row, James must remain in columns belonging to $C_{known}$ to avoid unknown obstacles.
Claim gap: The adversary strategy to maintain a non-contiguous $C_{known}$ and block all horizontal switches in $R_{known}$ is asserted without explicit construction or verification of consistency with James's adaptive path choices. The claim that Peter can always place $c_p$ between any $c, c' \in C_{known}$ to block switches lacks a step-by-step justification showing how this is maintained across multiple penalties.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The confinement lemma and upper bound are verified as written. No external lemmas or repairs were supplied.
Decisive checks: Line 12 correctly establishes that for $r \in R_{unc}$, only $c \in C_{known}$ are guaranteed safe, since the actual obstacle column $c_r$ must lie in $C_{unc}$ (all selected columns are distinct). Line 20's claim that Peter can force non-contiguous $C_{known}$ and block switches is an UNRESOLVED check; it assumes Peter can adaptively block all switch attempts without demonstrating a valid configuration consistent with James's strategy. The upper bound derivation (line 4) is VERIFIED: testing $m$ columns yields at most $m-1$ penalties.

## Proof B
Established theorem: James can guarantee success with at most $3000$ penalties ($n \le 3001$). The lower bound rigorously proves that for any $m < 3000$ penalties, James cannot guarantee a safe path to the last row. The $m=1$ bottleneck analysis concretely demonstrates that James cannot cross a hit row without either hitting the known obstacle or stepping into an unguaranteed column in an unhit row.
Claim gap: NONE supported by checks. The informal phrasing in line 18 ("declaring the first hit cell... to be a penalty") is a minor presentation defect but does not undermine the mathematical core, as the $m=1$ bottleneck analysis stands independently and rigorously.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The "guaranteed safe" definition and $m=1$ case are verified as written. No external lemmas or repairs were supplied.
Decisive checks: Line 14 correctly identifies that in unhit rows, only $C_{hit}$ columns are guaranteed safe. Line 21's $m=1$ analysis is VERIFIED: with $R_{hit}=\{r_1\}$ and $C_{hit}=\{c_1\}$, any path from above $r_1$ to below $r_1$ must cross row $r_1$. Crossing in column $c_1$ hits the obstacle. Crossing via horizontal move requires leaving $C_{hit}$ in an unhit row, which risks an unknown obstacle. This concretely proves the obstruction mechanism without auxiliary assumptions. The generalization to $m < k$ follows directly from the same geometric trap.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Both proofs correctly establish $n=3001$ and identify the key confinement property in unhit rows. Proof B is stronger because its lower bound justification is concrete and self-contained. B's explicit analysis of the $m=1$ bottleneck (line 21) rigorously verifies why James cannot bypass obstacles without incurring penalties, relying only on the geometry of a single hit row and safety constraints. Proof A correctly identifies the same constraints but asserts the adversary's blocking strategy without construction or verification, leaving a load-bearing gap in the lower bound justification. B's argument requires fewer auxiliary assumptions and provides a clearer, fully justified obstruction mechanism, making it mathematically more complete.
