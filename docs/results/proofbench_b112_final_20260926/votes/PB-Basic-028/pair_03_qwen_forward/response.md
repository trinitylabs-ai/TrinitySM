# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: For an acute $\triangle ABC$, the radius $r_W$ of a circle tangent to $AB, AC$ and externally tangent to the Euler circle satisfies a quadratic equation derived from $O_W O_E = r_W + R/2$. Direct substitution verifies that $r_W = \frac{r \cos A}{1+\cos A}$ is a root, which implies $AX = \frac{AI'}{2\cos(A/2)}$, establishing that $AXI'Y$ is a rhombus.
Claim gap: Fails to establish that $\angle A$ must be acute for the configuration to exist. The derivation implicitly assumes $\cos A > 0$ and standard vector orientations for $H$ and $O$ without justification. Additionally, the claim that the smaller root corresponds to "$(W)$ closer to $A$" is stated without geometric proof, leaving a gap in the selection criterion.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: I supplied the assumption $\angle A < 90^\circ$ to validate vector directions, sign conventions for $\cos A$, and the positivity of $r_W$. I accepted the standard geometric intuition that the smaller root corresponds to the circle closer to the vertex, which is routine for circles tangent to two intersecting lines and a third circle.
Decisive checks: Lines 6–9 correctly compute $AO_E^2$ and the projection of $\vec{AO_E}$ onto the angle bisector using standard triangle center vector identities. Lines 16–24 perform a direct substitution of the candidate $r_W$ into the quadratic; the trigonometric simplifications (using $\cos(B-C) = 2\Delta^2-1$ and $\cos A = 1-2s^2$) correctly yield zero, confirming the root. The algebraic verification is a verified fact. The omission of the obtuse case analysis is a demonstrated defect regarding quantifier/domain scope.

## Proof B
Established theorem: Derives the quadratic equation for $x = AX$ and explicitly identifies both roots $x_0$ and $x_1$. Proves that the configuration condition "$(W)$ closer to $A$ than $(E)$" forces $\angle A$ to be acute. For acute $A$, selects the smaller root $x_0$, verifies it matches the required length for $AXI'Y$ to be a rhombus, and concludes the proof.
Claim gap: Does not rigorously prove that the smaller root corresponds to the circle closer to $A$, though this is a standard property of this tangency configuration. The projection derivation in line 13 is compressed and relies on unexpanded dot product identities, though the final expression is correct.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: I supplied the standard geometric fact that for two circles tangent to two intersecting lines and a fixed circle, the one with smaller distance to the vertex is closer. I verified the compressed dot product calculation in line 13 yields the correct projection $d$, requiring no substantive repair.
Decisive checks: Lines 9–12 correctly compute $AO_E^2$ using vector magnitudes from the circumcenter. Lines 20–21 correctly state the two roots; their product $x_0 x_1 = 2R^2 \cos A \sin B \sin C$ exactly matches the constant term of the quadratic, verifying correctness. Lines 23–28 rigorously handle the obtuse case: showing $x_0 \le 0$ eliminates it, and proving $AO_W > AO_E$ for $x_1$ contradicts the problem statement, thereby establishing $\angle A < 90^\circ$. This domain analysis is a verified fact that resolves the quantifier scope issue.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Both proofs correctly derive the governing quadratic and identify the target length for the rhombus condition. Proof A provides a correct but dense direct substitution verification. Proof B is mathematically stronger because it explicitly addresses the domain of validity: it proves that the problem's geometric condition "$(W)$ closer to $A$" forces $\angle A$ to be acute (lines 23–28), a necessary scope check that A omits. B also cleanly identifies both roots and uses their product to implicitly verify correctness, while correctly applying the selection criterion. The obtuse-case analysis in B resolves a load-bearing gap in A regarding quantifier/domain restrictions, making B's argument complete and rigorous across all valid triangle configurations.
