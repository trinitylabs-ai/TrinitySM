## Proof A
Established theorem: Bob has a winning strategy for any choice of $P, Q, S$ by Alice. The proof correctly reduces the road condition to a killing zone $Z_{AB} = \{ a + (b-a)w : w \in W \}$ where $W = \mathbb{C} \setminus (\Omega \cup (1-\Omega))$. It partitions all subsets $W \subset \mathbb{C}$ into two topological cases: (1) $\text{int}(\text{cl}(W)) = \emptyset$ and (2) $\text{int}(\text{cl}(W)) \neq \emptyset$. In Case 1, Bob uses the Baire Category Theorem to greedily place cities avoiding the nowhere-dense killing zones and collinearity/distance constraints, preserving a $K_5$ subgraph (non-planar). In Case 2, Bob exploits the density of $W$ in some open disk to place cities inside the corresponding dense killing zones, destroying all roads (disconnected). Both cases guarantee Bob wins.
Claim gap: NONE supported by checks. The topological dichotomy covers all possible subsets $W$. The Baire category argument correctly handles the intersection of a dense set with a non-empty open region. The density argument correctly handles the placement of cities in open disks while avoiding finitely many closed obstacles.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The argument is self-contained. The minor omission in Case 2 regarding why finitely many unit disks cannot cover the killing zone disk is a routine topological fact (a finite union of closed disks of radius 1 cannot cover an open disk of sufficiently large radius, which Bob ensures by initial scaling) and does not require substantive repair.

## Proof B
Established theorem: Bob has a winning strategy, but the proof contains a load-bearing gap in Case 1. The setup correctly identifies the killing zone $L_{AB}(U)$ with $U = \mathbb{C} \setminus T$. The proof splits into Case 1 ($U$ contained in a finite union of lines and disks) and Case 2 (otherwise). Case 2 correctly argues that if $U$ is not contained in any finite union of lines/disks, then $L_{AB}(U)$ cannot be covered by the finite forbidden set of lines and disks, allowing Bob to place a city in $L_{AB}(U)$ to destroy each road sequentially.
Claim gap: Case 1 strategy is incomplete/flawed. To preserve a $K_5$, Bob must avoid $V_{C_i C_j}(U) = \{ C_i + \frac{C_j - C_i}{u} : u \in U \}$. The proof claims $V$ is a finite union of lines, disks, or complements of disks via Möbius transformation. To avoid complements of disks, Bob must pick cities inside the corresponding disks. The proof asserts that picking $|C_1 - C_2|$ large makes these disks large, so their intersection is non-empty. This fails to account for $U$ potentially containing multiple components near $0$ or at different locations, which would produce multiple disk constraints whose intersection could be empty or incompatible with distance/collinearity constraints. The non-empty intersection claim is unjustified and breaks the greedy construction.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The gap in Case 1 requires a substantive repair (e.g., replacing the arbitrary geometric case split with a topological dichotomy like Proof A, or rigorously proving the intersection of Möbius-transformed constraints remains non-empty under scaling, which is non-trivial). Case 2 is independently valid but relies on the arbitrary case split. No repairs were supplied; the gap stands as written.
Decisive checks:
- Lines 1-10: Correctly derives killing zone condition. Verified.
- Lines 14-23 (Case 1): Möbius map $u \mapsto C_i + (C_j-C_i)/u$ sends filled disks to disks or complements of disks. Avoiding complements requires staying inside disks. Claim that large $|C_1-C_2|$ guarantees non-empty intersection of these disks is unsupported and demonstrably false for general $U$ (e.g., $U$ containing disjoint disks near 0 and far from 0 can yield conflicting interior constraints). Demonstrated defect.
- Lines 26-34 (Case 2): If $U$ not contained in finite union of lines/disks, $L_{AB}(U)$ not contained in finite forbidden set. Difference is non-empty. Bob can pick $C_n$. Verified. Strategy works for this case.

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Proof A provides a complete, rigorous strategy for Bob using a standard topological dichotomy ($\text{int}(\text{cl}(W)) = \emptyset$ vs $\neq \emptyset$). Its Baire category argument for preserving $K_5$ and its density argument for destroying all roads are both correctly justified and cover all possible subsets $S$ without arbitrary restrictions. Proof B's Case 2 is valid, but its Case 1 relies on an unjustified geometric claim about the non-empty intersection of Möbius-transformed disk constraints, leaving a load-bearing gap in the $K_5$ construction. Proof A's topological framework avoids this pitfall entirely and correctly handles the measure/category properties of the killing zones, making it mathematically superior and complete.
