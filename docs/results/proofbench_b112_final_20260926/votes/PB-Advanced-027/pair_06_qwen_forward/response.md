# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: Bob has a winning strategy. The proof correctly translates the direct similarity condition into complex ratios, defines $U = \mathbb{C} \setminus (S' \cup (1-S'))$, and establishes that a road $AB$ exists iff no city $C$ lies in $L_{AB}(U) = \{a + (b-a)u : u \in U\}$. It splits into cases based on whether $U$ is meager. In Case 1, it correctly uses the Baire Category Theorem to show Bob can greedily place cities avoiding a finite union of meager sets, lines, and disks, thereby constructing a $K_\infty$ subgraph (non-planar).
Claim gap: Case 2 contains a verified sequencing defect in the area argument. The proof states that when picking $C_j$, Bob ensures the scaled ball $B_{i,j}$ has area $> (k_m-1)\pi$, where $k_m$ is the index of the future city assigned to destroy pair $(i,j)$. However, $B_{i,j}$ is fixed once $C_i$ and $C_j$ are placed, while $k_m$ is assigned later during the enumeration of pairs. Since $k_m$ can be arbitrarily large, Bob cannot guarantee the area condition at the time $C_{k_m}$ is actually chosen. The argument implicitly assumes Bob can retroactively control the size of $B_{i,j}$ or that $k_m$ is bounded, which is not justified in the text.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The Baire Category application in Case 1 is correct but relies on standard topology. The Case 2 area argument can be repaired by explicitly assigning $k_m = j+1$ (destroying each pair immediately after its second endpoint is placed) and making $|c_j-c_i|$ sufficiently large at step $j$, but this repair is absent from the submission. The handling of $0 \in U$ in inversion steps is glossed over but does not affect the topological conclusions.
Decisive checks: Verified complex ratio translation and $U$ definition. Verified Case 1 Baire argument: finite union of meager sets is meager; complement in $\mathbb{C}$ is dense $G_\delta$, hence non-empty open after removing disks. Case 2 area dependency: $B_{i,j}$ radius is $|c_j-c_i|\rho$. Area condition requires $\pi|c_j-c_i|^2\rho^2 > (k_m-1)\pi$. Since $k_m$ is chosen after $c_i, c_j$ are fixed, the inequality cannot be guaranteed as written. This is a demonstrated defect in the logical flow.

## Proof B
Established theorem: Bob has a winning strategy. The proof correctly translates the condition to $U = \mathbb{C} \setminus T$ and splits into cases based on whether $U$ is contained in a finite union of lines and disks. In Case 1, it shows Bob can greedily place 5 cities avoiding forbidden sets (images of $U$ under affine and Möbius maps), guaranteeing a $K_5$ subgraph (non-planar). In Case 2, it shows Bob can destroy every road by picking cities in $L_{C_i C_j}(U)$ while avoiding lines and disks.
Claim gap: NONE supported by checks. The dichotomy covers all subsets of $\mathbb{C}$. Case 1 correctly identifies that affine and inversion maps send lines/disks to lines/circles/disks/complements, and the complement of a finite union of such sets is always non-empty open, allowing greedy selection. Case 2 uses a direct set-theoretic argument: if $U \not\subseteq \mathcal{F}$ (finite union of lines/disks), then $L_{C_i C_j}(U) \not\subseteq \mathcal{F}$, so $L_{C_i C_j}(U) \setminus \mathcal{F} \neq \emptyset$, guaranteeing a valid placement. No area or category arguments are needed, avoiding timing or measure-theoretic pitfalls.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The handling of $u=0$ in the inversion map is implicitly resolved since $0$ corresponds to $c=a$ (impossible for distinct cities), so $U \setminus \{0\}$ suffices. The Möbius transformation claim is standard complex analysis.
Decisive checks: Verified ratio translation and $U$ definition. Verified Case 1: $V_{C_i C_j}(U)$ involves $u \mapsto 1/u$, mapping disks to disks or exteriors. Avoiding a finite union of such sets is always possible in $\mathbb{C}$. Verified Case 2: The non-containment property is preserved under homeomorphism $z \mapsto c_i + (c_j-c_i)z$. The intersection of a non-contained set with the complement of a finite union of lines/disks is non-empty. The greedy destruction strategy is logically airtight and requires no auxiliary bounds.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Both proofs correctly identify Bob as the winner and properly translate the geometric similarity condition into complex ratio constraints. Proof B is mathematically stronger because its Case 2 argument is elementary, self-contained, and free of the sequencing flaw present in Proof A. Proof A's Case 2 relies on an area comparison that requires the scaled ball $B_{i,j}$ to exceed a bound dependent on a future index $k_m$, but $B_{i,j}$ is fixed when $C_i, C_j$ are placed, making the guarantee impossible as written. Proof B avoids this entirely by using a direct set-difference argument: since $L_{C_i C_j}(U)$ is not contained in a finite union of lines and disks, it necessarily contains points outside them, allowing Bob to place a destroyer city unconditionally. Proof B's construction is rigorous as written, while Proof A requires a non-trivial repair to its inductive timing.
