Claim gap: NONE. All cases are exhaustively covered, and the inductive constructions satisfy distance, collinearity, and road-existence constraints at every step without logical leaps.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The argument is self-contained and mathematically complete as written.

## Proof B
Established theorem: Bob has a winning strategy. The proof correctly models the road condition and splits into two topological cases for $W = \mathbb{C} \setminus K$. In Case 1, it constructs a surviving $K_5$ subgraph to violate planarity. In Case 2, it argues that dense killing zones allow Bob to eliminate all roads.
Claim gap: Minor strategic imprecision in Case 2. The "batch" killing description informally covers all pairs (by processing edges incident to each new $z_m$), but lacks explicit enumeration to guarantee every pair is eventually targeted. Additionally, the claim "By picking $z_m$ to be sufficiently far from the origin, he ensures that the cities can be placed to satisfy all distance and collinearity constraints" is mathematically imprecise; distance constraints are local ($>1$ from all previous cities), and "far from origin" does not automatically satisfy them. The actual justification relies on the topological fact that a dense set minus a closed nowhere-dense set is non-empty, which is stated but not tightly linked to the distance constraint.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: Supplied a standard pair-enumeration diagonalization to make the Case 2 killing strategy rigorous. Clarified that the distance/collinearity constraints are satisfied by the topological intersection argument (dense killing zone $\cap$ complement of closed forbidden set $\neq \emptyset$), not by radial distance. Noted that citing the Baire Category Theorem for a finite union (Line 18) is technically correct but misapplies the theorem's strength (finite unions of nowhere-dense sets are trivially nowhere-dense).
Decisive checks:
- Lines 1-10: Correctly derives $K = \Omega \cup (1-\Omega)$, $W = \mathbb{C} \setminus K$, and killing zone $Z_{AB}$. Verified.
- Lines 14-19 (Case 1): Greedy construction of $K_5$ is valid. At each step $k$, avoiding $\bigcup_{i<j<k} Z_{z_i z_j}$ is possible since each $Z$ is nowhere-dense. For $n>5$, avoiding $\bigcup_{i<j\le 5} Z_{z_i z_j}$ preserves the $K_5$. Verified.
- Lines 21-26 (Case 2): Correctly identifies that $\text{int}(\text{cl}(W)) \neq \emptyset$ implies $W$ is dense in some disk, making killing zones dense in open disks. The intersection argument for picking a killer city is topologically sound. However, the batch-killing description lacks explicit ordering to guarantee global coverage, and the "sufficiently far" remark is mathematically imprecise for the distance constraint. Unresolved without supplying an enumeration scheme and tightening the topological justification.

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Both proofs correctly model the similarity condition and reach the right conclusion (Bob wins). Proof A is mathematically superior due to its rigorous, explicit inductive constructions across all cases. Specifically, A's Subcase 2b carefully handles the two-way killing condition (existing cities killing new edges and vice versa) using Möbius homeomorphism properties to prove forbidden sets have empty interior, guaranteeing the $K_\infty$ construction. A's dense case uses a precise pair-enumeration to kill all edges. Proof B's Case 1 is valid but less detailed, and its Case 2 relies on a vague "batch" killing strategy and an imprecise "sufficiently far from the origin" claim for distance constraints, leaving the global enumeration and constraint satisfaction under-specified. A's proof requires no external repairs and stands complete as written.
