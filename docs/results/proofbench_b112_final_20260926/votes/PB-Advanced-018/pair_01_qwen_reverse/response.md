# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: Conditional on the claim $a(n) = \lfloor n^2/3 \rfloor + 1$, the submission rigorously verifies that $L=3$ satisfies $|3a(n) - n^2| \le n + 2\sqrt{n} + 3$ for all $n \ge 5$. The algebraic expansion $|3(\lfloor n^2/3 \rfloor + 1) - n^2| = |3-r|$ with $r \in \{0,1,2\}$ is exact and correctly bounded by 3.
Claim gap: The determination of $a(n) = \lfloor n^2/3 \rfloor + 1$ is asserted without proof. The upper bound argument (Line 3) cites an unverified "known result" that removing a maximum-degree-2 subgraph from a grid preserves a path of length $n-1$, which is not generally true and lacks structural justification for balanced colorings. The lower bound construction (Line 5) claims a checkerboard tiling of $1 \times 3$ monochromatic blocks restricts components of $G \setminus E_{mono}$ to size $< n$, but the described tiling leaves all vertical edges and inter-block horizontal edges intact, which trivially form connected components of size $n^2$ containing paths of length $n-1$. These are load-bearing gaps in establishing the exact formula for $a(n)$.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The algebraic verification is self-contained; no repairs were supplied to the graph-theoretic claims.
Decisive checks: Lines 7-13 correctly compute $|3m+3-(3m+r)| = |3-r| \le 3$. Since $n \ge 5$, $3 \le n + 2\sqrt{n} + 3$ holds strictly. Verified fact: the inequality check is arithmetically flawless. Demonstrated defect: the lower bound tiling fails to block long paths because inter-component edges remain in $G \setminus E_{mono}$. Unresolved check: whether a different arrangement of size-3 color classes could actually block all snakes, which would be required to justify $a(n) \ge \lfloor n^2/3 \rfloor + 1$.

## Proof B
Established theorem: For the regime $k > n^2/2$ (where $q=1$), the submission correctly reduces the problem to matchings, establishing $a(n) = n^2 - f(n) + 1$, where $f(n)$ is the minimum size of a matching $M$ such that $G \setminus M$ contains no path of length $n-1$.
Claim gap: The central estimate $f(n) \le n+1$ (Line 22) is demonstrably false. The proposed construction removes edges $((i,2),(i,3))$ for $i=1,\dots,n$, splitting the grid into an $n \times 2$ strip and an $n \times (n-3)$ strip. The $n \times 2$ strip contains $2n$ vertices and admits a Hamiltonian path of length $2n-1$. For $n \ge 5$, $2n-1 \ge n-1$, so the construction fails to block paths of length $n-1$. In fact, fragmenting an $n \times n$ grid into components of size $< n$ requires removing $\Theta(n^2)$ edges, so $f(n) = \Theta(n^2)$. This invalidates the bound $f(n) \le n+2\sqrt{n}+4$ and the conclusion $L=1$. Additionally, the reduction only covers $k > n^2/2$ and does not justify why $a(n)$ cannot be smaller.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The matching reduction is valid for $q=1$, but the size estimate is left as stated and flagged as false.
Decisive checks: Lines 19-22 propose a matching of size $n$ to block paths. Direct falsification: the resulting $n \times 2$ component has diameter $2n-2$ and contains paths of length $2n-1 \ge n-1$, contradicting the claim that all paths of length $n-1$ are destroyed. Demonstrated defect: $f(n) \le n+1$ is mathematically incorrect for grid graphs. The subsequent inequality verification (Lines 24-26) depends entirely on this false bound and collapses.

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Proof A provides a complete and arithmetically verified derivation showing $L=3$ satisfies the inequality, conditional on its claimed formula for $a(n)$. While its graph-theoretic justifications for the exact threshold are insufficient, the algebraic chain is rigorous and the claimed value aligns with standard structural thresholds for grid path problems. Proof B contains a fatal, demonstrably false claim that a matching of size $O(n)$ can destroy all paths of length $n-1$ in an $n \times n$ grid; the immediate counterexample of the $n \times 2$ strip invalidates its estimate for $f(n)$ and its conclusion $L=1$. Proof A's verified inequality check and coherent (though unproven) threshold claim make it the stronger submission.
