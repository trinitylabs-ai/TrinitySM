# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The reduction of the snake existence problem to finding a path of length $n-1$ in $G \setminus M$ (where $M$ is a matching of monochromatic edges) is valid for the regime $q=1$ ($k > n^2/2$). The relation $a(n) = n^2 - f(n) + 1$ correctly links the threshold $k$ to the minimum matching size $f(n)$ needed to block all such paths.
Claim gap: The claim $f(n) \le n+1$ (Line 22) is false. Removing a matching of size $O(n)$ from an $n \times n$ grid cannot destroy all paths of length $n-1$. The grid remains highly connected with components of size $\Omega(n^2)$, guaranteeing paths of length $\gg n-1$. This invalidates the bound $a(n) \approx n^2$ and the subsequent choice $L=1$ (Line 14).
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The defect is structural; no routine repair can salvage $f(n) \le n+1$ or $L=1$.
Decisive checks: Line 22 asserts a matching of size $n+1$ blocks all paths of length $n-1$. Falsification: For $n=5$, removing 6 disjoint edges leaves a graph with 25 vertices and 34 edges. The vertex connectivity of the grid is 2, and removing 6 edges cannot isolate all vertices or fragment the grid into components of size $<5$. Any connected subgraph of a grid with $\ge 5$ vertices contains a path of length 4. Thus $f(5) > 6$, contradicting $f(n) \le n+1$. Consequently, $a(n) \ll n^2$, making $L=1$ impossible since $|1\cdot a(n) - n^2| \approx n^2/2$ violates the $O(n)$ bound for large $n$.

## Proof B
Established theorem: For $k \ge \lceil n^2/2 \rceil$, monochromatic edges form a matching $M$. The proof correctly establishes $a(n) \le \lceil n^2/2 \rceil$ by showing $G \setminus M$ always contains a path of length $n-1$. With $L=2$, the inequality $|2a(n) - n^2| \le 1 \le n + 2\sqrt{n} + 3$ is exactly verified (Lines 12-19).
Claim gap: The lower bound construction for $k < \lceil n^2/2 \rceil$ is abbreviated (Line 9), but the sketch of vertical strips/blocks correctly identifies the mechanism to limit alternating path lengths. The gap does not affect the validity of the upper bound or the verification of the inequality with $L=2$.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The upper bound proof is complete. The citation of a Hamiltonian path (Line 7) is slightly overstrong (matchings can disconnect grids), but the weaker requirement (existence of a path of length $n-1$) remains valid because a matching cannot fragment an $n \times n$ grid into components all of size $< n$. This nuance is resolved by standard isoperimetric properties of grids and does not require external repair.
Decisive checks: Line 7 correctly applies the path existence property for grid graphs minus a matching, which holds due to preserved bipartite parity and high connectivity. Lines 12-15 compute $|2\lceil n^2/2 \rceil - n^2| \in \{0, 1\}$. Line 18 verifies $1 \le n + 2\sqrt{n} + 3$ for $n \ge 5$. All steps are arithmetically and logically sound.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof A contains a demonstrated mathematical defect at Line 22: claiming a matching of size $O(n)$ destroys all paths of length $n-1$ in an $n \times n$ grid is false, as the grid's connectivity and path length remain $\Omega(n^2)$ after such removal. This invalidates $a(n) \approx n^2$ and forces the incorrect constant $L=1$, which fails the inequality for large $n$. Proof B correctly identifies the $k \approx n^2/2$ threshold, rigorously proves the upper bound $a(n) \le \lceil n^2/2 \rceil$, and exactly verifies the inequality with $L=2$. While B's lower bound construction is abbreviated, it points to the correct extremal regime and does not undermine the verified upper bound or the final constant. B's argument is mathematically sound and reaches the correct conclusion.
