# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: For any positive integers $a, c$ and integer $b$, there exists a positive integer $n$ such that $a^{F_n} + n \equiv b \pmod{c}$. The proof establishes this by demonstrating a shift property $g_c(n+L) \equiv g_c(n) + L \pmod{c}$ for large $n$, and then applying strong induction on $c$ to solve the congruence.
Claim gap: NONE supported by checks. The induction correctly manages the "sufficiently large $n$" requirement by quantifying over an arbitrary threshold $K$, and the linear congruence solvability condition exactly matches the induction hypothesis.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The application of the generalized Euler theorem for $x \ge \phi(m)$ and the $p$-adic valuation argument for $v_p(L)$ are standard and correctly executed. No substantive lemmas were missing or silently supplied.
Decisive checks: 
- Lines 3-8: The claim that $F_n \pmod m$ is eventually periodic with period $T(m) = \text{lcm}(m, \phi(m), \dots, 1)$ is standard for power-tower recurrences. The proof correctly notes this period applies for $n$ sufficiently large, which is sufficient for the subsequent steps.
- Lines 10-12: The shift property $g_c(n+L) \equiv g_c(n) + L \pmod c$ follows directly from $F_{n+L} \equiv F_n \pmod{\phi(c)}$ (by periodicity) and the generalized Euler theorem $a^x \equiv a^{x \pmod{\phi(c)} + \phi(c)} \pmod c$ for $x \ge \phi(c)$. Verified.
- Lines 17-20: The claim $c' = \gcd(L, c) < c$ is rigorously justified. Let $p$ be the largest prime factor of $c$ with $v_p(c)=k$. Since $p \nmid (q-1)$ for any prime $q|c$, $v_p(\phi(c)) = k-1$. Iterating $\phi$ decreases the $p$-adic valuation by 1 each step until 0. Thus $v_p(L) = \max_j v_p(\phi^{(j)}(c)) = k-1 < k$, so $c \nmid L$ and $c' < c$. Verified.
- Lines 22-27: The induction hypothesis $P(c')$ provides $n_0$ such that $g_{c'}(n_0) \equiv b \pmod{c'}$. Since $c' \mid c$, $g_c(n_0) \equiv g_{c'}(n_0) \pmod{c'}$. The linear congruence $mL \equiv b - g_c(n_0) \pmod c$ is solvable iff $\gcd(L,c) \mid (b - g_c(n_0))$, i.e., $c' \mid (b - g_c(n_0))$, which is exactly the condition provided. The choice $n = n_0 + mL$ with $m \ge 0$ preserves $n \ge K$. Verified.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The proof correctly reduces the problem to showing that for $L = \text{lcm}(\phi(c), \phi(\phi(c)), \dots, 1)$ and $d = \gcd(L, c)$, the map $h(r) = a^{m_1 + v_1(r)} + r \pmod d$ is surjective onto $\mathbb{Z}_d$, where $v_1(r)$ is the stabilized value of $F_n \pmod{\phi(c)}$ for $n \equiv r \pmod L$.
Claim gap: The surjectivity of $h(r) \pmod d$ is asserted without proof (Lines 19-26). The argument only examines $r \equiv 0, 1 \pmod{m_1}$, showing $h(r)$ covers two specific cosets modulo $g = \gcd(m_1, d)$. This does not establish coverage of all $d$ residues. Furthermore, the number-theoretic claim that $a^{m_1}(a-1) \equiv -1 \pmod d$ has no solution for $d \ge 2$ when $m_1$ is even is false in general and irrelevant to proving surjectivity. This is a load-bearing gap that prevents the conclusion.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: To complete the argument, one would need a substantive lemma proving that as $r$ varies over $\{1, \dots, L\}$, the values $v_1(r)$ distribute sufficiently to make $a^{m_1+v_1(r)} + r$ cover all residues modulo $d$. This is entirely absent from the submission.
Decisive checks:
- Lines 5-12: The stabilization of $F_n \pmod{m_1}$ to a constant $v_1(r)$ depending only on $r = n \pmod L$ is correctly argued via backward induction on the totient chain. Verified.
- Lines 14-18: The reduction to the linear congruence $qL \equiv b - r - W(r) \pmod c$ and the solvability condition $\gcd(L, c) \mid (b - r - W(r))$ are correct. Verified.
- Lines 19-26: The surjectivity claim fails. Covering two cosets modulo $g$ does not imply surjectivity modulo $d$. The assertion that $h(r)$ is surjective due to "flexibility of $v_1(r)$" is unsupported. The specific claim about $a^{m_1}(a-1) \equiv -1 \pmod d$ is mathematically incorrect and does not establish the required coverage. Demonstrated defect.

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Proof A provides a complete, rigorous argument. Its induction on $c$ elegantly bypasses the need to analyze the global distribution of $F_n \pmod{\phi(c)}$, instead reducing the modulus step-by-step using a verified shift property and a correctly justified $p$-adic valuation argument ($v_p(L) = v_p(c)-1$). The linear congruence condition exactly matches the induction hypothesis, and the "sufficiently large $n$" requirement is properly managed by the arbitrary threshold $K$. Proof B correctly reduces the problem to a surjectivity question but fails to prove it. Its argument only covers two cosets modulo $\gcd(m_1, d)$ and relies on a false number-theoretic claim to assert distinctness, which does not establish surjectivity onto $\mathbb{Z}_d$. This is a fatal gap in the central chain of implications. Proof A is mathematically complete while Proof B leaves its decisive obligation unproven.
