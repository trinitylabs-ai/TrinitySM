# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: Correctly reduces the problem to solving $a^{F_n} + n \equiv b \pmod c$. Correctly defines the modulus sequence $m_i$ and proves that for $n \equiv R \pmod M$, the exponent $F_n \pmod{m_0}$ stabilizes to a constant $V(R)$ for sufficiently large $n$. Correctly applies the generalized Euler theorem to reduce $a^{F_n} \pmod c$ to $a^{V(R) + \phi(c)} \pmod c$.
Claim gap: Incorrectly asserts on line 10 that $V(R)$ depends only on $r = R \pmod{m_0}$. In reality, the induction shows $V(R)$ depends on $R \pmod M$ (specifically on $R-i \pmod{m_i}$ for all $i$). This restriction artificially limits the range of $V(R)$, making the subsequent surjectivity claim for $f(r) \pmod h$ false in the restricted setting. The surjectivity argument for $h>2$ (line 22) is hand-wavy and relies on the incorrect dependency claim.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: Verified the stabilization induction and Euler reduction. Noted that $V(R)$ actually depends on $R \pmod M$, which restores flexibility but invalidates the proof's specific reduction to $r \pmod{m_0}$. No substantive repair supplied for the surjectivity gap.
Decisive checks: Line 10 claims $V(R)$ is determined by $r \pmod{m_0}$. Falsified: $V_0(n)$ depends on $n \pmod{m_0}, n-1 \pmod{m_1}, \dots$, i.e., $n \pmod M$. Restricting to $r \pmod{m_0}$ loses necessary information. Line 22's surjectivity claim for $h>2$ lacks justification and fails under the stated restriction.

## Proof B
Established theorem: Correctly reduces the problem to $a^{F_n} + n \equiv b \pmod c$. Correctly defines $L = \text{lcm}(m_1, \dots, m_k)$ and proves $F_n \pmod{m_1}$ stabilizes to $v_1(r)$ for $n \equiv r \pmod L$. Correctly applies generalized Euler to get $a^{F_n} \equiv W(r) \pmod c$. Crucially, substitutes $n = qL + r$ to derive the linear congruence $qL \equiv b - r - W(r) \pmod c$, correctly identifying the necessary and sufficient solvability condition $W(r) + r \equiv b \pmod d$ where $d = \gcd(L, c)$. Correctly analyzes the coset structure for $r \equiv 0, 1 \pmod{m_1}$.
Claim gap: The surjectivity of $h(r) = W(r) + r \pmod d$ for $d > 2$ is asserted via "flexibility" (line 25) without a complete case analysis. The claim that $a^{m_1}(a-1) \equiv -1 \pmod d$ has no solution for even $m_1$ is slightly overgeneralized but only used to show two specific cosets are distinct. The core reduction to the linear congruence and the modulus $d$ is fully justified.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: Verified the linear reduction and coset analysis. The surjectivity for $d>2$ follows from standard number theory (the map $r \mapsto r \pmod d$ is surjective, and $W(r)$ shifts cosets sufficiently as $r$ varies over $L$), but the proof omits the explicit covering argument. This is a minor omission compared to the structural correctness of the reduction.
Decisive checks: Lines 17-19 correctly derive $qL \equiv b - r - W(r) \pmod c$ and the condition $d \mid (b - r - W(r))$. This is a verified, load-bearing step that correctly isolates the problem to a residue covering question modulo $d = \gcd(L, c)$. Lines 21-23 correctly compute the cosets covered by $x=0, 1$. The framework is mathematically sound.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof B correctly reduces the problem to a linear congruence in $q$, identifying the precise solvability condition modulo $d = \gcd(L, c)$. This reduction is rigorous and standard. Proof A contains a demonstrable structural defect: it incorrectly claims the stabilized exponent $V(R)$ depends only on $R \pmod{m_0}$ rather than $R \pmod M$, which artificially restricts the range of values and invalidates its surjectivity argument. While B's surjectivity claim for $d>2$ is abbreviated, it rests on a correct and necessary reduction, whereas A's gap undermines the logical chain entirely. B's framework is mathematically superior and closer to a complete proof.
