# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The proof correctly establishes that for $n \equiv r \pmod L$ (where $L = \text{lcm}(\phi(c), \phi(\phi(c)), \dots)$) and $n$ sufficiently large, $F_n \pmod{\phi(c)}$ stabilizes to a constant $v_1(r)$. It correctly reduces the original congruence to the solvability of $qL \equiv b - r - W(r) \pmod c$, which requires the map $h(r) = W(r) + r \pmod d$ (with $d = \gcd(L, c)$) to be surjective.
Claim gap: The proof does not rigorously establish the surjectivity of $h(r) \pmod d$ for the general case. It relies on a heuristic claim about the "flexibility" of $v_1(r)$ to cover residues when $g < d$, and provides an incomplete justification for the $g=d$ case.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The induction on the totient chain and the linear congruence reduction are valid. The claim that $a^{m_1}(a-1) \equiv -1 \pmod d$ has no solution for even $m_1$ and $d \ge 2$ is actually true in this context due to parity: $d \mid L$ and $m_1 \mid L$ imply $d$ is even for $c>2$, while $a^{m_1}(a-1)+1$ is always odd, so an even $d$ cannot divide it. The proof omits this parity justification, making the claim appear as a general arithmetic fact, but it is not a mathematical defect. The surjectivity gap for $g<d$ remains unresolved.
Decisive checks: 
- Lines 8-12: Induction on $F_{n-j} \pmod{m_j}$ is verified. The exponent reduction modulo $m_{j+1}=\phi(m_j)$ correctly yields a constant $v_j(r)$ depending only on $r \pmod L$.
- Lines 17-19: Reduction to $qL \equiv b - r - W(r) \pmod c$ and the solvability condition $d \mid (b - r - W(r))$ are verified.
- Line 25: The distinctness of $h(L)$ and $h(1)$ modulo $d$ is verified via parity (odd vs even), though the proof's stated justification is incomplete. The subsequent assertion of surjectivity for $d>2$ lacks rigorous support.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The proof correctly establishes that for $n \equiv R \pmod M$ (where $M = \text{lcm}(c, \phi(c), \phi(\phi(c)), \dots)$) and $n$ sufficiently large, $F_n \pmod{\phi(c)}$ stabilizes to a constant $V(R)$. It correctly reduces the problem to showing that the union of cosets $a^{V(R) + \phi(c)} + r + h\mathbb{Z} \pmod c$ covers $\mathbb{Z}_c$, which is equivalent to the surjectivity of $f(r) = a^{V(r) + \phi(c)} + r \pmod h$ where $h = \gcd(c, \phi(c))$.
Claim gap: The proof does not provide a fully rigorous demonstration of surjectivity for $h > 2$ when $a \not\equiv 0, 1 \pmod h$. It relies on a heuristic statement that varying $R$ allows $V(R)$ to take various values modulo $\phi(h)$, which combines with the linear term to cover all residues.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The induction and modular reduction are valid. The notation $V(r)$ slightly abuses the dependency of $V$ on $R \pmod M$, but the intent (grouping $R$ by $r = R \pmod{\phi(c)}$ and varying $R$ within each group) is mathematically sound given $M$ is a common multiple. The cases $a \equiv 0 \pmod h$ and $a \equiv 1 \pmod h$ are handled rigorously and correctly. The gap for $h>2$ is a missing detailed number-theoretic argument, but it contains no false claims or unjustified arithmetic leaps.
Decisive checks:
- Lines 5-8: Induction on $V_i(n)$ is verified. The use of $M$ correctly aligns the periodicity of $F_n$ with the modulus $c$.
- Lines 13-15: The coset argument is verified. For fixed $r = R \pmod{m_0}$, $R \pmod c$ covers exactly $r + h\mathbb{Z} \pmod c$ because the step size $m_0$ generates the subgroup $\gcd(m_0, c)\mathbb{Z} = h\mathbb{Z}$ modulo $c$. The union covers $\mathbb{Z}_c$ iff the offsets cover $\mathbb{Z}_h$.
- Lines 18-22: Cases $a \equiv 0, 1 \pmod h$ are rigorously verified. The argument for $h>2$ is heuristic but logically consistent with the reduction.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Both proofs correctly identify the core strategy of exploiting the eventual periodicity of $F_n$ modulo the totient chain to reduce the problem to a surjectivity condition on a divisor of $c$. Proof B is stronger because its reduction to $h = \gcd(c, \phi(c))$ is more precise, and its coset-covering argument correctly identifies the exact subgroup generated by the linear term modulo $c$. While Proof A's parity-based claim is ultimately true, its justification is incomplete and its surjectivity argument relies on unverified heuristics for the general case. Proof B rigorously handles the $a \equiv 0, 1 \pmod h$ cases, maintains a cleaner modular framework, and avoids any ambiguous or poorly justified arithmetic claims. B's mathematical structure is more robust and closer to a complete solution.
