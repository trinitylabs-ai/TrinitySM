# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: For fixed positive integers $a, c$ and integer $b$, the sequence $F_n \pmod{\phi(c)}$ stabilizes to a value $V(R)$ depending only on the residue class $R \pmod M$, where $M = \text{lcm}(c, \phi(c), \phi(\phi(c)), \dots)$. For $n \equiv R \pmod M$ sufficiently large, the target congruence $a^{F_n} + n \equiv b \pmod c$ reduces to $a^{V(R) + \phi(c)} + R \equiv b \pmod c$.
Claim gap: The proof fails to establish the existence of $R$ satisfying the reduced congruence. The argument incorrectly claims that solvability modulo $c$ is equivalent to the surjectivity of $f(r) = a^{V(r) + \phi(c)} + r \pmod h$ where $h = \gcd(c, \phi(c))$. This equivalence is false because $V(R)$ depends on $R \pmod{m_0}$ (with $m_0 = \phi(c)$), not merely $R \pmod h$. As $R$ varies within a coset $r + h\mathbb{Z} \pmod c$, $V(R)$ changes, so the expression does not simply sweep out a coset modulo $c$. Furthermore, the surjectivity claim for $h > 2$ (lines 21-22) is hand-wavy and lacks any rigorous justification or case analysis.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The coset reduction and surjectivity argument are fundamentally flawed and cannot be repaired without replacing the core strategy. No silent repairs were supplied.
Decisive checks: Lines 5-8 correctly identify that $F_n \pmod{m_0}$ becomes constant on residue classes modulo $M$ due to the recursive structure of the power tower and Euler's theorem. Line 12 correctly applies the generalized Euler theorem to reduce the exponent. Lines 14-15 contain a demonstrated defect: the claim that the union of cosets covers $\mathbb{Z}/c\mathbb{Z}$ iff $f(r)$ is surjective mod $h$ ignores the variation of $V(R)$ within each coset. A counterexample to the surjectivity logic can be constructed when $h$ shares factors with $a$ but $V(R)$ is restricted by the tower structure; the proof provides no mechanism to handle this. The argument for $h>2$ is an unresolved check that collapses under scrutiny. The quantifier $\exists n$ is never actually secured.

## Proof B
Established theorem: For any positive integers $a, c$ and integer $b$, there exists $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ such that $a^{F_n} + n \equiv b \pmod c$. The proof establishes this by induction on $c$, using a shift property $g_c(n+L) \equiv g_c(n) + L \pmod c$ for large $n$, where $L = T(\phi(c))$ is a period of the exponent sequence modulo $\phi(c)$.
Claim gap: NONE supported by checks. All steps are rigorously justified.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The notation $\phi^{(m)}(c)$ in line 19 is slightly informal but unambiguously refers to iterates of the totient function, and the valuation argument correctly handles it. The threshold $K_{shift}$ and induction base cases are standard and correctly managed. No silent repairs were supplied.
Decisive checks: Lines 4-8 correctly construct $T(m)$ as a valid period for $F_n \pmod m$ using the standard lcm-chain of totients. Lines 11-12 correctly derive the shift property $g_c(n+L) \equiv g_c(n) + L \pmod c$ by combining the periodicity of $F_n \pmod{\phi(c)}$ with generalized Euler's theorem. Lines 17-20 contain a verified, rigorous proof that $c' = \gcd(L, c) < c$: letting $p$ be the largest prime factor of $c$ with $v_p(c)=k$, the proof correctly shows $v_p(\phi(c)) = k-1$ (since $q-1 < p$ for all other prime factors $q$), and thus $v_p(L) = k-1$, implying $v_p(c') = k-1 < k$. Lines 23-27 correctly reduce the problem to a linear congruence $mL \equiv b - g_c(n_0) \pmod c$, whose solvability condition exactly matches the induction hypothesis $P(c')$. The induction closes cleanly, preserving the $\forall a,c,b \exists n$ quantifier structure without domain violations.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof B provides a complete, rigorous proof by induction on $c$, correctly establishing a shift property for the target expression and proving the crucial inequality $\gcd(L, c) < c$ via prime valuation analysis. Every step is verified and logically sound, with correct handling of quantifiers and thresholds. Proof A contains a fatal logical defect in lines 14-15: it incorrectly assumes the exponential term $a^{V(R)+\phi(c)}$ is constant or behaves predictably on cosets modulo $h = \gcd(c, \phi(c))$, and its surjectivity argument for $h>2$ is unsubstantiated hand-waving. Proof B's inductive reduction is mathematically airtight and directly resolves the problem, while Proof A's direct residue-class approach collapses at the final solvability step.
