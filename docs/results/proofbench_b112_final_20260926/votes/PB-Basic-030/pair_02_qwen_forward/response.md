# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The proof establishes that if the tangency condition for circles $(W_1)$ and $(W_2)$ holds, then the tangency condition for $(W_3)$ and $(W_4)$ holds, provided the centers are defined by the formula $O = M + \frac{c}{2} \cot \theta \vec{n}$ where $\vec{n}$ is a specific normal vector. The algebraic derivation showing that the difference in the tangency equations vanishes is correct.
Claim gap: The proof contains a descriptive defect regarding the direction of the normal vectors and the location of the centers. It claims $\vec{n_1}$ always points inward and that the center $O_1$ always lies on the interior side of the chord. This is geometrically incorrect for cases where the leg slants such that the "inward" direction opposes the formula's output, or when $\alpha > 90^\circ$ (where the center should be on the exterior side for the specified arc, but the text claims interior). However, the algebraic formula used ($O_1 = M_1 + u \cot \alpha \vec{n_1}$) is robust and correctly places the center for the specified arc condition due to the sign of $\cot \alpha$, even if the text description is flawed. The proof assumes a specific orientation (AB as left leg) without explicit WLOG justification, though the algebra is invariant.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The audit assumes the standard convex trapezoid orientation where the algebraic signs align with the geometric "inward" claim. The descriptive error in Line 6 is noted but does not invalidate the algebraic result because the formula itself handles the geometry correctly via the cotangent sign.
Decisive checks: The expansion of the distance squared and the cancellation of the cross terms involving $\vec{M} \cdot \vec{n}$ are verified algebraically. The key step $u L_1 + v L_2 = 0$ is verified to be true based on the coordinate definitions.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The proof establishes the tangency of $(W_3)$ and $(W_4)$ using a specific coordinate system ($A$ at origin). It derives the tangency condition and shows that the difference between the conditions for $(W_1, W_2)$ and $(W_3, W_4)$ vanishes.
Claim gap: Similar to Proof A, it relies on a specific coordinate setup that implicitly assumes an orientation (A at top-left, D to the right). It does not explicitly claim the normals point inward, avoiding the descriptive error of Proof A. The algebraic derivation is explicit and easy to verify. The cancellation of the dot product terms is shown step-by-step.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The proof is valid for the assumed orientation. By symmetry, the result holds for other orientations. The specific coordinates simplify the verification of the dot product cancellation.
Decisive checks: The calculation of $AB(\vec{V} \cdot \vec{n_1})$ and $CD(\vec{V} \cdot \vec{n_2})$ is verified. The sum is zero. The tangency condition difference is correctly identified as depending on this sum.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Both proofs use essentially the same algebraic method and arrive at the correct result. Proof B is preferred because it avoids the descriptive geometric error found in Proof A (where Proof A incorrectly claims the center always lies on the interior side and the normal always points inward, which is false for certain leg slopes or obtuse angles). Proof B defines the vectors by formula without making incorrect geometric assertions about their direction relative to the interior, making it more rigorous in its presentation. The explicit coordinate calculation in Proof B also makes the verification of the key cancellation step clearer.
