# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The proof establishes that if the algebraic tangency condition $f(u, v) = \pm 8 R_1 R_2$ holds for circles defined by centers $O_1 = M_1 + \frac{u}{2}\vec{n_1}$ and $O_2 = M_2 - \frac{v}{2}\vec{n_2}$, then the swapped circles defined by $O_3 = M_1 + \frac{v}{2}\vec{n_1}$ and $O_4 = M_2 - \frac{u}{2}\vec{n_2}$ satisfy the same condition $f(v, u) = \pm 8 R_3 R_4$, implying tangency.
Claim gap: The proof relies on specific vector definitions for the centers ($O_1$ with $+$, $O_2$ with $-$) without explicitly justifying why the signs differ based on the geometric "side opposite" constraint. While the algebraic symmetry is correct, the geometric validity of the center formulas for obtuse angles (where $\cot \theta < 0$) is not verified; specifically, the formula may place the center on the incorrect side of the chord for obtuse inscribed angles, potentially describing a different circle than the one specified by the problem statement.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The audit assumes the standard interpretation that "inscribed angle corresponding to the arc on the side opposite to C and D" implies the vertex of the angle lies on the side containing C and D (so the center is on the side of C and D for acute angles). Under this interpretation, the signs in Proof A are consistent for acute angles. The proof is accepted as valid under the assumption that the algebraic symmetry holds for the specific geometric configuration intended, despite the lack of rigorous justification for the normal vector directions and sign choices.
Decisive checks: The derivation of $f(u, v)$ and its symmetry $f(u, v) = f(v, u)$ is algebraically correct. The invariance of the product of radii $R_1 R_2 = R_3 R_4$ is verified. The conclusion follows logically from the premises.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The proof explicitly calculates the squared distance between centers $O_1 O_2^2$ and $O_3 O_4^2$ using coordinate geometry and vector expansions. It demonstrates that the difference $O_1 O_2^2 - O_3 O_4^2$ is equal to the difference in the sums of squared radii $(R_1^2 + R_2^2) - (R_3^2 + R_4^2)$. Combined with the invariance of the product of radii $R_1 R_2 = R_3 R_4$, it proves that if $O_1 O_2 = R_1 \pm R_2$, then $O_3 O_4 = R_3 \pm R_4$.
Claim gap: NONE supported by checks. The proof provides explicit definitions for the normal vectors pointing towards the relevant sides of the trapezoid, justifying the direction of the center displacements. The algebraic cancellations are shown step-by-step.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The proof is self-contained and rigorous.
Decisive checks: The expansion of $|\vec{m} + d_2 \vec{n_2} - d_1 \vec{n_1}|^2$ is verified. The cancellation of the linear terms involving $\vec{m} \cdot \vec{n_i}$ is verified. The calculation of the radius squared differences matches the distance squared differences. The handling of the $\pm$ sign in the tangency condition is robust, showing that the squared distance relation holds regardless of internal or external tangency.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof B is superior because it provides a rigorous geometric justification for the position of the circle centers relative to the chords, explicitly defining normal vectors that point toward the correct sides of the trapezoid. Proof A uses a more elegant symmetry argument but fails to justify the sign differences in its center formulas ($+$ for $O_1$, $-$ for $O_2$) and does not verify that the formulas correctly place the centers for all angle ranges (particularly obtuse angles). Proof B's explicit algebraic verification of the distance and radius relationships leaves no ambiguity regarding the geometric setup.
