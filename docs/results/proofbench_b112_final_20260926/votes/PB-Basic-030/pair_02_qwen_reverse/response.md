# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The proof establishes that if the algebraic tangency condition holds for circles $(W_1)$ and $(W_2)$ defined by centers $O_1 = M_1 + d_1 \vec{n}_1$ and $O_2 = M_2 + d_2 \vec{n}_2$ (where $\vec{n}$ are specific normal vectors derived from coordinates), then the same condition holds for $(W_3)$ and $(W_4)$ with swapped angles. It demonstrates that the difference in the squared distance terms vanishes due to a specific geometric identity involving the dot products of the vector connecting midpoints and the normals.
Claim gap: The proof relies on a specific coordinate setup ($A$ at origin x, bases horizontal) and explicit formulas for normal vectors. It asserts these normals point "inward" but the formulas provided ($\vec{n}_1 \propto (h, b)$) only point inward for certain configurations (e.g., $b>0$). If the normal points outward, the center is placed on the wrong side of the chord for acute angles (based on standard interpretation of inscribed angles subtending the exterior arc). However, under the interpretation that $\alpha$ is the angle subtended by the exterior arc at the interior circumference (making the center inward for acute $\alpha$), the geometric setup is consistent. The proof does not explicitly address the sign of the normal or the side of the center, relying on the algebraic cancellation which holds regardless of the normal's direction sign as long as it is consistent.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The audit assumes the standard interpretation that "inscribed angle corresponding to the arc on the side opposite to C and D" implies the angle is measured from the interior side (or that the center location formula is consistent with the problem's implicit geometry). The algebraic derivation $L_1 = L_3$ is verified. The handling of the $\pm$ sign for internal/external tangency is algebraically sound ($O_1O_2^2 = (R_1 \pm R_2)^2$).
Decisive checks: 
- Line 35-39: The calculation $AB(\vec{V} \cdot \vec{n}_1) + CD(\vec{V} \cdot \vec{n}_2) = 0$ is verified. $\vec{V} = (\frac{b-a-c}{2}, 0)$. $\vec{n}_1 \propto (h, b)$. Dot product $\propto h(b-a-c)$. $AB \propto \sqrt{h^2+b^2}$. Term is $\frac{h(b-a-c)}{2}$. Similarly for CD, term is $\frac{-h(b-a-c)}{2}$. Sum is 0. This is the core of the proof and is correct.
- Line 18: The tangency condition $O_1O_2^2 - (R_1^2 + R_2^2) = \pm 2R_1R_2$ correctly captures both external and internal tangency.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The proof establishes the same result using a more general vector notation. It defines centers $O_1, O_2$ using inward normals and shows that the tangency condition for $(W_1, W_2)$ implies the condition for $(W_3, W_4)$ because the difference in the left-hand sides of the distance equations vanishes.
Claim gap: Similar to Proof A, it defines $\vec{n}_1$ as inward but provides a formula $\frac{1}{c_1}(h, x_B-x_A)$ which may point outward depending on the trapezoid's shape (specifically if $x_B < x_A$). However, the algebraic cancellation relies on the vector sum $u \vec{n}_1 + v \vec{n}_2$ having a zero x-component. This property holds for the formulas used regardless of whether they represent inward or outward normals, provided the same formulas are used for both cases. The proof explicitly tracks the tangency type $\epsilon$ and shows $\epsilon = \epsilon'$, which is a slight advantage in rigor regarding the type of tangency.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The audit accepts the vector definitions as consistent within the proof's own logic. The step $u \vec{n}_1 + v \vec{n}_2 = (0, \dots)$ is verified. $u \vec{n}_1 = \frac{1}{2}(h, x_B-x_A)$ and $v \vec{n}_2 = \frac{1}{2}(-h, x_D-x_C)$. Sum of x-components is $h/2 - h/2 = 0$. Since $\vec{M}$ is horizontal, the dot product is 0.
Decisive checks:
- Line 22-25: The vector sum calculation is correct. $u \vec{n}_1 + v \vec{n}_2$ has x-component 0. $\vec{M}$ has y-component 0. Dot product is 0.
- Line 27: The conclusion $\epsilon = \epsilon'$ follows from the equality of the LHS and the fact that $R_1 R_2 = R_3 R_4$.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Both proofs are mathematically sound in their algebraic derivations and rely on the same core geometric identity (the cancellation of terms involving the dot products of the midpoint vector and the normals). Proof B is preferred because it uses a more general vector notation that clearly isolates the geometric reason for the cancellation (the orthogonality of the horizontal midpoint vector and the vertical component of the normal sum). Proof B also explicitly handles the tangency type parameter $\epsilon$, demonstrating that the type of tangency (internal vs. external) is preserved, whereas Proof A uses a $\pm$ notation that is slightly less explicit about the correspondence. Both proofs share a potential ambiguity regarding the direction of the normal vectors relative to the "inward" description for all trapezoid shapes, but this does not affect the validity of the algebraic cancellation which is the decisive step. Proof B's presentation is cleaner and more robust.
