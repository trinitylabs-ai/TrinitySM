# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The problem reduces to counting vertices in the $\le p$-level of a line arrangement after a projective transformation mapping $O$ to infinity. The submission claims the minimum number of red points is $\binom{p+2}{2}$.
Claim gap: The lower-bound induction (Step 9) is heuristic and does not establish $v_k \ge k+1$ from first principles or cite a precise reference. The minimality construction (Step 11) is asserted without geometric verification; it does not prove that a placement of $n-(p+2)$ lines exists that preserves the red status of the base $\binom{p+2}{2}$ points while ensuring all new intersections have level $>p$.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The transformation in Steps 3–5 is correctly executed and rigorously justifies that $N(X)$ equals the number of lines separating $O$ and $X$. The citation of the $\le p$-level bound is standard in discrete geometry, but the inductive sketch provided is not a proof. To close the construction gap, one must explicitly verify slope/intercept conditions ensuring new intersections lie in regions where rays from $O$ cross $\ge p+1$ base lines; this verification is absent.
Decisive checks: Step 3 correctly derives $f_k(X)<0 \iff L_k$ separates $O$ and $X$. Step 5 correctly maps this to a vertical level-counting problem. Step 9's claim "$V(\le p) \ge V(\le p-1) + (p+1)$" is stated without proof; the justification ("must contain at least 2 vertices to connect the lines") is insufficient to establish the bound for general $p$. Step 11's construction lacks coordinate or topological verification, leaving the achievability of the bound unresolved within the text.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The minimum number of red points is $\binom{p+2}{2}$. The lower bound follows from the known level-vertex theorem $v_k \ge k+1$. The upper bound is achieved by an explicit tangent construction.
Claim gap: The handling of $p \ge n/2$ in Step 7 is informal; the cited bound $v_k \ge k+1$ strictly applies for $k < n/2$, and the text does not invoke symmetry or duality to extend it to $k \ge n/2$. However, since the minimum is achieved at $n=p+2$, this does not invalidate the final result. The placement of $S_{rest}$ lines (Step 11) is described qualitatively but relies on standard geometric positioning arguments.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: The projective mapping in Steps 3–4 is correct. The tangent construction in Step 9 is fully specified: for tangents at ordered points on a small arc with $O$ at the center, the separation count $d_{S_0}(X_{ij},O)=j-i-1$ is explicitly computed and verified to be $\le p$. This rigorously establishes that exactly $\binom{p+2}{2}$ points are red in the base configuration. No substantive repairs are needed for the construction; the qualitative description of $S_{rest}$ placement is standard and geometrically sound.
Decisive checks: Step 3 correctly maps $O$ to $-\infty$ in $y$, converting separation counts to lines strictly below $X$. Step 9's computation $j-i-1$ is verified: lines $L_k$ with $i<k<j$ geometrically interpose between $O$ and $X_{ij}$, while others do not. The maximum $p$ is correctly identified, confirming all $\binom{p+2}{2}$ base intersections are red. Step 11's claim that far-placed lines push new intersections to levels $>p$ is geometrically consistent with the fan configuration. The lower-bound citation is standard; the minor omission of duality for $p \ge n/2$ does not affect the verified minimality at $n=p+2$.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Both proofs correctly reduce the problem to level counting in line arrangements and cite the standard lower bound $v_k \ge k+1$. The decisive difference is the rigor of the minimality construction. Proof B provides an explicit, analytically verifiable configuration (tangents to a circle on a small arc) and computes the exact separation count $j-i-1$, rigorously confirming that exactly $\binom{p+2}{2}$ points are red. Proof A's construction is heuristic ("small arrangement near a point", "far above") and lacks any verification that the claimed placement preserves the red count or avoids creating new red points. Proof B's construction is mathematically complete as written, while Proof A leaves the achievability of the bound unresolved.
