# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The points $T_A, I, M$ are collinear (where $M$ is the midpoint of arc $BC$ not containing $A$), and $T_A I = AI$. The internal and external centers of similitude $X, Y$ of $\omega$ and the incircle lie on line $OI$.
Claim gap: The proof fails to establish that $X$ and $Y$ lie on $\mathcal{C}_A, \mathcal{C}_B, \mathcal{C}_C$. Step 13 asserts the conclusion without verifying the necessary power-of-point condition, leaving the central obligation unmet. The derivation chain is broken by a false geometric lemma.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The submission contains a demonstrably false premise and an unjustified final assertion. No repair can salvage the logical flow without reconstructing the proof from standard mixtilinear incircle properties.
Decisive checks: 
- Step 3 claims "$I$ is the midpoint of the chord $M_A N_A$". This is false. The chord of contact of the $A$-mixtilinear incircle is perpendicular to $AI$, but its midpoint lies at distance $r_A/\sin(A/2)$ from $A$, whereas $I$ is at distance $r/\sin(A/2)$. Since $r_A \neq r$, $I$ is not the midpoint. This invalidates the homothety mapping $h_A(I) = I'$ and the subsequent orthocenter argument.
- Step 7 correctly computes $T_A I = AI$ using power of a point and Euler's theorem, but this result is isolated and does not connect to the concyclicity claim.
- Step 13 states "$Y$ lies on $\mathcal{C}_A$ if and only if $\vec{YA} \cdot \vec{YT_A} = \vec{YD} \cdot \vec{YD'}$... By symmetry, $X$ and $Y$ lie on all three circles." This is a logical non-sequitur. It posits a condition for concyclicity, fails to verify it, and then asserts the conclusion. The quantifier scope over the three circles is never established; the proof assumes the result rather than deriving it.

## Proof B
Established theorem: The external center of similitude $X_{ext}$ and internal center of similitude $Y_{int}$ of the incircle $\gamma$ and circumcircle $\omega$ have equal power with respect to $\mathcal{C}_A, \mathcal{C}_B, \mathcal{C}_C$. Consequently, line $IO$ is the common radical axis of the three circles. Since the incenter $I$ lies on this axis and has negative power for each circle, the axis intersects all three circles at the same two distinct points $X$ and $Y$.
Claim gap: NONE. The argument is complete and logically closed. It correctly handles the universal quantifier over the three circles by showing the power functions coincide on line $IO$, guaranteeing identical intersection points.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: NONE. The proof cites two standard mixtilinear incircle lemmas (concurrency of $AT_A$ at $X_{ext}$, and tangency of $\mathcal{C}_A$ to $\gamma$ with $K_A$ related to $S_A$ via $Y_{int}$). These are well-established in Olympiad geometry and are correctly integrated into a self-contained homothety and radical axis framework. No silent repairs were required.
Decisive checks:
- Step 3: Correctly identifies $AT_A$ as the radical axis of $\mathcal{C}_A$ and $\omega$. Since $X_{ext} \in AT_A$, $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_A}(X_{ext}) = \mathcal{P}_\omega(X_{ext})$. By symmetric application to $B$ and $C$, $X_{ext}$ has equal power for all three circles. Verified.
- Step 5: Composition of homotheties $h(S_A, -R/r_A) \circ h(A, r_A/r)$ yields a homothety of ratio $-R/r$ mapping $\gamma$ to $\omega$. The center of such a composition must lie on the line connecting the original centers ($S_A$ and $A$). Since $Y_{int}$ is the unique center of homothety with ratio $-R/r$, $Y_{int} \in AS_A$. Verified.
- Step 8-9: Uses collinearity of $Y_{int}, A, S_A, K_A$ to compute $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_A}(Y_{int}) = \vec{Y_{int}A} \cdot \vec{Y_{int}K_A} = -\frac{r}{R} \mathcal{P}_\omega(Y_{int})$. The algebra is correct, and the result is independent of the vertex choice, proving $Y_{int}$ has equal power for all three circles. Verified.
- Step 11-13: Two points with equal power for three circles define their common radical axis. The axis is $IO$. Evaluating power at $I \in IO$ gives $\vec{IA} \cdot \vec{ID} < 0$, placing $I$ inside all three circles. A line intersecting a circle at a point with negative power must cross it at two real points. Since the power functions are identical on $IO$, the zero-power points (intersections) are identical for all three circles. Verified.

## Decision
Winner: B
Reason: Proof B provides a complete, logically rigorous derivation using homothety composition and radical axis theory. It correctly establishes that $X_{ext}$ and $Y_{int}$ share equal power with respect to all three circumcircles, identifies $IO$ as the common radical axis, and proves the existence of two common intersection points via a negative power evaluation at $I$. Proof A contains a demonstrably false lemma (claiming $I$ is the midpoint of the mixtilinear chord of contact), which breaks its homothety argument, and ultimately fails to prove the main claim, resorting to an unjustified assertion in Step 13. Proof B's reliance on standard mixtilinear properties is properly contextualized and integrated into a self-contained power-of-point framework, making it mathematically sound and complete.
