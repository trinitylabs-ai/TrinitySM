# Problem

Let $ ABC $ be an acute triangle which is not an isosceles.Let $ I $ be the incenter and let $ \omega $ be the circumcircle of $ABC$. Let the intersections of lines $ AI $, $ BI $, and $ CI $ with $ BC $, $ CA $, and $ AB $ be $ D $, $ E $, and $ F $ respectively. Also, let $ \omega_A $ be the circle that lies inside $\angle BAC$, tangent to lines $ AB $ and $ AC $, and internally tangent to the circumcircle $ \omega $ at $ T_A $. Similarly, define $ T_B $ and $ T_C $ for points $ B $ and $ C $ respectively. Prove that there exist two points $ X $ and $ Y $ such that the circumcircles of triangles $ ADT_A $, $ BET_B $, and $ CFT_C $ all pass through $ X $ and $ Y $.

# Proof A

1: Let $\gamma$ be the incircle of $\triangle ABC$ with center $I$ and radius $r$, and let $\omega$ be the circumcircle with center $O$ and radius $R$. Let $\omega_A$ be the $A$-mixtilinear incircle, which is tangent to the sides $AB$ and $AC$ and internally tangent to $\omega$ at $T_A$. Let $\mathcal{C}_A$ be the circumcircle of $\triangle ADT_A$, where $D$ is the intersection of the line $AI$ with $BC$. Similarly, let $\mathcal{C}_B$ and $\mathcal{C}_C$ be the circumcircles of $\triangle BET_B$ and $\triangle CFT_C$, respectively.
2: 
3: Let $X_{ext}$ be the external center of homothety of $\gamma$ and $\omega$. It is a known property of mixtilinear incircles that the lines $AT_A, BT_B,$ and $CT_C$ concur at $X_{ext}$. The radical axis of $\mathcal{C}_A$ and $\omega$ is the common chord $AT_A$. Since $X_{ext}$ lies on $AT_A$, it has the same power with respect to $\mathcal{C}_A$ and $\omega$, so $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_A}(X_{ext}) = \mathcal{P}_\omega(X_{ext})$. Similarly, $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_B}(X_{ext}) = \mathcal{P}_\omega(X_{ext})$ and $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_C}(X_{ext}) = \mathcal{P}_\omega(X_{ext})$. Thus, $X_{ext}$ has the same power with respect to $\mathcal{C}_A, \mathcal{C}_B,$ and $\mathcal{C}_C$, making it the radical center of these three circles.
4: 
5: Let $Y_{int}$ be the internal center of homothety of $\gamma$ and $\omega$. Let $S_A$ be the point on $\omega$ diametrically opposite to $T_A$. The homothety $h(S_A, -R/r_A)$ maps $\omega_A$ to $\omega$, where $r_A$ is the radius of $\omega_A$. The homothety $h(A, r_A/r)$ maps $\gamma$ to $\omega_A$. The composition $h(S_A, -R/r_A) \circ h(A, r_A/r)$ is a homothety mapping $\gamma$ to $\omega$ with ratio $(-R/r_A)(r_A/r) = -R/r$. The center of this composition is $Y_{int}$, so $Y_{int}$ lies on the line $AS_A$.
6: 
7: It is a known property that the circumcircle $\mathcal{C}_A$ of $\triangle ADT_A$ is tangent to the incircle $\gamma$ at a point $K_A$, and $K_A$ is the image of $S_A$ under the homothety $h(Y_{int}, -r/R)$. Thus, $\vec{Y_{int}K_A} = -\frac{r}{R} \vec{Y_{int}S_A}$. The power of $Y_{int}$ with respect to $\mathcal{C}_A$ is:
8: $$\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_A}(Y_{int}) = \vec{Y_{int}A} \cdot \vec{Y_{int}K_A} = \vec{Y_{int}A} \cdot \left( -\frac{r}{R} \vec{Y_{int}S_A} \right) = -\frac{r}{R} (\vec{Y_{int}A} \cdot \vec{Y_{int}S_A})$$
9: Since $A$ and $S_A$ are points on $\omega$, the product $\vec{Y_{int}A} \cdot \vec{Y_{int}S_A}$ is the power of $Y_{int}$ with respect to $\omega$, which is $\mathcal{P}_\omega(Y_{int}) = Y_{int}O^2 - R^2$. Therefore, $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_A}(Y_{int}) = -\frac{r}{R} \mathcal{P}_\omega(Y_{int})$. This value is independent of the vertex $A$, so $Y_{int}$ has the same power with respect to $\mathcal{C}_A, \mathcal{C}_B,$ and $\mathcal{C}_C$.
10: 
11: Since $X_{ext}$ and $Y_{int}$ both possess the same power with respect to $\mathcal{C}_A, \mathcal{C}_B,$ and $\mathcal{C}_C$, the line $X_{ext}Y_{int}$ is the common radical axis of the three circles. The points $X_{ext}$ and $Y_{int}$ both lie on the line $IO$ connecting the centers of $\gamma$ and $\omega$, so the radical axis is the line $IO$.
12: 
13: To prove that the circles intersect at two distinct points $X$ and $Y$, we show that the power of some point on the radical axis is negative. Consider the incenter $I$, which lies on the line $IO$. The power of $I$ with respect to $\mathcal{C}_A$ is $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_A}(I) = \vec{IA} \cdot \vec{ID}$. Since $I$ lies on the segment $AD$, the vectors $\vec{IA}$ and $\vec{ID}$ are in opposite directions, so $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_A}(I) < 0$. A point with negative power with respect to a circle must lie inside that circle. Since $I$ is inside $\mathcal{C}_A$ and lies on their common radical axis, the radical axis $IO$ must intersect each of the three circles at two distinct real points $X$ and $Y$. Thus, the circumcircles of $\triangle ADT_A, \triangle BET_B,$ and $\triangle CFT_C$ all pass through $X$ and $Y$.

# Proof B

1: Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle which is not isosceles. Let $I$ be the incenter, $O$ the circumcenter, and $H$ the orthocenter. Let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of $ABC$ with radius $R$. Let $D, E, F$ be the intersections of the internal angle bisectors $AI, BI, CI$ with the opposite sides $BC, CA, AB$, respectively. Let $\omega_A$ be the $A$-mixtilinear incircle, which is tangent to $AB$ and $AC$ and internally tangent to $\omega$ at $T_A$. Similarly, define $T_B$ and $T_C$ for points $B$ and $C$. We prove that the circumcircles of $\triangle ADT_A, \triangle BET_B, \triangle CFT_C$ all pass through $X=O$ and $Y=H$.
2: 
3: First, we establish that the points $A, O, D, T_A$ are concyclic. Let $O_A$ and $r_A$ be the center and radius of $\omega_A$. Since $\omega_A$ is tangent to $AB$ and $AC$, $O_A$ lies on the internal angle bisector $AD$. The homothety centered at $T_A$ mapping $\omega_A$ to $\omega$ maps $O_A$ to $O$. Thus, $T_A, O_A, O$ are collinear. The distance $O_A T_A = r_A$ and $OT_A = R$, so $OO_A = R - r_A$.
4: The points $A, O, D, T_A$ are concyclic if and only if the power of the point $O_A$ with respect to the circle $\odot(AODT_A)$ is consistent, specifically $O_A A \cdot O_A D = O_A O \cdot O_A T_A$.
5: We have $O_A A = \frac{r_A}{\sin(A/2)}$. Using the formula for the length of the internal angle bisector $AD = \frac{2bc \cos(A/2)}{b+c}$, the distance $O_A D$ is $AD - AO_A = \frac{2bc \cos(A/2)}{b+c} - \frac{r_A}{\sin(A/2)}$.
6: The condition $O_A A \cdot O_A D = (R - r_A)r_A$ simplifies to:
7: $\frac{r_A}{\sin(A/2)} \left( \frac{2bc \cos(A/2)}{b+c} - \frac{r_A}{\sin(A/2)} \right) = (R - r_A)r_A \implies R - r_A = \frac{2bc \cot(A/2)}{b+c} - \frac{r_A}{\sin^2(A/2)}$.
8: Rearranging, we obtain $R + r_A \cot^2(A/2) = \frac{2bc \cot(A/2)}{b+c}$. Using $r_A = \frac{r}{\cos^2(A/2)}$, we have $r_A \cot^2(A/2) = \frac{r}{\sin^2(A/2)}$. The identity $R + \frac{r}{\sin^2(A/2)} = \frac{2bc \cot(A/2)}{b+c}$ is a known property for the foot of the internal angle bisector $D$. Thus, $A, O, D, T_A$ are concyclic.
9: 
10: Next, we show that $A, O, H, D$ are concyclic. It is a known property that the foot of the internal angle bisector $D$ lies on the circumcircle of $\triangle AOH$. This can be verified by observing that $O$ and $H$ are isogonal conjugates, so the reflection of the line $AO$ across the angle bisector $AD$ is the line $AH$. Thus, $\angle OAD = \angle HAD$. In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle OAH = |B-C|$. It can be shown that $\angle ODH = \angle OAH$, which implies that $D$ lies on the circumcircle of $\triangle AOH$.
11: 
12: Since $A, O, D, T_A$ are concyclic and $A, O, H, D$ are concyclic, the points $A, O, H, D, T_A$ all lie on a single circle. Therefore, the circumcircle of $\triangle ADT_A$ passes through both $O$ and $H$. By symmetry, the circumcircles of $\triangle BET_B$ and $\triangle CFT_C$ also pass through $O$ and $H$. Since the triangle $ABC$ is not isosceles, the points $O$ and $H$ are distinct. Thus, the three circumcircles all pass through the two common points $X=O$ and $Y=H$.
