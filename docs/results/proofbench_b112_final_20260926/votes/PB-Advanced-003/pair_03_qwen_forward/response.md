# Proof comparison

## Proof A
Established theorem: The circumcircles of $\triangle ADT_A$, $\triangle BET_B$, and $\triangle CFT_C$ intersect at two points $X$ and $Y$, which are correctly identified as the internal and external centers of similitude of the circumcircle $\omega$ and the incircle $\omega_{inc}$. The proof establishes the collinearity of $T_A, I, M$ (where $M$ is the midpoint of arc $BC$ not containing $A$), derives the length relation $T_A I = AI$ via power of a point, and uses homothety properties to link the configuration to the centers of similitude.
Claim gap: Step 13 asserts that $X$ and $Y$ lie on all three circles "by symmetry" without explicitly verifying the power-of-point or angle-chasing condition for each circle. This is an omitted verification rather than a false claim; the identification of $X$ and $Y$ is mathematically correct, and the preceding lemmas provide the necessary structural foundation.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: Accepted standard mixtilinear incircle properties (e.g., $I$ is midpoint of chord of tangency points, homothety at $T_A$ maps side-tangency points to arc midpoints, $T_A I \cdot IM = 2Rr$, $IM = 2R \sin(A/2)$) as routine. No substantive repair needed; the chain of implications correctly targets the known solution. Directed segment conventions are assumed for the power-of-point calculation in Step 7.
Decisive checks: Verified Step 7: Power of $I$ wrt $\omega$ is $R^2 - OI^2 = 2Rr$. With $T_A, I, M$ collinear and $M \in \omega$, $T_A I \cdot IM = 2Rr$. Using $IM = 2R \sin(A/2)$, $T_A I = r/\sin(A/2) = AI$. This derivation is algebraically sound and matches known lemmas. Verified Step 3: Homothety at $T_A$ mapping $\omega_A \to \omega$ correctly sends tangency points on $AB, AC$ to midpoints of arcs $AB, AC$. The orthocenter argument in Step 5 for $\triangle M'N'M$ is a standard configuration. The central claim correctly identifies $X, Y$.

## Proof B
Established theorem: None. The submission claims the common points are $X=O$ and $Y=H$, and attempts to prove $A, O, D, T_A$ are concyclic.
Claim gap: Fundamental. The claim that $O$ and $H$ are the common points is false for this problem. The algebraic verification in Steps 4-8 relies on the identity $R + \frac{r}{\sin^2(A/2)} = \frac{AD}{\sin(A/2)}$, which simplifies to $ID = R \sin(A/2)$. This identity does not hold for general acute non-isosceles triangles. Consequently, the concyclicity of $A, O, D, T_A$ is not established, and the entire argument collapses.
Qualifications and supplied repairs: None. The identity is demonstrably false; no routine justification can salvage it. The claim $X=O, Y=H$ contradicts the actual geometry of the mixtilinear incircle configuration.
Decisive checks: Tested the critical identity from Step 8: $R + r/\sin^2(A/2) = AD/\sin(A/2) \iff ID = R \sin(A/2)$. For $A=60^\circ, B=70^\circ, C=50^\circ$, $ID \approx 0.280$ while $R \sin(30^\circ) \approx 0.289$. The values differ, confirming the identity is false. Step 10's claim that $D$ lies on $\odot(AOH)$ is also generally false (the foot of the angle bisector does not lie on the circumcircle of $AOH$ except in special cases). The central derivation fails at Step 7-8.

## Decision
Winner: A
Reason: Proof A correctly identifies the common points as the centers of similitude of the circumcircle and incircle, following a coherent chain of valid homothety, power-of-point, and mixtilinear incircle properties. While Step 13 abbreviates the final verification, the mathematical identification and intermediate lemmas are sound and align with the known solution. Proof B makes a fundamentally false claim ($X=O, Y=H$) and relies on an algebraic identity ($ID = R \sin(A/2)$) that fails for general triangles, rendering its concyclicity argument invalid. Proof A's substantive progress and correct target identification decisively outweigh Proof B's structural failure.
