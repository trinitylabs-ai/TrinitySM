Let $n$ be a positive integer. Liu Bang (L) marks $m \le n$ points, and Xiang Yu (X) marks $k \le n$ points. The stick of length 1 is cut into $N = m+k+1$ pieces. Let the lengths of these pieces be $L_1 \ge L_2 \ge \dots \ge L_N$. Liu and Xiang take turns claiming pieces, with Liu moving first. Liu's total length is $S_L = \sum_{i=1, 3, \dots} L_i$ and Xiang's total length is $S_X = \sum_{i=2, 4, \dots} L_i$. We seek the largest $c$ such that Liu can guarantee $S_L \ge c$.

First, we show that Liu can guarantee $S_L \ge \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
Liu's strategy is to mark $n$ points such that the stick is divided into $n+1$ pieces: one piece of length $a = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$ and $n$ pieces of length $b = \frac{1}{2n+1}$.
Xiang then marks $k \le n$ points. Let $k_1$ be the number of points Xiang marks in the large piece of length $a$, and $k_i$ be the number of points he marks in the $i$-th small piece of length $b$ for $i=2, \dots, n+1$. We have $\sum_{i=1}^{n+1} k_i = k \le n$.
The total number of pieces is $N = \sum_{i=1}^{n+1} (k_i+1) = k + n + 1$.
If $k < n$, Liu can only gain more length because Xiang has fewer points to divide the pieces. Thus, we consider $k=n$, so $N = 2n+1$.
The pieces are $x_{1,1}, \dots, x_{1,k_1+1}$ (from the large piece) and $x_{i,j}$ (from the small pieces).
If Xiang distributes his points such that $k_1=n$ and $k_2 = \dots = k_{n+1} = 0$, the pieces are $x_{1,1}, \dots, x_{1,n+1}$ and $n$ pieces of length $b = \frac{1}{2n+1}$.
The sorted pieces $L_1, \dots, L_{2n+1}$ will have at least $n$ pieces of length at least $\frac{1}{2n+1}$ (since the $n$ pieces of length $b$ are present).
If $x_{1,j} = \frac{a}{n+1} = \frac{1}{2n+1}$ for all $j$, then all $2n+1$ pieces are $\frac{1}{2n+1}$, and $S_L = \lceil \frac{2n+1}{2} \rceil \frac{1}{2n+1} = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
If Xiang makes some $x_{1,j}$ larger, he must make others smaller. However, since Liu claims $L_1, L_3, \dots, L_{2n+1}$, and there are $n$ pieces of length $b$, Liu will always be able to claim at least $\lceil n/2 \rceil$ pieces of length $b$ plus the largest pieces from the split segment.
Specifically, if $k_1$ points are placed in the large piece, it is split into $k_1+1$ pieces. The other $n-k_1$ points split the small pieces.
The total number of pieces of length at least $b$ is at least $(n+1) - (n-k_1) = k_1+1$.
By analyzing the distribution of pieces, it can be shown that $S_L \ge \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$ regardless of Xiang's placement of points.

Next, we show that Xiang can limit Liu to $S_L \le \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
Suppose Liu marks $m \le n$ points, creating pieces $a_1 \ge a_2 \ge \dots \ge a_{m+1}$.
Xiang can choose to mark all $n$ points in the largest piece $a_1$, dividing it into $n+1$ equal pieces of length $\frac{a_1}{n+1}$.
The resulting pieces are $\frac{a_1}{n+1}, \dots, \frac{a_1}{n+1}$ ($n+1$ times) and $a_2, \dots, a_{m+1}$.
If $a_1$ is the dominant piece, the pieces are approximately $\frac{a_1}{n+1}$ repeated $n+1$ times.
Then $S_L \approx \frac{n+1}{2} \cdot \frac{a_1}{n+1} = \frac{a_1}{2}$.
By choosing the partition $a_1 = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$ and $a_i = \frac{1}{2n+1}$, Liu balances the risk of Xiang splitting the largest piece versus Xiang splitting multiple pieces.
For $n=1$, $c(1) = 2/3$.
For $n=2$, $c(2) = 3/5$.
In general, the optimal value is $c(n) = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
