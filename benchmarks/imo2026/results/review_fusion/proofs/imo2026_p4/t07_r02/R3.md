To determine the values of $\theta \in (0^\circ, 180^\circ)$ for which Mulan can guarantee victory, we analyze the conditions under which she can force an angle of size $\theta$ to appear in the triangle $T$.

**1. Forcing $\theta$ from a multiple $k\theta$**
Suppose Mulan has a triangle $T$ containing an angle $\phi = k\theta$ for some integer $k \ge 2$. She can force the angle $\theta$ in finitely many steps. She splits $\phi$ into $\theta$ and $(k-1)\theta$. If Shan-Yu keeps the triangle with $\theta$, Mulan wins. If he keeps the triangle with $(k-1)\theta$, she now possesses an angle $(k-1)\theta$. She repeats this process, splitting the remaining angle into $\theta$ and $(k-2)\theta$. After at most $k-1$ steps, she will either win or reach a state where she splits $2\theta$ into $\theta$ and $\theta$, forcing the angle $\theta$ regardless of Shan-Yu's choice.

**2. The case $\theta = 180^\circ/k$**
Let $\theta = 180^\circ/k$ for some integer $k \ge 2$. Let $S = \{ n\theta \mid n \in \{1, 2, \dots, k-1\} \}$. If $T$ has an angle in $S$, Mulan wins by the strategy above. Suppose $T$ has no angle in $S$.
If $T$ has an angle $\gamma < \theta$, Mulan can pick a vertex with angle $\alpha$ and split it into $\alpha_1 = 180^\circ - \beta - \theta$ and $\alpha_2 = \alpha - \alpha_1$. The resulting triangles have angles:
$T_1: \alpha_1, \beta, 180^\circ - \alpha_1 - \beta = \theta \in S$.
$T_2: \alpha_2, \gamma, 180^\circ - \alpha_2 - \gamma = 180^\circ - (\alpha - (180^\circ - \beta - \theta)) - \gamma = 360^\circ - (\alpha + \beta + \gamma) - \theta = 180^\circ - \theta = (k-1)\theta \in S$.
Since both $T_1$ and $T_2$ contain an angle in $S$, Mulan forces an angle in $S$ and thus wins.

If $T$ has no angle $\gamma < \theta$, then all angles $\alpha, \beta, \gamma \ge \theta$. Let $\alpha = n_1\theta + \epsilon_1, \beta = n_2\theta + \epsilon_2, \gamma = n_3\theta + \epsilon_3$ where $0 \le \epsilon_i < \theta$. Since $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = k\theta$, we have $\sum \epsilon_i = m\theta$ for some $m \in \{0, 1, 2\}$. If $m=0$, all angles are in $S$ and Mulan wins. If $m > 0$, Mulan splits the largest angle $\alpha$ into $\theta$ and $\alpha - \theta$. $T_1$ has angle $\theta \in S$. $T_2$ has angles $\alpha - \theta, \gamma, \beta + \theta$. If Shan-Yu keeps $T_2$, the new angles are $(n_1-1)\theta + \epsilon_1, n_3\theta + \epsilon_3, (n_2+1)\theta + \epsilon_2$. The values $\epsilon_i$ remain unchanged. This process continues until $n_1$ becomes 0, at which point $T$ contains the angle $\epsilon_1 < \theta$, and Mulan wins in the next move.

**3. The case $\theta \neq 180^\circ/k$**
Let $V = \text{span}_\mathbb{Q} \{ \theta, 180^\circ \}$. Shan-Yu chooses an initial triangle $T_0$ with angles $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ such that $\alpha, \beta, \theta, 180^\circ$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$. Then $\gamma = 180^\circ - \alpha - \beta$.
Mulan wins if she can force an angle in $V$ (since $\theta \in V$). To force an angle in $V$ from a triangle with angles $\phi, \beta, \gamma$, she must pick $\phi_1$ such that both $T_1(\phi_1, \beta, 180^\circ - \phi_1 - \beta)$ and $T_2(\phi - \phi_1, \gamma, 180^\circ - \phi + \phi_1 - \gamma)$ have an angle in $V$.
Assuming $\beta, \gamma \notin V$, this requires $(\phi_1 \in V \text{ or } \phi_1 \in \beta + V)$ and $(\phi - \phi_1 \in V \text{ or } \phi_1 \in \phi + \gamma + V)$.
Checking the combinations:
- $\phi_1 \in V$ and $\phi - \phi_1 \in V \implies \phi \in V$.
- $\phi_1 \in V$ and $\phi_1 \in \phi + \gamma + V \implies \phi + \gamma \in V \iff \beta \in V$.
- $\phi_1 \in \beta + V$ and $\phi - \phi_1 \in V \implies \phi - \beta \in V \iff \gamma + 2\beta \in V$.
- $\phi_1 \in \beta + V$ and $\phi_1 \in \phi + \gamma + V \implies \phi + \gamma - \beta \in V \iff 2\gamma \in V$.
Given the linear independence of $\alpha, \beta, \theta, 180^\circ$, none of these conditions can be met in the first move. Furthermore, if Mulan chooses $\phi_1$ to make $T_1$ have an angle in $V$, Shan-Yu keeps $T_2$. The new angles of $T_2$ will still be linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$ with $\theta$ and $180^\circ$ (except for the sum), meaning Mulan can never force an angle in $V$. Thus, she can never force $\theta$.

Mulan can guarantee victory if and only if $\theta = \frac{180^\circ}{k}$ for some $k \in \{2, 3, 4, \dots\}$.
