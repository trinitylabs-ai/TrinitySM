To determine the values of $\theta \in (0^\circ, 180^\circ)$ for which Mulan can guarantee victory, we analyze the conditions under which she can force an angle of size $\theta$ to appear in the triangle $T$.

**1. Forcing a target angle from a known angle**
Suppose Mulan has a triangle $T$ containing an angle $\phi$. She can force the angle $\phi/k$ for any integer $k \ge 2$ in finitely many steps. She splits $\phi$ into $\phi/k$ and $(k-1)\phi/k$. If Shan-Yu keeps the triangle with $\phi/k$, she wins. If he keeps the triangle with $(k-1)\phi/k$, she now possesses an angle $(k-1)\phi/k$. She repeats this process, splitting the remaining angle into $\phi/k$ and $(k-2)\phi/k$. After at most $k-1$ steps, she will either win or reach a state where she splits $2\phi/k$ into $\phi/k$ and $\phi/k$, forcing the angle $\phi/k$ regardless of Shan-Yu's choice.

**2. The set of "dangerous" angles**
Let $D$ be the set of angles $\phi \in (0^\circ, 180^\circ)$ such that Mulan can force $\theta$ from any triangle containing $\phi$. By definition, $\theta \in D$. From the division strategy above, if $\phi \in D$, then $2\phi \in D$ (provided $2\phi < 180^\circ$).
Furthermore, if $\phi_1, \phi_2 \in D$, then $|\phi_1 - \phi_2| \in D$ (if non-zero). To see this, suppose $T$ has an angle $\beta = \phi_2 - \phi_1$. Mulan can split another angle $\alpha$ into $\alpha_1 = \phi_1$ and $\alpha_2 = \alpha - \phi_1$. The first resulting triangle $T_1$ contains $\phi_1 \in D$. The second triangle $T_2$ contains the angle $180^\circ - \alpha_2 - \gamma = 180^\circ - (\alpha - \phi_1) - \gamma = (180^\circ - \alpha - \gamma) + \phi_1 = \beta + \phi_1 = \phi_2 \in D$. Since both $T_1$ and $T_2$ contain angles in $D$, Mulan forces the game into a state where she possesses an angle in $D$.
Thus, $D$ is the intersection of a subgroup of $(\mathbb{R}, +)$ with the interval $(0^\circ, 180^\circ)$. Since $\theta \in D$, $D$ must contain all angles of the form $n\theta$ that lie in $(0^\circ, 180^\circ)$.

**3. Forcing $D$ from any initial triangle**
Mulan can force an angle in $D$ from any initial triangle $T_0$ if she can make a cut such that both resulting triangles $T_1$ and $T_2$ contain an angle in $D$. Let the angles of $T$ be $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$. Mulan splits $\alpha$ into $\alpha_1, \alpha_2$. She forces $D$ if:
$(\alpha_1 \in D \text{ or } 180^\circ - \alpha_1 - \beta \in D) \text{ and } (\alpha_2 \in D \text{ or } 180^\circ - \alpha_2 - \gamma \in D)$.
If there exist $\phi_1, \phi_2 \in D$ such that $\phi_1 + \phi_2 = 180^\circ$, Mulan can always achieve this. She chooses $\alpha_1 = 180^\circ - \beta - \phi_1$. Then $T_1$ contains $180^\circ - \alpha_1 - \beta = \phi_1 \in D$. Also, $\alpha_2 = \alpha - \alpha_1 = \alpha - (180^\circ - \beta - \phi_1) = \alpha + \beta - 180^\circ + \phi_1 = -\gamma + \phi_1$. Then $T_2$ contains $180^\circ - \alpha_2 - \gamma = 180^\circ - (-\gamma + \phi_1) - \gamma = 180^\circ - \phi_1 = \phi_2 \in D$.
Thus, if $D$ contains two angles that sum to $180^\circ$, Mulan can force an angle in $D$ from any $T_0$, and subsequently force $\theta$.

**4. Characterizing $\theta$**
$D$ contains $\phi_1, \phi_2$ such that $\phi_1 + \phi_2 = 180^\circ$ if and only if $n\theta + m\theta = 180^\circ$ for some integers $n, m \ge 1$. This simplifies to $(n+m)\theta = 180^\circ$, or $\theta = 180^\circ/k$ for some integer $k \ge 2$.
If $\theta = 180^\circ/k$, Mulan wins by forcing $D$ and then using the division strategy to reach $\theta$.
If $\theta \neq 180^\circ/k$, then $D = \{n\theta \mid n \in \mathbb{Z}\} \cap (0^\circ, 180^\circ)$ contains no pair summing to $180^\circ$, and $90^\circ \notin D$. Shan-Yu can choose $T_0$ such that its angles $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$ with $\theta$ and $180^\circ$. In this case, the condition for forcing $D$ in one move $(\alpha_1 \in D \text{ or } 180^\circ - \alpha_1 - \beta \in D) \text{ and } (\alpha_2 \in D \text{ or } 180^\circ - \alpha_2 - \gamma \in D)$ can never be satisfied, as it would require $\alpha, \beta,$ or $\gamma$ to be a linear combination of $\theta$ and $180^\circ$. Thus, Shan-Yu can prevent Mulan from ever creating an angle in $D$.

Mulan can guarantee victory if and only if $\theta = \frac{180^\circ}{k}$ for some $k \in \{2, 3, 4, \dots\}$.
