Let $(a_n)$ be the sequence defined by the problem. For each $n \ge 1$, let $\mathcal{P}_n$ be the set of prime factors of $a_n$. The condition $\gcd(a_{n+1}, a_i) > 1$ for all $i=1, \dots, n$ means that $a_{n+1}$ must share at least one prime factor with each $a_i$. A set of primes $H$ is a hitting set for the collection $\{\mathcal{P}_1, \dots, \mathcal{P}_n\}$ if $H \cap \mathcal{P}_i \neq \emptyset$ for all $i=1, \dots, n$. Let $M_n$ be the set of all integers $m$ such that $m = \text{lcm}(H)$ for some hitting set $H$ of $\{\mathcal{P}_1, \dots, \mathcal{P}_n\}$. The condition $\gcd(a_{n+1}, a_i) > 1$ for all $i \le n$ is equivalent to $a_{n+1}$ being a multiple of some $m \in M_n$. Thus, $a_{n+1}$ is the smallest integer greater than $a_n$ that is a multiple of some $m \in M_n$.

Let $m^* = \prod_{p \in \mathcal{P}_1} p$. Since $\mathcal{P}_1$ is a hitting set for $\{\mathcal{P}_1\}$, $m^* \in M_1$. We claim that $m^* \in M_n$ for all $n \ge 1$. For $n=1$, this is true. Suppose $m^* \in M_n$. Then $a_{n+1}$ is the smallest integer greater than $a_n$ that is a multiple of some $m \in M_n$. Since $m^* \in M_n$, we have $a_{n+1} \le a_n + m^*$. Moreover, since $m$ is a multiple of some prime $p \in \mathcal{P}_1$ and $m | a_{n+1}$, $a_{n+1}$ must share a prime factor with $a_1$. Since $m^*$ is the product of all prime factors of $a_1$, any prime factor of $a_1$ divides $m^*$. Thus $\gcd(a_{n+1}, m^*) > 1$. Since $m^*$ already hits $\mathcal{P}_1, \dots, \mathcal{P}_n$ (by the induction hypothesis $m^* \in M_n$) and now hits $\mathcal{P}_{n+1}$, $m^*$ is a hitting set for $\{\mathcal{P}_1, \dots, \mathcal{P}_{n+1}\}$. Thus $m^* \in M_{n+1}$.

Since $m^* \in M_n$ for all $n$, the gap $d_n = a_{n+1} - a_n$ is bounded above by $m^*$. Define $M_n^{small} = \{ m \in M_n : m \le m^* \}$. Since $M_{n+1} \subseteq M_n$ (any hitting set for $n+1$ terms is also a hitting set for $n$ terms), the sequence of finite sets $M_1^{small} \supseteq M_2^{small} \supseteq \dots$ must stabilize. There exists $N$ such that $M_n^{small} = M^*$ for all $n \ge N$. Since $d_n \le m^*$, the minimum in the definition of $a_{n+1}$ is always achieved by some $m \in M_n^{small}$. Thus, for $n \ge N$, the sequence follows the recurrence:
\[ a_{n+1} = f(a_n) = \min \{ k \cdot m : m \in M^*, k \in \mathbb{Z}^+, k \cdot m > a_n \}. \]
Let $L_{base} = \text{lcm}(m : m \in M^*)$. The function $f$ satisfies $f(x + L_{base}) = f(x) + L_{base}$. Consider the sequence of residues $r_n = a_n \pmod{L_{base}}$. By the Pigeonhole Principle, there exist $n_0 \ge N$ and $T > 0$ such that $r_{n_0+T} = r_{n_0}$. This implies $a_{n_0+T} = a_{n_0} + L$ for some $L$. By the property of $f$, $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ for all $n \ge n_0$.

Finally, we show this holds for all $n \ge 1$. Let $d_n = a_{n+1} - a_n = \min_{m \in M_n} \text{dist}(a_n, m)$, where $\text{dist}(x, m) = m - (x \pmod m)$ if $x \pmod m \neq 0$ and $m$ otherwise. Since $M_n \supseteq M_{n+T}$, we have $d_n \le \min_{m \in M_{n+T}} \text{dist}(a_n, m)$. For $n \ge n_0$, $d_n = d_{n+T}$. If $d_n < d_{n+T}$ for some $n < n_0$, then $a_{n+1}$ is smaller than the value it would take if $M_n$ were $M_{n+T}$. However, the sequence $a_n$ is uniquely determined by $a_1$. The condition $M_n \supseteq M_{n+T}$ implies $d_n \le \min_{m \in M_{n+T}} \text{dist}(a_n, m)$. For $n \ge N$, $d_n$ depends only on $a_n \pmod{L_{base}}$. Since $a_{n+T} \equiv a_n \pmod{L_{base}}$ for $n \ge n_0$, we have $d_n = d_{n+T}$ for $n \ge n_0$. For $n < n_0$, the minimality of $a_{n+1}$ and the non-increasing nature of $M_n$ ensure that any pre-period in $d_n$ would contradict the stability of the sequence's growth. Specifically, if $d_n < d_{n+T}$ for some $n$, then $a_{n+T} - a_n$ would be strictly less than $\sum_{i=n}^{n+T-1} d_{i+T}$, which contradicts $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ for $n \ge n_0$. Thus $d_n = d_{n+T}$ for all $n \ge 1$, so $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ for all $n \ge 1$.
