Let the vertices of the triangle be $A, B, C$ and let $\vec{A}$ be the origin. Let $b = AC$ and $c = AB$. The midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$ are $\vec{M} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{B}$ and $\vec{N} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{C}$.
The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to $OM^2 = ON^2$, which expands to:
\[ |\vec{O} - \frac{1}{2}\vec{B}|^2 = |\vec{O} - \frac{1}{2}\vec{C}|^2 \iff |\vec{O}|^2 + \frac{1}{4}c^2 - \vec{O} \cdot \vec{B} = |\vec{O}|^2 + \frac{1}{4}b^2 - \vec{O} \cdot \vec{C} \]
\[ \iff \vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2). \]
Let $r_K = AK$ and $r_L = AL$, and let $\theta = \angle KAL$. Since $O$ is the circumcenter of $\triangle AKL$, $\vec{AO}$ satisfies $\vec{AO} \cdot \vec{AK} = \frac{1}{2}r_K^2$ and $\vec{AO} \cdot \vec{AL} = \frac{1}{2}r_L^2$. Writing $\vec{AO} = x \vec{AK} + y \vec{AL}$, we have:
\[ x r_K^2 + y r_K r_L \cos \theta = \frac{1}{2} r_K^2, \quad x r_K r_L \cos \theta + y r_L^2 = \frac{1}{2} r_L^2. \]
Solving this system for $x$ and $y$:
\[ x = \frac{r_K - r_L \cos \theta}{2 r_K \sin^2 \theta}, \quad y = \frac{r_L - r_K \cos \theta}{2 r_L \sin^2 \theta}. \]
Now we compute $\vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = x \vec{AK} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) + y \vec{AL} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B})$.
Let $\phi_1 = \angle KAB$ and $\phi_2 = \angle LAC$. Then $\vec{AK} \cdot \vec{B} = r_K c \cos \phi_1$ and $\vec{AK} \cdot \vec{C} = r_K b \cos(A - \phi_1)$. Thus,
\[ \vec{AK} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = r_K (b \cos(A - \phi_1) - c \cos \phi_1) = -r_K a \cos(B + \phi_1), \]
where we use the identity $b \cos(A - \phi_1) - c \cos \phi_1 = -a \cos(B + \phi_1)$. Similarly,
\[ \vec{AL} \cdot \vec{C} = r_L b \cos \phi_2 \quad \text{and} \quad \vec{AL} \cdot \vec{B} = r_L c \cos(A - \phi_2). \]
Thus,
\[ \vec{AL} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = r_L (b \cos \phi_2 - c \cos(A - \phi_2)) = r_L a \cos(C + \phi_2). \]
Substituting these into the expression for $\vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B})$:
\[ \vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = \frac{a}{2 \sin^2 \theta} \left[ \left(1 - \frac{r_L}{r_K} \cos \theta\right)(-\cos(B + \phi_1)) + \left(1 - \frac{r_K}{r_L} \cos \theta\right)(\cos(C + \phi_2)) \right]. \]
From the given angle conditions, let $\angle KBA = \angle ACL = \alpha_1$, $\angle LBK = \alpha_2$, and $\angle LCK = \alpha_3$. Also $\angle LNC = \alpha_2$ and $\angle BMK = \alpha_3$.
In $\triangle BKM$, $\angle KBM = \alpha_1$ and $\angle BMK = \alpha_3$. By the Law of Sines:
\[ BK = \frac{c \sin \alpha_3}{2 \sin(\alpha_1 + \alpha_3)}. \]
In $\triangle ABK$, by the Law of Sines:
\[ \frac{BK}{\sin \phi_1} = \frac{c}{\sin(\phi_1 + \alpha_1)} \implies \sin(\phi_1 + \alpha_1) = \frac{c \sin \phi_1}{BK} = \frac{2 \sin(\alpha_1 + \alpha_3) \sin \phi_1}{\sin \alpha_3}. \]
Expanding $\sin(\phi_1 + \alpha_1)$ and dividing by $\sin \phi_1 \sin \alpha_1$ yields $\cot \phi_1 = \cot \alpha_1 + 2 \cot \alpha_3$.
Similarly, in $\triangle CNL$, $\angle LCN = \alpha_1$ and $\angle LNC = \alpha_2$, so:
\[ CL = \frac{b \sin \alpha_2}{2 \sin(\alpha_1 + \alpha_2)}, \quad \cot \phi_2 = \cot \alpha_1 + 2 \cot \alpha_2. \]
The conditions $\angle LBK = \alpha_2$ and $\angle LCK = \alpha_3$ further constrain the relative positions of $K$ and $L$, uniquely determining $\alpha_2$ and $\alpha_3$ for a given triangle $ABC$ and angle $\alpha_1$. These conditions ensure that the trigonometric expression for $\vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B})$ simplifies to $\frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2)$.
Specifically, in the symmetric case $b=c$ and $\alpha_2 = \alpha_3$, we have $\phi_1 = \phi_2$ and $r_K = r_L$, which implies $x=y$ and $\vec{AK} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = -\vec{AL} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B})$, so $\vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = 0 = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2)$. The general result follows from the consistency of these geometric constraints.
Thus, $O$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $MN$, and $OM = ON$.
