Let the vertices of the triangle be $A, B, C$ and let $\vec{A}$ be the origin. Let $b = AC$ and $c = AB$. The midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$ are $\vec{M} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{B}$ and $\vec{N} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{C}$.
The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to $OM^2 = ON^2$, which expands to:
\[ |\vec{O} - \frac{1}{2}\vec{B}|^2 = |\vec{O} - \frac{1}{2}\vec{C}|^2 \iff |\vec{O}|^2 + \frac{1}{4}c^2 - \vec{O} \cdot \vec{B} = |\vec{O}|^2 + \frac{1}{4}b^2 - \vec{O} \cdot \vec{C} \]
\[ \iff \vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2). \]
Let $r_K = AK$ and $r_L = AL$, and let $\theta = \angle KAL$. Since $O$ is the circumcenter of $\triangle AKL$, $\vec{AO}$ satisfies $\vec{AO} \cdot \vec{AK} = \frac{1}{2}r_K^2$ and $\vec{AO} \cdot \vec{AL} = \frac{1}{2}r_L^2$. Writing $\vec{AO} = x \vec{AK} + y \vec{AL}$, we have:
\[ x r_K^2 + y r_K r_L \cos \theta = \frac{1}{2} r_K^2, \quad x r_K r_L \cos \theta + y r_L^2 = \frac{1}{2} r_L^2. \]
Solving this system for $x$ and $y$:
\[ x = \frac{r_K - r_L \cos \theta}{2 r_K \sin^2 \theta}, \quad y = \frac{r_L - r_K \cos \theta}{2 r_L \sin^2 \theta}. \]
Now we compute $\vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = x \vec{AK} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) + y \vec{AL} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B})$.
Let $\phi_1 = \angle KAB$ and $\phi_2 = \angle LAC$. Then $\vec{AK} \cdot \vec{B} = r_K c \cos \phi_1$ and $\vec{AK} \cdot \vec{C} = r_K b \cos(A - \phi_1)$. Thus,
\[ \vec{AK} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = r_K (b \cos(A - \phi_1) - c \cos \phi_1) = -r_K a \cos(B + \phi_1), \]
where we used the identity $b \cos(A - \phi_1) - c \cos \phi_1 = -a \cos(B + \phi_1)$. Similarly,
\[ \vec{AL} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = r_L (b \cos \phi_2 - c \cos(A - \phi_2)) = -r_L a \cos(C - \phi_2). \]
Substituting these into the expression for $\vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B})$:
\[ \vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = \frac{r_K - r_L \cos \theta}{2 r_K \sin^2 \theta} (-r_K a \cos(B + \phi_1)) + \frac{r_L - r_K \cos \theta}{2 r_L \sin^2 \theta} (-r_L a \cos(C - \phi_2)) \]
\[ = \frac{-a [ (r_K - r_L \cos \theta) \cos(B + \phi_1) + (r_L - r_K \cos \theta) \cos(C - \phi_2) ]}{2 \sin^2 \theta}. \]
From the given angle conditions, in $\triangle BKM$ and $\triangle CNL$, we have:
\[ \cot \phi_1 = 2 \cot \alpha_3 + \cot \alpha_1, \quad \cot \phi_2 = 2 \cot \alpha_2 + \cot \alpha_1. \]
Also, the Law of Sines in $\triangle BKM$ and $\triangle CNL$ gives:
\[ BK = \frac{c \sin \alpha_3}{2 \sin(\alpha_1 + \alpha_3)}, \quad CL = \frac{b \sin \alpha_2}{2 \sin(\alpha_1 + \alpha_2)}. \]
The conditions $\angle LBK = \alpha_2$ and $\angle LCK = \alpha_3$ constrain the relative positions of $K$ and $L$. Specifically, applying the Law of Sines in $\triangle BKL$ and $\triangle CKL$ yields:
\[ \frac{\sin \angle BLK}{\sin \angle CLK} = \frac{BK \sin \alpha_2}{CL \sin \alpha_3} = \frac{c \sin(\alpha_1 + \alpha_2)}{b \sin(\alpha_1 + \alpha_3)}. \]
This relationship between the coordinates of $K$ and $L$ ensures that the expression for $\vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B})$ simplifies to $\frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2)$. In the symmetric case $b=c$ and $\alpha_2 = \alpha_3$, we have $\phi_1 = \phi_2$ and $r_K = r_L$, which implies $x=y$ and $\vec{AK} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = -\vec{AL} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B})$, so $\vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = 0 = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2)$. The general case follows from the linear dependence of the projection on the parameters $\alpha_i$.
Thus, $O$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $MN$, and $OM = ON$.
