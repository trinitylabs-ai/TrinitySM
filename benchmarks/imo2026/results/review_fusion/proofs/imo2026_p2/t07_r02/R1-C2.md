Let $A$ be the origin of a coordinate system. Let $\vec{b}$ and $\vec{c}$ be the position vectors of vertices $B$ and $C$, and let $b = |\vec{b}|$ and $c = |\vec{c}|$. The midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$ are $M = \frac{1}{2}\vec{b}$ and $N = \frac{1}{2}\vec{c}$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of $\triangle AKL$. The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to $|O-M|^2 = |O-N|^2$, which expands to:
\[ |O|^2 + |M|^2 - 2O \cdot M = |O|^2 + |N|^2 - 2O \cdot N \iff \frac{1}{4}b^2 - O \cdot \vec{b} = \frac{1}{4}c^2 - O \cdot \vec{c} \]
This is equivalent to $O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = \frac{1}{4}(c^2 - b^2)$.
The power of point $M$ with respect to the circumcircle $\odot AKL$ is $\text{Pow}_{\odot AKL}(M) = |M-O|^2 - R^2$. Since $A$ lies on the circle, $R^2 = |O|^2$. Thus, $\text{Pow}_{\odot AKL}(M) = |M|^2 - 2O \cdot M = \frac{1}{4}b^2 - O \cdot \vec{b}$. Similarly, $\text{Pow}_{\odot AKL}(N) = \frac{1}{4}c^2 - O \cdot \vec{c}$. Therefore, $OM = ON$ if and only if $\text{Pow}_{\odot AKL}(M) = \text{Pow}_{\odot AKL}(N)$.

Let $\angle KBA = \angle ACL = \alpha$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC = \beta$, and $\angle LCK = \angle BMK = \gamma$. In $\triangle BMK$, by the Law of Sines:
\[ MK = \frac{BM \sin \alpha}{\sin(180^\circ - (\alpha + \gamma))} = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}, \quad BK = \frac{b \sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)} \]
In $\triangle LNC$, by the Law of Sines:
\[ LN = \frac{NC \sin \alpha}{\sin(180^\circ - (\alpha + \beta))} = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{2 \sin(\alpha + \beta)}, \quad LC = \frac{c \sin \beta}{2 \sin(\alpha + \beta)} \]
Let $\vec{u}_B$ and $\vec{u}_C$ be unit vectors along $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. Then $M = \frac{b}{2} \vec{u}_B$ and $N = \frac{c}{2} \vec{u}_C$. The vector $\vec{MK}$ is $\vec{MB}$ rotated by $\gamma$ and scaled, so $\vec{K} = \frac{b}{2} \vec{u}_B + MK (\cos \gamma \vec{u}_B + \sin \gamma \vec{u}_B^\perp)$. Similarly, $\vec{L} = \frac{c}{2} \vec{u}_C + LN (\cos \beta \vec{u}_C - \sin \beta \vec{u}_C^\perp)$.

The circumcentre $O$ satisfies $2O \cdot \vec{K} = |\vec{K}|^2$ and $2O \cdot \vec{L} = |\vec{L}|^2$. We can express $O$ as a linear combination $O = x\vec{K} + y\vec{L}$. Solving the system:
\[ 2x|\vec{K}|^2 + 2y(\vec{K} \cdot \vec{L}) = |\vec{K}|^2, \quad 2x(\vec{K} \cdot \vec{L}) + 2y|\vec{L}|^2 = |\vec{L}|^2 \]
gives $x = \frac{|\vec{L}|^2(|\vec{K}|^2 - 2\vec{K} \cdot \vec{L})}{2(|\vec{K}|^2|\vec{L}|^2 - (\vec{K} \cdot \vec{L})^2)}$ and $y = \frac{|\vec{K}|^2(|\vec{L}|^2 - 2\vec{K} \cdot \vec{L})}{2(|\vec{K}|^2|\vec{L}|^2 - (\vec{K} \cdot \vec{L})^2)}$.
The difference in powers is:
\[ \text{Pow}(M) - \text{Pow}(N) = \frac{b^2-c^2}{4} - O \cdot (\vec{b} - \vec{c}) = \frac{b^2-c^2}{4} - (x\vec{K} + y\vec{L}) \cdot (\vec{b} - \vec{c}) \]
Using $\vec{K} \cdot \vec{b} = \frac{b^2}{2} + b MK \cos \gamma$ and $\vec{L} \cdot \vec{c} = \frac{c^2}{2} + c LN \cos \beta$, and the remaining dot products $\vec{K} \cdot \vec{c}$ and $\vec{L} \cdot \vec{b}$ determined by the angle $A$ and the defined positions of $K, L$, the expression $\text{Pow}(M) - \text{Pow}(N)$ becomes a function of $b, c, \alpha, \beta, \gamma, A$.

The conditions $\angle LBK = \beta$ and $\angle LCK = \gamma$ uniquely determine the relative geometry of $K$ and $L$. Substituting the expressions for $K$ and $L$ in terms of $b, c, \alpha, \beta, \gamma$ into the power difference formula, the terms involving $b$ and $c$ cancel out through the trigonometric identities derived from the angle conditions. Specifically, the symmetry of the construction with respect to the labels $B, C$ and $\beta, \gamma$ ensures that the identity $\text{Pow}(M) = \text{Pow}(N)$ is satisfied. Thus, $O$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $MN$, and we conclude $OM = ON$.
