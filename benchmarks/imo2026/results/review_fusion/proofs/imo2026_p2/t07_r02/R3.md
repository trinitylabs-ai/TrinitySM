To prove that $OM = ON$, we let $A$ be the origin of a coordinate system. The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to $|O-M|^2 = |O-N|^2$. Expanding this, we have:
\[ |O|^2 + |M|^2 - 2O \cdot M = |O|^2 + |N|^2 - 2O \cdot N \iff |M|^2 - 2O \cdot M = |N|^2 - 2O \cdot N \]
Let $R$ be the circumradius of $\triangle AKL$. Since $A$ lies on the circumcircle $\odot AKL$, we have $R^2 = |O|^2$. The power of a point $P$ with respect to $\odot AKL$ is $\text{Pow}_{\odot AKL}(P) = |P-O|^2 - R^2$. Thus, $\text{Pow}_{\odot AKL}(M) = |M|^2 - 2O \cdot M$ and $\text{Pow}_{\odot AKL}(N) = |N|^2 - 2O \cdot N$. Therefore, $OM = ON$ if and only if $\text{Pow}_{\odot AKL}(M) = \text{Pow}_{\odot AKL}(N)$.

Let $f$ be the homothety centered at $A$ with ratio 2. Then $f(M) = B$ and $f(N) = C$. Let $\Gamma$ be the circumcircle of $\triangle AKL$ and $\Gamma'$ be its image under $f$. $\Gamma'$ is the circumcircle of $\triangle AK'L'$, where $K' = f(K)$ and $L' = f(L)$. The power of a point $P$ with respect to $\Gamma'$ is related to the power of $f^{-1}(P)$ with respect to $\Gamma$ by $\text{Pow}_{\Gamma'}(P) = 2^2 \text{Pow}_{\Gamma}(f^{-1}(P))$. Thus, $\text{Pow}_{\Gamma'}(B) = 4 \text{Pow}_{\Gamma}(M)$ and $\text{Pow}_{\Gamma'}(C) = 4 \text{Pow}_{\Gamma}(N)$. Consequently, $OM = ON$ if and only if $\text{Pow}_{\Gamma'}(B) = \text{Pow}_{\Gamma'}(C)$, which is equivalent to $|B-O'| = |C-O'|$, where $O'$ is the circumcenter of $\triangle AK'L'$. This means $O'$ must lie on the perpendicular bisector of $BC$, or $2O' \cdot (B-C) = |B|^2 - |C|^2 = c^2 - b^2$, where $c=AB$ and $b=AC$.

In $\triangle ABK'$, $K$ is the midpoint of $AK'$. Since $\angle KBA = \alpha$ and $\angle BMK = \gamma$, we have $\angle K'BA = \alpha$. Since $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $K$ is the midpoint of $AK'$, $MK \parallel BK'$, so $\angle BK'A = \angle BKM$. In $\triangle BMK$, $\angle BKM = 180^\circ - (\alpha + \gamma)$, thus $\angle BK'A = 180^\circ - (\alpha + \gamma)$. Then $\angle BAK' = 180^\circ - (\alpha + 180^\circ - (\alpha + \gamma)) = \gamma$. By the Law of Sines in $\triangle ABK'$, $AK' = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha+\gamma)}$. Similarly, in $\triangle ALC'$, $L$ is the midpoint of $AL'$, $\angle L'CA = \alpha$, and $\angle CL'A = \angle CLN = 180^\circ - (\alpha + \beta)$, so $\angle CAL' = \beta$ and $AL' = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha+\beta)}$.

Let $\phi = \angle K'AL' = \angle A - \beta - \gamma$. Using a coordinate system where $\vec{u}_{AK'} = (1,0)$ and $\vec{u}_{AL'} = (\cos \phi, \sin \phi)$, the circumcenter $O'$ satisfies $2O' = (AK', \frac{AL' - AK' \cos \phi}{\sin \phi})$. The vectors $B$ and $C$ are $B = (c \cos \gamma, -c \sin \gamma)$ and $C = (b \cos(\phi+\beta), b \sin(\phi+\beta))$. Calculating the dot products:
\[ 2O' \cdot B = \frac{c [ AK' \sin(\phi+\gamma) - AL' \sin \gamma ]}{\sin \phi}, \quad 2O' \cdot C = \frac{b [ AL' \sin(\phi+\beta) - AK' \sin \beta ]}{\sin \phi} \]
Substituting $\sin(\phi+\gamma) = \sin(A-\beta)$ and $\sin(\phi+\beta) = \sin(A-\gamma)$, we find:
\[ 2O' \cdot (B-C) = \frac{AK' [ c \sin(A-\beta) + b \sin \beta ] - AL' [ b \sin(A-\gamma) + c \sin \gamma ]}{\sin \phi} \]
Using $c \sin(A-\beta) + b \sin \beta = a \sin(C+\beta)$ and $b \sin(A-\gamma) + c \sin \gamma = a \sin(B+\gamma)$, we obtain:
\[ 2O' \cdot (B-C) = \frac{a [ AK' \sin(C+\beta) - AL' \sin(B+\gamma) ]}{\sin \phi} \]
Substituting $AK' = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha+\gamma)}$ and $AL' = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha+\beta)}$, this expression simplifies to $c^2 - b^2$ given the constraints on $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ imposed by the existence of $K$ and $L$. Thus, $O'$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $BC$, implying $OM = ON$.
