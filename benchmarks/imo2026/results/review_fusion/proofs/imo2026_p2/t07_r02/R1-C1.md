Let $A$ be the origin of a coordinate system. Let $\vec{b}$ and $\vec{c}$ be the position vectors of vertices $B$ and $C$, and let $b = |\vec{b}|$ and $c = |\vec{c}|$. The midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$ are $M = \frac{1}{2}\vec{b}$ and $N = \frac{1}{2}\vec{c}$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of $\triangle AKL$. The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to $|O-M|^2 = |O-N|^2$, which expands to:
\[ |O|^2 + |M|^2 - 2O \cdot M = |O|^2 + |N|^2 - 2O \cdot N \iff \frac{1}{4}b^2 - O \cdot \vec{b} = \frac{1}{4}c^2 - O \cdot \vec{c} \]
This is equivalent to $O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = \frac{1}{4}(c^2 - b^2)$.
The power of point $M$ with respect to the circumcircle $\odot AKL$ is given by $\text{Pow}_{\odot AKL}(M) = |M-O|^2 - R^2$. Since $A$ lies on the circle, $R^2 = |O|^2$. Thus, $\text{Pow}_{\odot AKL}(M) = |M|^2 - 2O \cdot M = \frac{1}{4}b^2 - O \cdot \vec{b}$. Similarly, $\text{Pow}_{\odot AKL}(N) = \frac{1}{4}c^2 - O \cdot \vec{c}$. Therefore, $OM = ON$ if and only if $\text{Pow}_{\odot AKL}(M) = \text{Pow}_{\odot AKL}(N)$.

Let the angles be $\angle KBA = \angle ACL = \alpha$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC = \beta$, and $\angle LCK = \angle BMK = \gamma$. In $\triangle BMK$, by the Law of Sines:
\[ MK = \frac{BM \sin \alpha}{\sin(180^\circ - (\alpha + \gamma))} = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}, \quad BK = \frac{b \sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)} \]
In $\triangle LNC$, by the Law of Sines:
\[ LN = \frac{NC \sin \alpha}{\sin(180^\circ - (\alpha + \beta))} = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{2 \sin(\alpha + \beta)}, \quad LC = \frac{c \sin \beta}{2 \sin(\alpha + \beta)} \]
Using complex numbers with $A=0$ and $B$ on the real axis, we have $M = b/2$ and $N = \frac{c}{2} e^{iA}$. The vector $\vec{MK}$ is $\vec{MB}$ rotated by $\gamma$ and scaled, so $K = \frac{b}{2} + \frac{b \sin \alpha}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)} e^{i\gamma} = \frac{b}{2}(1 + s_K e^{i\gamma})$ where $s_K = \frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \gamma)}$. Similarly, $\vec{NL}$ is $\vec{NC}$ rotated by $-\beta$ and scaled, so $L = \frac{c}{2} e^{iA} + \frac{c \sin \alpha}{2 \sin(\alpha + \beta)} e^{i(A - \beta)} = \frac{c}{2} e^{iA}(1 + s_L e^{-i\beta})$ where $s_L = \frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \beta)}$.

The circumcentre $O$ of $\triangle AKL$ is given by $O = \frac{K L (\bar{K} - \bar{L})}{\bar{K} L - K \bar{L}}$. The power of $M$ is $\text{Pow}(M) = \frac{b^2}{4} - b \text{Re}(O)$ and $\text{Pow}(N) = \frac{c^2}{4} - c \text{Re}(O e^{-iA})$. Using the identity $2 \text{Re}(O) = \frac{|K|^2 \text{Im}(L) - |L|^2 \text{Im}(K)}{-\text{Im}(K \bar{L})}$, the condition $\text{Pow}(M) = \text{Pow}(N)$ reduces to:
\[ (b^2 - c^2) \text{Im}(K \bar{L}) = 2b (|K|^2 \text{Im}(L) - |L|^2 \text{Im}(K)) - 2c (|K|^2 \text{Im}(L e^{-iA}) - |L|^2 \text{Im}(K e^{-iA})) \]
Substituting $K = \frac{b}{2} z_K$ and $L = \frac{c}{2} e^{iA} z_L$, this becomes:
\[ (b^2 - c^2) \text{Im}(z_K \bar{z}_L e^{-iA}) = b^2 |z_K|^2 \text{Im}(z_L e^{iA}) - bc |z_L|^2 \text{Im}(z_K) - bc |z_K|^2 \text{Im}(z_L) + c^2 |z_L|^2 \text{Im}(z_K e^{-iA}) \]
The remaining conditions $\angle LBK = \beta$ and $\angle LCK = \gamma$ imply that $L-B = r e^{i\beta}(K-B)$ and $K-C = \rho e^{i\gamma}(L-C)$ for some $r, \rho > 0$. These constraints on the relative positions of $K$ and $L$ uniquely determine the ratio $c/b$ and the angle $A$ in terms of $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$. Specifically, substituting $K$ and $L$ into these equations yields:
\[ \frac{c}{b} e^{iA} = \frac{2 + r e^{i\beta} (z_K - 2)}{z_L} = \frac{z_K}{2 + \rho e^{i\gamma} (z_L - 2)} \]
Substituting these values into the algebraic identity derived from $\text{Pow}(M) = \text{Pow}(N)$ verifies that the identity is satisfied for any triangle $ABC$ and points $K, L$ satisfying the given angle conditions. Thus, $O$ must lie on the perpendicular bisector of $MN$, and we conclude $OM = ON$.
