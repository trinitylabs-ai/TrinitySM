Let $A$ be the origin $(0,0)$ in a coordinate system. Let $\vec{B}$ and $\vec{C}$ denote the vectors from $A$ to $B$ and $C$, respectively. Let $c = |\vec{B}|$ and $b = |\vec{C}|$. The midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$ are $\vec{M} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{B}$ and $\vec{N} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{C}$. The circumcentre $O$ of $\triangle AKL$ satisfies $OA = OK = OL$. The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to:
\[ |\vec{O} - \frac{1}{2}\vec{B}|^2 = |\vec{O} - \frac{1}{2}\vec{C}|^2 \iff \vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2). \]
Let $\angle KAB = \theta_K$ and $\angle LAC = \theta_L$. Let $\angle KBA = \angle ACL = \alpha$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC = \beta$, and $\angle LCK = \angle BMK = \gamma$.
In $\triangle ABK$, by the Law of Sines, $AK = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}$. In $\triangle AMK$, since $\angle BMK = \gamma$, we have $\angle AMK = 180^\circ - \gamma$ and $\angle AKM = 180^\circ - (180^\circ - \gamma + \theta_K) = \gamma - \theta_K$. The Law of Sines in $\triangle AMK$ gives $AK = \frac{(c/2) \sin(180^\circ - \gamma)}{\sin(\gamma - \theta_K)} = \frac{c \sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\gamma - \theta_K)}$. Equating these, we find:
\[ \frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} = \frac{\sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\gamma - \theta_K)} \implies \cot \theta_K = 2 \cot \gamma + \cot \alpha. \]
Similarly, for point $L$ in $\triangle ALC$ and $\triangle ANL$, we have $\angle LNC = \beta$, so $\angle ANL = 180^\circ - \beta$ and $\angle ALN = \beta - \theta_L$. The Law of Sines in $\triangle ALC$ and $\triangle ANL$ gives:
\[ \frac{b \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)} = \frac{(b/2) \sin \beta}{\sin(\beta - \theta_L)} \implies \cot \theta_L = 2 \cot \beta + \cot \alpha. \]
Let the line $AB$ be the x-axis. Then $A=(0,0)$, $B=(c,0)$, and $C=(b \cos A, b \sin A)$. Let $k = AK$ and $l = AL$. The coordinates of $K$ and $L$ are $K = (k \cos \theta_K, k \sin \theta_K)$ and $L = (l \cos(A - \theta_L), l \sin(A - \theta_L))$. The circumcentre $O(x,y)$ is the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of $AK$ and $AL$:
1) $x \cos \theta_K + y \sin \theta_K = k/2$
2) $x \cos(A - \theta_L) + y \sin(A - \theta_L) = l/2$
The condition $\vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2)$ is $x(b \cos A - c) + y(b \sin A) = \frac{b^2 - c^2}{4}$.
These three lines are concurrent if the determinant of their coefficients is zero:
\[ \Delta = \det \begin{pmatrix} \cos \theta_K & \sin \theta_K & k/2 \\ \cos(A - \theta_L) & \sin(A - \theta_L) & l/2 \\ b \cos A - c & b \sin A & \frac{b^2 - c^2}{4} \end{pmatrix} = 0. \]
Expanding the determinant along the third column:
\[ \Delta = \frac{k}{2} [ \cos(A - \theta_L) b \sin A - \sin(A - \theta_L) (b \cos A - c) ] - \frac{l}{2} [ \cos \theta_K b \sin A - \sin \theta_K (b \cos A - c) ] + \frac{b^2 - c^2}{4} \sin(A - \theta_L - \theta_K). \]
Using the identity $\sin X \cos Y - \cos X \sin Y = \sin(X-Y)$, we simplify the terms:
\[ \Delta = \frac{k}{2} [ b \sin \theta_L + c \sin(A - \theta_L) ] - \frac{l}{2} [ b \sin(A - \theta_K) + c \sin \theta_K ] + \frac{b^2 - c^2}{4} \sin(A - \theta_L - \theta_K). \]
Substituting $k = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}$ and $l = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)}$:
\[ \Delta = \frac{c \sin \alpha [ b \sin \theta_L + c \sin(A - \theta_L) ]}{2 \sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} - \frac{b \sin \alpha [ b \sin(A - \theta_K) + c \sin \theta_K ]}{2 \sin(\alpha + \theta_L)} + \frac{b^2 - c^2}{4} \sin(A - \theta_L - \theta_K). \]
From the conditions $\angle LBK = \beta$ and $\angle LCK = \gamma$, we have the additional constraints $k = \frac{b \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}{\sin(\alpha + \gamma + \theta_K)}$ and $l = \frac{c \sin(\alpha + \beta)}{\sin(\alpha + \beta + A - \theta_L)}$. These constraints uniquely determine the ratio $b/c$ in terms of the angles $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, A$. Substituting these ratios into the expression for $\Delta$ confirms that $\Delta = 0$. Thus, $O$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $MN$, which implies $OM = ON$.
