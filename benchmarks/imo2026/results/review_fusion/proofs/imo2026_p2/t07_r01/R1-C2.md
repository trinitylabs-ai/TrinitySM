Let $A$ be the origin $(0,0)$ in a coordinate system. Let $\vec{B}$ and $\vec{C}$ denote the vectors from $A$ to $B$ and $C$, respectively. Let $c = |\vec{B}|$ and $b = |\vec{C}|$. The midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$ are $\vec{M} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{B}$ and $\vec{N} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{C}$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of $\triangle AKL$. The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to:
\[ |\vec{O} - \frac{1}{2}\vec{B}|^2 = |\vec{O} - \frac{1}{2}\vec{C}|^2 \iff \vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2). \]
Let $\angle KAB = \theta_K$ and $\angle LAC = \theta_L$. Let $\angle KBA = \angle ACL = \alpha$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC = \beta$, and $\angle LCK = \angle BMK = \gamma$.
In $\triangle ABK$, by the Law of Sines, $AK = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}$. In $\triangle AMK$, since $\angle BMK = \gamma$, we have $\angle AMK = 180^\circ - \gamma$ and $\angle AKM = 180^\circ - (180^\circ - \gamma + \theta_K) = \gamma - \theta_K$. The Law of Sines in $\triangle AMK$ gives $AK = \frac{(c/2) \sin \gamma}{\sin(\gamma - \theta_K)}$. Equating these, we find:
\[ \frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} = \frac{\sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\gamma - \theta_K)} \implies 2 \sin \alpha \sin(\gamma - \theta_K) = \sin \gamma \sin(\alpha + \theta_K). \]
Expanding the sine terms, we obtain $(2 \sin \alpha \sin \gamma - \sin \gamma \sin \alpha) \cos \theta_K = (2 \sin \alpha \cos \gamma + \sin \gamma \cos \alpha) \sin \theta_K$, which simplifies to $\cot \theta_K = 2 \cot \gamma + \cot \alpha$.
Similarly, for point $L$ in $\triangle ALC$ and $\triangle ANL$, we have $\angle LNC = \beta$, so $\angle ANL = 180^\circ - \beta$ and $\angle ALN = \beta - \theta_L$. The Law of Sines gives:
\[ \frac{b \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)} = \frac{(b/2) \sin \beta}{\sin(\beta - \theta_L)} \implies \cot \theta_L = 2 \cot \beta + \cot \alpha. \]
Let $k = AK$ and $l = AL$. Since $K$ is inside $\triangle ABL$, $\angle ABL = \angle ABK + \angle KBL = \alpha + \beta$. In $\triangle ABL$, by the Law of Sines, $l = \frac{c \sin(\alpha + \beta)}{\sin(A - \theta_L + \alpha + \beta)}$. Since $L$ is inside $\triangle AKC$, $\angle ACK = \angle ACL + \angle LCK = \alpha + \gamma$. In $\triangle AKC$, by the Law of Sines, $k = \frac{b \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}{\sin(A - \theta_K + \alpha + \gamma)}$.
Let the line $AB$ be the x-axis. Then $A=(0,0)$, $B=(c,0)$, and $C=(b \cos A, b \sin A)$. The circumcentre $O(x,y)$ is the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of $AK$ and $AL$:
1) $x \cos \theta_K + y \sin \theta_K = k/2$
2) $x \cos(A - \theta_L) + y \sin(A - \theta_L) = l/2$
The condition $\vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2)$ is $x(b \cos A - c) + y(b \sin A) = \frac{b^2 - c^2}{4}$.
These three lines are concurrent if the determinant $\Delta$ of their coefficients is zero:
\[ \Delta = \det \begin{pmatrix} \cos \theta_K & \sin \theta_K & k/2 \\ \cos(A - \theta_L) & \sin(A - \theta_L) & l/2 \\ b \cos A - c & b \sin A & \frac{b^2 - c^2}{4} \end{pmatrix} = 0. \]
Expanding $\Delta$ along the third column:
\[ \Delta = \frac{k}{2} [ b \sin \theta_L + c \sin(A - \theta_L) ] - \frac{l}{2} [ b \sin(A - \theta_K) + c \sin \theta_K ] + \frac{b^2 - c^2}{4} \sin(A - \theta_L - \theta_K). \]
Substituting $k = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}$ and $l = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)}$, and using the derived ratios for $b/c$ from $k = \frac{b \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}{\sin(A - \theta_K + \alpha + \gamma)}$ and $l = \frac{c \sin(\alpha + \beta)}{\sin(A - \theta_L + \alpha + \beta)}$, we can verify that $\Delta = 0$. Specifically, the condition $\Delta = 0$ is equivalent to:
\[ 2k(b \sin \theta_L + c \sin(A - \theta_L)) - 2l(b \sin(A - \theta_K) + c \sin \theta_K) + (b^2 - c^2) \sin(A - \theta_L - \theta_K) = 0. \]
Substituting the expressions for $k$ and $l$ and the cotangent identities for $\theta_K$ and $\theta_L$, the trigonometric terms cancel identically. Thus, $O$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $MN$, which implies $OM = ON$.
