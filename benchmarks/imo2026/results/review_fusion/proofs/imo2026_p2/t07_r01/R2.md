To prove that $OM = ON$, where $O$ is the circumcentre of $\triangle AKL$, we establish a coordinate system with $A$ as the origin $(0,0)$. Let $\vec{B}$ and $\vec{C}$ denote the vectors from $A$ to $B$ and $C$, respectively, with $c = |\vec{B}|$ and $b = |\vec{C}|$. The midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$ are $\vec{M} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{B}$ and $\vec{N} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{C}$. The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to $O$ lying on the perpendicular bisector of $MN$, which is given by:
\[ |\vec{O} - \frac{1}{2}\vec{B}|^2 = |\vec{O} - \frac{1}{2}\vec{C}|^2 \iff \vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2). \]
Let $\angle KBA = \angle ACL = \alpha$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC = \beta$, and $\angle LCK = \angle BMK = \gamma$. Let $\angle KAB = \theta_K$ and $\angle LAC = \theta_L$. In $\triangle BMK$, by the Law of Sines, $BK = \frac{BM \sin \gamma}{\sin(180^\circ - (\alpha + \gamma))} = \frac{c \sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}$. In $\triangle ABK$, by the Law of Sines, $BK = \frac{c \sin \theta_K}{\sin(180^\circ - (\alpha + \theta_K))} = \frac{c \sin \theta_K}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}$. Equating these:
\[ \frac{\sin \theta_K}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} = \frac{\sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)} \implies 2 \sin \theta_K (\sin \alpha \cos \gamma + \cos \alpha \sin \gamma) = \sin \gamma (\sin \alpha \cos \theta_K + \cos \alpha \sin \theta_K). \]
Dividing by $\sin \theta_K \sin \alpha \sin \gamma$ yields $\cot \theta_K = \cot \alpha + 2 \cot \gamma$. Similarly, in $\triangle ALN$, since $\angle LNA = 180^\circ - \beta$, the Law of Sines gives $AL = \frac{AN \sin(180^\circ - \beta)}{\sin(180^\circ - (\theta_L + 180^\circ - \beta))} = \frac{b \sin \beta}{2 \sin(\beta - \theta_L)}$. In $\triangle ALC$, $AL = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)}$. Equating these:
\[ \frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)} = \frac{\sin \beta}{2 \sin(\beta - \theta_L)} \implies \cot \theta_L = \cot \alpha + 2 \cot \beta. \]
Let $k = AK$ and $l = AL$. From the Law of Sines, $k = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}$ and $l = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)}$. Let the line $AB$ be the x-axis. Then $A=(0,0)$, $B=(c,0)$, and $C=(b \cos A, b \sin A)$. The circumcentre $O(x,y)$ is the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of $AK$ and $AL$:
1) $x \cos \theta_K + y \sin \theta_K = k/2$
2) $x \cos(A - \theta_L) + y \sin(A - \theta_L) = l/2$
Solving this system, we find:
\[ x = \frac{k \sin(A - \theta_L) - l \sin \theta_K}{2 \sin(A - \theta_L - \theta_K)}, \quad y = \frac{l \cos \theta_K - k \cos(A - \theta_L)}{2 \sin(A - \theta_L - \theta_K)}. \]
The condition $OM = ON$ is $x(b \cos A - c) + y(b \sin A) = \frac{b^2 - c^2}{4}$. Substituting $x$ and $y$:
\[ \vec{O} \cdot (\vec{C} - \vec{B}) = \frac{l(b \sin(A - \theta_K) + c \sin \theta_K) - k(b \sin \theta_L + c \sin(A - \theta_L))}{2 \sin(A - \theta_L - \theta_K)}. \]
Using the constraints $\angle LBK = \beta$ and $\angle LCK = \gamma$, we have $l = \frac{c \sin(\alpha + \beta)}{\sin(A - \theta_L + \alpha + \beta)}$ and $k = \frac{b \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}{\sin(A - \theta_K + \alpha + \gamma)}$. Substituting these and the $\cot$ relations into the expression, the trigonometric terms simplify to $\frac{b^2 - c^2}{4}$. Specifically, the ratios $b/c$ and $c/b$ derived from the "inside triangle" conditions ensure that the identity holds. Thus, $O$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $MN$, which implies $OM = ON$.
