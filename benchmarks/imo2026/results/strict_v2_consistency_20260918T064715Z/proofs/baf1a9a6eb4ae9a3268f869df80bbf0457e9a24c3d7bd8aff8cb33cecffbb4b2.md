Let $S_n$ be the set of positive integers $x$ such that $\gcd(x, a_i) > 1$ for all $i = 1, \dots, n$. The sequence is defined by $a_{n+1} = \min \{ x \in \mathbb{Z}^+ : x > a_n \text{ and } x \in S_n \}$.

First, we observe that $a_1, \dots, a_n \in S_n$ for all $n \ge 1$. For $n=1$, $a_1 > 1$, so $\gcd(a_1, a_1) > 1$. For $n > 1$, $a_n$ is chosen such that $a_n \in S_{n-1}$, and since $\gcd(a_n, a_n) = a_n > 1$, we have $a_n \in S_n$. Since $S_n \subseteq S_{n-1}$, it follows that $a_1, \dots, a_{n-1} \in S_n$. We prove by induction that $a_1, \dots, a_n$ are exactly the first $n$ elements of $S_n$ that are greater than or equal to $a_1$. For $n=1$, this is trivial. Suppose it holds for $n$. Then $a_1, \dots, a_n$ are the first $n$ elements of $S_n$ in $[a_1, \infty)$. By definition, $a_{n+1}$ is the smallest element of $S_n$ greater than $a_n$, so $a_1, \dots, a_{n+1}$ are the first $n+1$ elements of $S_n$ in $[a_1, \infty)$. Since $S_{n+1} \subseteq S_n$ and $a_1, \dots, a_{n+1} \in S_{n+1}$, they must be the first $n+1$ elements of $S_{n+1}$ in $[a_1, \infty)$.

Let $P_i$ be the set of prime factors of $a_i$. An integer $x \in S_n$ if and only if the set of prime factors of $x$, $P(x)$, is a hitting set for the collection $\mathcal{P}_n = \{P_1, \dots, P_n\}$. Let $\mathcal{H}_n$ be the set of minimal hitting sets for $\mathcal{P}_n$. Then $x \in S_n$ if and only if $P(x)$ contains some $H \in \mathcal{H}_n$, which is equivalent to $x$ being a multiple of $m(H) = \prod_{p \in H} p$. Thus $S_n = \bigcup_{H \in \mathcal{H}_n} m(H)\mathbb{Z}$, which is a periodic set.

Since $S_1 \supseteq S_2 \supseteq \dots$, let $m_n = \min S_n$. The sequence $(m_n)$ is non-decreasing. Since it consists of positive integers, it must eventually stabilize. Let $m_n = m$ for all $n \ge N$. Then $m \in S_n$ for all $n \ge N$, meaning $\gcd(m, a_n) > 1$ for all $n \ge N$. Thus, for all $n \ge N$, $P_n \cap P(m) \neq \emptyset$.

Now consider the minimal hitting sets $\mathcal{H}_n$ for $n \ge N$. Let $H \in \mathcal{H}_n$. Since $H$ must hit $P_1, \dots, P_N$, $H$ must contain some minimal hitting set $H_N \in \mathcal{H}_N$. For any $p \in H$, by the minimality of $H$, there exists some $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$ such that $H \cap P_i = \{p\}$. If $i > N$, then since $P_i \cap P(m) \neq \emptyset$ and $H \cap P_i = \{p\}$, we must have $p \in P(m)$. Thus, $H \subseteq H_N \cup P(m)$. Since $\mathcal{H}_N$ and $P(m)$ are finite, there are only finitely many such sets $H$. Consequently, the sequence of sets $S_n$ (which are determined by $\mathcal{H}_n$) is a non-increasing sequence of sets chosen from a finite collection. Thus, $S_n$ must stabilize to some $S_N$ for all $n \ge N$.

Once $S_n = S_N$ for $n \ge N$, the sequence $(a_n)$ for $n \ge N$ consists of the elements of $S_N$ in increasing order. Since $a_1, \dots, a_N$ are the first $N$ elements of $S_N$ in $[a_1, \infty)$, the entire sequence $(a_n)_{n \ge 1}$ consists of the elements of $S_N$ in $[a_1, \infty)$ in increasing order. Let $L$ be the period of $S_N$ and $T$ be the number of elements of $S_N$ in any interval of length $L$. Then $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ for every positive integer $n$.
