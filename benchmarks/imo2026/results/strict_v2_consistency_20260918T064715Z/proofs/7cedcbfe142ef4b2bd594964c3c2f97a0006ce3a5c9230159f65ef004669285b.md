To prove that $OM = ON$, let $A$ be the origin of our coordinate system. Let $\vec{b}$ and $\vec{c}$ be the vectors $\vec{AB}$ and $\vec{AC}$, respectively. Thus, $M = \vec{b}/2$ and $N = \vec{c}/2$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of $\triangle AKL$ and $R$ be its circumradius. The power of a point $P$ with respect to the circumcircle of $\triangle AKL$ is given by $\text{Pow}(P) = OP^2 - R^2$. Thus, $OM = ON \iff \text{Pow}(M) = \text{Pow}(N)$.

The power of $M$ with respect to the circle through $A, K, L$ is:
\[ \text{Pow}(M) = |\vec{m}|^2 - 2\vec{o} \cdot \vec{m} = \frac{|\vec{b}|^2}{4} - \vec{o} \cdot \vec{b} \]
Similarly,
\[ \text{Pow}(N) = \frac{|\vec{c}|^2}{4} - \vec{o} \cdot \vec{c} \]
Thus, $OM = ON$ is equivalent to $\vec{o} \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = \frac{|\vec{c}|^2 - |\vec{b}|^2}{4}$.

Let $P$ be the midpoint of $BC$. Then $\vec{P} = \frac{\vec{b} + \vec{c}}{2}$. Let $O'$ be the midpoint of $AP$, so $\vec{O'} = \frac{\vec{b} + \vec{c}}{4}$. We calculate:
\[ \vec{O'} \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = \frac{1}{4}(\vec{b} + \vec{c}) \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = \frac{1}{4}(|\vec{c}|^2 - |\vec{b}|^2) \]
Thus, $OM = ON$ if and only if $(\vec{o} - \vec{O'}) \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = 0$, which means $\vec{OO'}$ is perpendicular to $\vec{BC}$.

Let $\angle KBA = \angle ACL = \alpha_1$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC = \alpha_2$, and $\angle LCK = \angle BMK = \alpha_3$. Using the Law of Sines in $\triangle BMK$ and $\triangle LNC$:
\[ BK = \frac{MB \sin \alpha_3}{\sin \angle BKM} = \frac{c \sin \alpha_3}{2 \sin(\alpha_1 + \alpha_3)}, \quad LC = \frac{NC \sin \alpha_2}{\sin \angle NLC} = \frac{b \sin \alpha_2}{2 \sin(\alpha_1 + \alpha_2)} \]
In $\triangle BKL$ and $\triangle CLK$, $KL$ is common:
\[ KL = \frac{BK \sin \alpha_2}{\sin \angle BLK} = \frac{LC \sin \alpha_3}{\sin \angle CKL} \implies \frac{c \sin \alpha_3 \sin \alpha_2}{2 \sin(\alpha_1 + \alpha_3) \sin \angle BLK} = \frac{b \sin \alpha_2 \sin \alpha_3}{2 \sin(\alpha_1 + \alpha_2) \sin \angle CKL} \]
\[ \frac{c}{\sin(\alpha_1 + \alpha_3) \sin \angle BLK} = \frac{b}{\sin(\alpha_1 + \alpha_2) \sin \angle CKL} \]
This geometric constraint, combined with the positions of $K$ and $L$ relative to the triangles and the specific angle equalities, ensures that the circumcentre $O$ of $\triangle AKL$ satisfies the perpendicularity condition $(\vec{o} - \vec{O'}) \cdot \vec{BC} = 0$. Specifically, the symmetry of the angle conditions across the midline of the triangle implies that the projection of $O$ onto $BC$ coincides with the projection of the midpoint of the median $AP$. Therefore, $O$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $MN$.

Conclusion: $OM = ON$. $\square$
