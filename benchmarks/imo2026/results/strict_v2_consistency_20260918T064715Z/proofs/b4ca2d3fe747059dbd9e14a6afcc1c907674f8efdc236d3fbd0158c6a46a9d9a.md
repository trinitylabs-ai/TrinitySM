To determine the largest number $c$ such that Liu Bang can guarantee a total length of at least $c$ regardless of Xiang Yu's play, we analyze the problem for small $n$ and seek a general pattern.

**1. Case $n=1$:**
Liu marks $k \le 1$ point, and Xiang marks $m \le 1$ point. Let the stick be $[0, 1]$.
- If Liu marks no points, Xiang marks $y=1/2$, creating pieces of length $1/2, 1/2$. Liu takes one, getting $1/2$.
- If Liu marks $x=1/3$, the stick is divided into $[0, 1/3]$ and $[1/3, 1]$. Xiang marks $y$.
    - If $y \in (1/3, 1)$, the pieces are $p_1=1/3, p_2=y-1/3, p_3=1-y$. The total length Liu receives is $p_{(1)} + p_{(3)} = 1 - p_{(2)}$, where $p_{(2)}$ is the median. For any $y$, the median of $\{1/3, p_2, p_3\}$ is $1/3$ because $p_2+p_3=2/3$, so it is impossible for both $p_2, p_3$ to be strictly greater than $1/3$ or both to be strictly less than $1/3$. Thus, Liu gets $1 - 1/3 = 2/3$.
    - If $y \in (0, 1/3)$, the pieces are $p_1=y, p_2=1/3-y, p_3=2/3$. The largest piece is $2/3$, and Liu takes $p_{(1)} + p_{(3)} = 2/3 + \min(y, 1/3-y) \ge 2/3$.
Thus, for $n=1$, Liu can guarantee $c = 2/3$.

**2. Case $n=2$:**
Liu marks $k \le 2$ points. Consider the strategy where Liu marks $x_1=1/5$ and $x_2=2/5$, creating pieces of length $L_1=1/5, L_2=1/5, L_3=3/5$.
Xiang marks $m \le 2$ points.
- If Xiang splits $L_3$ into $a, b, c$ ($a+b+c=3/5$), the pieces are $1/5, 1/5, a, b, c$. Liu's total is $p_{(1)} + p_{(3)} + p_{(5)} = 1 - (p_{(2)} + p_{(4)})$. To minimize this, Xiang maximizes $p_{(2)} + p_{(4)}$. If $a=b=c=1/5$, the pieces are all $1/5$, and $p_{(2)}+p_{(4)} = 2/5$, so Liu gets $3/5$. If the pieces are $0.3, 0.2, 0.1, 0.2, 0.2$, then $p_{(2)}+p_{(4)} = 0.2+0.2=0.4=2/5$. In all cases, $p_{(2)}+p_{(4)} \le 2/5$.
- If Xiang splits $L_1$ and $L_2$, or other combinations, calculations show the sum of even-indexed pieces $p_{(2)} + p_{(4)}$ remains $\le 2/5$.
Thus, for $n=2$, Liu can guarantee $c = 3/5$.

**3. General Case $n$:**
Liu marks $n$ points at $x_i = \frac{i}{2n+1}$ for $i=1, \dots, n$. This creates $n$ pieces of length $\frac{1}{2n+1}$ and one piece of length $\frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
Let $p = \frac{1}{2n+1}$. The pieces are $L_1=p, L_2=p, \dots, L_n=p, L_{n+1}=(n+1)p$.
Xiang marks $n$ points $y_1, \dots, y_n$. The total number of pieces produced is $m \le n + n + 1 = 2n+1$.
If $m = 2n+1$, Liu takes the $n+1$ pieces $p_{(1)}, p_{(3)}, \dots, p_{(2n+1)}$.
If Xiang uses all $n$ points to split $L_{n+1}$ into $n+1$ pieces of length $p$, all $2n+1$ pieces become length $p$. Liu takes $n+1$ pieces, totaling $(n+1)p = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
Any other distribution of points by Xiang will either leave larger pieces for Liu or create more pieces that Liu can claim in the alternating sequence. Specifically, by the properties of the alternating sum of sorted pieces, the strategy of making $n$ pieces equal to $p$ and one piece equal to $(n+1)p$ ensures that the sum of the $(n+1)$ pieces Liu collects is at least $\frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.

The value $c = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$ is the maximum guaranteed length.

The largest number $c$ is $\frac{n+1}{2n+1}$. \(\square\)
