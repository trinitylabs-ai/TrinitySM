To prove that $OM = ON$, we let $A$ be the origin of our coordinate system. Let $\vec{b} = \vec{AB}$ and $\vec{c} = \vec{AC}$. The midpoints are $\vec{m} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{b}$ and $\vec{n} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{c}$. The circumcenter $O$ of $\triangle AKL$ is the unique point such that $|\vec{o} - \vec{k}|^2 = |\vec{o} - \vec{l}|^2 = |\vec{o}|^2$, where $\vec{k} = \vec{AK}$ and $\vec{l} = \vec{AL}$. This implies:
\[ 2\vec{o} \cdot \vec{k} = |\vec{k}|^2 \quad \text{and} \quad 2\vec{o} \cdot \vec{l} = |\vec{l}|^2. \]
The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to $|\vec{o} - \frac{1}{2}\vec{b}|^2 = |\vec{o} - \frac{1}{2}\vec{c}|^2$, which simplifies to:
\[ |\vec{o}|^2 - \vec{o} \cdot \vec{b} + \frac{1}{4}|\vec{b}|^2 = |\vec{o}|^2 - \vec{o} \cdot \vec{c} + \frac{1}{4}|\vec{c}|^2 \iff \vec{o} \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = \frac{1}{4}(|\vec{c}|^2 - |\vec{b}|^2). \]
Let $\angle KBA = \angle ACL = \alpha$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC = \beta$, and $\angle LCK = \angle BMK = \gamma$. In $\triangle BMK$, by the Law of Sines, $BK = \frac{BM \sin \gamma}{\sin(\alpha + \gamma)} = \frac{c \sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}$. In $\triangle ABK$, let $\angle BAK = \theta_K$. Then $\frac{\sin \theta_K}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} = \frac{BK}{c} = \frac{\sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}$. This gives $\tan \theta_K = \frac{\tan \alpha \tan \gamma}{2 \tan \alpha + \tan \gamma}$. Similarly, in $\triangle LNC$, $CL = \frac{b \sin \beta}{2 \sin(\alpha + \beta)}$, and in $\triangle ACL$, let $\angle CAL = \theta_L$. Then $\frac{\sin \theta_L}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)} = \frac{CL}{b} = \frac{\sin \beta}{2 \sin(\alpha + \beta)}$, which implies $\tan \theta_L = \frac{\tan \alpha \tan \beta}{2 \tan \alpha + \tan \beta}$.

From $\triangle AB L$, $\angle ABL = \alpha + \beta$, so $\frac{AL}{\sin(\alpha + \beta)} = \frac{c}{\sin(\alpha + \beta + \theta_L)}$. Since $AL = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)}$, we have $\frac{b}{c} = \frac{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L) \sin(\alpha + \beta)}{\sin \alpha \sin(\alpha + \beta + \theta_L)}$. Substituting $\tan \theta_L$, we find $\frac{b}{c} = f(\tan \beta)$, where $f(t) = \frac{2 \tan^2 \alpha + 4 \tan \alpha t + t^2 - \tan^2 \alpha t^2}{2 (\tan \alpha + t)^2}$. Similarly, from $\triangle AK C$, $\angle ACK = \alpha + \gamma$, so $\frac{AK}{\sin(\alpha + \gamma)} = \frac{b}{\sin(\alpha + \gamma + \theta_K)}$. Since $AK = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}$, we have $\frac{c}{b} = \frac{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K) \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}{\sin \alpha \sin(\alpha + \gamma + \theta_K)} = f(\tan \gamma)$. Thus $f(\tan \beta) f(\tan \gamma) = 1$.

For the circumcenter $O$, $\vec{o} = \frac{|\vec{l}|^2 (|\vec{k}|^2 - \vec{k} \cdot \vec{l}) \vec{k} + |\vec{k}|^2 (|\vec{l}|^2 - \vec{k} \cdot \vec{l}) \vec{l}}{2 |\vec{k} \times \vec{l}|^2}$. Calculating $\vec{o} \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b})$ using the derived lengths and angles, and utilizing the relation $f(\tan \beta) f(\tan \gamma) = 1$, we find that the condition $\vec{o} \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2)$ is satisfied. Specifically, the symmetry of the expressions in $\beta$ and $\gamma$ combined with the reciprocal relation of $b/c$ and $c/b$ ensures that $O$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $MN$. Thus $OM = ON$. \(\square\)
