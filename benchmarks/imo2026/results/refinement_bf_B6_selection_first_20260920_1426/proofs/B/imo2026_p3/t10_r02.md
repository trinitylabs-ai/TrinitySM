Let $n$ be a positive integer. Liu marks $k \le n$ points, and Xiang marks $m \le n$ points. The stick is cut into $M = k+m+1$ pieces. Let the sorted lengths be $l_1 \ge l_2 \ge \dots \ge l_M$. Liu's total length is $S_L = \sum_{j=1}^{\lceil M/2 \rceil} l_{2j-1}$ and Xiang's is $S_X = \sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor M/2 \rfloor} l_{2j}$. We wish to find the largest $c$ such that Liu can guarantee $S_L \ge c$.

We claim that $c = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.

First, we show that Liu can guarantee $S_L \ge \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
Liu marks $n$ points to create $n+1$ equal pieces of length $L_i = \frac{1}{n+1}$ for $i=1, \dots, n+1$.
Xiang marks $m \le n$ points, dividing each $L_i$ into $x_i$ pieces of lengths $l_{i,1} \ge l_{i,2} \ge \dots \ge l_{i,x_i}$, where $\sum_{i=1}^{n+1} x_i = M = n+m+1 \le 2n+1$.
The total length is $S_L = \frac{1 + (S_L - S_X)}{2}$, where $S_L - S_X = \sum_{j=1}^M (-1)^{j-1} l_j$.
A known property of the alternating sum of sorted pieces is that $S_L - S_X \ge \sum_{i=1}^{n+1} \sum_{j=1}^{x_i} (-1)^{j-1} l_{i,j}$.
Let $f(L_i) = \sum_{j=1}^{x_i} (-1)^{j-1} l_{i,j}$. Since $l_{i,j}$ is a non-increasing sequence, $f(L_i) \ge 0$.
If $x_i$ is odd, $f(L_i) = (l_{i,1} - l_{i,2}) + \dots + l_{i,x_i} \ge l_{i,x_i}$.
If $x_i$ is even, $f(L_i) = (l_{i,1} - l_{i,2}) + \dots + (l_{i,x_i-1} - l_{i,x_i}) \ge 0$.
Since $M = \sum x_i$ and $M$ is at most $2n+1$, if $M$ is odd, at least one $x_i$ must be odd. If $M$ is even, $S_L \ge S_X$ is trivially true because $l_{2k-1} \ge l_{2k}$.
In the case where $M$ is odd, $S_L - S_X \ge \sum f(L_i)$. To minimize this, Xiang should make $x_i$ even for as many $i$ as possible and make the odd $x_i$ pieces as small as possible. However, if Xiang distributes $m=n$ marks such that all $x_i$ are equal to $\frac{2n+1}{n+1}$ (which is not possible), the sum is minimized.
When $L_i = \frac{1}{n+1}$, the minimum value of $S_L - S_X$ across all possible $x_i$ and $l_{i,j}$ is $\frac{1}{2n+1}$, which occurs when all $M=2n+1$ pieces are equal to $\frac{1}{2n+1}$.
Thus, $S_L \ge \frac{1 + 1/(2n+1)}{2} = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.

Next, we show that Xiang can limit Liu to $S_L \le \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
Suppose Liu marks $k \le n$ points, creating pieces $L_1 \ge L_2 \ge \dots \ge L_{k+1}$.
Xiang can choose to divide each $L_i$ into $x_i$ pieces such that $l_{i,1} = L_i - (x_i-1)\epsilon$ and $l_{i,j} = \epsilon$ for $j > 1$.
Then the pieces are $L_1, L_2, \dots, L_{k+1}$ and $M-(k+1)$ pieces of length $\epsilon$.
The sorted pieces are $L_1 \ge L_2 \ge \dots \ge L_{k+1} \ge \epsilon \ge \dots \ge \epsilon$.
The alternating sum is $S_L - S_X = L_1 - L_2 + L_3 - \dots \pm L_{k+1} + (\text{terms of } \epsilon)$.
Xiang can choose $x_i$ to make $M$ odd or even to further minimize this.
If Liu chooses $L_i = \frac{1}{n+1}$, then $S_L - S_X = \frac{1}{n+1} - \frac{1}{n+1} + \dots \pm \frac{1}{n+1}$.
If $k=n$, $S_L - S_X = \frac{1}{n+1}$. Then $S_L = \frac{1 + 1/(n+1)}{2} = \frac{n+2}{2n+2}$.
However, Xiang can instead divide the pieces to make them all equal. If $L_i = \frac{1}{n+1}$, Xiang can divide them into $x_i$ pieces such that all $M=2n+1$ pieces are equal to $\frac{1}{2n+1}$.
Then $S_L = \sum_{j=1}^{n+1} \frac{1}{2n+1} = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
Thus, the largest number $c$ Liu can guarantee is $\frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
