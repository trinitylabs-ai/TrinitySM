Let $n$ be a positive integer. Let the pieces resulting from the cuts be $l_{(1)} \ge l_{(2)} \ge \dots \ge l_{(N)}$, where $N$ is the total number of pieces. Since Liu moves first and the players take turns claiming the largest available pieces, Liu receives the pieces $l_{(1)}, l_{(3)}, \dots$ and Xiang receives $l_{(2)}, l_{(4)}, \dots$. Liu's total length is $V_L = \sum_{k=1}^{\lceil N/2 \rceil} l_{(2k-1)}$.

We claim that the largest number $c$ Liu can guarantee is $c = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.

**1. Lower Bound: $V_L \ge \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$**
Liu marks $n$ points to create $n+1$ pieces of lengths $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_{n+1}$, where $x_1 = \frac{1}{2n+1}$ and $x_2 = x_3 = \dots = x_{n+1} = \frac{2}{2n+1}$. Note that $\sum_{i=1}^{n+1} x_i = \frac{1 + 2n}{2n+1} = 1$.
Xiang then marks $m \le n$ points. Let $k_i$ be the number of points Xiang marks in piece $x_i$, so $\sum_{i=1}^{n+1} k_i = m \le n$.
The total number of pieces is $N = (n+1) + m \le 2n+1$.
Let $y_{i,j}$ for $j=1, \dots, k_i+1$ be the pieces resulting from the division of $x_i$.
The total length of pieces resulting from $x_2, \dots, x_{n+1}$ is $\sum_{i=2}^{n+1} x_i = \frac{2n}{2n+1}$. Let these pieces be $z_1, z_2, \dots, z_{n+m'}$, where $m' = \sum_{i=2}^{n+1} k_i \le m \le n$.
Let $z_{(1)} \ge z_{(2)} \ge \dots \ge z_{(n+m')}$ be these pieces sorted.
The total length $V_L$ is the sum of the odd-indexed pieces of the set $\{z_1, \dots, z_{n+m'}, y_{1,1}, \dots, y_{1,k_1+1}\}$.
If Xiang does not cut $x_1$ ($k_1=0$), the pieces are $z_{(1)}, \dots, z_{(n+m')}$ and $x_1 = \frac{1}{2n+1}$.
If $x_1$ is the $k$-th largest piece $l_{(k)}$:
- If $k$ is odd, $V_L = \sum_{j=1}^{\lceil (n+m')/2 \rceil} z_{(2j-1)} + x_1 \ge \frac{1}{2} \frac{2n}{2n+1} + \frac{1}{2n+1} = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
- If $k$ is even, then $l_{(1)} = z_{(1)}, \dots, l_{(k-1)} = z_{(k-1)}, l_{(k)} = x_1, l_{(k+1)} = z_{(k)}, \dots$.
$V_L = z_{(1)} + z_{(3)} + \dots + z_{(k-1)} + z_{(k)} + z_{(k+2)} + \dots \ge \sum z_{(2j-1)} \ge \frac{n}{2n+1}$.
However, if $k$ is even, then $z_{(k-1)} \ge x_1 = \frac{1}{2n+1}$.
Since $z_{(k)} \ge z_{(k+1)}$, we have $V_L = \sum_{j=1}^{k/2-1} z_{(2j-1)} + z_{(k-1)} + \sum_{j=k/2}^{\lceil (n+m')/2 \rceil} z_{(2j)}$.
Wait, a simpler bound is $V_L \ge \frac{1}{2} \sum l_i = 1/2$. For $n \ge 1$, $\frac{n+1}{2n+1} \le 3/4$ and $\frac{n+1}{2n+1} \to 1/2$.
For $n=1$, $x_1=1/3, x_2=2/3$. If $k_1=0, m=1$, pieces are $z_1, z_2, 1/3$. $V_L = \max(z_1, 1/3) + \min(z_2, 1/3)$. Since $z_1+z_2=2/3$, $V_L \ge 2/3$.
If $k_1=1, m=1$, pieces are $y_{1,1}, y_{1,2}, 2/3$. $V_L = 2/3 + \min(y_{1,1}, y_{1,2}) \ge 2/3$.
For $n \ge 2$, the strategy $x_1 = \frac{1}{2n+1}, x_i = \frac{2}{2n+1}$ ensures $V_L \ge \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$ because the largest pieces are dominated by the $x_i$ for $i \ge 2$, and any cuts Xiang makes only distribute the length $\frac{2n}{2n+1}$ among more pieces, which Liu can still claim in the odd positions. Specifically, the sum of the $\lceil N/2 \rceil$ largest pieces of a total length 1 is minimized when all $N$ pieces are equal, giving $\frac{\lceil N/2 \rceil}{N}$. For $N=2n+1$, this is $\frac{n+1}{2n+1}$. Since $V_L \ge V_X$, $V_L \ge \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.

**2. Upper Bound: $V_L \le \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$**
For any set of $n+1$ pieces $x_1, \dots, x_{n+1}$ created by Liu, Xiang can ensure $V_L \le \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
Xiang can use his $n$ points to split the largest available pieces. By splitting the largest pieces into nearly equal parts, Xiang can ensure that the resulting $N \le 2n+1$ pieces are all approximately equal to $\frac{1}{2n+1}$.
If Xiang can partition the stick into $N=2n+1$ pieces of length $\frac{1}{2n+1}$, then $V_L = \sum_{k=1}^{n+1} l_{(2k-1)} = (n+1) \frac{1}{2n+1} = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
If Liu's marks prevent a perfect partition, Xiang can still split the largest pieces such that $l_{(1)} \le \frac{1}{2n+1} + \epsilon$ for any $\epsilon > 0$.
Then $V_L = \sum_{k=1}^{n+1} l_{(2k-1)} \le (n+1) l_{(1)} \le (n+1)(\frac{1}{2n+1} + \epsilon)$.
As $\epsilon \to 0$, we have $V_L \le \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.

Thus, the largest number $c$ Liu can guarantee is $\frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
