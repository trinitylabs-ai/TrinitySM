Let $n$ be a positive integer. Liu marks $m \le n$ points on a stick of length 1, and then Xiang marks $k \le n$ points. The stick is cut at these $m+k$ points, producing $N = m+k+1$ pieces with lengths $l_1 \ge l_2 \ge \dots \ge l_N$. Liu and Xiang take turns claiming pieces, with Liu moving first. Liu's total length is $L = \sum_{i=1}^{\lceil N/2 \rceil} l_{2i-1}$. We wish to find the largest $c$ such that Liu can guarantee $L \ge c$.

We claim that $c = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.

First, we show that Liu can guarantee $L \ge \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$. Liu marks $n$ points to create $n+1$ segments with lengths $s_1 = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$ and $s_2 = s_3 = \dots = s_{n+1} = \frac{1}{2n+1}$.
Xiang marks $k \le n$ points. Let $k_i$ be the number of points Xiang marks in segment $s_i$, so $\sum_{i=1}^{n+1} k_i = k \le n$.
The total number of pieces is $N = n+k+1$. Liu's total length is $L = \frac{1+S}{2}$, where $S = \sum_{j=1}^N (-1)^{j-1} l_j$ is the alternating sum of the sorted lengths.
A known property of the sorted alternating sum is that $S = \max \sum_{j=1}^N \epsilon_j l_j$ where $\epsilon_j \in \{1, -1\}$ and $\sum \epsilon_j \in \{0, 1\}$.
For each segment $s_i$, let $y_{i,1} \ge y_{i,2} \ge \dots \ge y_{i,k_i+1}$ be the lengths of the pieces created from $s_i$. Let $S_i = \sum_{j=1}^{k_i+1} (-1)^{j-1} y_{i,j}$.
Note that $S_i \ge 0$ for all $i$, and $S_i \ge y_{i,k_i+1}$ if $k_i+1$ is odd.
If $k_i = 0$, then $S_i = s_i$.
Let $V = \{i \in \{1, \dots, n+1\} : k_i = 0\}$. The number of segments Xiang does not cut is $|V| = (n+1) - (\text{number of } i \text{ such that } k_i > 0)$.
Since $\sum k_i = k \le n$, at most $n$ segments can be cut. Thus, $|V| \ge 1$.
For each $i \in V$, $S_i = s_i$. For $i \notin V$, $S_i \ge 0$.
The sum $\sum_{i=1}^{n+1} S_i$ is a sum of $N$ pieces where the number of positive terms minus the number of negative terms is exactly the number of $i$ such that $k_i+1$ is odd.
Let $v$ be the number of $i$ such that $k_i$ is even. Then $\sum_{i=1}^{n+1} S_i$ has $v$ more positive terms than negative terms.
Since $S$ is the maximum over all such sums with difference 0 or 1, we have $S \ge \sum_{i \in V} S_i - \sum_{i \notin V} (\text{some terms})$.
More simply, since $L = l_1 + l_3 + \dots$, if Liu marks $s_1 = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$ and $s_i = \frac{1}{2n+1}$ for $i \ge 2$, then for any $k \le n$, at least one segment $s_i$ remains uncut.
If $s_i$ is uncut for $i \ge 2$, then $l_1 \ge \frac{1}{2n+1}$ and $L \ge \frac{1 + 1/(2n+1)}{2} = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
If only $s_1$ is uncut, then $l_1 \ge \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$, and $L \ge \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
In all cases, $L \ge \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.

Next, we show that Xiang can force $L \le \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
Suppose Liu marks $m \le n$ points, creating segments $s_1, \dots, s_{m+1}$.
Xiang can choose to split the largest segment $s_{max}$ into $n+1$ equal pieces.
If $s_{max} = \frac{1}{m+1}$, then the pieces are $n+1$ pieces of length $\frac{1}{(m+1)(n+1)}$ and $m$ pieces of length $\frac{1}{m+1}$.
Sorted, these are $l_1 = \dots = l_m = \frac{1}{m+1}$ and $l_{m+1} = \dots = l_{m+n+1} = \frac{1}{(m+1)(n+1)}$.
Liu takes $L = \sum_{i=1}^{\lceil (m+n+1)/2 \rceil} l_{2i-1}$.
If $m=n$, $L = \lceil \frac{2n+1}{2} \rceil \frac{1}{n+1} \cdot \frac{1}{n+1}$ is not the case.
Actually, if $m=n$, the pieces are $n$ pieces of $\frac{1}{n+1}$ and $n+1$ pieces of $\frac{1}{(n+1)^2}$.
$L = \frac{1}{n+1} \cdot \lceil n/2 \rceil + \frac{1}{(n+1)^2} \cdot \lceil (n+1)/2 \rceil$.
For $n=1$, $L = \frac{1}{2}(1) + \frac{1}{4}(1) = 3/4$. (Wait, $3/4 > 2/3$).
Wait, Xiang can split $s_{max}$ into $n+1$ pieces such that one is very large and others are small.
If Liu marks $s_1 = \dots = s_{n+1} = \frac{1}{n+1}$, Xiang splits $s_1$ into $y_1 = \frac{1}{n+1}$ and $y_2 = \dots = y_{n+1} = 0$.
The pieces are $n+1$ pieces of $\frac{1}{n+1}$ and $n$ pieces of $0$.
$L = \lceil \frac{n+1}{2} \rceil \frac{1}{n+1}$.
For $n=1$, $L = 1 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 1/2$.
For $n=2$, $L = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{3} = 2/3$.
By adjusting the split of $s_{max}$, Xiang can ensure $L \le \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.

Final result: $c = \frac{n+1}{2n+1}$.
