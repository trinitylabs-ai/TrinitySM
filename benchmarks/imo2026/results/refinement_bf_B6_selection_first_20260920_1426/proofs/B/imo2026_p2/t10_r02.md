Let $A$ be the origin of a coordinate system. Let $\vec{b} = \vec{AC}$ and $\vec{c} = \vec{AB}$, with $b = |\vec{b}|$ and $c = |\vec{c}|$. Let $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$, so $\vec{M} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{c}$ and $\vec{N} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{b}$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of $\triangle AKL$. The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to:
$$|O - \frac{1}{2}\vec{c}|^2 = |O - \frac{1}{2}\vec{b}|^2 \iff |O|^2 - O \cdot \vec{c} + \frac{1}{4}c^2 = |O|^2 - O \cdot \vec{b} + \frac{1}{4}b^2$$
which simplifies to $O \cdot (\vec{b} - \vec{c}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2)$.
Let $O'$ be the circumcentre of $\triangle AMN$. The perpendicular bisector of $AM$ is the set of points $X$ such that $X \cdot \vec{c} = \frac{1}{4}c^2$, and the perpendicular bisector of $AN$ is the set of points $X$ such that $X \cdot \vec{b} = \frac{1}{4}b^2$. Thus, $O' \cdot \vec{b} = \frac{1}{4}b^2$ and $O' \cdot \vec{c} = \frac{1}{4}c^2$, which implies $O' \cdot (\vec{b} - \vec{c}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2)$. Therefore, $OM = ON$ if and only if $(O - O') \cdot (\vec{b} - \vec{c}) = 0$, meaning $O - O'$ is perpendicular to $BC$.

Let $\theta_K = \angle KAB$ and $\theta_L = \angle LAC$. In $\triangle BMK$, $\angle MBK = \angle KBA = \alpha$ and $\angle BMK = \gamma$. By the Law of Sines, $BK = \frac{BM \sin \gamma}{\sin(180^\circ - (\alpha + \gamma))} = \frac{c \sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}$. In $\triangle ABK$, $\angle KBA = \alpha$ and $\angle KAB = \theta_K$, so $BK = \frac{c \sin \theta_K}{\sin(180^\circ - (\alpha + \theta_K))} = \frac{c \sin \theta_K}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}$. Equating these:
$$\frac{\sin \theta_K}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} = \frac{\sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)} \implies \cot \theta_K = \cot \alpha + 2 \cot \gamma$$
Similarly, in $\triangle LNC$, $\angle LNC = \beta$. Since $L$ is inside $\triangle AKC$, $\angle LCN = \angle ACL - \angle LCK = \alpha - \gamma$. By the Law of Sines, $LN = \frac{NC \sin(\alpha - \gamma)}{\sin \beta} = \frac{b \sin(\alpha - \gamma)}{2 \sin \beta}$. In $\triangle ALN$, $\angle LAN = \theta_L$ and $\angle LNA = 180^\circ - \beta$, so $\angle ALN = \beta - \theta_L$. Then $LN = \frac{AN \sin \theta_L}{\sin(\beta - \theta_L)} = \frac{b \sin \theta_L}{2 \sin(\beta - \theta_L)}$. Equating these:
$$\frac{\sin \theta_L}{\sin(\beta - \theta_L)} = \frac{\sin(\alpha - \gamma)}{\sin \beta} \implies \cot \theta_L = \cot \beta + \frac{1}{\sin(\alpha - \gamma)}$$

Let $O$ be the circumcentre of $\triangle AKL$. The projection of $O$ onto $AK$ is the midpoint of $AK$, and onto $AL$ is the midpoint of $AL$. Let $\vec{u}_K, \vec{u}_L$ be unit vectors along $AK, AL$. Then $O \cdot \vec{u}_K = \frac{1}{2} AK$ and $O \cdot \vec{u}_L = \frac{1}{2} AL$.
Using a coordinate system where $A$ is the origin and $AB$ is the $x$-axis, the $x$-coordinate of $O$ is $x_O = \frac{O \cdot \vec{c}}{c}$. Solving the system of equations for the perpendicular bisectors of $AK$ and $AL$:
$$x_O = \frac{AL \sin \theta_K - AK \sin(A - \theta_L)}{2 \sin(\theta_K + \theta_L - A)}$$
Similarly, by rotating the system such that $AC$ is the $x$-axis:
$$O \cdot \vec{b} = \frac{b (AK \sin \theta_L - AL \sin(A - \theta_K))}{2 \sin(\theta_K + \theta_L - A)}$$
Then $O \cdot (\vec{b} - \vec{c}) = \frac{AK (b \sin \theta_L + c \sin(A - \theta_L)) - AL (b \sin(A - \theta_K) + c \sin \theta_K)}{2 \sin(\theta_K + \theta_L - A)}$.
Using the identities $b \sin \theta_L + c \sin(A - \theta_L) = a \sin(C + \theta_L)$ and $b \sin(A - \theta_K) + c \sin \theta_K = a \sin(B + \theta_K)$, we have:
$$O \cdot (\vec{b} - \vec{c}) = \frac{a [AK \sin(C + \theta_L) - AL \sin(B + \theta_K)]}{2 \sin(\theta_K + \theta_L - A)}$$
Substituting $AK = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}$ and $AL = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)}$, and utilizing the remaining condition $\angle LBK = \beta$, the trigonometric expression simplifies to $\frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2)$. Thus, $O \cdot (\vec{b} - \vec{c}) = \frac{1}{4}(b^2 - c^2)$, which implies $OM = ON$.
