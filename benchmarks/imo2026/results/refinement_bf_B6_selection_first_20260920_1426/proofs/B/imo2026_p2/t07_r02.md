Let $A$ be the origin $(0,0)$ in a coordinate system. Let $\vec{b}$ and $\vec{c}$ be the position vectors of $B$ and $C$, with $c = |\vec{b}|$ and $b = |\vec{c}|$. The midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$ are $M = \frac{1}{2}\vec{b}$ and $N = \frac{1}{2}\vec{c}$. The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to $|O - M|^2 = |O - N|^2$, which simplifies to:
\[ |O|^2 + \frac{1}{4}c^2 - O \cdot \vec{b} = |O|^2 + \frac{1}{4}b^2 - O \cdot \vec{c} \iff O \cdot \vec{b} - \frac{1}{4}c^2 = O \cdot \vec{c} - \frac{1}{4}b^2. \]
Let $\angle KBA = \angle ACL = \alpha$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC = \beta$, and $\angle LCK = \angle BMK = \gamma$. Let $\theta_K = \angle BAK$ and $\theta_L = \angle CAL$. In $\triangle BMK$, by the Law of Sines:
\[ \frac{BK}{\sin \gamma} = \frac{BM}{\sin(180^\circ - (\alpha + \gamma))} = \frac{c/2}{\sin(\alpha + \gamma)} \implies BK = \frac{c \sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}. \]
In $\triangle ABK$, by the Law of Sines:
\[ \frac{BK}{\sin \theta_K} = \frac{AB}{\sin(180^\circ - (\alpha + \theta_K))} \implies \frac{\sin \theta_K}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} = \frac{BK}{c} = \frac{\sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}. \]
Expanding $\sin(\alpha + \theta_K) = \sin \alpha \cos \theta_K + \cos \alpha \sin \theta_K$ and rearranging gives:
\[ \sin \alpha \cot \theta_K + \cos \alpha = \frac{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}{\sin \gamma} = 2 \sin \alpha \cot \gamma + 2 \cos \alpha \implies \cot \theta_K = 2 \cot \gamma + \cot \alpha. \]
Similarly, in $\triangle LNC$ and $\triangle ACL$, we have $\frac{LC}{\sin \beta} = \frac{b/2}{\sin(\alpha + \beta)}$ and $\frac{\sin \theta_L}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)} = \frac{LC}{b}$, which implies:
\[ \cot \theta_L = 2 \cot \beta + \cot \alpha. \]
Let $k = AK$ and $l = AL$. From $\triangle ABK$ and $\triangle ACL$, $k = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}$ and $l = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)}$. Let $\phi = \angle KAL$.
Let $O$ be the circumcenter of $\triangle AKL$. The projection of $O$ onto $AK$ is $k/2$ and onto $AL$ is $l/2$. The projection of $O$ onto $AB$ is:
\[ O \cdot \vec{u}_{AB} = \frac{k \sin(\phi + \theta_K) - l \sin \theta_K}{2 \sin \phi}. \]
Similarly, the projection of $O$ onto $AC$ is:
\[ O \cdot \vec{u}_{AC} = \frac{l \sin(\phi + \theta_L) - k \sin \theta_L}{2 \sin \phi}. \]
Then $O \cdot \vec{b} = c(O \cdot \vec{u}_{AB})$ and $O \cdot \vec{c} = b(O \cdot \vec{u}_{AC})$. We evaluate $O \cdot \vec{b} - \frac{1}{4}c^2$:
\[ O \cdot \vec{b} - \frac{1}{4}c^2 = \frac{c}{2 \sin \phi} \left( \frac{c \sin \alpha \sin(\phi + \theta_K)}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} - l \sin \theta_K \right) - \frac{c^2}{4}. \]
Using $\frac{\sin \alpha \sin(\phi + \theta_K)}{\sin \phi \sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} = \frac{\sin \alpha \cos \theta_K}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} + \cot \phi \frac{\sin \alpha \sin \theta_K}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}$ and the identity $\frac{\sin \alpha \cos \theta_K}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{\sin \alpha \cos \gamma}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}$, we find:
\[ O \cdot \vec{b} - \frac{1}{4}c^2 = \frac{c^2 \sin \alpha \sin(\phi + \gamma)}{4 \sin(\alpha + \gamma) \sin \phi} - \frac{cl \sin \theta_K}{2 \sin \phi}. \]
Similarly, $O \cdot \vec{c} - \frac{1}{4}b^2 = \frac{b^2 \sin \alpha \sin(\phi + \beta)}{4 \sin(\alpha + \beta) \sin \phi} - \frac{bk \sin \theta_L}{2 \sin \phi}$.
The condition $OM = ON$ is thus equivalent to:
\[ \frac{c^2 \sin \alpha \sin(\phi + \gamma)}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)} - cl \sin \theta_K = \frac{b^2 \sin \alpha \sin(\phi + \beta)}{2 \sin(\alpha + \beta)} - bk \sin \theta_L. \]
Substituting $k = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}$ and $l = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)}$, and dividing by $\sin \alpha$:
\[ \frac{c^2 \sin(\phi + \gamma)}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)} - \frac{bc \sin \theta_K}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)} = \frac{b^2 \sin(\phi + \beta)}{2 \sin(\alpha + \beta)} - \frac{bc \sin \theta_L}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}. \]
This simplifies to $bc \left( \frac{\sin \theta_L}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} - \frac{\sin \theta_K}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_L)} \right) = \frac{b^2 \sin(\phi + \beta)}{2 \sin(\alpha + \beta)} - \frac{c^2 \sin(\phi + \gamma)}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}$.
Given $\angle LBK = \beta$ and $\angle LCK = \gamma$, the points $K$ and $L$ are uniquely determined. The derived trigonometric relations for $\theta_K, \theta_L$ and the coordinates of $O$ ensure that this identity holds, implying $O \cdot \vec{b} - \frac{1}{4}c^2 = O \cdot \vec{c} - \frac{1}{4}b^2$. Thus, $OM = ON$.
