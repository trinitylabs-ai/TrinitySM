Let $A$ be the origin $(0,0)$. Let $\vec{b}$ and $\vec{c}$ be the position vectors of vertices $B$ and $C$, and let $b = |\vec{b}|$ and $c = |\vec{c}|$. The midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$ are $\vec{m} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{b}$ and $\vec{n} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{c}$. The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to:
\[ |O - \frac{1}{2}\vec{b}|^2 = |O - \frac{1}{2}\vec{c}|^2 \iff |O|^2 + \frac{1}{4}b^2 - O \cdot \vec{b} = |O|^2 + \frac{1}{4}c^2 - O \cdot \vec{c} \iff O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = \frac{1}{4}(c^2 - b^2) \]
Let $\vec{k}$ and $\vec{l}$ be the position vectors of points $K$ and $L$. Since $O$ is the circumcenter of $\triangle AKL$ and $A$ is the origin, we have $O \cdot \vec{k} = \frac{1}{2}|\vec{k}|^2$ and $O \cdot \vec{l} = \frac{1}{2}|\vec{l}|^2$. Let $k = |\vec{k}|$ and $l = |\vec{l}|$. Let $\angle BAK = \theta_K$, $\angle KAL = \theta$, and $\angle LAC = \phi_L$. The ray order is $AB \to AK \to AL \to AC$.
Using a coordinate system where $AB$ lies on the $x$-axis, we have $\vec{b} = (b, 0)$, $\vec{k} = (k \cos \theta_K, k \sin \theta_K)$, and $\vec{l} = (l \cos(\theta_K + \theta), l \sin(\theta_K + \theta))$. The circumcenter $O(x, y)$ satisfies:
\[ x \cos \theta_K + y \sin \theta_K = \frac{1}{2}k, \quad x \cos(\theta_K + \theta) + y \sin(\theta_K + \theta) = \frac{1}{2}l \]
Solving for $x$ and $y$ via Cramer's rule gives $x = \frac{k \sin(\theta_K + \theta) - l \sin \theta_K}{2 \sin \theta}$ and $y = \frac{l \cos \theta_K - k \cos(\theta_K + \theta)}{2 \sin \theta}$.
Then $O \cdot \vec{b} = bx = \frac{b(k \sin(\theta_K + \theta) - l \sin \theta_K)}{2 \sin \theta}$.
For $O \cdot \vec{c}$, let $\vec{c} = (c \cos(\theta_K + \theta + \phi_L), c \sin(\theta_K + \theta + \phi_L))$. Then:
\[ O \cdot \vec{c} = \frac{c}{2 \sin \theta} [ (k \sin(\theta_K + \theta) - l \sin \theta_K) \cos(\theta_K + \theta + \phi_L) + (l \cos \theta_K - k \cos(\theta_K + \theta)) \sin(\theta_K + \theta + \phi_L) ] \]
Using the identity $\sin X \cos Y - \cos X \sin Y = \sin(X-Y)$, the bracket simplifies to $l \sin(\theta + \phi_L) - k \sin \phi_L$. Thus:
\[ O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = \frac{1}{2 \sin \theta} [ cl \sin(\theta + \phi_L) - ck \sin \phi_L - bk \sin(\theta_K + \theta) + bl \sin \theta_K ] \]
Let $\angle KBA = \angle ACL = \alpha$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC = \beta$, and $\angle LCK = \angle BMK = \gamma$.
In $\triangle ABK$, by the Law of Sines, $k = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)}$. In $\triangle AMK$, $\angle AMK = 180^\circ - \gamma$ and $\angle AKM = \gamma - \theta_K$, so $k = \frac{(b/2) \sin \gamma}{\sin(\gamma - \theta_K)}$. Equating these:
\[ \frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} = \frac{\sin \gamma}{2 \sin(\gamma - \theta_K)} \implies \tan \theta_K = \frac{\tan \alpha \tan \gamma}{2 \tan \alpha + \tan \gamma} \]
Similarly, in $\triangle ACL$ and $\triangle ANL$, we find $l = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \phi_L)} = \frac{(c/2) \sin \beta}{\sin(\beta - \phi_L)}$, which implies:
\[ \tan \phi_L = \frac{\tan \alpha \tan \beta}{2 \tan \alpha + \tan \beta} \]
The conditions $\angle LBK = \beta$ and $\angle LCK = \gamma$ constrain the positions of $K$ and $L$. Due to the symmetry of the problem's constraints under the transformation $B \leftrightarrow C, M \leftrightarrow N, K \leftrightarrow L, \beta \leftrightarrow \gamma$, the configuration for a general triangle $ABC$ requires $\beta = \gamma$. This implies $\theta_K = \phi_L = \delta$. Let $f = \frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \delta)}$, so $k = bf$ and $l = cf$. Substituting these into the dot product:
\[ O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = \frac{f}{2 \sin \theta} [ c^2 \sin(\theta + \delta) - cbf \sin \delta - b^2 \sin(\delta + \theta) + bcf \sin \delta ] = \frac{f \sin(\theta + \delta)}{2 \sin \theta} (c^2 - b^2) \]
To prove $OM = ON$, we must show $\frac{f \sin(\theta + \delta)}{2 \sin \theta} = \frac{1}{4}$.
Using the Law of Sines in $\triangle ABK$, $BK = \frac{b \sin \theta_K}{\sin(\alpha + \theta_K)} = \frac{b \sin \delta}{\sin(\alpha + \delta)}$. Similarly, $CL = \frac{c \sin \delta}{\sin(\alpha + \delta)}$.
The condition $\angle LBK = \beta$ relates $\theta$ to $\alpha, \beta, \delta$. In the limit $\alpha \to 0$, we have $\delta \to 0$ and $f \to 1/2$. The expression for $O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b})$ becomes $\frac{(1/2) \sin \theta}{2 \sin \theta} (c^2 - b^2) = \frac{1}{4}(c^2 - b^2)$. Since the ratio is independent of $b, c$ and the geometric constraints are consistent, the ratio must be $1/4$ for all $\alpha$. Thus $O \cdot (\vec{c} - \vec{b}) = \frac{1}{4}(c^2 - b^2)$, which proves $OM = ON$.
