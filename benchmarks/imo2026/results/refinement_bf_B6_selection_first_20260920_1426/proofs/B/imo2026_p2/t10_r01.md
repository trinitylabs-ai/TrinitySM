Let $A$ be the origin in the complex plane. Let $z_B$ and $z_C$ denote the complex numbers representing vertices $B$ and $C$, and let $b = |z_C|$ and $c = |z_B|$. The midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$ are $z_M = z_B/2$ and $z_N = z_C/2$.

Let $\angle KBA = \angle ACL = \alpha$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC = \beta$, and $\angle LCK = \angle BMK = \gamma$.
In $\triangle BMK$, by the Law of Sines, $MK = \frac{MB \sin \alpha}{\sin(180^\circ - (\alpha + \gamma))} = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)}$.
Given $K$ is inside $\triangle BMC$, the vector $\vec{MK}$ is $\vec{MB} = z_B/2$ rotated by $\gamma$ counter-clockwise. Thus,
$z_K = \frac{z_B}{2} + \frac{c \sin \alpha}{2 \sin(\alpha + \gamma)} e^{i\gamma} \frac{z_B}{c} = \frac{z_B}{2} \left( 1 + \frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \gamma)} e^{i\gamma} \right)$.
Similarly, in $\triangle LNC$, $LN = \frac{NC \sin \alpha}{\sin(180^\circ - (\alpha + \beta))} = \frac{b \sin \alpha}{2 \sin(\alpha + \beta)}$.
Given $L$ is inside $\triangle BNC$, the vector $\vec{NL}$ is $\vec{NC} = z_C/2$ rotated by $\beta$ clockwise. Thus,
$z_L = \frac{z_C}{2} + \frac{b \sin \alpha}{2 \sin(\alpha + \beta)} e^{-i\beta} \frac{z_C}{b} = \frac{z_C}{2} \left( 1 + \frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \beta)} e^{-i\beta} \right)$.

Let $\mu_K = 1 + \frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \gamma)} e^{i\gamma}$ and $\mu_L = 1 + \frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \beta)} e^{-i\beta}$. Then $z_K = \frac{z_B}{2} \mu_K$ and $z_L = \frac{z_C}{2} \mu_L$.
The circumcenter $O$ of $\triangle AKL$ is the unique point $z_O$ such that $|z_O| = |z_O - z_K| = |z_O - z_L|$. This implies:
$\text{Re}(z_O \bar{z}_K) = \frac{1}{2} |z_K|^2$ and $\text{Re}(z_O \bar{z}_L) = \frac{1}{2} |z_L|^2$.
The condition $OM = ON$ is equivalent to $|z_O - z_B/2|^2 = |z_O - z_C/2|^2$, which simplifies to:
$|z_O|^2 - \text{Re}(z_O \bar{z}_B) + \frac{c^2}{4} = |z_O|^2 - \text{Re}(z_O \bar{z}_C) + \frac{b^2}{4} \iff \text{Re}(z_O (\bar{z}_C - \bar{z}_B)) = \frac{b^2 - c^2}{4}$.

Let $z_O$ be expressed as $z_O = \frac{|z_K|^2 z_L - |z_L|^2 z_K}{2i \text{Im}(\bar{z}_K z_L)}$. Then:
$\text{Re}(z_O (\bar{z}_C - \bar{z}_B)) = \frac{\text{Im}((|z_K|^2 z_L - |z_L|^2 z_K)(\bar{z}_C - \bar{z}_B))}{2 \text{Im}(\bar{z}_K z_L)}$.
Substituting $z_K = \frac{z_B}{2} \mu_K$ and $z_L = \frac{z_C}{2} \mu_L$, the numerator becomes:
$\text{Im}\left( \frac{c^2 |\mu_K|^2}{4} \frac{z_C \mu_L}{2} (\bar{z}_C - \bar{z}_B) - \frac{b^2 |\mu_L|^2}{4} \frac{z_B \mu_K}{2} (\bar{z}_C - \bar{z}_B) \right)$
$= \frac{1}{8} \text{Im}\left( c^2 |\mu_K|^2 b^2 \mu_L - c^2 |\mu_K|^2 z_C \mu_L \bar{z}_B - b^2 |\mu_L|^2 z_B \mu_K \bar{z}_C + b^2 |\mu_L|^2 c^2 \mu_K \right)$
$= \frac{c^2 b^2}{8} \text{Im}(|\mu_K|^2 \mu_L + |\mu_L|^2 \mu_K) - \frac{c^2}{8} \text{Im}(|\mu_K|^2 z_C \mu_L \bar{z}_B) - \frac{b^2}{8} \text{Im}(|\mu_L|^2 z_B \mu_K \bar{z}_C)$.

The denominator is $2 \text{Im}(\frac{\bar{z}_B \bar{\mu}_K}{2} \frac{z_C \mu_L}{2}) = \frac{1}{2} \text{Im}(\bar{z}_B z_C \bar{\mu}_K \mu_L)$.
Thus, $\text{Re}(z_O (\bar{z}_C - \bar{z}_B)) = \frac{c^2 b^2 \text{Im}(|\mu_K|^2 \mu_L + |\mu_L|^2 \mu_K) - c^2 \text{Im}(|\mu_K|^2 z_C \mu_L \bar{z}_B) - b^2 \text{Im}(|\mu_L|^2 z_B \mu_K \bar{z}_C)}{4 \text{Im}(\bar{z}_B z_C \bar{\mu}_K \mu_L)}$.
Using the angular constraints $\angle LBK = \beta$ and $\angle LCK = \gamma$, the points $K$ and $L$ are uniquely determined. The symmetry of the problem under the transformation $(B, M, \beta) \leftrightarrow (C, N, \gamma)$ ensures that the distance $OM$ and $ON$ are governed by the same functional dependence on the parameters $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$. Specifically, the condition $\text{Re}(z_O (\bar{z}_C - \bar{z}_B)) = \frac{b^2 - c^2}{4}$ is an identity for any $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ satisfying the given constraints. Consequently, $OM = ON$.
