To determine the values of $\theta \in (0^\circ, 180^\circ)$ for which Mulan can guarantee victory in finitely many steps, let $S = \{k\theta \mid k \in \mathbb{Z}^+, k\theta < 180^\circ\}$ be the set of positive integer multiples of $\theta$ less than $180^\circ$.

### 1. Winning State Lemma
If a triangle $T$ has at least one angle $\phi \in S$, Mulan can guarantee victory.
Proof: Let $\phi = n\theta$ for some $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$. If $n=1$, then $T$ has an angle $\theta$, and Mulan wins immediately. If $n > 1$, Mulan chooses the vertex with angle $\phi$ and splits it into $\alpha_1 = \theta$ and $\alpha_2 = (n-1)\theta$. Since $\theta > 0$ and $(n-1)\theta > 0$, this cut is valid. The resulting triangles are:
- $T_1$ with angles $(\theta, \beta, 180^\circ - \theta - \beta)$
- $T_2$ with angles $((n-1)\theta, \gamma, 180^\circ - (n-1)\theta - \gamma)$
$T_1$ contains the angle $\theta$, so Mulan wins if Shan-Yu keeps $T_1$. $T_2$ contains the angle $(n-1)\theta \in S$, so Mulan is in a winning state with a smaller coefficient $n-1$ if Shan-Yu keeps $T_2$. By induction on $n$, Mulan guarantees victory in at most $n$ steps.

### 2. Sufficiency of $\theta = 180^\circ/m$
Suppose $180^\circ = m\theta$ for some integer $m \ge 2$. We show Mulan can force $T$ to have an angle in $S$ in at most one step. Let the angles of $T$ be $\alpha \ge \beta \ge \gamma$.
- **Case 1: $\alpha > \theta$.**
Mulan chooses the vertex with angle $\alpha$ and splits it into $\alpha_1 = 180^\circ - \beta - k\theta$ and $\alpha_2 = k\theta - \gamma$. For this cut to be valid, we need $\alpha_1, \alpha_2 > 0$, which requires $k\theta \in (\gamma, 180^\circ - \beta)$. The length of this interval is $(180^\circ - \beta) - \gamma = \alpha$. Since $\alpha > \theta$, any interval of length $\alpha$ must contain at least one multiple $k\theta$. Since $0 < \gamma < k\theta < 180^\circ - \beta < 180^\circ$, we have $1 \le k \le m-1$.
The resulting triangles have angles:
- $T_1$: $\delta_1 = 180^\circ - \alpha_1 - \beta = 180^\circ - (180^\circ - \beta - k\theta) - \beta = k\theta \in S$.
- $T_2$: $\delta_2 = 180^\circ - \alpha_2 - \gamma = 180^\circ - (k\theta - \gamma) - \gamma = 180^\circ - k\theta = (m-k)\theta \in S$.
Both triangles have an angle in $S$, so Mulan wins.

- **Case 2: $\alpha \le \theta$.**
Since $\alpha \ge 60^\circ$, we must have $\theta \ge 60^\circ$. Given $180^\circ = m\theta$, $m$ can only be 2 or 3.
If $m=3$, then $\theta = 60^\circ$. Since $\alpha \ge 60^\circ$ and $\alpha \le 60^\circ$, we have $\alpha = \beta = \gamma = 60^\circ$, and Mulan wins immediately.
If $m=2$, then $\theta = 90^\circ$. If $\alpha = 90^\circ$, Mulan wins. If $\alpha < 90^\circ$, Mulan splits $\alpha$ into $\alpha_1 = 90^\circ - \beta$ and $\alpha_2 = \alpha - (90^\circ - \beta)$. Since $\beta \le \alpha < 90^\circ$, $\alpha_1 > 0$. Since $\alpha + \beta = 180^\circ - \gamma > 90^\circ$ (as $\gamma < 90^\circ$), $\alpha_2 = \alpha + \beta - 90^\circ > 0$.
$T_1$ has angles $(90^\circ - \beta, \beta, 180^\circ - (90^\circ - \beta) - \beta = 90^\circ)$.
$T_2$ has angles $(\alpha + \beta - 90^\circ, \gamma, 180^\circ - (\alpha + \beta - 90^\circ) - \gamma = 270^\circ - (\alpha + \beta + \gamma) = 90^\circ)$.
Both have an angle of $90^\circ \in S$, so Mulan wins.

### 3. Necessity of $\theta = 180^\circ/m$
Suppose $180^\circ/\theta \notin \mathbb{Z}$. We show that Shan-Yu can prevent Mulan from ever winning.
First, Shan-Yu chooses an initial triangle $T$ such that none of its angles are in $S$. Since $S$ is a finite set, the set of triangles whose angles avoid $S$ is non-empty (the condition $\alpha, \beta, \gamma \in S$ defines a finite union of lines in the space of triangles).
We now show that if $T$ has no angle in $S$, then any cut Mulan makes results in at most one triangle having an angle in $S$.
Let $T$ have angles $\alpha, \beta, \gamma \notin S$. Mulan splits $\alpha$ into $\alpha_1, \alpha_2$.
$T_1$ has angles $(\alpha_1, \beta, \delta_1 = 180^\circ - \alpha_1 - \beta)$.
$T_2$ has angles $(\alpha_2, \gamma, \delta_2 = 180^\circ - \alpha_2 - \gamma)$.
Since $\beta, \gamma \notin S$, for $T_1$ to have an angle in $S$, we must have $\alpha_1 \in S$ or $\delta_1 \in S$. Similarly for $T_2$, $\alpha_2 \in S$ or $\delta_2 \in S$.
1. If $\alpha_1 \in S$ and $\alpha_2 \in S$, then $\alpha = \alpha_1 + \alpha_2$ is a sum of two multiples of $\theta$, so $\alpha \in S$, contradiction.
2. If $\delta_1 \in S$ and $\delta_2 \in S$, then $\delta_1 + \delta_2 = (180^\circ - \alpha_1 - \beta) + (180^\circ - \alpha_2 - \gamma) = 360^\circ - (\alpha_1 + \alpha_2) - (\beta + \gamma) = 360^\circ - \alpha - (180^\circ - \alpha) = 180^\circ$. Thus $180^\circ$ is a sum of two multiples of $\theta$, so $180^\circ/\theta \in \mathbb{Z}$, contradiction.
3. If $\alpha_1 \in S$ and $\delta_2 \in S$, then $\delta_2 - \alpha_1 = (180^\circ - \alpha_2 - \gamma) - \alpha_1 = 180^\circ - \alpha - \gamma = \beta$. Since $\delta_2, \alpha_1 \in S$, $\beta$ is a difference of multiples of $\theta$. Since $\beta > 0$, $\beta \in S$, contradiction.
4. If $\delta_1 \in S$ and $\alpha_2 \in S$, then $\delta_1 - \alpha_2 = (180^\circ - \alpha_1 - \beta) - \alpha_2 = 180^\circ - \alpha - \beta = \gamma$. Similarly, $\gamma \in S$, contradiction.

Thus, at most one of $T_1, T_2$ can have an angle in $S$. Shan-Yu can always discard that triangle, ensuring $T$ never has an angle in $S$, and thus never has an angle $\theta$.

Mulan can guarantee victory if and only if $\theta = \frac{180^\circ}{m}$ for $m \in \{2, 3, 4, \dots\}$.
