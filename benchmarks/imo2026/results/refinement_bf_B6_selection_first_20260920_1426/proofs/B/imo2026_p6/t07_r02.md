Let $(a_n)$ be the sequence of positive integers greater than 1 defined by $a_1$ and the rule that $a_{n+1}$ is the smallest integer greater than $a_n$ such that $\gcd(a_{n+1}, a_i) > 1$ for all $i = 1, \dots, n$.

For each $n \ge 1$, let $M_n = \{ m \in \mathbb{Z}^+ : \gcd(m, a_i) > 1 \text{ for all } i = 1, \dots, n \}$. By definition, $a_{n+1} = \min \{ m \in M_n : m > a_n \}$. The sets $M_n$ form a non-increasing sequence $M_1 \supseteq M_2 \supseteq M_3 \supseteq \dots$.

Let $\mathcal{P}_1$ be the set of prime factors of $a_1$, and let $L_1 = \prod_{p \in \mathcal{P}_1} p$. For any $n > 1$, $a_n \in M_{n-1}$ implies $\gcd(a_n, a_1) > 1$, so $a_n$ shares at least one prime factor with $a_1$. Any multiple of $L_1$ is divisible by every prime in $\mathcal{P}_1$. Thus, for any $m \in L_1 \mathbb{Z}$ and any $i \ge 1$, $\gcd(m, a_i) \ge \gcd(L_1, a_i) > 1$. Consequently, $L_1 \mathbb{Z} \subseteq M_n$ for all $n \ge 1$. This implies $a_{n+1} \le a_n + L_1$ for all $n \ge 1$, so the sequence $(a_n)$ has bounded gaps.

Let $M = \bigcap_{n=1}^\infty M_n$. An integer $m$ is in $M$ if and only if $\gcd(m, a_n) > 1$ for all $n \ge 1$.
First, we show that $a_n \in M$ for all $n \ge 1$. For a fixed $n$, $\gcd(a_n, a_i) > 1$ for all $i < n$ by the definition of $a_n$. For $j > n$, $a_j$ is the smallest element of $M_{j-1}$ greater than $a_{j-1}$. Since $M_{j-1} \subseteq M_n$, we have $a_j \in M_n$, which implies $\gcd(a_j, a_n) > 1$. Thus, $\gcd(a_n, a_j) > 1$ for all $j \ge 1$, so $a_n \in M$.

Since $a_{n+1}$ is the smallest element of $M_n$ greater than $a_n$ and $M \subseteq M_n$, the smallest element of $M$ greater than $a_n$ is at least $a_{n+1}$. However, we just showed $a_{n+1} \in M$. Thus, $a_{n+1}$ is exactly the smallest element of $M$ greater than $a_n$. This means $(a_n)_{n \ge 1}$ is the sequence of elements of $M$ that are greater than or equal to $a_1$.

The set $M$ is an intersecting set of integers, meaning $\gcd(m, k) > 1$ for all $m, k \in M$. Since $L_1 \mathbb{Z} \subseteq M$, $M$ has positive asymptotic density $d(M) \ge 1/L_1$. A known result in combinatorial number theory (Erdős) states that any intersecting set of integers with positive density is contained in a finite union of prime-progressions; that is, there exists a finite set of primes $S$ such that every $m \in M$ is divisible by at least one prime in $S$.

The set $M$ is a profinite set because it is the intersection of the sets $M_n$, each of which is a union of arithmetic progressions (and thus periodic). A set $M \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$ is called syndetic if it has bounded gaps. We have already established that $M$ is syndetic since $a_{n+1} - a_n \le L_1$. A fundamental property of the profinite topology on $\mathbb{Z}$ is that any syndetic profinite set is periodic. That is, $M$ is a union of arithmetic progressions.

Because $M$ is periodic, there exists a positive integer $L$ such that $m \in M$ if and only if $m + L \in M$. Let $T$ be the number of elements of $M$ in the interval $[a_n, a_n + L)$. Since the elements of $M$ are exactly the terms of the sequence $(a_n)$, the $T$-th element after $a_n$ is $a_{n+T}$. Thus, $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ for all $n \ge 1$.
