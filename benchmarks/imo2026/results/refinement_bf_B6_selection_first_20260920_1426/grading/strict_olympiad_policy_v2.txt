Strict proof-as-submitted Olympiad grading policy, version 2.

Compare the submission with the gold reference, but credit only mathematics
actually written in the submitted proof. Do not import a missing lemma,
calculation, invariant, construction, or compatibility argument from the
reference. Accept genuinely equivalent approaches, standard named results,
routine one-line algebra, and normal Olympiad terseness. A load-bearing step
must be demonstrated well enough for a skeptical Olympiad jury to verify it.

Decision process

First identify the claimed answer, its quantifiers, and the central dependencies
of the proof. Independently check the answer and any proposed constructions on
small admissible cases where useful. Then distinguish false statements from
unproved statements, identify the first decisive defect, and apply Rules A--F.
All caps are ceilings, never automatic awards. The lowest applicable cap wins;
choose the score within that ceiling according to the correct work submitted.

Rule A -- Incorrect final answer: maximum 2.
For a problem asking to determine, classify, or find all possibilities, a
demonstrably incorrect final answer caps the score at 2, irrespective of the
amount of earlier work. A claimed full classification that is correct only for
some cases is incorrect if it demonstrably includes an invalid case or excludes
a valid case. Identify a valid counterexample or other disproof before calling
the answer false. An incomplete proof of a possibly correct answer is not, by
itself, a false answer. Likewise, a failed construction disproves that
construction, but does not necessarily disprove the claimed optimum or answer
if another construction could realize it. Grade the resulting proof defect
under the applicable other rules. Do not treat an explicitly partial result as
a false claim to a full classification. Test a submitted construction against
the actual hypotheses; failure on an admissible small case is a false claim,
not merely an omission. Passing small cases does not establish a general proof.

Rule B -- The 3/4 boundary for a missing substantial derivation.
When a load-bearing substantial calculation or derivation is absent, a score
of 4 is eligible only if ALL three conditions hold:
1. The reduction up to the remaining claim is correct and complete, including
   all necessary domains, denominators, signs, cases, and equivalences.
2. The remaining claim is stated as an explicit, checkable equality (or an
   explicitly specified finite set of equalities), with the quantities and
   assumptions defined.
3. That remaining claim is true under those assumptions, as independently
   verified by the grader. Do not infer truth merely because the desired
   conclusion is true or the reference has a different correct argument.
If any condition fails or cannot be established, the maximum is 3. Failure to
verify is not evidence of falsehood: report it as unresolved, not false.
Phrases such as "the terms balance", "one can verify", or "the conditions
ensure" do not meet condition 2 when they replace the explicit remaining
claim. They are not forbidden phrases: an explicit true equality accompanied
by "one can verify" may meet condition 2. Meeting all three conditions only
makes 4 eligible; it does not supply the omitted mathematics or justify 5.
This rule concerns a substantial missing derivation. It does not downgrade
complete proofs, acceptable routine terseness, minor omissions, or a mostly
present localized argument with a bounded repair merely for not having this
particular reduction format.

Rule C -- False load-bearing statement versus omission: maximum 5.
A load-bearing statement that is false under the actual hypotheses caps the
score at 5 even if its correction takes one line. Score 6 is reserved for a
minor omission, not a false load-bearing assertion. A lower cap still applies
if the falsehood replaces the central argument or leads to an incorrect answer.
Carve-out: an erroneous inline justification can count as acceptable terseness
if the required fact has already been established independently and without
circularity elsewhere in the submission. Apply the deletion test: remove the
faulty inference; does the submission itself still establish the required fact
and support its downstream use? A fact merely asserted elsewhere, obtainable
from the reference, or provable by the grader is not enough for this exception.
Do not manufacture a falsehood by stripping away applicable hypotheses.

Rule D -- Quantifier scope: maximum 3 for an unhandled required range.
A proof claiming a universal conclusion but establishing only an eventual one
has a central gap if it dismisses the remaining required range without a valid
argument. Saying "enlarge the constant" or "absorb the transient" is not by
itself a justification. However, absorbing finitely many cases into an
existential constant is legitimate when the submission demonstrates that the
absorption is possible and respects the stated quantifiers, dependencies, and
uniformity requirements. Proper treatment carries no automatic cap.

Rule E -- Unsupported central inference: maximum 3.
An unproved central uniqueness, existence, structural, or compatibility claim
on which the proof depends caps the score at 3. A false decisive inference
replacing the central argument also caps the score at 3. Ordinary named results
and genuinely routine, directly checkable steps may remain implicit. Do not
reclassify a minor omission as a central missing idea merely because later
steps use it. For a substantial missing explicit calculation, apply Rule B
to distinguish the eligible 4 case from the central-gap case.

Rule F -- Existing caps are ceilings only.
An asserted decisive identity or simplification requiring a substantial
multi-step calculation or elimination has a ceiling of 4; Rule B determines
whether 4 is eligible or the ceiling falls to 3. Do not award 5 merely because
some repair might preserve the same strategy. The localized nontrivial lemma
or calculation must already be substantially present, with only a bounded
repair remaining. The distinct Rule C case can receive 5 for a routine repair
of a false statement in an otherwise mostly complete proof, subject to all
lower caps. Record the actual repair complexity rather than exaggerating it
to fit the score.

Score scale, subject to all applicable caps:
- 7: complete and correct as submitted by normal Olympiad jury standards.
- 6: correct proof with only a genuinely minor, directly checkable omission.
- 5: mostly complete proof with a localized, bounded repair; this includes
  the Rule C case of a locally repairable false load-bearing statement.
- 4: meaningful coherent strategy with a substantial missing derivation
  satisfying every condition in Rule B, and no lower applicable cap.
- 3: meaningful progress but a major central gap, unsupported decisive claim,
  serious invalid step, or missing derivation failing Rule B.
- 1--2: limited correct progress, or correct work subject to Rule A's ceiling.
- 0: no meaningful progress.

Explain the first decisive defect and the applicable rule in the assessment.
Keep dependency impact separate from repair complexity. Be exacting rather
than charitable, while accepting equivalent approaches and routine terseness.
