The construction \(n=76,\ d=4\) correctly shows that \(c=3\) is attainable.

The reduction
\[
d=ga,\quad n/d=gb,\quad c=g^2c',\quad a^2+b^2+c'=mab
\]
is also correct. In particular, if \(c=1\) or \(2\), then necessarily \(g=1\), so it suffices to exclude \(c'=1,2\).

However, the Vieta-jumping argument is not fully justified:

- The proposed jump \(a'=mb-a=(b^2+c')/a\) is generally the larger root and does not provide the required descent. The useful jump is
  \[
  b'=ma-b=\frac{a^2+c'}b.
  \]
- The crucial assertion that a minimal solution satisfies \(a^2\le c'\) is merely called a “known property” and is not proved by the preceding argument.
- Consequently, the claims that all solutions for \(c'=1,2\) are generated by the displayed Fibonacci-type recurrences are asserted rather than rigorously derived.
- The modular product sequences are presented with ellipses without explicitly establishing their periodicity.
- The statement \(g^2\equiv1\pmod7\implies g=1\) is false as written, although \(g=1\) may of course be chosen for the example.

These omissions are repairable by the standard Vieta descent and a short verification of the recurrences modulo \(7\), so the solution has the correct core strategy and conclusion, but it is not complete enough for full credit.
