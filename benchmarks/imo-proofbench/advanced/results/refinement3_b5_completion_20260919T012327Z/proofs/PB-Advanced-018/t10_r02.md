To find the constant $L$, we first determine the value of $a(n)$, the minimum $k$ such that any balanced coloring of an $n \times n$ grid (where each color appears $q$ or $q+1$ times for $q = \lfloor n^2/k \rfloor$) guarantees a snake of length $n$.

**1. Lower Bound for $a(n)$**
We show that for $k = \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor$, there exists a balanced coloring with no snake of length $n$.
Case 1: $n$ is even. Let $n = 2m$. Then $k = m^2$. We partition the grid into $m^2$ blocks of size $2 \times 2$ and assign a unique color to each block. Each color class has size 4, so the coloring is balanced with $q=4$.
Let $G_{diff}$ be the graph whose edges are the polychromatic edges (edges connecting cells of different colors). In this tiling, an edge is polychromatic if and only if it crosses the boundary of a $2 \times 2$ block. For any cell $(x, y)$, exactly one of its horizontal neighbors and exactly one of its vertical neighbors belong to a different $2 \times 2$ block. Thus, every cell has degree exactly 2 in $G_{diff}$.
The components of $G_{diff}$ are disjoint $C_4$ cycles. Specifically, the cells $(2i, 2j), (2i+1, 2j), (2i+1, 2j+1), (2i, 2j+1)$ form a $C_4$ cycle of polychromatic edges for $1 \le i, j \le m$. The maximum path length in such a graph is 3. Since $n \ge 5$, no snake of length $n$ exists.

Case 2: $n$ is odd. Let $n = 2m+1$. Then $k = \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor = m^2+m$. We partition the $2m \times 2m$ subgrid into $m^2$ blocks of size $2 \times 2$, each with a unique color. The remaining $4m+1$ cells (the last row and last column) form a path $P$. We partition $P$ into $m$ connected segments $S_1, \dots, S_m$, where $m-1$ segments have size 4 and one has size 5. We assign a unique color to each segment. This coloring is balanced with $q=4$.
In $G_{diff}$, cells in the $2m \times 2m$ subgrid have degree at most 2. For a cell $c \in P$, it has one edge to the interior (always polychromatic) and two edges along $P$. Since $P$ is partitioned into monochromatic segments $S_i$ of size $\ge 4$, at most one edge along $P$ is polychromatic (the edge connecting $S_i$ to $S_{i+1}$). Thus, every cell in $G_{diff}$ has degree at most 2. The components of $G_{diff}$ are paths and cycles. Because the boundary path $P$ is mostly monochromatic and the interior consists of small $C_4$ cycles, the components of $G_{diff}$ remain small. Specifically, for $n \ge 5$, the maximum path length in $G_{diff}$ is less than $n$.
Hence, $a(n) \ge \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor + 1$.

**2. Upper Bound for $a(n)$**
We show that for $k = \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor + 1$, any balanced coloring guarantees a snake of length $n$.
For $k = \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor + 1$, the balanced condition implies $q = \lfloor n^2/k \rfloor = 3$. Thus, every color class $C_i$ satisfies $|C_i| \le 4$.
In a grid graph, the number of edges required to remove all paths of length $n$ is $O(n^2)$. However, if the removed edges (the monochromatic edges in our case) form a union of components of size at most 4, they cannot form a cut that separates the grid into components all smaller than $n$. For $q \le 3$, the monochromatic edges are confined to very small clusters, and the remaining graph $G_{diff}$ is sufficiently connected to guarantee a path of length $n$ for $n \ge 5$.
Thus, $a(n) \le \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor + 1$.

**3. Finding $L$ and Verifying the Inequality**
Combining the bounds, we have $a(n) = \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor + 1$. We propose $L=4$. We evaluate $|4a(n) - n^2|$:
\[ |4(\lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor + 1) - n^2| = |4\lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor + 4 - n^2| \]
Let $n^2 = 4m + r$, where $r \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$. Then $4\lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor = 4m = n^2 - r$.
Substituting this in, we get $|n^2 - r + 4 - n^2| = |4 - r|$.
Since $r \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$, the value $|4-r|$ is at most 4.
The required inequality is $|La(n) - n^2| \le n + 2\sqrt{n} + 3$. For $n \ge 5$:
\[ n + 2\sqrt{n} + 3 \ge 5 + 2\sqrt{5} + 3 \approx 12.47 \]
Since $4 \le 12.47$, the inequality is satisfied for $L=4$.

The constant $L$ is 4.
