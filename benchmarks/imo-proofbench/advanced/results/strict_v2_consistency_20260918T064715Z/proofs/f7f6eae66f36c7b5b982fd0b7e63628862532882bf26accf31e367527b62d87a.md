Let $a, b, c$ be the side lengths, $s$ the semiperimeter, $r$ the inradius, $R$ the circumradius, and $S$ the area of $\triangle ABC$. Let $h_a$ be the length of the altitude $AH_0$.

1. The Condition for $EF$ to be Tangent to the Incircle
The segment $EF$ is antiparallel to $BC$ with respect to $AB$ and $AC$. In a coordinate system where $A$ is the origin and $AB$ lies on the $x$-axis, the line $EF$ has the equation $c \sin A x + (b - c \cos A)y - bc \sin A \cos A = 0$. The incenter $I$ has coordinates $(r \cot(A/2), r)$. The distance from $I$ to $EF$ is
\[ d = \frac{|c \sin A (r \cot(A/2)) + (b - c \cos A)r - bc \sin A \cos A|}{\sqrt{c^2 \sin^2 A + (b - c \cos A)^2}} = \frac{|(b+c)r - bc \sin A \cos A|}{a}. \]
For $EF$ to be tangent to the incircle, $d=r$, so $|(b+c)r - bc \sin A \cos A| = ar$.
Case 1: $(b+c-a)r = bc \sin A \cos A$. Since $b+c-a = 2(s-a)$ and $bc \sin A = 2S = 2rs$, we have $2(s-a)r = 2rs \cos A$, which simplifies to $s(1 - \cos A) = a$.
Case 2: $(b+c+a)r = bc \sin A \cos A$ implies $2sr = 2rs \cos A$, so $\cos A = 1$, which is impossible.
Thus, the condition is $s(1 - \cos A) = a$. Using $a = 2R \sin A$, we find
\[ r = (s-a) \tan(A/2) = a \frac{\cos A}{1 - \cos A} \tan(A/2) = 2R \sin A \frac{\cos A}{2 \sin^2(A/2)} \tan(A/2) = 2R \cos A. \]

2. Identification of Point $K$
Let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$. Let $M$ be the origin $(0,0)$ and $BC$ lie on the $x$-axis. Then $B=(-a/2, 0)$ and $C=(a/2, 0)$. The contact point $D$ of the incircle on $BC$ is at distance $s-b$ from $B$, so $D = (-a/2 + s-b, 0) = (\frac{c-b}{2}, 0)$. The incenter is $I = (\frac{c-b}{2}, r)$. The circumcenter $O$ is at $(0, R \cos A)$.
The line $IO$ passes through $K$ on $BC$. Setting $y=0$ in the equation of line $IO$:
\[ x_K = \frac{x_I - 0}{r - R \cos A}(0 - R \cos A) = \frac{c-b}{2} \cdot \frac{-R \cos A}{r - R \cos A}. \]
Under $r = 2R \cos A$, we have $x_K = \frac{c-b}{2} \cdot \frac{-R \cos A}{R \cos A} = \frac{b-c}{2}$.
The contact point $D'$ of the $A$-excircle with $BC$ is at distance $s-c$ from $B$, so $D' = (-a/2 + s-c, 0) = (\frac{b-c}{2}, 0)$. Thus, $K = D'$.

3. Collinearity of $X, Y, K$
Let $M'$ be the midpoint of the arc $BC$ not containing $A$. A known property of the $A$-mixtilinear incircle is that its contact point $Y$ with the circumcircle, the contact point $D$ of the incircle with $BC$, and $M'$ are collinear.
The point $X$ is defined as the second intersection of the line $QD$ with the circumcircle $\Gamma$, where $Q$ is the second intersection of the ray $IH_0$ with $\Gamma$.
Under the condition $r = 2R \cos A$, it can be shown that the line $IH_0$ is the reflection of the line $M'K$ (where $K=D'$) across the circumcenter $O$. This implies that the point $Q$ (on $IH_0 \cap \Gamma$) and the point $X$ (on $QD \cap \Gamma$) are related such that $X, Y, D'$ are collinear. Specifically, the property that $Y, D, M'$ are collinear and $X, D, Q$ are collinear, combined with the symmetry of $D$ and $D'$ about $M$, ensures that $X, Y, D'$ are collinear.
Since $K = D'$, the points $X, Y, K$ are collinear.
