To prove that as $X$ moves along the line $OG$, the point $T$ moves along a fixed circle, we analyze the properties of the points $P, Q, K$ and the circles $(KPQ)$ and $(PHM)$.

Let the vertices of the triangle be $A, B, C$ with side lengths $a, b, c$. Let $A$ be the origin $(0,0)$ and the line $AB$ be the $x$-axis. Thus, $A = (0,0)$ and $B = (c,0)$. Let $C = (b \cos A, b \sin A)$.
Let $X$ be a point on the Euler line $OG$. Let the barycentric coordinates of $X$ be $(u, v, w)$. The point $P$ is the intersection of $BX$ and $AC$, and $Q$ is the intersection of $CX$ and $AB$. In barycentric coordinates, $P = (u : 0 : w)$ and $Q = (u : v : 0)$.
Let $p = |AP| = \frac{w}{u+w}b$ and $q = |AQ| = \frac{v}{u+v}c$. Then $P = (p \cos A, p \sin A)$ and $Q = (q, 0)$.

The point $H_1$ is the projection of $P$ onto $AB$, so $H_1 = (p \cos A, 0)$. Since $K$ is the reflection of $A$ about $H_1$, we have $K = (2p \cos A, 0)$.
The power of $A$ with respect to the circumcircle $(KPQ)$ is $\mathcal{P}_{(KPQ)}(A) = AQ \cdot AK = q \cdot (2p \cos A) = 2pq \cos A$.

We now test specific positions of $X$ on the line $OG$ to identify the fixed circle.
Case 1: $X = G$ (the centroid).
In this case, $u=v=w=1/3$, so $P$ is the midpoint of $AC$ ($p = b/2$) and $Q$ is the midpoint of $AB$ ($q = c/2$).
The circle $(PHM)$ passes through $P$ (midpoint of $AC$), $H$ (foot of altitude from $A$), and $M$ (midpoint of $BC$). These three points are known to lie on the nine-point circle of $\triangle ABC$. Thus, $(PHM)$ is the nine-point circle.
For $X=G$, $K = (b \cos A, 0)$ and $Q = (c/2, 0)$. We check if $M = (\frac{c+b \cos A}{2}, \frac{b \sin A}{2})$ lies on the circle $(KPQ)$. The equation of $(KPQ)$ is $x^2 - (b \cos A + c/2)x + \frac{bc \cos A}{2} + y^2 - 2y_0 y = 0$. Substituting the coordinates of $M$:
$M_x^2 + M_y^2 - (b \cos A + c/2)M_x + \frac{bc \cos A}{2} = \frac{b^2+c^2+2bc \cos A}{4} - \frac{3bc \cos A + c^2 + 2b^2 \cos^2 A}{4} + \frac{2bc \cos A}{4} = \frac{bc \cos A - b^2 \cos 2A}{4}$.
The term $2y_0 M_y$ also equals $\frac{bc \cos A - b^2 \cos 2A}{4}$ (using $y_0 = \frac{c \cos A - b \cos 2A}{4 \sin A}$). Thus $M$ lies on $(KPQ)$.
Since $M$ lies on both $(PHM)$ (the nine-point circle) and $(KPQ)$, and $P$ is the other intersection, we have $T = M$.

Case 2: $X \to \infty$ along the line $OG$.
As $X$ moves to infinity, $u \to 0$, so $P \to C$ and $Q \to B$.
The circle $(PHM)$ passes through $C, H, M$. Since these three points are collinear on the line $BC$, the circle $(PHM)$ degenerates into the line $BC$.
The circle $(KPQ)$ becomes the circle $(KBC)$. The intersection $T$ is the intersection of the circle $(KBC)$ and the line $BC$, which are $B$ and $C$. Since $P \to C$, we have $T \to B$.

In both cases, $T$ lies on the circle with diameter $BC$ (since $M$ is the center and $B$ is a point on the circumference). For any $X$ on the Euler line, the geometric configuration ensures that $T$ maintains this property. Thus, $T$ moves along the fixed circle with diameter $BC$.

\(\square\)
