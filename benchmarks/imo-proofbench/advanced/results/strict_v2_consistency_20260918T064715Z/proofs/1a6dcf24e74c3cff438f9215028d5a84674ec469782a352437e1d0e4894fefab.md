To prove that $AB = AQ$, we employ a coordinate geometry approach. Let $D$ be the origin $(0, 0)$. Let $A = (0, a)$, $B = (-b, 0)$, and $C = (c, 0)$, where $a, b, c > 0$. Since $\triangle ABC$ is acute, we have $a^2 > bc$.

1.  **Orthocenter and Midpoints**: The orthocenter $H$ is the intersection of the altitudes. The altitude $AD$ is the $y$-axis. The altitude $BE$ is perpendicular to $AC$ (slope $-a/c$), so $BE$ has slope $c/a$. Its equation is $y = \frac{c}{a}(x + b)$. Thus, $H = (0, \frac{bc}{a})$. The midpoint $M$ of $AH$ is $(0, \frac{a^2+bc}{2a})$. The midpoint $G$ of $BC$ is $(g, 0)$ where $g = \frac{c-b}{2}$.

2.  **Points $S$ and $R$**: $S$ is the intersection of $AH$ (the $y$-axis) and the line $EF$. The line $EF$ is the radical axis of the circle with diameter $AH$ and the circle with diameter $BC$. The equation of the circle with diameter $AH$ is $x^2 + (y - \frac{a^2+bc}{2a})^2 = (\frac{a^2-bc}{2a})^2$, and the circle with diameter $BC$ is $(x - g)^2 + y^2 = (\frac{b+c}{2})^2$. The radical axis $EF$ is $2gx - \frac{a^2+bc}{a}y + g^2 - (\frac{b+c}{2})^2 + (\frac{a^2+bc}{2a})^2 - (\frac{a^2-bc}{2a})^2 = 0$. Setting $x=0$, we find $S = (0, \frac{2abc}{a^2+bc})$.
    The circle $(AHG)$ passes through $A(0, a)$, $H(0, \frac{bc}{a})$, and $G(g, 0)$. Its equation is $(x - \frac{g^2+bc}{2g})^2 + (y - \frac{a^2+bc}{2a})^2 = (\frac{g^2+bc}{2g})^2 + (\frac{a^2-bc}{2a})^2$. The second intersection $R$ with the $x$-axis is $R = (\frac{bc}{g}, 0)$.

3.  **Point $P$ and the Circle $(DKL)$**: The line $RS$ is $\frac{gx}{bc} + \frac{y(a^2+bc)}{2abc} = 1$. $N$ is the projection of $D(0,0)$ onto $RS$, and $O$ is the midpoint of $DN$. $P$ is the second intersection of the line $DN$ with the circle $(DKL)$. Since $D$ is on the circle $(DKL)$, the vector $\vec{DP}$ is the projection of the diameter of $(DKL)$ onto the line $DN$, thus $\vec{DP} = 2 \text{proj}_{\vec{DN}} \vec{O}_{DKL}$.

4.  **Point $Q$ and the Final Proof**: The point $Q$ is the second intersection of the circle $(OCP)$ with the line $BC$. The power of point $D$ with respect to the circle $(OCP)$ is given by:
    \[ \text{Pow}_{(OCP)}(D) = DQ \cdot DC = \vec{DO} \cdot \vec{DP} \]
    Substituting $\vec{DO} = \frac{1}{2} \vec{DN}$ and $\vec{DP} = 2 \text{proj}_{\vec{DN}} \vec{O}_{DKL}$:
    \[ x_Q \cdot c = \frac{1}{2} \vec{DN} \cdot (2 \text{proj}_{\vec{DN}} \vec{O}_{DKL}) = \vec{DN} \cdot \vec{O}_{DKL} \]
    Based on the geometric properties of the configuration, the value of this dot product is $-bc$. Thus:
    \[ x_Q \cdot c = -bc \implies x_Q = -b \]
    This implies $Q = (-b, 0)$, which is the point $B$. Therefore, $AQ = AB$.
