\[ E = \left(d + \frac{n}{d}\right)^2 = d^2 + 2n + \left(\frac{n}{d}\right)^2 = d^2 + 2n + d'^2. \]
The remainder when $E$ is divided by $n$ is $r \equiv d^2 + d'^2 \pmod n$. We are given that $r = n-c$, which implies:
\[ d^2 + d'^2 = qn - c \]
for some positive integer $q$. Rearranging this, we get the equation:
\[ d^2 - qdd' + d'^2 = -c. \]
Let $k = q$. Since $d, d' > 0$, for $c$ to be positive, we must have $k > 2$ (as $k=1 \implies d^2 - dd' + d'^2 > 0$ and $k=2 \implies (d-d')^2 \geq 0$). We wish to find the smallest $c > 0$ for which there exist $n \equiv 6 \pmod 7$ and $d|n$ satisfying this equation.

First, we test $c=3$ with $k=5$. We look for $d, d'$ such that $d^2 - 5dd' + d'^2 = -3$.
If $d=4$, then $16 - 20d' + d'^2 = -3$, which gives $d'^2 - 20d' + 19 = 0$. This factors as $(d'-1)(d'-19) = 0$.
Choosing $d'=19$, we have $n = dd' = 4 \times 19 = 76$.
Checking the condition $n \equiv 6 \pmod 7$: $76 = 7 \times 10 + 6$, so $n \equiv 6 \pmod 7$ is satisfied.
Calculating the remainder: $(4 + 19)^2 = 23^2 = 529$. Dividing by 76, we get $529 = 6 \times 76 + 73$.
The remainder is $73 = 76 - 3$, so $c=3$ is possible.

However, testing $k=5, 7, 9, \dots$ modulo 7 shows that $d^2 - kdd' + d'^2 = -1$ cannot be satisfied for $n \equiv 6 \pmod 7$.
