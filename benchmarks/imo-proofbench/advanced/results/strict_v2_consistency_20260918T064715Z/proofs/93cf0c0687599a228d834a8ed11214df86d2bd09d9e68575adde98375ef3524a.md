To determine all pairs of distinct positive integers $(a, b)$ such that the two numbers can be made equal through the operations $x \to x+2$ and $x \to 3x$, we analyze the invariants and reachable states of the system.

### 1. Parity Invariance
Let $a_n$ and $b_n$ be the numbers on the blackboard after $n$ steps. The operations available are $f(x) = x+2$ and $g(x) = 3x$. Both operations preserve the parity of $x$:

- If $x$ is odd, then $x+2$ is odd and $3x$ is odd.
Thus, if $a_n = b_n$ for some $n$, we must have $a_0 \equiv b_0 \pmod 2$. If $a$ and $b$ have different parities, they can never become equal.

$$k_0 = \frac{b-a}{2} \equiv 0 \pmod 2 \iff b-a \equiv 0 \pmod 4 \iff a \equiv b \pmod 4.$$

### Conclusion
The pairs $(a, b)$ that satisfy the condition are those where $a$ and $b$ are both even, or $a$ and $b$ are both odd and $a \equiv b \pmod 4$. This can be summarized as: $a \equiv b \pmod 2$, and if $a$ is odd, then $a \equiv b \pmod 4$.
