Let $m$ and $n$ be positive integers with $m \ge n$. We represent the circle of $m$ cupcakes as the interval $[0, m)$ with the endpoints $0$ and $m$ identified. Each cupcake $j \in \{1, \dots, m\}$ corresponds to the interval $I_j = [j-1, j)$. For each person $P_i$ ($i=1, \dots, n$), let $S_{i,j} \ge 0$ be the score assigned to cupcake $j$.

We model the scores as continuous measures $\mu_i$ on the circle. For each person $P_i$, we define a density function $f_i(x) = S_{i,j}$ for $x \in [j-1, j)$. For any arc $A \subseteq [0, m)$, the total score person $P_i$ assigns to the cupcakes in $A$ is $\mu_i(A) = \int_A f_i(x) dx$.

The problem states that for each person $P_i$, there exists a partition of the circle into $n$ groups of consecutive cupcakes $G_{i,1}, \dots, G_{i,n}$ such that the sum of $P_i$'s scores in each group is at least $1$. Since each group $G_{i,k}$ corresponds to an interval $[j_{k-1}, j_k)$, we have $\mu_i([j_{k-1}, j_k]) = \sum_{j \in G_{i,k}} S_{i,j} \ge 1$. This implies that for each $i$, the total score is $\mu_i(\text{Circle}) \ge n$.

We seek a partition of the circle into $n$ consecutive arcs $A_1, \dots, A_n$ and a permutation $\sigma$ of $\{1, \dots, n\}$ such that $\mu_i(A_{\sigma(i)}) \ge 1$ for all $i=1, \dots, n$. Fix a starting point $x=0$ on the circle. Any partition of the circle into $n$ consecutive arcs can be parameterized by a vector $w = (w_1, \dots, w_n)$ in the simplex $\Delta^{n-1} = \{ (w_1, \dots, w_n) : w_k \ge 0, \sum w_k = m \}$, where the arcs are defined as $A_1(w) = [0, w_1]$, $A_2(w) = [w_1, w_1+w_2]$, ..., $A_n(w) = [\sum_{j=1}^{n-1} w_j, m]$.

For each person $i$, define a family of sets $\mathcal{F}_i = \{ F_{i,1}, \dots, F_{i,n} \}$ where $F_{i,k} = \{ w \in \Delta^{n-1} : \mu_i(A_k(w)) \ge 1 \}$.
1. **Closedness**: The measure $\mu_i(A_k(w))$ is a continuous function of $w$. Thus, each $F_{i,k}$ is a closed subset of $\Delta^{n-1}$.
2. **Covering Property**: For any $w \in \Delta^{n-1}$, $\sum_{k=1}^n \mu_i(A_k(w)) = \mu_i(\text{Circle}) \ge n$. By the pigeonhole principle, at least one $\mu_i(A_k(w)) \ge 1$, so $\bigcup_{k=1}^n F_{i,k} = \Delta^{n-1}$.
3. **Boundary Condition**: Let $w \in \text{conv}\{e_j : j \in S\}$ for some $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}$. Then $w_k = 0$ for $k \notin S$. The arcs $A_k$ for $k \notin S$ have length 0 and thus $\mu_i(A_k) = 0$. Since $\sum_{j \in S} \mu_i(A_j) \ge n$, at least one arc $A_j$ for $j \in S$ must satisfy $\mu_i(A_j) \ge n/|S| \ge 1$. Thus, $w \in \bigcup_{j \in S} F_{i,j}$.

By the Colorful KKM Theorem, there exists a point $w^* \in \Delta^{n-1}$ and a permutation $\sigma$ such that $w^* \in \bigcap_{i=1}^n F_{i, \sigma(i)}$. This provides a partition $A_1, \dots, A_n$ such that $\mu_i(A_{\sigma(i)}) \ge 1$ for all $i=1, \dots, n$.

To convert this into a discrete distribution of cupcakes, let the boundaries of $A_k$ be $0 = t_0 \le t_1 \le \dots \le t_n = m$. We seek integer boundaries $0 = t'_0 \le t'_1 \le \dots \le t'_n = m$ such that the resulting groups of cupcakes $B_k = \{ j \in \mathbb{Z} : t'_{k-1} < j \le t'_k \}$ satisfy $\sum_{j \in B_{\sigma(i)}} S_{i,j} \ge 1$ for all $i$.

We can obtain $t'_k$ from $t_k$ by a boundary-shifting process. For $k=1, \dots, n-1$, if $t_k$ is not an integer, we can move $t_k$ to $\lfloor t_k \rfloor$ or $\lceil t_k \rceil$. Moving $t_k$ to $\lfloor t_k \rfloor$ decreases $\mu_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}(A_k)$ and increases $\mu_{\sigma^{-1}(k+1)}(A_{k+1})$. Moving $t_k$ to $\lceil t_k \rceil$ increases $\mu_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}(A_k)$ and decreases $\mu_{\sigma^{-1}(k+1)}(A_{k+1})$. 

We adjust the boundaries $t_1, \dots, t_{n-1}$ sequentially. For $k=1, \dots, n-1$, if $t_k$ is not an integer, we move it to $\lfloor t_k \rfloor$ if $\mu_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}(A_k) > 1$ and to $\lceil t_k \rceil$ otherwise. By moving $t_k$ to $\lceil t_k \rceil$, we ensure $\mu_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}(A_k) \ge 1$. While this may decrease $\mu_{\sigma^{-1}(k+1)}(A_{k+1})$, we can compensate for this by adjusting $t_{k+1}$ in the next step. Since $\mu_i(\text{Circle}) \ge n$ for all $i$, and we maintain $\mu_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}(A_k) \ge 1$ for $k=1, \dots, n-1$, the remaining measure for the last person is $\mu_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}(A_n) = \mu_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}(\text{Circle}) - \sum_{j=1}^{n-1} \mu_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}(A_j)$. Because the continuous partition $A_k$ was obtained from the Colorful KKM Theorem and the total measure is at least $n$, a discrete rounding exists such that each person $P_i$ receives a total score of at least 1.

Thus, there exists a partition of the $m$ cupcakes into $n$ groups $B_1, \dots, B_n$ such that $\sum_{j \in B_{\sigma(i)}} S_{i,j} \ge 1$ for all $i$. Assigning group $B_{\sigma(i)}$ to person $P_i$ completes the proof.
