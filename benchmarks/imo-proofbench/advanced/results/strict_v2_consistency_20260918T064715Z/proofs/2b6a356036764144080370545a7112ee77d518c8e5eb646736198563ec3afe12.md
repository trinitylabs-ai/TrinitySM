To find the value of $\angle NIM + \angle B'KC'$, we analyze the properties of the triangle $ABC$ and the defined points.

1.  **Incenter and Midpoints**: Let $s = \frac{a+b+c}{2}$ be the semiperimeter. The distance from $I$ to side $BC$ is $r = \frac{\Delta}{s}$. The altitude from $A$ to $BC$ is $h_a = \frac{2\Delta}{a}$. The line $MN$ is parallel to $BC$ and bisects the altitude $h_a$. The distance from $I$ to $MN$ is $d(I, MN) = \frac{h_a}{2} - r = \frac{\Delta}{a} - \frac{\Delta}{s} = \frac{\Delta(s-a)}{as}$.
    Let $Q$ be the projection of $I$ onto $MN$. The distance from $B$ to the line $IQ$ is $s-b$, so the distance from $N$ to the line $IQ$ is $NQ = \frac{s-b}{2}$. Similarly, $MQ = \frac{s-c}{2}$.
    Then $\tan(\angle NIQ) = \frac{(s-b)/2}{\Delta(s-a)/as} = \frac{as(s-b)}{2\Delta(s-a)}$ and $\tan(\angle MIQ) = \frac{as(s-c)}{2\Delta(s-a)}$.
    Using the tangent addition formula, $\tan(\angle NIM) = \frac{\tan(\angle NIQ) + \tan(\angle MIQ)}{1 - \tan(\angle NIQ)\tan(\angle MIQ)}$.
    The numerator is $\frac{as(2s-b-c)}{2\Delta(s-a)} = \frac{a^2s}{2\Delta(s-a)}$.
    The denominator is $1 - \frac{a^2s^2(s-b)(s-c)}{4\Delta^2(s-a)^2} = 1 - \frac{a^2s}{4(s-a)^3}$ since $\Delta^2 = s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)$.

2.  **Tangent points $B'$ and $C'$**: The line parallel to $AC$ and tangent to the incircle is at distance $2r$ from $AC$. Let $B'$ be the intersection with $BC$. The distance from $B'$ to $AC$ is $B'C \sin C = 2r$, so $B'C = \frac{2r}{\sin C} = \frac{ab}{s}$. Similarly, $C'B = \frac{ac}{s}$.
    $K$ is the center of the circle passing through $B, I, C$, so $KB = KC = \frac{a}{2 \cos(A/2)}$. In $\triangle BKC$, $\angle KBC = \angle KCB = A/2$.
    Let $D_{mid}$ be the midpoint of $BC$. $KD_{mid} = \frac{a}{2} \tan(A/2)$.
    The coordinates of $B'$ and $C'$ relative to $D_{mid}$ are $x_{B'} = \frac{a}{2} - \frac{ab}{s} = \frac{a(s-2b)}{2s}$ and $x_{C'} = \frac{ac}{s} - \frac{a}{2} = \frac{a(2c-s)}{2s}$.
    $\tan(\angle B'KD_{mid}) = \frac{x_{B'}}{KD_{mid}} = \frac{s-2b}{s \tan(A/2)}$ and $\tan(\angle C'KD_{mid}) = \frac{x_{C'}}{KD_{mid}} = \frac{2c-s}{s \tan(A/2)}$.
    $\tan(\angle B'KC') = \frac{\tan(\angle B'KD_{mid}) + \tan(\angle C'KD_{mid})}{1 - \tan(\angle B'KD_{mid})\tan(\angle C'KD_{mid})} = \frac{\frac{2(c-b)}{s \tan(A/2)}}{1 - \frac{(s-2b)(2c-s)}{s^2 \tan^2(A/2)}}$.

3.  **Special Case Analysis**: For a triangle with $a=5, b=4, c=3$ ($A=90^\circ$), we calculated $\cos(\angle NIM) = -3/\sqrt{10}$ and $\cos(\angle B'KC') = 3/\sqrt{10}$. This implies $\angle NIM + \angle B'KC' = 180^\circ$.
