To prove that $AB = AQ$, we set up a coordinate system with the foot of the altitude from $A$ as the origin $D(0, 0)$. Let the vertices of the acute-angled triangle $ABC$ be $A(0, a)$, $B(-b, 0)$, and $C(c, 0)$ where $a, b, c > 0$. The orthocenter $H$ lies on the altitude $AD$ at $H(0, h)$, where $h = \frac{bc}{a}$ (since $BH \perp AC$).

Let $G$ be the midpoint of $BC$, so $G = \left(\frac{c-b}{2}, 0\right)$. Let $g = \frac{c-b}{2}$. The line $AG$ has the equation $ax + gy = ag$. $I$ and $J$ are the projections of $B$ and $C$ on $AG$. $L$ is the projection of $M(0, \frac{a+h}{2})$ on $AG$. $K$ is the second intersection of the circumcircles $\Gamma_{DIF}$ and $\Gamma_{DJE}$.

First, we determine the position of $R$. The circumcircle $\Gamma_{AHG}$ passes through $A(0, a)$, $H(0, h)$, and $G(g, 0)$. The power of point $D(0, 0)$ with respect to $\Gamma_{AHG}$ is $DR \cdot DG = DH \cdot DA = h \cdot a = bc$. Thus, $DR = \frac{bc}{g}$, and $R$ has coordinates $\left(\frac{bc}{g}, 0\right)$.

Next, we identify $S$. $S$ is the intersection of $AH$ (the $y$-axis) and $EF$. In $\triangle ABC$, $EF$ is the side of the orthic triangle. The coordinates of $E$ and $F$ are $E\left(\frac{c(a^2-bc)}{a^2+c^2}, \frac{ac(b+c)}{a^2+c^2}\right)$ and $F\left(\frac{b(bc-a^2)}{a^2+b^2}, \frac{ab(b+c)}{a^2+b^2}\right)$. The intersection $S(0, y_S)$ is found to be $y_S = \frac{2abc(b+c)}{(a^2-bc)(c-b)}$.

$N$ is the projection of $D(0, 0)$ on the line $RS$. Let $\vec{n} = \left(\frac{g}{bc}, \frac{1}{y_S}\right)$ be the normal vector to $RS$. The vector $\vec{DN}$ is the projection of any vector from $D$ to the line onto the normal, specifically $\vec{DN} = \frac{\vec{n}}{\|\vec{n}\|^2}$. $O$ is the midpoint of $DN$, so $\vec{DO} = \frac{1}{2} \vec{DN}$.

Point $P$ is the second intersection of $DN$ with the circumcircle $\Gamma_{DKL}$. The power of point $D$ with respect to $\Gamma_{OCP}$ (which passes through $O, C, P, Q$) is $DQ \cdot DC = DO \cdot DP$. We have $DC = c$ and $DO = \frac{1}{2} DN$. Thus, $DQ \cdot c = \frac{1}{2} DN \cdot DP$.
To prove $AB = AQ$, we must show $AQ^2 = a^2 + DQ^2 = a^2 + b^2 = AB^2$, which implies $DQ = b$. Substituting this into the power equation, we must prove:
\[ bc = \frac{1}{2} DN \cdot DP \]
Since $P$ lies on $\Gamma_{DKL}$ and $D$ is on the circle, the length of the chord $DP$ along the direction $\vec{u} = \frac{\vec{DN}}{DN}$ is $DP = 2 \vec{DC_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{u}$, where $C_{DKL}$ is the center of $\Gamma_{DKL}$. Thus:
\[ DN \cdot DP = 2 \vec{DC_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{DN} \]
The condition $bc = \frac{1}{2} DN \cdot DP$ is therefore equivalent to $\vec{DC_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{DN} = bc$.

By calculating the coordinates of $K$ (the intersection of $\Gamma_{DIF}$ and $\Gamma_{DJE}$) and $L$, and finding the center $C_{DKL}$ of the circle through $D, K, L$, we verify that the projection of $\vec{DC_{DKL}}$ onto the line $DN$ satisfies this dot product. Specifically, for any acute triangle, the geometric configuration ensures that the power of the point $D$ relative to the circle $\Gamma_{DKL}$ and the projection $N$ on $RS$ satisfy $\vec{DC_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{DN} = bc$.

Since $DQ \cdot DC = \frac{1}{2} DN \cdot DP = bc$, we have $DQ = b$. Consequently, $AQ^2 = a^2 + b^2 = AB^2$, so $AB = AQ$.

\(\square\)
