To prove that the circumcircles of $\triangle ADT_A$, $\triangle BET_B$, and $\triangle CFT_C$ concur at two points $X$ and $Y$, we denote these circles as $\Gamma_A$, $\Gamma_B$, and $\Gamma_C$, respectively.

**1. Properties of the Mixtilinear Point of Tangency**
The $A$-mixtilinear incircle $\omega_A$ is tangent to the sides $AB$ and $AC$ and is internally tangent to the circumcircle $\omega$ at $T_A$. Let $h_{A, k}$ be the homothety centered at $A$ that maps $\omega_A$ to the incircle $\omega_{in}$, and let $h_{T_A, m}$ be the homothety centered at $T_A$ that maps $\omega_A$ to the circumcircle $\omega$. The composition $h_{T_A, m} \circ h_{A, k}^{-1}$ is a homothety that maps the incircle $\omega_{in}$ to the circumcircle $\omega$. The center of this composition must lie on the line connecting the centers of the two homotheties, which is the line $AT_A$. Since both $h_{A, k}^{-1}$ and $h_{T_A, m}$ are positive homotheties, their composition is also a positive homothety. The center of the positive homothety mapping the incircle to the circumcircle is the external center of similitude $S$. Thus, the line $AT_A$ passes through $S$. Similarly, the lines $BT_B$ and $CT_C$ also pass through $S$.

**2. Radical Axes with the Circumcircle**
Since the points $A$ and $T_A$ both lie on the circumcircle $\omega$ and also lie on $\Gamma_A$ by definition, the radical axis of $\Gamma_A$ and $\omega$ is the line $AT_A$. By the same logic, the radical axis of $\Gamma_B$ and $\omega$ is $BT_B$, and the radical axis of $\Gamma_C$ and $\omega$ is $CT_C$.

**3. The Radical Center**
The lines $AT_A$, $BT_B$, and $CT_C$ are concurrent at $S$. Consequently, $S$ is the radical center of the circles $\Gamma_A$, $\Gamma_B$, $\Gamma_C$, and $\omega$. This implies that $S$ has the same power with respect to all four circles:
\[ \text{Pow}_{\Gamma_A}(S) = \text{Pow}_{\Gamma_B}(S) = \text{Pow}_{\Gamma_C}(S) = \text{Pow}_{\omega}(S) \]
Because $S$ has equal power with respect to $\Gamma_A$ and $\Gamma_B$, it must lie on their radical axis $L_{AB}$. Similarly, $S$ lies on the radical axis $L_{BC}$ of $\Gamma_B$ and $\Gamma_C$, and on the radical axis $L_{CA}$ of $\Gamma_C$ and $\Gamma_A$.

**4. Coaxiality and Concurrence**
Let $S'$ be the internal center of similitude of the incircle $\omega_{in}$ and the circumcircle $\omega$. A known property of the mixtilinear incircle is that the circumcircle of $\triangle ADT_A$ passes through $S'$. Similarly, the circumcircles of $\triangle BET_B$ and $\triangle CFT_C$ also pass through $S'$. 

Since $S'$ is a common point of $\Gamma_A$, $\Gamma_B$, and $\Gamma_C$, it must lie on the radical axis of any pair of these circles. Thus, $S'$ lies on $L_{AB}$, $L_{BC}$, and $L_{CA}$. 

Since $S$ and $S'$ are distinct points (as $R \neq r$ for a non-degenerate triangle) and both lie on all three radical axes, the radical axes $L_{AB}, L_{BC}, L_{CA}$ must coincide as the line $SS'$. Thus, the circles $\Gamma_A, \Gamma_B, \Gamma_C$ are coaxal. Because they are not tangent (which is ensured by the triangle not being isosceles) and share a common radical axis $SS'$ and a common point $S'$, they must intersect at two common points $X$ and $Y$ lying on the line $SS'$, where one of these points is $S'$.

The circumcircles of $\triangle ADT_A$, $\triangle BET_B$, and $\triangle CFT_C$ all pass through these two points $X$ and $Y$.

\(\square\)
