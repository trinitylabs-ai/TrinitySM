To determine which player has a winning strategy, we analyze the condition for a road to be constructed between two cities $A$ and $B$.

Let $\Sigma = \{ \frac{R-P}{Q-P} : R \in S \}$. The condition for a road $AB$ is that for every city $C \in \mathcal{C} \setminus \{A, B\}$, $\triangle PQR$ is directly similar to $\triangle ABC$ or $\triangle BAC$ for some $R \in S$. In terms of complex numbers, this means:
$$\frac{C-A}{B-A} = \frac{R-P}{Q-P} \in \Sigma \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{C-B}{A-B} = \frac{R-P}{Q-P} \in \Sigma$$
Let $u = \frac{C-A}{B-A}$. Then $\frac{C-B}{A-B} = \frac{C-B}{-(B-A)} = \frac{B-C}{B-A} = \frac{(B-A) - (C-A)}{B-A} = 1 - u$.
Thus, the condition for a road $AB$ is that for all $C \in \mathcal{C} \setminus \{A, B\}$, $u \in \Sigma \cup (1-\Sigma)$. Let $T = \Sigma \cup (1-\Sigma)$. Alice wins if she can choose $T$ such that the resulting graph is connected and planar for any set of cities $\mathcal{C}$ Bob chooses.

Consider the case where $T$ is the complement of the open disk with diameter $[0, 1]$ in the complex plane:
$$T = \{ z \in \mathbb{C} : |z - 1/2| \ge 1/2 \}$$
Note that $z \in T \iff |z - 1/2| \ge 1/2 \iff |(1-z) - 1/2| = |1/2 - z| \ge 1/2 \iff 1-z \in T$. Thus, $T$ is symmetric about $1/2$, and we can simply set $\Sigma = T$. Alice can achieve this by choosing $P=0, Q=1$, and $S = T$.

For any two cities $A, B$, the condition for a road $AB$ is:
$$\forall C \in \mathcal{C} \setminus \{A, B\}, \quad \left| \frac{C-A}{B-A} - \frac{1}{2} \right| \ge \frac{1}{2}$$
Multiplying by $|B-A|$ and simplifying:
$$\left| (C-A) - \frac{1}{2}(B-A) \right| \ge \frac{1}{2} |B-A| \implies \left| C - \frac{A+B}{2} \right| \ge \frac{1}{2} |B-A|$$
This means that for every city $C \neq A, B$, the distance from $C$ to the midpoint of $AB$ is at least half the distance between $A$ and $B$. In other words, the open disk with diameter $AB$ contains no other cities. This is precisely the definition of the **Gabriel graph**.

We now verify the properties of the Gabriel graph:
1. **Planarity**: The Gabriel graph is a subgraph of the Delaunay triangulation. Since the Delaunay triangulation is a planar graph (its edges are the boundaries of Voronoi cells), the Gabriel graph is also planar.
2. **Connectivity**: For any finite set of points, the Gabriel graph contains the Minimum Spanning Tree (MST), which is connected. For an infinite set of points $\mathcal{C}$ where $d(C_i, C_j) > 1$, the set is locally finite. In such cases, the Gabriel graph remains connected because any edge of the MST must be a Gabriel edge (if the disk with diameter $AB$ contained a point $C$, then $d(A, C) < d(A, B)$ and $d(B, C) < d(A, B)$, meaning $AB$ could not be an edge of the MST).

Since the Gabriel graph is always connected and planar, Alice has a winning strategy.

Alice wins. \(\square\)
