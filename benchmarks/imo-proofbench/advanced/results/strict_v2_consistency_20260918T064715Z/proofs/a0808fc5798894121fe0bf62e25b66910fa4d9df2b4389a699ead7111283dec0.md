To prove that $C$ is the midpoint of the segment $XY$, where $X$ and $Y$ are the intersections of the circumcircle of $\triangle AFP$ with the line $BC$, we will use a coordinate geometry approach.

Let $C$ be the origin $(0,0)$ and let the line $BC$ lie on the $x$-axis. Let $B = (a, 0)$ and $A = (b, c)$. Since the triangle $ABC$ is acute, we have $a, b, c > 0$ and $b < a$.

1.  **Coordinates of $H$ and $P$:**
    The altitude from $A$ to $BC$ is the vertical line $x = b$. The slope of $AB$ is $\frac{c-0}{b-a} = \frac{c}{b-a}$. The altitude from $C$ to $AB$ is perpendicular to $AB$, so its slope is $\frac{a-b}{c}$. The equation of the line $CF$ is $y = \frac{a-b}{c} x$.
    The orthocenter $H$ is the intersection of these two altitudes: $x_H = b$ and $y_H = \frac{a-b}{c} b = \frac{b(a-b)}{c}$.
    $P$ is the reflection of $H$ across $BC$ (the $x$-axis), so $P = (b, -y_H) = (b, -\frac{b(a-b)}{c})$.

2.  **Coordinates of $F$:**
    $F$ is the intersection of the line $CF$ ($y = \frac{a-b}{c} x$) and the line $AB$ ($y = \frac{c}{b-a}(x-a)$).
    Equating the two expressions for $y$:
    $\frac{a-b}{c} x = \frac{c}{b-a}(x-a) \implies x \left( \frac{a-b}{c} - \frac{c}{b-a} \right) = -\frac{ca}{b-a}$
    $x \left( \frac{-(a-b)^2 - c^2}{c(b-a)} \right) = -\frac{ca}{b-a} \implies x_F = \frac{c^2 a}{c^2 + (a-b)^2}$
    Then $y_F = \frac{a-b}{c} x_F = \frac{ca(a-b)}{c^2 + (a-b)^2}$.

3.  **Center of the Circle $AFP$:**
    Let $O = (x_0, y_0)$ be the center of the circumcircle of $\triangle AFP$. $O$ must lie on the perpendicular bisector of $AP$. Since $A = (b, c)$ and $P = (b, -y_H)$, the perpendicular bisector of $AP$ is the horizontal line $y = \frac{c - y_H}{2}$. Thus,
    $y_0 = \frac{c - \frac{b(a-b)}{c}}{2} = \frac{c^2 - ab + b^2}{2c}$.
    $O$ also satisfies $OA^2 = OF^2$:
    $(x_0 - b)^2 + (y_0 - c)^2 = (x_0 - x_F)^2 + (y_0 - y_F)^2$
    $x_0^2 - 2bx_0 + b^2 + (y_0 - c)^2 = x_0^2 - 2x_F x_0 + x_F^2 + (y_0 - y_F)^2$
    $2(x_F - b) x_0 = x_F^2 + y_F^2 - b^2 - c^2 + 2y_0(c - y_F)$

4.  **Verifying $x_0 = 0$:**
    We calculate the right-hand side of the equation. Let $S = c^2 + (a-b)^2$.
    $x_F^2 + y_F^2 = \frac{c^4 a^2 + c^2 a^2 (a-b)^2}{S^2} = \frac{c^2 a^2 S}{S^2} = \frac{c^2 a^2}{S}$.
    $c - y_F = c - \frac{ca(a-b)}{S} = \frac{c(S - a(a-b))}{S} = \frac{c(c^2 + (a-b)^2 - a(a-b))}{S} = \frac{c(c^2 - b(a-b))}{S}$.
    $2y_0(c - y_F) = 2 \left( \frac{c^2 - ab + b^2}{2c} \right) \frac{c(c^2 - ab + b^2)}{S} = \frac{(c^2 - ab + b^2)^2}{S}$.
    Substituting these into the expression:
    $x_F^2 + y_F^2 - (b^2 + c^2) + 2y_0(c - y_F) = \frac{c^2 a^2 + (c^2 - ab + b^2)^2 - (b^2 + c^2)S}{S}$
    The numerator is:
    $c^2 a^2 + (c^4 + a^2 b^2 + b^4 - 2abc^2 + 2b^2 c^2 - 2ab^3) - (b^2 c^2 + b^2(a-b)^2 + c^4 + c^2(a-b)^2)$
    $= c^2 a^2 + a^2 b^2 + b^4 - 2abc^2 + b^2 c^2 - 2ab^3 - (b^2 a^2 - 2ab^3 + b^4 + c^2 a^2 - 2abc^2 + c^2 b^2) = 0$.
    Thus, $2(x_F - b) x_0 = 0$, which implies $x_0 = 0$.

Since the center $O$ of the circle $AFP$ has $x$-coordinate $0$, the line $OC$ is perpendicular to the line $BC$. Since $X$ and $Y$ are the intersections of the circle with $BC$, $C$ is the projection of the center $O$ onto the chord $XY$, which means $C$ is the midpoint of $XY$.

\(\square\)
