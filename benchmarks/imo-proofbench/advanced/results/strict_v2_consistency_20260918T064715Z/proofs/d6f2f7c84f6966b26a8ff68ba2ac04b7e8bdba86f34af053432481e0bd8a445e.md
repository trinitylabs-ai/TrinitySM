To determine which player has a winning strategy, we analyze the condition for the construction of roads.

Let the positions of the cities be represented by complex numbers. Alice chooses points $P$ and $Q$ and a subset $S$ of the plane. This choice defines a set of complex numbers $W = \left\{ \frac{r-p}{q-p} : R \in S \right\}$, where $p, q, r$ are the complex numbers associated with $P, Q, R$. Let $X = W \cup (1-W)$.
A road exists between cities $A$ and $B$ (represented by complex numbers $a$ and $b$) if for every city $C$ (represented by $c$) distinct from $A$ and $B$, we have:
$\triangle PQR$ is directly similar to either $\triangle ABC$ or $\triangle BAC$ for some $R \in S$.
This is equivalent to $\frac{c-a}{b-a} \in W$ or $\frac{c-b}{a-b} \in W$. Since $\frac{c-b}{a-b} = 1 - \frac{c-a}{b-a}$, the condition is $\frac{c-a}{b-a} \in X$.

Thus, an edge $AB$ exists if and only if $V \setminus \{A, B\} \subseteq a + X(b-a)$, where $V$ is the set of all cities. Alice wins if the resulting graph $G = (V, E)$ is both connected and planar. Bob wins if $G$ is either disconnected or non-planar.

**Case 1: $X = \mathbb{C}$.**
If Alice chooses $S$ such that $X = \mathbb{C}$, then for any pair of cities $A, B$ and any other city $C$, the condition $\frac{c-a}{b-a} \in X$ is always satisfied. Consequently, every pair of cities is connected by a road, and $G$ is a complete graph $K_\infty$. Since $K_\infty$ contains $K_5$ as a subgraph, it is not planar. Thus, Bob wins.

**Case 2: $X \neq \mathbb{C}$.**
If $X$ is not the entire plane, Bob can choose $V$ to ensure the graph is disconnected by making it empty. An edge $AB$ exists if and only if there is no city $C$ such that $\frac{c-a}{b-a} \notin X$. To make the graph empty, Bob must ensure that for every pair $A, B \in V$, there exists at least one city $C \in V \setminus \{A, B\}$ such that $\frac{c-a}{b-a} \notin X$.

Let $z_0 \in \mathbb{C} \setminus X$. Bob can construct $V$ as follows:
Start with two points $v_1, v_2$ such that $|v_1 - v_2| > 1$. Define a sequence of sets $V_n$ where $V_1 = \{v_1, v_2\}$ and $V_{n+1}$ is obtained by adding, for every pair $a, b \in V_n$, the point $a + z_0(b-a)$. Let $V = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty V_n$.
Since $z_0 \notin \mathbb{R}$, any three points $a, b, a + z_0(b-a)$ are not collinear. Bob can ensure that no three points in the entire set $V$ are collinear and that the distance between any two points is greater than 1 by choosing $z_0$ and the initial points $v_1, v_2$ carefully, or by applying a sufficiently large scaling factor to the resulting set $V$.
In this set $V$, for any pair $a, b \in V$, there exists $c = a + z_0(b-a) \in V$ such that $\frac{c-a}{b-a} = z_0$. Since $z_0 \notin X$, the edge $ab$ cannot exist. Thus, the graph $G$ is empty. An empty graph with infinitely many vertices is disconnected.

In both cases, Bob has a strategy to prevent Alice from winning.

Bob has a winning strategy. \(\square\)
