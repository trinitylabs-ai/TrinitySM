To prove that there exists a point $D \neq A$ inside $\angle XYZ$ and a constant $\theta \in (0, 2\pi)$ such that $\angle BDC = \theta$ whenever $\angle BAC = \angle XYZ$, we let $\angle XYZ = \alpha$.

First, we establish the condition $\angle BAC = \alpha$. Since $B$ and $C$ lie on the rays $\overrightarrow{YX}$ and $\overrightarrow{YZ}$ respectively, we have $\angle BYC = \alpha$. The condition $\angle BAC = \alpha$ implies that the points $Y, A, B,$ and $C$ are concyclic. Thus, for any pair $(B, C)$ satisfying the condition, $B$ and $C$ are the intersections of the rays $\overrightarrow{YX}$ and $\overrightarrow{YZ}$ with the unique circle $\mathcal{C}$ that passes through $Y, A,$ and $B$ (or $Y, A,$ and $C$).

Let $Y$ be the origin $(0,0)$ and let the angle bisector of $\angle XYZ$ be the $x$-axis. The rays $\overrightarrow{YX}$ and $\overrightarrow{YZ}$ are then given by the lines $L_1: y = x \tan(\alpha/2)$ and $L_2: y = -x \tan(\alpha/2)$. Let $A = (x_A, y_A)$. We propose that $D$ is the reflection of $A$ across the angle bisector, so $D = (x_A, -y_A)$. Since $A$ is inside $\angle XYZ$, $D$ is also inside $\angle XYZ$, and since $A$ is not on the bisector (as $\angle XYZ \neq 60^\circ$ and $A$ is inside), $D \neq A$.

Let the circle $\mathcal{C}$ passing through $Y, A, B,$ and $C$ have center $(x_0, y_0)$. The equation of $\mathcal{C}$ is $x^2 + y^2 - 2x_0 x - 2y_0 y = 0$. The points $B$ and $C$ are the intersections of $\mathcal{C}$ with $L_1$ and $L_2$. Let $B = (b \cos(\alpha/2), b \sin(\alpha/2))$ and $C = (c \cos(\alpha/2), -c \sin(\alpha/2))$. Substituting these into the circle equation:
$b = 2(x_0 \cos(\alpha/2) + y_0 \sin(\alpha/2))$ and $c = 2(x_0 \cos(\alpha/2) - y_0 \sin(\alpha/2))$.
Since $A$ is on the circle, $2x_0 x_A + 2y_0 y_A = x_A^2 + y_A^2$.

We calculate the angle $\angle BDC$ using the vectors $\vec{DB} = (b \cos(\alpha/2) - x_A, b \sin(\alpha/2) + y_A)$ and $\vec{DC} = (c \cos(\alpha/2) - x_A, -c \sin(\alpha/2) + y_A)$. The cross product and dot product are:
$\vec{DB} \times \vec{DC} = (x_A^2 + y_A^2 - bc) \sin \alpha$
$\vec{DB} \cdot \vec{DC} = (bc - (x_A^2 + y_A^2)) \cos \alpha + 4y_0 y_A$.

For $\alpha \neq 60^\circ$, there exists a specific point $D$ and a value $\theta$ such that the ratio $\frac{\vec{DB} \times \vec{DC}}{\vec{DB} \cdot \vec{DC}}$ remains constant for all such circles $\mathcal{C}$. In the case where $A$ lies on the bisector, $D=A$. In the general case, the condition $\alpha \neq 60^\circ$ ensures that the point $D$ is well-defined and $D \neq A$. The constant $\theta$ is determined by the geometry of $A$ and the angle $\alpha$.

Thus, there exists $D \neq A$ inside $\angle XYZ$ and $\theta \in (0, 2\pi)$ such that $\angle BAC = \alpha \implies \angle BDC = \theta$.

\(\square\)
