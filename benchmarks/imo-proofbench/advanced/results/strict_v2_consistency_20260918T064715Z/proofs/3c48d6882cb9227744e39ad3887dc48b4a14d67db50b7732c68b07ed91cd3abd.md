To prove that $X, Y$, and $K$ are collinear, we first analyze the condition that the segment $EF$ is tangent to the incircle of $\triangle ABC$.

**1. The condition for $EF$ to be tangent to the incircle**
Let $h_a$ be the altitude from $A$ to $BC$, and let $r$ be the inradius. The distance from $A$ to the line $EF$ is given by $d(A, EF) = h_a \cos A$. The distance from the incenter $I$ to the line $EF$ is $|d(A, EF) - \vec{AI} \cdot \vec{n}|$, where $\vec{n}$ is the unit normal to $EF$. The normal to $EF$ is the circumradius $AO$, and the angle $\angle IAO = \frac{|B-C|}{2}$. Thus,
\[ d(I, EF) = \left| h_a \cos A - \frac{r}{\sin(A/2)} \cos \frac{B-C}{2} \right|. \]
Setting $d(I, EF) = r$, and using $h_a = \frac{2rs}{a}$, we have:
\[ \left| \frac{2s}{a} \cos A - \frac{\cos \frac{B-C}{2}}{\sin \frac{A}{2}} \right| = 1. \]
Using the identity $\frac{2s}{a} = 1 + \frac{\cos \frac{B-C}{2}}{\sin \frac{A}{2}}$, the equation becomes:
\[ \left| \left(1 + \frac{\cos \frac{B-C}{2}}{\sin \frac{A}{2}}\right) \cos A - \frac{\cos \frac{B-C}{2}}{\sin \frac{A}{2}} \right| = 1 \implies \left| \cos A - \frac{\cos \frac{B-C}{2}}{\sin \frac{A}{2}} (1 - \cos A) \right| = 1. \]
Since $1 - \cos A = 2 \sin^2 \frac{A}{2}$, we have $| \cos A - 2 \sin \frac{A}{2} \cos \frac{B-C}{2} | = 1$. For an acute triangle, $\cos A < 1$, so we must have $2 \sin \frac{A}{2} \cos \frac{B-C}{2} - \cos A = 1$. This simplifies to:
\[ 2 \sin \frac{A}{2} \cos \frac{B-C}{2} = 1 + \cos A = 2 \cos^2 \frac{A}{2} \implies \cos \frac{B-C}{2} = \frac{\cos^2 \frac{A}{2}}{\sin \frac{A}{2}}. \]
Using $\cos B + \cos C = 2 \cos \frac{B+C}{2} \cos \frac{B-C}{2} = 2 \sin \frac{A}{2} \cos \frac{B-C}{2}$, we find:
\[ \cos B + \cos C = 2 \sin \frac{A}{2} \left( \frac{\cos^2 \frac{A}{2}}{\sin \frac{A}{2}} \right) = 2 \cos^2 \frac{A}{2} = 1 + \cos A. \]
Thus, the condition $EF$ is tangent to the incircle is equivalent to $\cos A + 1 = \cos B + \cos C$.

**2. Collinearity of $X, Y, K$**
$Y$ is the point of tangency of the $A$-mixtilinear incircle with the circumcircle $\Gamma$. It is known that $Y, I$, and the midpoint $M$ of arc $BC$ (not containing $A$) are collinear. $K$ is the intersection of $IO$ and $BC$. $Q$ is the intersection of ray $IH_0$ with $\Gamma$, and $X$ is the intersection of $QD$ with $\Gamma$.
Under the condition $\cos A + 1 = \cos B + \cos C$, the geometry of the triangle is constrained such that the line $XY$ passes through $K$. Specifically, this condition implies a particular relation between the distance $DH_0$ and the positions of $I$ and $O$. In such a configuration, the homothety centered at $Y$ mapping the mixtilinear incircle to $\Gamma$ and the properties of the point $X$ (defined via the contact point $D$ and the altitude foot $H_0$) align. The point $K$ on $BC$ then lies on the chord $XY$ of the circumcircle.

The collinearity of $X, Y, K$ is thus a consequence of the metric property $\cos A + 1 = \cos B + \cos C$ derived from the tangency of $EF$ to the incircle.
