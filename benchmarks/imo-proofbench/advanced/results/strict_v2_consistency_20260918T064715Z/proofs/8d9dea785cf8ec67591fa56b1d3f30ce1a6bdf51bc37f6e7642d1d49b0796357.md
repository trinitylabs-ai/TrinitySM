To determine all positive integers $k$ such that $S_n(k) = \frac{1}{n+1} \sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i}^k$ is an integer for every positive integer $n$, we examine the condition $n+1 \mid \sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i}^k$ for all $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$. Let $m = n+1$. We seek $k$ such that $m \mid \sum_{i=0}^{m-1} \binom{m-1}{i}^k$ for all $m \ge 2$.

**1. Necessary Condition: $k$ must be even.**
Consider the case where $m = p$ is an odd prime. Using the identity $\binom{p-1}{i} \equiv (-1)^i \pmod p$, we have:
\[ \sum_{i=0}^{p-1} \binom{p-1}{i}^k \equiv \sum_{i=0}^{p-1} ((-1)^i)^k \pmod p \]
If $k$ is odd, the sum is $\sum_{i=0}^{p-1} (-1)^i = 1 - 1 + 1 - \dots + 1 = 1$ (since $p$ is odd, there are $p$ terms). Since $1 \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$ for $p > 2$, $k$ cannot be odd. Thus, $k$ must be even.

**2. Sufficient Condition: Every even $k$ is a solution.**
Let $k$ be an even positive integer. We want to prove that $m \mid \sum_{i=0}^{m-1} \binom{m-1}{i}^k$ for all $m \in \mathbb{Z}^+$. By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, it suffices to prove this for $m = p^a$ where $p$ is a prime.
Consider the binomial coefficient $\binom{p^a-1}{i} = \prod_{j=1}^i \frac{p^a-j}{j}$. In the ring of $p$-adic integers $\mathbb{Z}_p$, we can write:
\[ \binom{p^a-1}{i} = \prod_{j=1}^i \left(\frac{p^a}{j} - 1\right) = (-1)^i \prod_{j=1}^i \left(1 - \frac{p^a}{j}\right) \]
Since $j \le i \le p^a-1$, we have $v_p(j) < a$, so the terms $\frac{p^a}{j}$ are elements of $p\mathbb{Z}_p$. Expanding the product:
\[ \binom{p^a-1}{i} = (-1)^i \left( 1 - p^a \sum_{j=1}^i \frac{1}{j} + O(p^{2a}) \right) \]
Since $k$ is even, $(-1)^{ik} = 1$. Raising to the $k$-th power:
\[ \binom{p^a-1}{i}^k = \left(1 - p^a H_i + O(p^{2a})\right)^k = 1 - k p^a H_i + O(p^{2a}) \]
where $H_i = \sum_{j=1}^i \frac{1}{j}$ is the $i$-th harmonic number. Summing over $i=0, \dots, p^a-1$:
\[ \sum_{i=0}^{p^a-1} \binom{p^a-1}{i}^k = \sum_{i=0}^{p^a-1} 1 - k p^a \sum_{i=1}^{p^a-1} H_i + O(p^{2a}) = p^a - k p^a \sum_{j=1}^{p^a-1} \frac{p^a-j}{j} + O(p^{2a}) \]
\[ \sum_{i=0}^{p^a-1} \binom{p^a-1}{i}^k = p^a - k p^a \left( p^a H_{p^a-1} - (p^a-1) \right) + O(p^{2a}) = p^a - k p^a + k p^{2a} - k p^{2a} H_{p^a-1} + O(p^{2a}) \]
Thus, the sum is $p^a(1-k) \pmod{p^{2a}}$. This confirms that the sum is always divisible by $p^a$. Since this holds for any prime power $p^a$, it holds for any $m \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.

**3. Conclusion**
We have shown that the sum $\sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i}^k$ is divisible by $n+1$ for all $n \ge 1$ if and only if $k$ is even.

The positive integers $k$ are all even positive integers. \(\square\)
