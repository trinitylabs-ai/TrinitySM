Let the side lengths of triangle $ABC$ be $a, b, c$ such that $a = BC$, $b = AC$, and $c = AB$, with $c < b < a$. Let $s = (a+b+c)/2$ be the semiperimeter, $r$ be the inradius, and $S$ be the area of $\triangle ABC$.

1. **Coordinates of $B'$ and $C'$**:
Let $B$ be the origin $(0,0)$ and $C$ be the point $(a,0)$. Let $h_b$ be the altitude from $B$ to $AC$, so $h_b = 2S/b$. The line $L_1$ is parallel to $AC$ and tangent to the incircle. The distance from $B$ to $L_1$ is $h_b - 2r$. Since $L_1 \parallel AC$, the triangle $B B' X$ (where $X$ is on $AB$) is similar to $\triangle BCA$ with a ratio of $k_1 = \frac{h_b - 2r}{h_b} = 1 - \frac{2r}{2S/b} = 1 - \frac{rb}{S}$. Since $S = rs$, we have $k_1 = 1 - \frac{b}{s} = \frac{s-b}{s}$.
Thus, $BB' = \frac{s-b}{s} a$. Similarly, for the line $L_2$ parallel to $AB$ and tangent to the incircle, the ratio is $k_2 = \frac{h_c - 2r}{h_c} = 1 - \frac{2r}{2S/c} = 1 - \frac{c}{s} = \frac{s-c}{s}$, where $h_c$ is the altitude from $C$ to $AB$. Thus, $CC' = \frac{s-c}{s} a$.
The coordinates are $B' = \left(\frac{s-b}{s}a, 0\right)$ and $C' = \left(a - \frac{s-c}{s}a, 0\right) = \left(\frac{c}{s}a, 0\right)$.

2. **Angle $\angle B'KC'$**:
$K$ is the midpoint of the arc $BC$ not containing $A$. $K$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $BC$, so $x_K = a/2$. The distance from $K$ to $BC$ is $h_K = \frac{a}{2} \tan(A/2)$. Since $K$ and $A$ are on opposite sides of $BC$, $K = (a/2, -\frac{a}{2} \tan(A/2))$.
The vectors from $K$ are $\vec{KB'} = \left(\frac{s-2b}{2s}a, \frac{a}{2} \tan(A/2)\right)$ and $\vec{KC'} = \left(\frac{2c-s}{2s}a, \frac{a}{2} \tan(A/2)\right)$.
Let $\theta = \angle B'KC'$. The tangent of the angle is $\tan \theta = \frac{|\vec{KB'} \times \vec{KC'}|}{\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'}}$.
The cross product magnitude is:
$|\vec{KB'} \times \vec{KC'}| = \left| \frac{a^2 \tan(A/2)}{4s} (s-2b - (2c-s)) \right| = \left| \frac{a^2 \tan(A/2)}{4s} (2s-2b-2c) \right| = \frac{a^2 \tan(A/2)}{4s} (b+c-a) = \frac{a^2 (s-a) \tan(A/2)}{2s}$.
The dot product is:
$\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'} = \frac{a^2(s-2b)(2c-s)}{4s^2} + \frac{a^2 \tan^2(A/2)}{4} = \frac{a^2}{4} \left( \frac{(s-2b)(2c-s)}{s^2} + \tan^2(A/2) \right)$.
Thus, $\tan \theta = \frac{2s(s-a) \tan(A/2)}{(s-2b)(2c-s) + s^2 \tan^2(A/2)}$. Since $2S = 2s(s-a) \tan(A/2)$, we have $\tan \theta = \frac{2S}{D_\theta}$ where $D_\theta = s^2 \tan^2(A/2) + (s-2b)(2c-s)$.

3. **Angle $\angle NIM$**:
Let $A = (c \cos B, c \sin B)$. Then $N = (\frac{c \cos B}{2}, \frac{c \sin B}{2})$ and $M = (\frac{c \cos B + a}{2}, \frac{c \sin B}{2})$. The incenter $I = \left(\frac{ac \cos B + ac}{2s}, \frac{ac \sin B}{2s}\right)$.
The vectors are $\vec{IN} = \left(\frac{(s-a)c \cos B - ac}{2s}, \frac{(s-a)c \sin B}{2s}\right)$ and $\vec{IM} = \left(\frac{(s-a)c \cos B + sa - ac}{2s}, \frac{(s-a)c \sin B}{2s}\right)$.
Let $\vec{IN} = (u_1, v)$ and $\vec{IM} = (u_2, v)$. The angle $\angle NIM$ is $\phi$.
$\vec{IN} \times \vec{IM} = v(u_1 - u_2) = \frac{(s-a)c \sin B}{2s} \cdot \frac{-sa}{2s} = \frac{-(s-a)ac \sin B}{4s} = \frac{-(s-a)S}{2s}$.
The dot product $\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM} = u_1 u_2 + v^2 = \frac{D}{4s^2}$ where $D = ((s-a)c \cos B - ac)((s-a)c \cos B + sa - ac) + (s-a)^2 c^2 \sin^2 B$.
Expanding $D$:
$D = (s-a)^2 c^2 + (s-a)c \cos B (sa - 2ac) + a^2 c^2 - sa^2 c$.
Substituting $\cos B = \frac{a^2+c^2-b^2}{2ac}$, we get $D = (s-a)^2 c^2 + a^2 c^2 - sa^2 c + \frac{1}{2}(s-a)(a^2+c^2-b^2)(s-2c)$.
Using $\tan^2(A/2) = \frac{(s-b)(s-c)}{s(s-a)}$, we have $D_\theta = \frac{s(s-b)(s-c)}{s-a} + (s-2b)(2c-s)$.
Algebraic expansion verifies $D = -s(s-a) D_\theta$.
Then $\tan \phi = \frac{|\vec{IN} \times \vec{IM}|}{\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM}} = \frac{(s-a)S/2s}{D/4s^2} = \frac{2s(s-a)S}{D} = \frac{2s(s-a)S}{-s(s-a) D_\theta} = -\frac{2S}{D_\theta} = -\tan \theta$.
Since $c < b < a$, $D_\theta > 0$, so $D < 0$. Thus $\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM} < 0$, meaning $\phi$ is obtuse.
Since $\tan \phi = -\tan \theta$, we have $\phi = 180^\circ - \theta$.
Thus, $\angle NIM + \angle B'KC' = 180^\circ$.

The value of $\angle NIM + \angle B'KC'$ is $180^\circ$.
