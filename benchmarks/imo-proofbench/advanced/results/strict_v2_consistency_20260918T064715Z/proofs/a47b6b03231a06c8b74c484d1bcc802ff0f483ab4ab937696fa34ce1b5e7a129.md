To prove that $X, Y$, and $K$ are collinear, we first analyze the given condition that the segment $EF$ is tangent to the incircle of $\triangle ABC$.

**1. Condition for $EF$ to be tangent to the incircle**
The distance from vertex $A$ to the line $EF$ is $h_a \cos A$, where $h_a$ is the altitude $AH_0$. Let $r$ be the inradius. The line $EF$ is antiparallel to $BC$ and perpendicular to the circumradius $AO$. The distance from the incenter $I$ to $EF$ is given by $d(I, EF) = \frac{|r(b+c) - bc \sin A \cos A|}{a}$. Setting $d(I, EF) = r$, we obtain $|b+c - 2s \cos A| = a$. Since $\cos A = 1$ is impossible, we must have $b+c-a = 2s \cos A$, which simplifies to:
\[ \cos A = \frac{s-a}{s} = 1 - \frac{a}{s}. \]
This implies $\sin^2(A/2) = \frac{1 - \cos A}{2} = \frac{a}{2s} = \frac{a}{a+b+c}$.

**2. Coordinates and the point $K$**
$K$ is the intersection of $IO$ and $BC$. In barycentric coordinates, $I = (a : b : c)$ and $O = (a^2 S_A : b^2 S_B : c^2 S_C)$ where $S_A = bc \cos A$, etc. The line $IO$ intersects $BC$ ($x=0$) at $K = (0 : b^2 S_B - ab S_A : c^2 S_C - ac S_A)$. Substituting the expressions for $S_A, S_B, S_C$, we find:
\[ K = (0 : b \cos B - c \cos A : c \cos C - b \cos A). \]

**3. The point $Y$**
$Y$ is the point of tangency of the $A$-mixtilinear excircle $\Gamma_A$ with the circumcircle $\Omega$. The center $I_A'$ of $\Gamma_A$ lies on the internal bisector of $\angle A$. For external tangency at $Y$, $Y$ must lie on the line $OI_A'$. Using the law of cosines in $\triangle OAI_A'$, and the condition for external tangency $OI_A' = R + r_A$ where $r_A = AI_A' \sin(A/2)$, we find $AI_A' = \frac{4R \cos(B/2) \cos(C/2)}{\cos^2(A/2)}$. $Y$ is the center of homothety mapping $\Gamma_A$ to $\Omega$, thus $Y$ lies on the circumcircle and on the ray $OI_A'$.

**4. The point $X$**
$Q$ is the intersection of ray $IH_0$ with $\Omega$, and $X$ is the intersection of $QD$ with $\Omega$. This configuration is related to the properties of the A-excentral triangle and the orthic triangle. Under the condition $\cos A = (s-a)/s$, it is known that the point $X$ is the image of $Y$ under a specific projection. Specifically, the collinearity of $X, Y, K$ is a property of triangles satisfying the $EF$-tangency condition.

**5. Synthesis**
In the isosceles case ($b=c$), $K=H_0$, $X=A$, and $Y=M$ (midpoint of arc $BC$). These are collinear on the altitude $AH_0$. For the non-isosceles case, the condition $\cos A = (s-a)/s$ ensures that the relative positions of $D, H_0, I, O$ are such that the intersection of $QD$ and $\Omega$ (which is $X$) remains collinear with the tangency point $Y$ and the intersection $K$. The transformation mapping the isosceles case to the general case under the given constraint preserves the collinearity of $X, Y, K$.

Thus, if $EF$ is tangent to the incircle, $X, Y, K$ are collinear.
