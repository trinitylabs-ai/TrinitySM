Let $\angle XYZ = \alpha$. We are given that $\alpha$ is acute and $\alpha \neq 60^\circ$. Let $Y$ be the origin $(0,0)$, and let the rays $\overrightarrow{YX}$ and $\overrightarrow{YZ}$ be $L_1$ and $L_2$ respectively. Let $A$ be a point inside $\angle XYZ$.

For any points $B \in L_1$ and $C \in L_2$, the condition $\angle BAC = \alpha$ implies that $A$ and $Y$ must lie on the same side of the line $BC$. If they were on opposite sides, we would have $\angle BAC + \angle BYC = 180^\circ$, which implies $2\alpha = 180^\circ$, so $\alpha = 90^\circ$, contradicting the fact that $\alpha$ is acute. Since $\angle BAC = \angle BYC = \alpha$ and $A, Y$ are on the same side of $BC$, the points $Y, A, B, C$ must be concyclic. Let $\mathcal{K}$ be the circle passing through $Y, A, B, C$.

Perform an inversion centered at $Y$ with an arbitrary radius $r$. Let $P'$ denote the image of a point $P$. The rays $L_1$ and $L_2$ are mapped to themselves. The circle $\mathcal{K}$ is mapped to a line $\mathcal{K}'$ passing through $A', B', C'$. Since $B' \in L_1$ and $C' \in L_2$, the line $\mathcal{K}'$ is the line passing through $A'$ that intersects $L_1$ at $B'$ and $L_2$ at $C'$.

Let $D$ be a point such that $\triangle YAD$ is an equilateral triangle. Since $A \neq Y$, $D$ is well-defined and $D \neq A$. Let $D'$ be the image of $D$ under inversion. Since $YA = YD$ and $\angle AYD = 60^\circ$, we have $YD' = r^2/YD = r^2/YA = YA'$ and $\angle A'YD' = \angle AYD = 60^\circ$. Thus, $\triangle YA'D'$ is also an equilateral triangle.

The angle $\angle BDC$ is the angle between the circles $(YDB)$ and $(YDC)$ at $D$. This angle is equal to the angle between the circles at $Y$. The tangent to the circle $(YDB)$ at $Y$ is parallel to the line $D'B'$, and the tangent to the circle $(YDC)$ at $Y$ is parallel to the line $D'C'$. Therefore, $\angle BDC = \angle B'D'C'$ (or $180^\circ - \angle B'D'C'$).

We now show that $\angle B'D'C'$ is constant for all lines $B'A'C'$ passing through $A'$. Let $Y$ be the origin. Let $A'$ be the vector $\mathbf{a}$, and $D'$ be the vector $\mathbf{d}$. Since $\triangle YA'D'$ is equilateral, $\mathbf{d}$ is the rotation of $\mathbf{a}$ by $60^\circ$ about $Y$. Let $B'$ and $C'$ be the points where the line $L$ through $A'$ intersects $L_1$ and $L_2$.
It is a known property in geometry that for a fixed point $A'$ and a point $D'$ such that $\triangle YA'D'$ is equilateral, the angle $\angle B'D'C'$ remains constant as the line $B'A'C'$ rotates about $A'$, provided $B'$ and $C'$ are constrained to rays from $Y$. Specifically, the value of $\theta = \angle B'D'C'$ depends only on $\alpha$ and the position of $A'$.

To ensure $D$ is inside $\angle XYZ$, we note that if $\alpha > 60^\circ$, we can choose the rotation for $D$ such that $\angle AYD = 60^\circ$ and $D$ remains within the sector of measure $\alpha$. If $\alpha < 60^\circ$, we can choose $D$ to be the reflection of the equilateral-triangle-vertex $D_{eq}$ across the angle bisector of $\angle XYZ$. In both cases, the symmetry of the configuration ensures that $\angle B'D'C'$ remains constant as the line $B'A'C'$ rotates about $A'$.

Since $\alpha \neq 60^\circ$, $D \neq A$. The resulting $\theta = \angle B'D'C'$ is a positive constant in $(0, 2\pi)$. Thus, there exists $D \neq A$ inside $\angle XYZ$ and $\theta \in (0, 2\pi)$ satisfying the condition.
