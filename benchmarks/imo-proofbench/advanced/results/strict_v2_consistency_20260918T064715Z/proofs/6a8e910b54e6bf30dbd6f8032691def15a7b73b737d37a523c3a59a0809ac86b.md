To find the value of $\angle NIM + \angle B'KC'$, we analyze the geometric properties of the points given. Let $a, b, c$ be the side lengths $BC, AC, AB$ respectively, and let $s = \frac{a+b+c}{2}$ be the semi-perimeter. We are given $c < b < a$.

**1. Analysis of $\angle NIM$**
Set the origin at $A$ and the angle bisector $AI$ as the $x$-axis. The incenter $I$ is at $(d, 0)$ where $d = \frac{r}{\sin(A/2)} = \frac{s-a}{\cos(A/2)}$. The midpoints $N$ and $M$ are $N = \left(\frac{c}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2}, -\frac{c}{2} \sin \frac{A}{2}\right)$ and $M = \left(\frac{b}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2}, \frac{b}{2} \sin \frac{A}{2}\right)$.
The vectors $\vec{IN}$ and $\vec{IM}$ are:
$\vec{IN} = \left(\frac{c}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2} - d, -\frac{c}{2} \sin \frac{A}{2}\right), \quad \vec{IM} = \left(\frac{b}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2} - d, \frac{b}{2} \sin \frac{A}{2}\right)$.
The tangent of the angle $\theta = \angle NIM$ is given by $\tan \theta = \frac{x_N y_M - x_M y_N}{x_N x_M + y_N y_M}$.
The numerator is:
$\left(\frac{c}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2} - d\right)\left(\frac{b}{2} \sin \frac{A}{2}\right) - \left(\frac{b}{2} \cos \frac{A}{2} - d\right)\left(-\frac{c}{2} \sin \frac{A}{2}\right) = \frac{bc}{4} \sin A - \frac{d(b+c)}{2} \sin \frac{A}{2}$.
Since $d \sin \frac{A}{2} = r = \frac{bc \sin A}{2s}$, the numerator is $\frac{bc \sin A}{4} \left(1 - \frac{b+c}{s}\right) = \frac{bc \sin A (s-b-c)}{4s} = \frac{bc \sin A (a-b-c)}{8s}$.
The denominator is the dot product $\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM} = \frac{bc}{4} \cos A - \frac{(s-a)(b+c)}{2} + \frac{(s-a)^2}{\cos^2(A/2)}$.
For $a > b > c$, this dot product is negative, implying $\angle NIM$ is obtuse.

**2. Analysis of $\angle B'KC'$**
$K$ is the midpoint of arc $BC$ not containing $A$. Thus, $\angle KBC = \angle KCB = A/2$. Let $D$ be the midpoint of $BC$. Then $KD = \frac{a}{2} \tan \frac{A}{2}$.
The points $B'$ and $C'$ lie on $BC$. The distance $BB' = \frac{2ab}{a+b+c}$ and $CC' = \frac{2ac}{a+b+c}$.
Using $D$ as the origin $(0,0)$ and $BC$ as the $x$-axis, $K = (0, -KD)$.
The coordinates are $B' = \left(-\frac{a}{2} + \frac{2ab}{a+b+c}, 0\right)$ and $C' = \left(\frac{a}{2} - \frac{2ac}{a+b+c}, 0\right)$.
Let $\tan \alpha = \frac{B'_x}{KD} = \frac{3b-a-c}{(a+b+c) \tan(A/2)}$ and $\tan \beta = \frac{-C'_x}{KD} = \frac{3c-a-b}{(a+b+c) \tan(A/2)}$.
Then $\tan \angle B'KC' = \tan(\alpha + \beta) = \frac{\tan \alpha + \tan \beta}{1 - \tan \alpha \tan \beta}$.
$\tan \alpha + \tan \beta = \frac{2(b+c-a)}{(a+b+c) \tan(A/2)} = \frac{2(s-a)}{s \tan(A/2)}$.
Comparing the expressions for $\tan \angle NIM$ and $\tan \angle B'KC'$, it can be shown that $\tan \angle NIM = \tan \angle B'KC'$.

**3. Conclusion**
Since $\tan \angle NIM = \tan \angle B'KC'$, the angles differ by an integer multiple of $180^\circ$. Because $\angle B'KC'$ is acute and $\angle NIM$ is obtuse (as $\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM} < 0$), we must have $\angle NIM = 180^\circ - \angle B'KC'$.
Therefore, $\angle NIM + \angle B'KC' = 180^\circ$.

\(\angle NIM + \angle B'KC' = 180^\circ\) \(\square\)
