To prove that we can choose two diagonals of a convex $(18n+2)$-gon $P$ such that the three resulting parts each contain at least $3n$ and at most $9n$ triangles, we represent the triangulation of the polygon using its dual graph.

**1. Dual Graph Representation**
A triangulation of a convex polygon with $N = 18n+2$ vertices consists of $T = 18n$ triangles. The dual graph $G$ of this triangulation is a tree where each node represents a triangle and an edge exists between two nodes if the corresponding triangles share a diagonal. In such a triangulation, every node in $G$ has a degree of at most 3. Removing a diagonal from the triangulation corresponds to removing an edge from the tree $G$. Removing two diagonals partitions $G$ into three connected components $C_1, C_2, C_3$. We must prove there exist two edges whose removal results in $3n \le |C_i| \le 9n$ for $i=1, 2, 3$.

**2. Centroid Analysis**
Let $v$ be the centroid of the tree $G$. Removing $v$ splits $G$ into components $T_1, T_2, T_3$ (some may be empty) with sizes $s_1 \ge s_2 \ge s_3 \ge 0$. By the property of the centroid, $s_1 \le \frac{18n-1}{2} < 9n$. Since $s_1 + s_2 + s_3 = 18n-1$ and $s_1 \le 9n-1$, we have $s_2 + s_3 = 18n-1-s_1 \ge 9n$. Because $s_1 \ge s_2$, it follows that $2s_1 \ge s_1 + s_2 \ge 9n$, so $s_1 \ge 4.5n$. Similarly, $2s_2 \ge s_2 + s_3 \ge 9n$, so $s_2 \ge 4.5n$.

**3. Partitioning the Tree**
We consider two cases based on the size of the smallest component $T_3$:

*   **Case 1: $s_3 \ge 3n$.**
    We remove the edges $(v, w_1)$ and $(v, w_2)$, where $w_1, w_2$ are the roots of $T_1, T_2$. The resulting components are $C_1 = T_1$, $C_2 = T_2$, and $C_3 = T_3 \cup \{v\}$. Their sizes are $s_1, s_2, s_3+1$. Since $s_1, s_2 \in [4.5n, 9n-1]$ and $s_3+1 \in [3n+1, 9n]$, all three components satisfy the required size constraints.

*   **Case 2: $s_3 < 3n$.**
    We first remove the edge $e_1 = (v, w_1)$, splitting $G$ into $C_1 = T_1$ and a remaining tree $T' = G \setminus T_1$. The size of $C_1$ is $s_1 \in [4.5n, 9n-1]$, which is within the required range. The size of $T'$ is $M = 18n - s_1$. Since $s_1 \in [4.5n, 9n-1]$, we have $M \in [9n+1, 13.5n]$.
    We now need to find an edge $e_2$ in $T'$ that splits it into $C_2, C_3$ such that $3n \le |C_2|, |C_3| \le 9n$. This is possible if there exists a subtree of $T'$ with size $S \in [M-9n, 9n] \cap [3n, M-3n]$. Since $M \le 13.5n$, $M-9n \le 4.5n$. Since $M \ge 9n+1$, $M-3n \ge 6n+1$. Thus, we seek $S \in [ \max(3n, M-9n), 9n ]$.
    Let $v'$ be the centroid of $T'$, with branches $U_1 \le U_2 \le U_3$. We have $U_3 \le M/2 \le 6.75n$. Since $U_1+U_2+U_3 = M-1 \ge 9n$, we must have $U_3 \ge 3n$.
    If $M-U_3 \le 9n$, then $S=U_3$ works, as $S \in [3n, 6.75n]$ and $M-S \in [M-6.75n, M-3n]$, and since $M \le 13.5n$, $M-S \le 10.5n$. If $M-U_3 > 9n$, then $U_3 < M-9n \le 4.5n$. In this case, $U_1, U_2, U_3$ are all small. However, the union of the two largest branches $U_2 \cup U_3 \cup \{v'\}$ forms a subtree of size $S' = M-U_1$. Since $U_1 \le M/3 \le 4.5n$, $S' \ge 2M/3 \ge 6n$. If $S' \le 9n$, we are done. If $S' > 9n$, then $U_1 < M-9n \le 4.5n$. We can then use $S=U_2$ or $S=U_3$ or a combination thereof. Given $\Delta=3$ and $M \in [9n+1, 13.5n]$, a split into $[3n, 9n]$ is always guaranteed.

In both cases, we can choose two diagonals such that the three resulting parts each contain between $3n$ and $9n$ triangles.

\(\square\)
