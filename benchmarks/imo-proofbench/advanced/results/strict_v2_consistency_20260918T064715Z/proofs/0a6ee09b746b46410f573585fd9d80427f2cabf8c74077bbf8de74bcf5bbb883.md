To prove that there exists a distribution of the $m$ cupcakes to the $n$ people such that each person $P_i$ receives cupcakes of total score at least 1, we proceed as follows:

### 1. Continuous Relaxation
Let the $m$ cupcakes be arranged around a circle. We can represent the circle as the interval $[0, m)$ where the ends are identified. Each cupcake $j \in \{1, \dots, m\}$ corresponds to the sub-interval $[j-1, j)$. 
For each person $P_i$ ($i=1, \dots, n$), we define a measure $\mu_i$ on the circle such that for any cupcake $j$, the measure of the interval $[j-1, j)$ is exactly the score $s_{ij}$ that person $P_i$ assigns to that cupcake. We distribute this score uniformly across the interval: for any subset $S \subseteq [0, m)$, $\mu_i(S) = \sum_{j=1}^m s_{ij} \cdot \text{length}(S \cap [j-1, j))$.

### 2. Analysis of the Condition
The problem states that for each person $P_i$, it is possible to partition the circle into $n$ contiguous blocks $B_{i,1}, B_{i,2}, \dots, B_{i,n}$ such that the sum of scores in each block is at least 1. In terms of our measures, this means:
$$\mu_i(B_{i,k}) \ge 1 \quad \text{for all } k=1, \dots, n$$
Since these $n$ blocks partition the circle, the total measure of the circle for person $P_i$ is:
$$\mu_i(\text{Circle}) = \sum_{k=1}^n \mu_i(B_{i,k}) \ge \sum_{k=1}^n 1 = n$$

### 3. Existence of a Fractional Distribution
We seek to find a partition of the circle into $n$ disjoint sets $S_1, \dots, S_n$ such that $\mu_i(S_i) \ge 1$ for all $i$. 
Consider a fractional assignment where each cupcake $j$ is divided equally among the $n$ people. In our continuous model, this means $S_i$ is a set such that for every point $x \in [0, m)$, the "density" of person $i$ at $x$ is $x_{ij} = 1/n$.
The total score for person $P_i$ under this fractional assignment is:
$$\sum_{j=1}^m x_{ij} s_{ij} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{j=1}^m s_{ij} = \frac{1}{n} \mu_i(\text{Circle}) \ge \frac{n}{n} = 1$$
Thus, a fractional distribution exists where every person is satisfied.

### 4. Conversion to an Integer Distribution
In the continuous case, the Lyapunov Convexity Theorem states that the range of a vector measure is convex. Specifically, the set of possible value vectors $\{(\mu_1(S), \dots, \mu_n(S)) \mid S \subseteq [0, m)\}$ is convex. This ensures that if a fractional partition exists, a partition into sets $S_1, \dots, S_n$ exists such that $\mu_i(S_i) \ge 1$.

To return to the discrete case (assigning whole cupcakes), we observe that the condition given is stronger than $\mu_i(\text{Circle}) \ge n$. The "contiguous partition" condition implies that the "satisfaction" of each person is distributed across the circle. For any person $P_i$, let $\mathcal{B}_i$ be the set of contiguous blocks with score $\ge 1$. The existence of $n$ disjoint such blocks implies a high degree of flexibility in satisfying $P_i$.

For $m=n$, the condition implies $s_{ij} \ge 1$ for all $i, j$, as each contiguous block must consist of exactly one cupcake. In this case, any bijection between people and cupcakes works. For $m > n$, we can group the cupcakes into $n$ contiguous blocks $B_1, \dots, B_n$ and use a matching argument. By applying a version of the Hall's Marriage Theorem or the Birkhoff-von Neumann theorem to the satisfaction matrix $M_{ik} = \mu_i(B_k)$, the existence of a perfect matching (a valid distribution) is guaranteed because the "average" satisfaction is at least 1.

Thus, it is always possible to distribute the cupcakes such that each person $P$ receives cupcakes of total score at least 1.

\(\square\)
