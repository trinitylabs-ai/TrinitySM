To prove that we can choose two diagonals such that the three parts of the convex $18n+2$-gon $P$ each contain at least $3n$ and at most $9n$ triangles, we utilize the dual graph of the triangulation.

**1. Dual Graph Representation**
Let the triangulation of the convex $18n+2$-gon $P$ be given. We construct the dual graph $T$ of this triangulation as follows:
- Each node in $T$ corresponds to one of the $18n$ triangles.
- Two nodes are connected by an edge if the corresponding triangles share a common diagonal.
Since $P$ is a convex polygon and the triangulation consists of $18n-1$ non-crossing diagonals, the dual graph $T$ is a tree with $N = 18n$ nodes. Furthermore, because each triangle has exactly three sides, each node in $T$ has a degree of at most 3.
A diagonal in the triangulation corresponds to an edge in the dual tree. Removing an edge from a tree splits it into two connected components. Removing two edges splits the tree into three connected components. These components correspond to the three parts of the polygon $P$ divided by the two chosen diagonals.

**2. First Split**
We use the property of the centroid of a tree. A centroid is a node $v$ such that the removal of $v$ splits the tree into components each having a size at most $N/2$. 
For $N = 18n$, the centroid $v$ splits $T$ into components $S_1, S_2, \dots, S_k$ with sizes $s_1, s_2, \dots, s_k$. Since the maximum degree of any node is 3, we have $k \le 3$. By the definition of the centroid:
- $s_i \le \frac{18n}{2} = 9n$ for all $i=1, \dots, k$.
- $\sum_{i=1}^k s_i = N - 1 = 18n - 1$.

Since $k \le 3$ and the sum of the sizes is $18n-1$, at least one component $S_j$ must satisfy:
$$s_j \ge \left\lceil \frac{18n-1}{3} \right\rceil = 6n$$
Let $e_1$ be the edge in $T$ connecting $v$ to the component $S_j$. Removing $e_1$ splits $T$ into two components $T_1$ and $T_2$ with sizes $t_1$ and $M$:
- $t_1 = s_j \in [6n, 9n]$.
- $M = 18n - t_1 \in [9n, 12n]$.

**3. Second Split**
We now consider the tree $T_2$ with $M$ nodes. Since $T_2$ is a subtree of $T$, its maximum degree is also at most 3. Let $v'$ be the centroid of $T_2$. The removal of $v'$ splits $T_2$ into components $S'_1, S'_2, \dots, S'_m$ with sizes $s'_1, s'_2, \dots, s'_m$, where $m \le 3$. By the centroid property:
- $s'_i \le \frac{M}{2} \le \frac{12n}{2} = 6n$ for all $i=1, \dots, m$.
- $\sum_{i=1}^m s'_i = M - 1 \ge 9n - 1$.

Again, since $m \le 3$, at least one component $S'_l$ must satisfy:
$$s'_l \ge \left\lceil \frac{M-1}{3} \right\rceil \ge \left\lceil \frac{9n-1}{3} \right\rceil = 3n$$
Let $e_2$ be the edge in $T_2$ connecting $v'$ to the component $S'_l$. Removing $e_2$ splits $T_2$ into two components $T_{2a}$ and $T_{2b}$ with sizes $t_2$ and $t_3$:
- $t_2 = s'_l \in [3n, 6n]$.
- $t_3 = M - t_2$.

**4. Final Verification of Bounds**
We have divided the original tree $T$ into three components with sizes $t_1, t_2, t_3$. We verify that each size is within the range $[3n, 9n]$:
- $t_1 \in [6n, 9n]$, which is clearly within $[3n, 9n]$.
- $t_2 \in [3n, 6n]$, which is clearly within $[3n, 9n]$.
- For $t_3 = 18n - t_1 - t_2$:
    - The minimum value of $t_3$ occurs when $t_1$ and $t_2$ are maximized: $t_{3, \text{min}} = 18n - 9n - 6n = 3n$.
    - The maximum value of $t_3$ occurs when $t_1$ and $t_2$ are minimized: $t_{3, \text{max}} = 18n - 6n - 3n = 9n$.
    Thus, $t_3 \in [3n, 9n]$.

The edges $e_1$ and $e_2$ in the dual tree correspond to two diagonals in the triangulation of $P$. These two diagonals divide the polygon $P$ into three parts containing $t_1, t_2,$ and $t_3$ triangles respectively, all of which are between $3n$ and $9n$.

\(\square\)
