To find the smallest positive integer $n$ such that James can reach the last row of a $3002 \times 3001$ table before receiving $n$ penalties, let $R = 3002$ and $C = 3001$. Peter selects $N = R-2 = 3000$ cells, one from each row $r \in \{2, 3, \dots, R-1\}$, such that no two cells are in the same column. Let the selected cells be $S = \{(r, c_r) : r = 2, \dots, R-1\}$, where $c_r \in \{1, \dots, C\}$ are distinct. Since there are $N$ cells and $C = N+1$ columns, exactly one column $U \in \{1, \dots, C\}$ contains no selected cells.

James wins if he reaches any cell in row $R$. If he enters a cell $(r, c) \in S$, he receives a penalty and is reset to $(1, 1)$. Once he hits a cell $(r, c)$, he knows $c_r = c$. This implies that for any other row $r' \neq r$, the cell $(r', c)$ is safe, and for any other column $c' \neq c$, the cell $(r, c')$ is safe.

**Strategy Analysis:**
If James tries to move down a column $j$, he follows the path $(1, j) \to (2, j) \to \dots \to (R, j)$. If $j = U$, he reaches the goal with 0 penalties. If $j \neq U$, he will hit a mine at some row $r$ where $c_r = j$, incurring 1 penalty and resetting. He then knows $c_r = j$ and $j \neq U$. By repeating this for columns $j = 1, 2, \dots, N$, he will either find $U$ or identify $N$ of the $c_r$ values. If he hits mines in $N$ different columns, the remaining column must be $U$. In the worst case, he hits $N$ mines before finding $U$, resulting in $N = 3000$ penalties.

**Can James do better?**
To avoid a penalty in row $r$, James must be in a column $j \neq c_r$. To reach row $R$ without a penalty, he must find a sequence of columns $j_2, j_3, \dots, j_{R-1}$ such that $j_r \neq c_r$ for all $r$ and he can transition from $(r, j_r)$ to $(r+1, j_{r+1})$. A transition requires moving through $(r, j_{r+1})$ or $(r+1, j_r)$.
If James has identified $k < N$ mines, he knows $k$ values of $c_r$. For the remaining $N-k$ rows, he must guess columns. If he chooses a column $j$ that is not $U$ and not one of the $k$ identified $c_r$, he risks hitting a mine. If he uses one of the $k$ identified columns $c_{r_i}$, he is safe in all rows $r \neq r_i$. However, to move from row $r_i$ to $r_i+1$ using column $c_{r_i}$, he must pass through $(r_i, c_{r_i})$, which is a mine. To avoid this, he must switch to another column $j'$. If $j'$ is not $U$, he again risks hitting a mine in some row. Thus, without finding $U$, he cannot guarantee a mine-free path through the remaining rows.

The worst-case number of penalties is $N = 3000$. James achieves his goal *before* receiving $n$ points, meaning $P < n$. For $P_{max} = 3000$, the smallest $n$ is $3000 + 1 = 3001$.

The smallest positive integer $n$ is 3001.
