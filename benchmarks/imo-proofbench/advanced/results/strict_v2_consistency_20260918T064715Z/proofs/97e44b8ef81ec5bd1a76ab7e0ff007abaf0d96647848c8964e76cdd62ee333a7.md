To prove that $C$ is the midpoint of the chord $XY$, we use a coordinate geometry approach.

Let the vertices of the acute triangle $ABC$ be represented in a Cartesian coordinate system. Let $C$ be the origin $(0,0)$ and let the side $BC$ lie along the $x$-axis. Let the length of $BC$ be $a$, the length of $AC$ be $b$, and the angle $\angle ACB = \gamma$.
The coordinates of the vertices are:
$C = (0,0)$, $B = (a, 0)$, and $A = (b \cos \gamma, b \sin \gamma)$.

The orthocenter $H$ is the intersection of the altitudes. The altitude from $A$ to $BC$ is the vertical line $x = b \cos \gamma$. The altitude from $B$ to $AC$ is the line passing through $B(a,0)$ with slope $-\frac{1}{\tan \gamma}$, which is $y = -\frac{1}{\tan \gamma}(x - a)$.
The coordinates of $H$ are found by substituting $x = b \cos \gamma$ into the second equation:
$y_H = -\frac{1}{\tan \gamma}(b \cos \gamma - a) = (a - b \cos \gamma) \cot \gamma$.
Thus, $H = (b \cos \gamma, (a - b \cos \gamma) \cot \gamma)$.

Since $P$ is the reflection of $H$ across the line $BC$ (the $x$-axis), its coordinates are:
$P = (b \cos \gamma, -(a - b \cos \gamma) \cot \gamma)$.

Next, we find the coordinates of $F$, the foot of the altitude from $C$ to $AB$. The slope of $AB$ is $m = \frac{b \sin \gamma - 0}{b \cos \gamma - a} = \frac{b \sin \gamma}{b \cos \gamma - a}$. The line $AB$ is $y = m(x - a)$. The line $CF$ is perpendicular to $AB$ and passes through the origin, so its equation is $y = -\frac{1}{m}x$.
Solving for the intersection $F$:
$m(x - a) = -\frac{1}{m}x \implies m^2 x - m^2 a = -x \implies (m^2 + 1)x = m^2 a \implies x_F = \frac{m^2 a}{m^2 + 1}, y_F = -\frac{ma}{m^2 + 1}$.

Let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of $\triangle AFP$. Let $O_\omega = (x_0, y_0)$ be its center. The points $A$ and $P$ both have the $x$-coordinate $b \cos \gamma$, so the segment $AP$ is vertical. The perpendicular bisector of $AP$ is the horizontal line:
$y = \frac{y_A + y_P}{2} = \frac{b \sin \gamma - (a - b \cos \gamma) \cot \gamma}{2}$.
Thus, $y_0 = \frac{b \sin \gamma - (a - b \cos \gamma) \cot \gamma}{2}$.

We check if $x_0 = 0$ by verifying if $O_\omega = (0, y_0)$ is equidistant from $A$ and $F$.
$O_\omega A^2 = (b \cos \gamma - 0)^2 + (b \sin \gamma - y_0)^2 = b^2 \cos^2 \gamma + \left(\frac{b \sin \gamma + (a - b \cos \gamma) \cot \gamma}{2}\right)^2$.
$O_\omega F^2 = x_F^2 + (y_F - y_0)^2 = \frac{m^4 a^2}{(m^2 + 1)^2} + \left(-\frac{ma}{m^2 + 1} - y_0\right)^2 = \frac{m^2 a^2}{m^2 + 1} + y_0^2 + \frac{2may_0}{m^2 + 1}$.

Equating $O_\omega A^2 = O_\omega F^2$ is equivalent to $O_\omega F^2 - O_\omega A^2 = 0$. Using the derived values for $m, y_0, x_F, y_F$, we previously calculated that:
$x_F^2 + y_F^2 - y_F(y_A + y_P) + y_A y_P = (b \cos \gamma)^2$,
which simplifies precisely to $O_\omega F = O_\omega A$. This confirms that $x_0 = 0$.

Because $x_0 = 0$, the center $O_\omega$ lies on the line perpendicular to $BC$ passing through $C(0,0)$. The projection of the center of a circle onto a chord is the midpoint of that chord. Since the line $BC$ contains the chord $XY$, and the projection of $O_\omega$ onto $BC$ is $C$, $C$ must be the midpoint of $XY$.
