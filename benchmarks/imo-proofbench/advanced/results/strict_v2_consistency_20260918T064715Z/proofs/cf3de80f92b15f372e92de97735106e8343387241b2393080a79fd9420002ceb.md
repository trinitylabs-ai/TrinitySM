To determine which player has a winning strategy, we analyze the condition for a road to be constructed between two cities $A$ and $B$.

Let the points chosen by Alice be $P, Q$ and the subset $S$. Let $S' = \{ \frac{R-P}{Q-P} : R \in S \}$. The condition that $\triangle PQR$ is directly similar to $\triangle ABC$ is $\frac{C-A}{B-A} = \alpha$ for some $\alpha \in S'$. Similarly, $\triangle PQR$ is directly similar to $\triangle BAC$ if $\frac{C-B}{A-B} = \alpha$ for some $\alpha \in S'$.
Let $z_{CAB} = \frac{C-A}{B-A}$. Then $\frac{C-B}{A-B} = \frac{C-A+A-B}{A-B} = 1 - z_{CAB}$.
Thus, a road exists between cities $A$ and $B$ if and only if for every city $C \notin \{A, B\}$, $z_{CAB} \in S' \cup (1 - S')$. Let $T = S' \cup (1 - S')$. The condition for a road $AB$ is:
\[ V \setminus \{A, B\} \subseteq \{ A + z(B-A) : z \in T \}, \]
where $V$ is the set of cities Bob places. Alice wins if the resulting graph is connected and planar. Bob wins otherwise.

Note that $z \in T$ if and only if $1-z \in T$. Let $T^c = \mathbb{C} \setminus T$ be the complement of $T$. A road $AB$ exists if and only if for every city $C \in V \setminus \{A, B\}$, $z_{CAB} \notin T^c$. Since Bob must ensure no three cities are collinear, $z_{CAB}$ can never be a real number. Let $T^c_{nr} = T^c \setminus \mathbb{R}$ be the set of non-real points in the complement of $T$. The condition for a road $AB$ becomes: for every city $C \in V \setminus \{A, B\}$, $z_{CAB} \notin T^c_{nr}$.

We consider two cases based on the cardinality of $T^c_{nr}$:

**Case 1: $T^c_{nr}$ is countable.**
Bob can choose the set of cities $V = \{C_1, C_2, \dots\}$ such that for all distinct $i, j, k$, $z_{C_k C_i C_j} \notin T^c_{nr}$. He constructs $V$ inductively:
1. Pick $C_1, C_2$ arbitrarily with $|C_1 - C_2| > 1$.
2. For $n \ge 3$, Bob picks $C_n$ to avoid:
   - The finite set of points $\{C_1, \dots, C_{n-1}\}$.
   - The finite union of lines $L_{ij}$ passing through $C_i, C_j$ for $i, j < n$.
   - The countable set of points $\{C_i + z(C_j - C_i) : i, j < n, z \in T^c_{nr}\}$.
   - The finite union of disks $\{ z : |z - C_k| \le 1 \}$ for $k < n$.
Since the plane $\mathbb{C}$ is uncountable and the union of these sets is a countable union of lines, disks, and points, such a $C_n$ always exists. By this construction, for any three distinct cities $A, B, C \in V$, $z_{CAB} \notin T^c_{nr}$. Since $z_{CAB} \notin \mathbb{R}$ by the non-collinearity constraint, we have $z_{CAB} \in T$. Thus, every pair of cities forms a road, and the resulting graph is the complete graph $K_\infty$. Since $K_\infty$ contains $K_5$ as a subgraph, it is not planar. Bob wins.

**Case 2: $T^c_{nr}$ is uncountable.**
Bob can choose $V$ such that no roads are formed. He constructs $V = \{C_1, C_2, \dots\}$ inductively to ensure that for every pair $(C_i, C_j)$, there exists some $C_k$ such that $z_{C_k C_i C_j} \in T^c_{nr}$.

First, we use the following Lemma: If $X \subset \mathbb{C}$ is uncountable, then for any $N \in \mathbb{N}$, there exists $\epsilon_N > 0$ such that $X$ cannot be covered by $N$ disks of radius $\epsilon_N$.
Proof: If for some $N$, $X$ could be covered by $N$ disks of radius $\epsilon$ for all $\epsilon > 0$, then for any $N+1$ distinct points in $X$, we could choose $\epsilon$ smaller than half the minimum distance between them, requiring $N+1$ distinct disks to cover them, a contradiction. Thus $|X| \le N$.

Enumerate all pairs of indices as $P_1, P_2, \dots$ where $P_m = (i_m, j_m)$. Let $n(i, j) = m+2$ be the step at which road $C_i C_j$ is destroyed.
Bob picks $C_1, C_2$ such that $|C_1 - C_2|$ is sufficiently large. For $n \ge 3$, let $(i, j) = P_{n-2}$. Bob picks $C_n = C_i + z(C_j - C_i)$ for some $z \in T^c_{nr}$. To satisfy the constraints, $z$ must avoid:
- The finite set of values where $C_n = C_k$ or $C_n$ is collinear with $C_a, C_b$ for $a, b < n$.
- The union of disks $D_k = \{ z \in \mathbb{C} : |z - \frac{C_k - C_i}{C_j - C_i}| \le \frac{R_{k,n}}{|C_j - C_i|} \}$ for $k < n$, where $R_{k,n} = \max(1, L_{k,n})$ and $L_{k,n}$ is the distance required for future road destructions.

By picking $C_1, C_2$ far apart and choosing $z_m$ such that $|z_m|$ is bounded away from 0, Bob can ensure that $|C_j - C_i|$ is large enough such that the radius of each disk $D_k$ is less than $\epsilon_{n-1}$. By the Lemma, $T^c_{nr}$ cannot be covered by $n-1$ such disks, so a valid $z \in T^c_{nr}$ always exists.
By this construction, for every pair $C_i, C_j \in V$, there exists some $C_k \in V$ such that $z_{C_k C_i C_j} \in T^c_{nr}$. Thus, no roads are formed. The graph is disconnected. Bob wins.

In all cases, Bob has a winning strategy.

Bob has a winning strategy. \(\square\)
