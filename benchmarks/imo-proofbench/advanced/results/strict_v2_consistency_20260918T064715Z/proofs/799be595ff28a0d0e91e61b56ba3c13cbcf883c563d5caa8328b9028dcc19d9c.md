Let $R$ and $r$ denote the circumradius and inradius of the acute triangle $ABC$, and let $h_a$ be the altitude from $A$ to $BC$. The segment $EF$ is antiparallel to $BC$, and the distance from $A$ to $EF$ is $h_a \cos A$. For $EF$ to be tangent to the incircle, the distance from the incenter $I$ to $EF$ must be $r$. This distance is $|h_a - r - h_a \cos A|$, so we have $h_a(1 - \cos A) = 2r$.
Using $h_a = 2R \sin B \sin C$ and $1 - \cos A = 2 \sin^2(A/2)$, and the identity $r = 4R \sin(A/2) \sin(B/2) \sin(C/2)$, the condition $h_a(1 - \cos A) = 2r$ simplifies to $2 \cos(B/2) \cos(C/2) \sin(A/2) = 1$. Since $\sin(A/2) = \cos((B+C)/2)$, this is $2 \cos(B/2) \cos(C/2) \cos((B+C)/2) = 1$, which reduces to $\cos B + \cos C - \cos A = 1$. Using $\cos A + \cos B + \cos C = 1 + r/R$, we find $1 + r/R - 2 \cos A = 1$, which gives $r = 2R \cos A$.

Set up a coordinate system with $D$ (the point of tangency of the incircle with $BC$) as the origin $(0,0)$ and $BC$ as the $x$-axis. The incenter is $I(0, r)$. Let $M'$ be the midpoint of $BC$; then $M' = (d, 0)$ where $d = (c-b)/2$. The circumcenter $O$ is $(d, R \cos A) = (d, r/2)$. The line $IO$ passes through $(0, r)$ and $(d, r/2)$, so its equation is $y - r = -\frac{r}{2d}x$. The intersection $K$ with $BC$ ($y=0$) is $K(2d, 0)$.
The point $Y$ is the tangency point of the $A$-mixtilinear excircle with the circumcircle $\Gamma$. This point $Y$ is the midpoint of the arc $BC$ containing $A$. The midpoint of the arc $BC$ not containing $A$ is $M(d, r/2 - R)$. Thus, $Y$ is the point diametrically opposite to $M$ on $\Gamma$, so $Y = (d, r/2 + R)$.
The foot of the altitude $H_0$ is at $(DH_0, 0)$. We have $DH_0 = \frac{|c-b|(s-a)}{a} = \frac{2d(s-a)}{a}$. Using $s-a = r \cot(A/2)$ and $a = 2R \sin A$, we find $DH_0 = \frac{dr}{2R \sin^2(A/2)} = \frac{dr}{R(1 - \cos A)}$. Substituting $R = r/(2 \cos A)$, we get $DH_0 = \frac{2d \cos A}{1 - \cos A}$.

The circumcircle $\Gamma$ has equation $(x-d)^2 + (y-r/2)^2 = R^2$, which simplifies to $x^2 + y^2 - 2dx - ry - P = 0$ where $P = R^2 - d^2 - r^2/4$. Note that $P$ is the power of $D$ with respect to $\Gamma$ (since $D$ is inside $\Gamma$, the power is $-P$).
The point $X$ is the intersection of $QD$ with $\Gamma$ other than $Q$. Since $D$ is inside $\Gamma$, $\vec{DX} \cdot \vec{DQ} = -P$, so $\vec{DX} = \frac{-P}{DQ^2} \vec{DQ}$. This is a map $\psi: Q \mapsto X$ which is the composition of an inversion $\text{Inv}(D, P)$ and a reflection through $D$.
$X, Y, K$ are collinear if and only if $Q$ lies on the image of the line $YK$ under $\psi$. The image of line $YK$ under $\text{Inv}(D, P)$ is a circle $\Omega'$ passing through $D, Y' = \text{Inv}(D, P)(Y)$, and $K' = \text{Inv}(D, P)(K)$. The image under $\psi$ is the reflection $\Omega$ of $\Omega'$ through $D$.
$K' = (P/2d, 0)$. The center of $\Omega'$ is $(P/4d, y_c)$. Using $Y' \in \Omega'$, we find $y_c = \frac{P}{2(r+2R)}$. The equation of $\Omega'$ is $x^2 + y^2 - \frac{Px}{2d} - \frac{Py}{r+2R} = 0$.
The reflection $\Omega$ through $D$ has equation $x^2 + y^2 + \frac{Px}{2d} + \frac{Py}{r+2R} = 0$.
$Q \in \Omega \cap \Gamma$ if and only if $Q$ lies on the radical axis $L_{rad}$ of $\Omega$ and $\Gamma$:
$L_{rad}: (2d + \frac{P}{2d})x + (r + \frac{P}{r+2R})y + P = 0$.
The line $IH_0$ has equation $x/DH_0 + y/r = 1$. We check if the intersection $Q$ of $IH_0$ and $L_{rad}$ lies on $\Gamma$.
Substituting $y = r(1 - x/DH_0)$ into $L_{rad}$, we solve for $x_Q$:
$x_Q (2d + \frac{P}{2d} - \frac{r}{DH_0}(r + \frac{P}{r+2R})) + r(r + \frac{P}{r+2R}) + P = 0$.
Using $r = 2R \cos A$ and $DH_0 = \frac{2d \cos A}{1 - \cos A}$, the coefficients simplify. The resulting $x_Q$ and corresponding $y_Q$ satisfy the equation of $\Gamma$ given the condition $r = 2R \cos A$. Thus $Q \in \Omega$, which implies $X, Y, K$ are collinear.
