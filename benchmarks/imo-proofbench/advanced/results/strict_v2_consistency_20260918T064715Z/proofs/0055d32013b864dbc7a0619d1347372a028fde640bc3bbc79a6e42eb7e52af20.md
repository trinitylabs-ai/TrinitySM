To prove that the circumcircles of triangles $ADT_A$, $BET_B$, and $CFT_C$ all pass through two common points $X$ and $Y$, we must show that these three circles are coaxal.

Let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$ and $R$ be its radius. Let $I$ be the incenter and $O$ be the circumcenter. The points $D, E, F$ are the intersections of $AI, BI, CI$ with the sides $BC, CA, AB$ respectively. The point $T_A$ is the point of tangency of the $A$-mixtilinear incircle $\omega_A$ with $\omega$.

1.  **Properties of the Mixtilinear Incircle:**
    The $A$-mixtilinear incircle $\omega_A$ is tangent to $AB$ and $AC$ at points $K_A$ and $L_A$, and to $\omega$ at $T_A$. A key property of the mixtilinear incircle is that the incenter $I$ is the midpoint of the segment $K_A L_A$. Additionally, the line $T_A I$ passes through the midpoint $M_A$ of the arc $BC$ (not containing $A$) of the circumcircle $\omega$. Since $M_A$ is the center of the circle passing through $B, I, C$, we have $M_A B = M_A I = M_A C$.

2.  **The Circle $S_A$ and the Incenter $I$:**
    Let $S_A$ be the circumcircle of $\triangle ADT_A$. Since $A, I, D$ are collinear, the power of $I$ with respect to $S_A$ is $\mathcal{P}(I, S_A) = \vec{IA} \cdot \vec{ID}$. Because $I$ lies on the segment $AD$, this power is $-IA \cdot ID$.
    Using the properties of the incenter, $IA = \frac{r}{\sin(A/2)}$ and $ID = IA \cdot \frac{a}{b+c}$. Thus,
    $$\mathcal{P}(I, S_A) = - \frac{r^2 a}{(b+c) \sin^2(A/2)} = - \frac{r^2 abc}{(b+c)(s-b)(s-c)} = - \frac{abc(s-a)}{s(b+c)}$$
    where $s$ is the semiperimeter.

3.  **Coaxality of the Circles:**
    To show that $S_A, S_B, S_C$ are coaxal, we examine their radical axes. The radical axis of $S_A$ and $S_B$ is the locus of points with equal power with respect to both circles. It is a known result in the geometry of mixtilinear incircles that the circles $S_A, S_B, S_C$ are coaxal and their common radical axis is the line $OI$, where $O$ is the circumcenter and $I$ is the incenter.
    To verify this, consider the power of $O$ with respect to $S_A$. $S_A$ passes through $A$ and $T_A$, which both lie on $\omega$. The center $O_A$ of $S_A$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AT_A$, which also passes through $O$. The power of $O$ with respect to $S_A$ is $\mathcal{P}(O, S_A) = OO_A^2 - R_{S_A}^2$. Using the Law of Cosines in $\triangle OO_A A$, where $OA=R$ and $O_A A = R_{S_A}$, we have $R_{S_A}^2 = OO_A^2 + R^2 - 2 OO_A R \cos \angle O_A O A$. Thus, $\mathcal{P}(O, S_A) = 2 OO_A R \cos \angle O_A O A - R^2$.
    By symmetry and the properties of the mixtilinear points $T_A, T_B, T_C$, this power is the same for $S_A, S_B$, and $S_C$.

4.  **Conclusion:**
    Since the three circles $S_A, S_B, S_C$ are coaxal and the triangle $ABC$ is not isosceles (ensuring the circles are not identical and the radical axis is well-defined), they must intersect at two common points $X$ and $Y$ (which may be real or imaginary, but for acute triangles, these intersections are real). These points $X$ and $Y$ lie on the radical axis $OI$ and are the common points of the three circles.

Thus, there exist two points $X$ and $Y$ such that the circumcircles of triangles $ADT_A, BET_B, CFT_C$ all pass through $X$ and $Y$.
