To find the smallest positive integer $n$ such that James has a strategy to reach the last row before receiving $n$ penalty points, we analyze the game's constraints and the information James gains from each penalty.

**1. Problem Analysis**
The table has $M = 3002$ rows and $N = 3001$ columns. Peter selects $M-2 = 3000$ cells, one from each row $r \in \{2, \dots, M-1\}$, such that no two selected cells are in the same column. Let $f(r)$ be the column of the selected cell in row $r$. The set of traps is $S = \{(r, f(r)) : r=2, \dots, M-1\}$, where $f: \{2, \dots, M-1\} \to \{1, \dots, N\}$ is an injective function. Since there are $N = 3001$ columns and only 3000 traps, there is exactly one column $k \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ that contains no traps.

**2. James's Strategy and Information Gain**
James does not know $f$, but if he enters a cell $(r, j)$ and it is a trap, he receives a penalty and returns to $(1, 1)$. Crucially, he now knows that $f(r) = j$. Because $f$ is injective, he also knows that for all other rows $r' \neq r$, the cell $(r', j)$ is safe.

If James tries to traverse the table using a column $j$, he may hit a trap at some row $r$. If he hits a trap $(r, j)$, he has "cleared" row $r$ (he knows exactly where its trap is) and has "cleared" column $j$ for all other rows.

**3. Worst-Case Scenario**
Consider the case $N=3, M=4$. There are two traps in rows 2 and 3.
- James tries column 1. He hits a trap at $(2, 1)$. He now knows $f(2)=1$ and $f(3) \in \{2, 3\}$.
- He needs to cross row 2 and row 3 to reach row 4.
- Row 2 is safe at $j=2, 3$. Row 3 is safe at $j=1$ (since $f(3) \neq f(2)$).
- To use the safe cell $(3, 1)$, he must reach it from $(2, 1)$ or $(3, 2)$. But $(2, 1)$ is a trap. Thus, he must pass through $(3, 2)$.
- If Peter placed the trap at $(3, 2)$, James hits it. Now he knows $f(2)=1$ and $f(3)=2$.
- This leaves column 3 entirely empty. James traverses column 3 to reach row 4.
- Total penalties: 2.

In the general case, Peter can force James to hit a trap in every row $r \in \{2, \dots, M-1\}$ by placing traps in different columns. Each time James attempts to cross the remaining "unknown" rows, Peter can ensure he hits a trap in a row he has not yet cleared, provided Peter has traps remaining. Since there are $M-2 = 3000$ such rows, Peter can force up to 3000 penalties.

**4. Upper Bound on Penalties**
Once James has received 3000 penalties, he has identified the trap in every row $r \in \{2, \dots, M-1\}$. Because $N = 3001$, there is one column $k$ that was never hit. This column is guaranteed to be empty. James can then move from $(1, 1) \to (1, k) \to (M, k)$ without any further penalties. Thus, James can always achieve his goal with at most 3000 penalties.

**5. Conclusion**
The maximum number of penalties James can be forced to receive is 3000. The problem asks for the smallest positive integer $n$ such that he can achieve his goal *before* receiving $n$ points. This means the number of penalties $P$ must satisfy $P \le n-1$.
Setting $n-1 = 3000$, we find $n = 3001$.
