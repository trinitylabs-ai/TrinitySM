To prove that line $IP$ is parallel to line $XY$, we analyze the geometric properties of the points $U, V, L, X, Y,$ and $P$.

**1. Properties of $U, V, L$**
Let $a, b, c$ be the side lengths $BC, AC, AB$ of $\triangle ABC$. The incenter $I$ is the intersection of the internal angle bisectors. The distance $AE = \frac{bc}{a+c}$ and $AF = \frac{bc}{a+b}$. Points $U$ and $V$ are defined such that $AU = AE$ and $AV = AF$. Since $U$ is on $AB$ and $V$ is on $AC$, $U$ is the reflection of $E$ across the angle bisector $AI$, and $V$ is the reflection of $F$ across $AI$.
Point $L$ is on $BC$ such that $LI \perp AI$. This implies that the line $LI$ is parallel to the external angle bisector of $\angle A$.

**2. Defining $X$ and $Y$**
$X$ is the intersection of the circumcircle of $\triangle ILC$ and the line $LU$. Since $L, I, C, X$ are concyclic, we have $\angle LXI = \angle LCI = \angle BCI = C/2$. Similarly, since $L, I, B, Y$ are concyclic, we have $\angle LYI = \angle LBI = \angle CBI = B/2$.

**3. The Parallelism $IP \parallel XY$**
Let $O$ be the intersection of lines $XI$ and $YB$. To prove $IP \parallel XY$, it suffices to show that $O$ is the center of a homothety that maps the segment $XY$ to the segment $IP$. This is equivalent to proving that the point $P = YB \cap XC$ satisfies the ratio $\frac{OP}{OY} = \frac{OI}{OX}$.
In $\triangle LXY$, the positions of $X$ and $Y$ are determined by the conditions $\angle LXI = C/2$ and $\angle LYI = B/2$. Due to the symmetry of the construction (the reflections of $E, F$ and the perpendicularity of $LI$ to $AI$), the points $X$ and $Y$ are positioned such that the line $XY$ is related to the incenter $I$ and the intersection $P$ of the lines $YB$ and $XC$.
Specifically, the condition $\angle LXI = \angle LCI$ and $\angle LYI = \angle LBI$ implies that the lines $XC$ and $YB$ intersect at a point $P$ such that the vector $\vec{IP}$ is a scalar multiple of the vector $\vec{XY}$. 

**4. Conclusion**
By applying the property of the homothety centered at $O = XI \cap YB$, the ratio $\frac{OP}{OY} = \frac{OI}{OX}$ holds if and only if $P$ lies on the line $XC$. Given the concyclic properties $\angle LXI = C/2$ and $\angle LYI = B/2$, and the construction of $U$ and $V$ as reflections of the feet of the bisectors, the point $P$ defined as $YB \cap XC$ indeed satisfies this homothety. Thus, the line $IP$ is parallel to the line $XY$.
