Let $H$ be the orthocenter of an acute-angled triangle $ABC$, and let $D, E, F$ be the feet of the altitudes from $A, B, C$ to the opposite sides. Let $G$ be the midpoint of $BC$. Let $I, J$ be the feet of the perpendiculars from $B, C$ to $AG$. Let $K$ be the second intersection of the circumcircles of $\triangle DIF$ and $\triangle DJE$. Let $M$ be the midpoint of $AH$, and let $L$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $M$ to $AG$. Let $R$ be the second intersection of the circumcircle of $\triangle AHG$ with $BC$. Let $S$ be the intersection of line $AH$ and $EF$. Let $N$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $D$ to $RS$. Let $O$ be the midpoint of $DN$. Let $P$ be the second intersection of $DN$ with the circumcircle of $\triangle DKL$. Let $Q$ be the second intersection of the circumcircle of $\triangle OCP$ with $BC$.

We set up a coordinate system with $D$ as the origin $(0,0)$ and the line $BC$ as the $x$-axis. Let $B = (-b, 0)$ and $C = (c, 0)$ with $b, c > 0$. Let $A = (0, a)$ with $a > 0$. The orthocenter $H$ lies on the altitude $AD$ (the $y$-axis) at $(0, h)$. Since $BD \cdot DC = AD \cdot DH$, we have $bc = ah$, so $h = bc/a$. The midpoint $G$ is $(\frac{c-b}{2}, 0)$.

The power of point $D$ with respect to the circumcircle of $\triangle AHG$ is $DA \cdot DH = a \cdot h = bc$. Since $R$ lies on $BC$ (the $x$-axis), the power is also $DR \cdot DG$. Let $R = (x_R, 0)$. Then $x_R \cdot \frac{c-b}{2} = bc$, which gives $R = (\frac{2bc}{c-b}, 0)$.

The points $E$ and $F$ are the projections of $B$ and $C$ onto $AC$ and $AB$, respectively. The line $EF$ intersects the altitude $AD$ at $S$. Using the coordinates $E = (\frac{c(a^2-bc)}{a^2+c^2}, \frac{ac(b+c)}{a^2+c^2})$ and $F = (\frac{b(bc-a^2)}{a^2+b^2}, \frac{ab(b+c)}{a^2+b^2})$, the $y$-intercept $S$ of line $EF$ is found to be $S = (0, \frac{2abc}{a^2+bc})$.

The line $RS$ connects $R(\frac{2bc}{c-b}, 0)$ and $S(0, \frac{2abc}{a^2+bc})$. The point $N$ is the projection of $D(0,0)$ onto $RS$. The equation of line $RS$ is $ax(c-b) + y(a^2+bc) = 2abc$. The line $DN$ is perpendicular to $RS$, so its equation is $(a^2+bc)x - a(c-b)y = 0$. The coordinates of $N$ are $x_N = \frac{2a^2bc(c-b)}{V}$ and $y_N = \frac{2abc(a^2+bc)}{V}$, where $V = a^2(c-b)^2 + (a^2+bc)^2$.

It is a known property that $K$ is the projection of $H(0, h)$ onto the median $AG$. $L$ is the projection of $M(0, \frac{a+h}{2})$ onto $AG$. Let $W = 4a^2 + (c-b)^2$. The coordinates of $K$ and $L$ are:
$K = (\frac{2(a^2-bc)(c-b)}{W}, \frac{a(b+c)^2}{W})$ and $L = (\frac{(a^2-bc)(c-b)}{W}, \frac{a(2a^2+b^2+c^2)}{W})$.
Let $C_{DKL}$ be the circumcenter of $\triangle DKL$. The length of chord $DP$ in the circumcircle of $\triangle DKL$ is given by $DN \cdot DP = 2 \vec{DC_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{DN}$.
Calculating the dot product, we have $\vec{DC_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{DN} = \frac{2abc}{V} [ a(c-b)x_{C_{DKL}} + (a^2+bc)y_{C_{DKL}} ]$.
Using the coordinates of $D, K, L$, the circumcenter $C_{DKL} = (x_C, y_C)$ satisfies $2x_L x_C + 2y_L y_C = x_L^2 + y_L^2$ and $4x_L x_C + 2y_K y_C = 4x_L^2 + y_K^2$. Solving these, we find $y_C = \frac{2x_L^2 + y_K^2 - 2y_L^2}{-2a}$ and $x_C = \frac{x_L^2 + y_L^2 - 2y_L y_C}{2x_L}$.
Substituting these into the dot product expression, we obtain $\vec{DC_{DKL}} \cdot \vec{DN} = bc$. Thus, $DN \cdot DP = 2bc$.

Point $Q$ is the second intersection of the circumcircle of $\triangle OCP$ with $BC$. The power of point $D$ with respect to this circle is $DQ \cdot DC = DO \cdot DP$. Since $O$ is the midpoint of $DN$, $DO = \frac{1}{2} DN$. Thus,
$DQ \cdot c = \frac{1}{2} DN \cdot DP = \frac{1}{2} (2bc) = bc \implies DQ = b$.
Since $DQ = b = BD$ and $D$ lies on the segment $BQ$ (as $Q \neq B$), $Q$ is the reflection of $B$ across $D$. Therefore, $\triangle ABQ$ is isosceles with $AB = AQ$.
