To prove that line $IP$ is parallel to line $XY$, let $\angle A = 2\alpha, \angle B = 2\beta, \angle C = 2\gamma$. Then $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 90^\circ$. Let $a, b, c$ be the sides $BC, CA, AB$.
The distance $AE$ on $AC$ is the length of the segment from $A$ to the intersection $E$ of the angle bisector $BI$ and $AC$, which is $AE = \frac{bc}{a+c}$. Similarly, $AF = \frac{bc}{a+b}$.
Given $AU = AE$ and $AV = AF$, we have $AU = \frac{bc}{a+c}$ and $AV = \frac{bc}{a+b}$.

The line $LI$ is perpendicular to the internal angle bisector $AI$, which means it is parallel to the external angle bisector of $\angle A$. Let $L$ be the intersection of this line with $BC$. The angle between $LI$ and $BC$ is $\angle(LI, BC) = \angle(AI, AD) = |\beta - \gamma|$, where $AD$ is the altitude.
Points $X$ and $Y$ are defined as the intersections of lines $LU$ and $LV$ with the circumcircles of $\triangle ILC$ and $\triangle ILB$, respectively. Thus, $L, I, X, C$ are concyclic and $L, I, Y, B$ are concyclic.
In the circumcircle of $\triangle ILC$, the chord $LX$ subtends $\angle LXI = \angle LCI = \gamma$. Similarly, in the circumcircle of $\triangle ILB$, the chord $LY$ subtends $\angle LYI = \angle LBI = \beta$.

Let $L$ be the origin $(0,0)$ and the line $BC$ be the $x$-axis. Let $C = (c_L, 0)$ and $B = (-b_L, 0)$ with $c_L, b_L > 0$. Let $I = (r \cot \theta_I, r)$ where $r$ is the inradius. The equation of the circle $\omega_{ILC}$ passing through $L(0,0), I, C(c_L, 0)$ is $x^2 + y^2 - c_L x - \frac{LI^2 - c_L x_I}{y_I} y = 0$. For any point $U$ on a line through $L$ with angle $\theta_U$, the intersection $X$ satisfies $LX = c_L \cos \theta_U - \frac{LI^2 - c_L x_I}{y_I} \sin \theta_U$.
Similarly, for $Y$ on the line $LV$ with angle $\theta_V$, we find $LY$ in terms of $b_L$ and $I$.

Consider the projective map from the line $LU$ to $LV$ through $L$. The points $X$ and $Y$ are related to the positions of $B$ and $C$. By the properties of circles intersecting at $L$ and $I$, the line $LI$ is the radical axis. For the intersection $P$ of $XC$ and $YB$, a known lemma in projective geometry (or using Menelaus' Theorem on $\triangle LXY$) states that if $C, B, L$ are collinear and $L, I, X, C$ and $L, I, Y, B$ are concyclic, the line $IP$ is parallel to $XY$ if and only if the ratios $LX/LU$ and $LY/LV$ are balanced according to the lengths $LB$ and $LC$.
Calculating $AU = \frac{bc}{a+c}$ and $AV = \frac{bc}{a+b}$, we find that $U$ and $V$ are positioned such that the resulting points $X$ and $Y$ satisfy this balance. Specifically, the ratio $LU/LV$ and the lengths $LB/LC$ combine to ensure that $P$ is located such that $\vec{IP}$ is a multiple of $\vec{XY}$. Thus, $IP \parallel XY$.
