To find all pairs of distinct positive integers $(a, b)$ such that the two numbers can be made equal after a finite number of steps, let the operations be $f(x) = x+2$ and $g(x) = 3x$. Let $x_n$ and $y_n$ be the values of the two numbers after $n$ steps.

**1. Parity Invariant**
Observe that both operations $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ preserve the parity of the number:
- If $x$ is even, $x+2$ is even and $3x$ is even.
- If $x$ is odd, $x+2$ is odd and $3x$ is odd.
Thus, $x_n \equiv a \pmod 2$ and $y_n \equiv b \pmod 2$ for all $n$. For $x_n = y_n$ to be possible, we must have $a \equiv b \pmod 2$.

**2. Analysis for Odd Integers**
Suppose $a$ and $b$ are odd. We consider the values modulo 4:
- If $x \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, then $f(x) = x+2 \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ and $g(x) = 3x \equiv 3 \pmod 4$.
- If $x \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, then $f(x) = x+2 \equiv 5 \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ and $g(x) = 3x \equiv 9 \equiv 1 \pmod 4$.
In both cases, the value modulo 4 flips every step. Therefore, after $n$ steps:
- $x_n \equiv a + 2n \pmod 4$
- $y_n \equiv b + 2n \pmod 4$
For $x_n = y_n$, we must have $x_n \equiv y_n \pmod 4$, which implies $a + 2n \equiv b + 2n \pmod 4$, so $a \equiv b \pmod 4$.
If $a \equiv b \pmod 4$, we can make the numbers equal. For example, if $a=1, b=5$, then in $n=2$ steps, we can reach $x_2 = g(f(1)) = 3(3) = 9$ and $y_2 = f(f(5)) = 5+2+2 = 9$. In general, the exponential growth of $g(x)$ and the linear growth of $f(x)$ allow us to synchronize the values provided the modulo 4 condition is met.

**3. Analysis for Even Integers**
Suppose $a$ and $b$ are even. Let $a=2m$ and $b=2n$. The operations $x \to x+2$ and $x \to 3x$ on the original numbers correspond to $m \to m+1$ and $m \to 3m$ on the halved numbers.
In the system $m \to m+1$ and $m \to 3m$, the parity is not invariant. For any two distinct positive integers $m$ and $n$, we can always find a value $z$ reachable from both $m$ and $n$ in the same number of steps $k$. For instance, if $m=1, n=2$, they meet at $z=3$ in $k=1$ step ($1 \to 1+2=3$ is not possible here, but $1 \to 1+1=2$ is not it; rather $1 \to 1+1=2$ or $1 \to 3(1)=3$, and $2 \to 2+1=3$ or $2 \to 3(2)=6$). Thus $x_1=3, y_1=3$ for $m=1, n=2$, which corresponds to $a=2, b=4$ meeting at $x_1=6, y_1=6$ ($2 \times 3 = 6$ and $4+2 = 6$).
Since we can always adjust the number of steps by choosing $x \to x+1$ (linear) or $x \to 3x$ (exponential), any pair of even integers $(a, b)$ can be made equal.

**Conclusion**
The necessary and sufficient conditions are:
- If $a, b$ are even, they can always be made equal.
- If $a, b$ are odd, they can be made equal if and only if $a \equiv b \pmod 4$.
- If one is even and the other is odd, they can never be made equal.

These conditions can be summarized as: $a \equiv b \pmod 2$ and, if $a$ is odd, $a \equiv b \pmod 4$. This is equivalent to saying that $a$ and $b$ are both even, or $a$ and $b$ are both odd and $a \equiv b \pmod 4$.

The pairs $(a, b)$ are all pairs of distinct positive integers such that $a \equiv b \pmod 2$ and ($a$ is even or $a \equiv b \pmod 4$).
