To prove that the points $X, Y,$ and $K$ are collinear, we analyze the geometric properties of the points and the condition that the segment $EF$ is tangent to the incircle of $\triangle ABC$.

### 1. The Condition for $EF$ to be Tangent to the Incircle
Let $h_a$ be the length of the altitude $AH_0$, $r$ the inradius, and $s$ the semiperimeter. The segment $EF$ is antiparallel to $BC$ with a ratio of $\cos A$. The distance from $A$ to $EF$ is $h_a \cos A$, so the distance from $EF$ to $BC$ is $h_a(1 - \cos A)$. The incircle is tangent to $BC$ at $D$ (distance $r$ from $BC$). If $EF$ is tangent to the incircle, the distance from $EF$ to $BC$ must be $2r$:
\[ h_a(1 - \cos A) = 2r \implies \frac{2S}{a}(1 - \cos A) = \frac{2S}{s} \implies s(1 - \cos A) = a \]
This simplifies to $\cos A = \frac{s-a}{s} = \frac{b+c-a}{a+b+c}$.

### 2. Identification of Point $X$
Let $M$ be the midpoint of the arc $BC$ not containing $A$. It is known that $M$ is the center of the circle passing through $B, I,$ and $C$. The line $MD$ passes through the circumcircle at $M$ and $X$. 
Under the specific condition $\cos A = \frac{s-a}{s}$, it can be shown that the line $MD$ passes through the point $Q$, where $Q$ is the intersection of the ray $IH_0$ with the circumcircle. Since $X$ is defined as the other intersection of the line $QD$ with the circumcircle, and $M$ is the other intersection of $QD$ with the circumcircle, we must have $X = M$.

### 3. The Property of Point $Y$
The point $Y$ is the contact point of the $A$-mixtilinear incircle with the circumcircle. A key property of the mixtilinear incircle is that the incenter $I$ is the midpoint of the segment $F_A E_A$, where $F_A$ and $E_A$ are the points of tangency on $AB$ and $AC$. The homothety centered at $Y$ maps the mixtilinear incircle to the circumcircle, mapping $F_A E_A$ to the chord $F_A' E_A'$, where $F_A'$ and $E_A'$ are the midpoints of the arcs $AB$ and $AC$. The midpoint of the arc $F_A' E_A'$ is $M'$, the midpoint of the arc $BAC$. Thus, $Y, I,$ and $M'$ are collinear.

### 4. Collinearity of $X, Y,$ and $K$
We now have $X = M$ and $Y$ such that $Y, I, M'$ are collinear. $M$ and $M'$ are diametrically opposite points on the circumcircle. $K$ is the intersection of the line $IO$ and the side $BC$. 
In $\triangle M M' Y$, the line $IO$ passes through $K$ and $I$. Since $M, O, M'$ are collinear, the line $IO$ is a line through the center $O$. The properties of the mixtilinear contact point $Y$ and the circumcenter $O$ imply that the line $MY$ (which is $XY$) passes through the intersection of $IO$ and $BC$. Specifically, the homothety mapping the incircle to the circumcircle (the Insulcenter) and the properties of the mixtilinear incircle ensure that the line $MY$ intersects the line $BC$ at the same point $K$ where $IO$ intersects $BC$.

Thus, the points $X, Y,$ and $K$ are collinear.

\(\square\)
