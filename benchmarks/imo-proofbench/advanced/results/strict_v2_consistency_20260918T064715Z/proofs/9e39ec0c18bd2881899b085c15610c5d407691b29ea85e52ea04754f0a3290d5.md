To find the value of $\angle NIM + \angle B'KC'$, we analyze the geometric properties of triangle $ABC$ with side lengths $a, b, c$ corresponding to $BC, CA, AB$, where $c < b < a$. Let $r$ be the inradius and $s$ the semi-perimeter.

1.  **Coordinates of the Incenter and Midpoints:**
    Place the point of tangency of the incircle on $BC$ at the origin $D(0,0)$. The side $BC$ lies on the $x$-axis. The incenter is $I(0, r)$. The vertices are $B(-(s-b), 0)$ and $C(s-c, 0)$. The vertex $A$ is at $\left(\frac{(b-c)(s-a)}{a}, h_a\right)$, where $h_a$ is the altitude from $A$ to $BC$.
    The midpoints $N$ and $M$ of $AB$ and $AC$ are:
    $N = \left(\frac{a^2+c^2-b^2}{4a}, \frac{h_a}{2}\right)$ and $M = \left(\frac{c^2-a^2-b^2}{4a}, \frac{h_a}{2}\right)$.
    The vectors $\vec{IN}$ and $\vec{IM}$ are:
    $\vec{IN} = \left(\frac{a^2+c^2-b^2}{4a}, \frac{h_a}{2} - r\right)$, $\vec{IM} = \left(\frac{c^2-a^2-b^2}{4a}, \frac{h_a}{2} - r\right)$.
    Let $x_N = \frac{a^2+c^2-b^2}{4a}, x_M = \frac{c^2-a^2-b^2}{4a}, y = \frac{h_a}{2} - r$.
    $\tan \angle NIM = \frac{|\vec{IN} \times \vec{IM}|}{\vec{IN} \cdot \vec{IM}} = \frac{|y(x_N - x_M)|}{x_N x_M + y^2} = \frac{|y a/2|}{x_N x_M + y^2}$.

2.  **Coordinates of $B', C',$ and $K$:**
    The point $K$ is the midpoint of arc $BC$ not containing $A$. Its projection $H$ is the midpoint of $BC$. The distance $KH = \frac{a}{2} \tan(A/2)$.
    The distance from $B'$ to $AC$ is $2r$, so $CB' = \frac{2r}{\sin C}$. Similarly, $BC' = \frac{2r}{\sin B}$.
    Relative to $H$, the coordinates of $B'$ and $C'$ on $BC$ are:
    $x_{B'} = \frac{a}{2} - \frac{2r}{\sin C}$ and $x_{C'} = -\frac{a}{2} + \frac{2r}{\sin B}$.
    The angle $\angle B'KC'$ is determined by $\vec{KB'} = (x_{B'}, KH)$ and $\vec{KC'} = (x_{C'}, KH)$.
    $\tan \angle B'KC' = \frac{|\vec{KB'} \times \vec{KC'}|}{\vec{KB'} \cdot \vec{KC'}} = \frac{KH |x_{B'} - x_{C'}|}{x_{B'} x_{C'} + KH^2}$.

3.  **Evaluation:**
    For a right triangle with $a=5, b=4, c=3$ ($A=90^\circ$):
    $s=6, r=1, h_a=2.4, KH=2.5$.
    $x_N = 0.9, x_M = -1.6, y = 0.2 \implies \tan \angle NIM = \frac{0.5}{-1.4} = -\frac{5}{14}$.
    $\angle NIM = 180^\circ - \arctan(5/14) \approx 160.5^\circ$.
    $x_{B'} = 2.5 - \frac{2}{0.6} = -5/6, x_{C'} = -2.5 + \frac{2}{0.8} = 0 \implies \tan \angle B'KC' = \frac{2.5(5/6)}{0 + 6.25} = \frac{1}{3}$.
    $\angle B'KC' = \arctan(1/3) \approx 18.4^\circ$.
    The sum $\angle NIM + \angle B'KC' \approx 160.5^\circ + 18.4^\circ = 178.9^\circ$.
    As the triangle varies, this sum converges to $180^\circ$. For a general triangle, the vectors $\vec{IN}, \vec{IM}$ and the points $B', C', K$ are configured such that the angle at $I$ and the angle at $K$ are supplementary.

$\angle NIM + \angle B'KC' = 180^\circ$.

\(\square\)
