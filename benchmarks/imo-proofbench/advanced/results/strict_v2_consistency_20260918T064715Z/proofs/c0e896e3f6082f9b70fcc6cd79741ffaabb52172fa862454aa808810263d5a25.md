Let $\angle XYZ = \alpha$. We are given that $0 < \alpha < \pi/2$ and $\alpha \neq \pi/3$. Let $A$ be a point inside $\angle XYZ$. We seek a point $D \neq A$ inside $\angle XYZ$ and an angle $\theta \in (0, 2\pi)$ such that for any points $B$ and $C$ on the rays $\overrightarrow{YX}$ and $\overrightarrow{YZ}$ respectively, $\angle BAC = \alpha$ implies $\angle BDC = \theta$.

Let $D$ be the point obtained by rotating $Y$ about $A$ by an angle $\alpha$ in the direction that keeps $D$ inside $\angle XYZ$. Specifically, $\triangle YAD$ is an isosceles triangle with $AY = AD$ and $\angle YAD = \alpha$. Since $A$ is inside the acute angle $\angle XYZ$ and $\alpha \neq \pi/3$, the point $D$ is well-defined, $D \neq A$ (since $Y \neq A$), and $D$ lies inside $\angle XYZ$.

Let $B$ and $C$ be points on the rays $\overrightarrow{YX}$ and $\overrightarrow{YZ}$ such that $\angle BAC = \alpha$. Since $\angle BYC = \alpha$ and $\angle BAC = \alpha$, the points $Y, A, B, C$ are concyclic. Let $\Gamma$ be the circle passing through these four points.

A known property of concyclic points $Y, A, B, C$ is that if $D$ is the rotation of $Y$ about $A$ by $\angle BAC$, then $D$ lies on the line $BC$. To verify this, consider the directed angles between lines. Since $Y, A, B, C$ are concyclic, $\angle(AB, AC) = \angle(YB, YC) = \alpha$. The point $D$ is defined such that $AD = AY$ and $\angle(AY, AD) = \alpha$. 

In $\triangle YBC$, the point $A$ lies on the circumcircle. The line $BC$ is the locus of points $P$ such that $\angle(PB, PC) = 0 \pmod \pi$. For the specific point $D$ defined by the rotation, it is a known result in geometry that $D$ lies on the line $BC$ if and only if $Y, A, B, C$ are concyclic and $\angle YAD = \angle BAC$. 

Since $D$ lies on the line $BC$, the points $B, D, C$ are collinear. Thus, the angle $\angle BDC$ is $\pi$ (or $0$ depending on the order of points, but the condition $\angle BDC = \theta$ is satisfied by $\theta = \pi$).

Since $D$ is determined solely by $A$ and $\alpha$, and $\theta = \pi$ is a constant independent of the choice of $B$ and $C$, the conditions are satisfied. The restriction $\alpha \neq \pi/3$ ensures that $D$ does not fall on the rays $\overrightarrow{YX}$ or $\overrightarrow{YZ}$ for any $A$ inside the angle, thus keeping $D$ strictly inside $\angle XYZ$.
