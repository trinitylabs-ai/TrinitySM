To determine which player has a winning strategy, let's analyze the conditions for forming a road between two cities. Let the points $P$ and $Q$ be represented by complex numbers $p$ and $q$. For any point $R$, let $w = \frac{r-p}{q-p}$. Alice's choice of $S$ defines a set $W = \{ \frac{r-p}{q-p} : R \in S \}$. A road exists between cities $A$ and $B$ if for every other city $C$, $\triangle PQR \sim_{dir} \triangle ABC$ or $\triangle PQR \sim_{dir} \triangle BAC$ for some $R \in S$. In terms of complex numbers, this means:
\[ \frac{c-a}{b-a} \in W \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{c-b}{a-b} \in W. \]
Note that $\frac{c-b}{a-b} = 1 - \frac{c-a}{b-a}$. Thus, a road $AB$ exists if and only if for every city $C \in V \setminus \{A, B\}$, the ratio $z_{ABC} = \frac{c-a}{b-a}$ belongs to the set $X = W \cup (1-W)$. This condition can be rewritten as $V \setminus \{A, B\} \subseteq a + X(b-a)$, where $a + X(b-a) = \{ a + x(b-a) : x \in X \}$.

We consider two cases for Alice's choice of $X$:

**Case 1: $X = \mathbb{C}$**
If $X$ is the entire complex plane, then for any pair of cities $A$ and $B$, the condition $V \setminus \{A, B\} \subseteq X$ is always satisfied. Consequently, every pair of cities is connected by a road. The resulting graph is the complete graph $K_\infty$. A complete graph with five or more vertices is non-planar. Since Bob places infinitely many cities, the graph is $K_\infty$, which is not planar. Thus, Bob wins.

**Case 2: $X \neq \mathbb{C}$**
If $X$ is not the entire plane, there exists at least one complex number $z_0 \notin X$. Bob's goal is to ensure the graph is either disconnected or non-planar. He can achieve this by making the graph empty (no edges), as a graph with infinitely many vertices and no edges is disconnected.
An edge $AB$ exists only if all other cities $C$ lie in $a + X(b-a)$. To prevent the edge $AB$ from forming, Bob only needs to place a single city $C$ such that $C \notin a + X(b-a)$. Since $z_0 \notin X$, the point $C = a + z_0(b-a)$ is not in $a + X(b-a)$.
Bob can construct $V$ inductively. He picks $v_1, v_2$ arbitrarily. For each new city $v_n$, he ensures:
1. $v_n$ is not collinear with any two previous cities.
2. $v_n$ is at distance $> 1$ from all previous cities.
3. $v_n$ destroys any potential edges $v_i v_j$ for $i, j < n$ by choosing $v_n \notin v_i + X(v_j - v_i)$. Since $X \neq \mathbb{C}$, the complement of $X$ is non-empty, and Bob can always find such a $v_n$ while avoiding the finite number of forbidden lines and disks.
4. He ensures no new edges $v_i v_n$ are created. For $v_i v_n$ to be an edge, all $v_j$ ($j \neq i, n$) must be in $v_i + X(v_n - v_i)$, which implies $v_n \in \bigcap_{j \neq i, n} (v_i + \frac{1}{X}(v_j - v_i))$. Because $X \neq \mathbb{C}$, this intersection is a proper subset of the plane (and typically a very small set). Bob can easily pick $v_n$ outside these sets.

By this construction, Bob can ensure the resulting graph has no edges, meaning it is disconnected. Thus, Bob wins.

In both possible scenarios for Alice's strategy, Bob has a winning response.

Bob has a winning strategy. \(\square\)
