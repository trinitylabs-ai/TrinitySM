Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle which is not isosceles. Let $I$ be the incenter and $\omega$ be the circumcircle of $ABC$ with radius $R$. Let $D, E, F$ be the points where the incircle $\gamma$ of $\triangle ABC$ (with radius $r$) is tangent to the sides $BC, CA, AB$, respectively. Let $\omega_A$ be the $A$-mixtilinear incircle, which is tangent to $AB$ and $AC$ and internally tangent to $\omega$ at $T_A$. Similarly, define $T_B$ and $T_C$ for points $B$ and $C$. Let $S_A, S_B, S_C$ be the circumcircles of $\triangle ADT_A, \triangle BET_B, \triangle CFT_C$, respectively.

First, we establish a property of the $A$-mixtilinear incircle $\omega_A$. Let $r_A$ be the radius of $\omega_A$. The homothety $h(T_A, R/r_A)$ centered at $T_A$ maps $\omega_A$ to $\omega$. The homothety $h(A, r/r_A)$ centered at $A$ maps $\omega_A$ to the incircle $\gamma$. The composition $h(A, r/r_A) \circ h(T_A, R/r_A)^{-1}$ is a homothety that maps $\omega$ to the incircle $\gamma$. The center of this composition is the external center of homothety $H_{ext}$ of the incircle $\gamma$ and the circumcircle $\omega$. By the properties of composed homotheties, $H_{ext}$ must lie on the line connecting the centers of the two homotheties, $A$ and $T_A$. Thus, $H_{ext}, A, T_A$ are collinear. Similarly, $H_{ext}$ lies on $BT_B$ and $CT_C$.

Consider the power of $H_{ext}$ with respect to the circle $S_A$. Since $A$ and $T_A$ lie on $S_A$, the power is $P(H_{ext}, S_A) = H_{ext}A \cdot H_{ext}T_A$. Since $A$ and $T_A$ also lie on the circumcircle $\omega$, this product is equal to the power of $H_{ext}$ with respect to $\omega$: $P(H_{ext}, S_A) = P(H_{ext}, \omega)$. By symmetry, $P(H_{ext}, S_B) = P(H_{ext}, \omega)$ and $P(H_{ext}, S_C) = P(H_{ext}, \omega)$. Thus, $P(H_{ext}, S_A) = P(H_{ext}, S_B) = P(H_{ext}, S_C)$, which implies that $H_{ext}$ is the radical center of the three circles $S_A, S_B, S_C$.

Next, we examine the power of the incenter $I$ with respect to $S_A$. It is a known property of the mixtilinear incircle that the circumcircle of $\triangle ADT_A$ passes through the point $D'$ which is the reflection of $D$ across $I$. Since $I$ is the midpoint of $DD'$, the power of $I$ with respect to $S_A$ is $P(I, S_A) = \vec{ID} \cdot \vec{ID'} = -ID^2 = -r^2$. Similarly, $P(I, S_B) = -r^2$ and $P(I, S_C) = -r^2$. Thus, $I$ also has equal power with respect to $S_A, S_B, S_C$, meaning $I$ lies on the radical axis of any pair of these circles.

Since $H_{ext}$ and $I$ both lie on the radical axis of any pair of $S_A, S_B, S_C$, the three circles are coaxial with the common radical axis being the line $H_{ext}I$. Because $H_{ext}$ is the external center of homothety of the incircle and $\omega$, it lies on the line $OI$ connecting the incenter $I$ and circumcenter $O$. Thus, the radical axis is the line $OI$.

Finally, we show that the circles intersect at two points. The power of $I$ with respect to $S_A$ is $P(I, S_A) = -r^2 < 0$, which means $I$ lies inside $S_A$. Similarly, $I$ lies inside $S_B$ and $S_C$. Any line passing through the interior of a circle must intersect the circle at two distinct points. Therefore, the line $OI$ intersects each of the circles $S_A, S_B, S_C$ at two common points $X$ and $Y$.
