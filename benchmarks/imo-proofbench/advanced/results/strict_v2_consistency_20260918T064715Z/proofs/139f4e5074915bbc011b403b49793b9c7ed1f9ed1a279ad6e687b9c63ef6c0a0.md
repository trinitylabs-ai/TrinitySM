To prove that we can choose two diagonals of a convex $18n+2$-gon $P$ such that the three resulting parts each contain between $3n$ and $9n$ triangles, we translate the problem into the language of graph theory.

**1. Dual Graph Representation**
The triangulation of a convex polygon $P$ can be represented by its dual graph $T$. Each of the $18n$ triangles is a vertex in $T$, and two vertices are connected by an edge if the corresponding triangles share a diagonal. Since $P$ is a convex polygon and the triangulation uses $18n-1$ diagonals, the dual graph $T$ is a tree with $18n$ vertices. Furthermore, each vertex in $T$ has a degree of at most 3, because each triangle has at most 3 sides.

Removing an edge in $T$ corresponds to choosing a diagonal in $P$. Removing two edges in $T$ splits the tree into three components of sizes $s_1, s_2, s_3$, where $s_1 + s_2 + s_3 = 18n$. We must show that there exist two edges such that $3n \le s_1, s_2, s_3 \le 9n$.

**2. Existence of a Balanced Edge**
A known property of trees with maximum degree 3 is that for any such tree with $N$ vertices, there exists an edge that splits the tree into two components of sizes $s$ and $N-s$ such that $\frac{N}{3} \le s \le \frac{2N}{3}$.
*Proof sketch:* Let $v$ be the centroid of $T$, which splits $T$ into components of sizes $w_1, w_2, w_3$ such that $w_i \le \frac{N}{2}$. If any $w_i \ge \frac{N}{3}$, the edge connecting $v$ to that component satisfies the condition. If all $w_i < \frac{N}{3}$, then $w_1+w_2+w_3 = N-1 < N$, which is consistent. However, the component formed by $C_1 \cup C_2 \cup \{v\}$ has size $N-w_3 > \frac{2N}{3}$. By moving the edge into $C_1$, one can find a split in the range $[\frac{N}{3}, \frac{2N}{3}]$ because the maximum degree is 3.

**3. Finding the Two Diagonals**
For our tree $T$ with $N=18n$, there exists an edge $e_1$ that splits $T$ into components $T_1$ and $T_2$ with sizes $s_1$ and $18n-s_1$ such that $6n \le s_1 \le 12n$. 
If $s_1 > 9n$, we can simply swap the labels of $T_1$ and $T_2$ so that $s_1 \in [6n, 9n]$. Now, $T_2$ is a tree of size $M = 18n - s_1$. Since $s_1 \in [6n, 9n]$, we have $M \in [9n, 12n]$.

We now seek an edge $e_2$ in $T_2$ that splits it into two components of sizes $s_2$ and $s_3$, where $s_2 + s_3 = M$. We want $3n \le s_2, s_3 \le 9n$.
Applying the same property to $T_2$ (which also has maximum degree 3), there exists an edge $e_2$ such that $\frac{M}{3} \le s_2 \le \frac{2M}{3}$.
Given $M \in [9n, 12n]$, we have:
- $s_2 \ge \frac{M}{3} \ge \frac{9n}{3} = 3n$.
- $s_2 \le \frac{2M}{3} \le \frac{2(12n)}{3} = 8n \le 9n$.
- $s_3 = M - s_2$. Since $s_2 \le \frac{2M}{3}$, we have $s_3 \ge \frac{M}{3} \ge 3n$.
- Since $s_2 \ge \frac{M}{3}$, we have $s_3 \le \frac{2M}{3} \le 8n \le 9n$.

Thus, the three components have sizes $s_1 \in [6n, 9n]$, $s_2 \in [3n, 8n]$, and $s_3 \in [3n, 8n]$. All three sizes fall within the required interval $[3n, 9n]$.

The two diagonals corresponding to edges $e_1$ and $e_2$ divide the polygon $P$ into three parts containing $s_1, s_2,$ and $s_3$ triangles, respectively. $\square$
