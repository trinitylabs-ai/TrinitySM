To prove that for any convex $18n+2$-gon $P$ divided into $18n$ triangles by $18n-1$ diagonals, we can choose two diagonals such that the three parts divided by these diagonals each contain at least $3n$ and at most $9n$ triangles, we model the triangulation using its dual graph.

**1. Dual Graph Construction**
The dual graph of a triangulation of a convex polygon is a tree $T$ where each node corresponds to a triangle, and an edge exists between two nodes if the corresponding triangles share a diagonal. Since the polygon has $18n$ triangles, $T$ has $N=18n$ nodes. Because each triangle shares at most 3 edges with other triangles, the maximum degree of any node in $T$ is 3. Removing a diagonal in the polygon corresponds to removing an edge in $T$. Removing two diagonals corresponds to removing two edges, which splits $T$ into three components with sizes $t_1, t_2, t_3$ such that $t_1 + t_2 + t_3 = 18n$.

**2. The Centroid and Initial Partition**
Every tree $T$ has a centroid $v$, a node such that every component formed by removing $v$ has size at most $N/2$. Let $T_1, T_2, T_3$ be the components of $T \setminus \{v\}$ with sizes $s_1 \ge s_2 \ge s_3$. 
We have $s_1 + s_2 + s_3 = 18n-1$ and $s_1 \le 9n$.
Since $s_1 \le 9n$, we have $s_2 + s_3 = 18n - 1 - s_1 \ge 9n - 1$. Because $s_2 \ge s_3$, it follows that $s_2 \ge \frac{9n-1}{2} \ge 3n$ for $n \ge 1$. 
Thus, $s_1, s_2 \in [3n, 9n]$.

**3. Case Analysis**
We consider two cases based on the size of $s_3$:

*   **Case 1: $s_3 + 1 \ge 3n$.**
    We remove the edges incident to $v$ that lead to $T_1$ and $T_2$. The three components are $T_1$, $T_2$, and $T_3 \cup \{v\}$. Their sizes are $t_1 = s_1$, $t_2 = s_2$, and $t_3 = s_3 + 1$.
    We already have $t_1, t_2 \in [3n, 9n]$. Since $s_1 \ge 3n$ and $s_2 \ge 3n$, $t_3 = 18n - s_1 - s_2 \le 18n - 6n = 12n$. However, $s_3 \le s_2 \le 9n$, so $t_3 \le 9n+1$. If $t_3 = 9n+1$, then $s_1+s_2 = 9n-1$, which contradicts $s_1, s_2 \ge 3n$ for $n \ge 2$ (for $n=1$, $s_3+1 \le 9$ is trivial since $N=18$). In all cases, $t_1, t_2, t_3 \in [3n, 9n]$.

*   **Case 2: $s_3 + 1 < 3n$.**
    In this case, $s_1 + s_2 = 18n - (s_3+1) > 15n$. Since $s_1 \le 9n$, we must have $s_2 > 6n$.
    Root $T_2$ at $u$, the neighbor of $v$. Let $size(x)$ be the size of the subtree rooted at node $x \in T_2$. Since $size(u) = s_2 > 6n$, we can find a node $x \in T_2$ such that $size(x) \ge 3n$ and for all children $y$ of $x$, $size(y) < 3n$. 
    Since the maximum degree of any node is 3, $x$ has at most 2 children. Thus, $size(x) \le 1 + 2(3n-1) = 6n-1$.
    We now remove the edge leading to $T_1$ and the edge leading to the subtree at $x$. The component sizes are:
    $t_1 = s_1 \in (6n, 9n]$,
    $t_2 = size(x) \in [3n, 6n-1]$,
    $t_3 = 18n - s_1 - size(x)$.
    Checking the bounds for $t_3$:
    $t_3 \ge 18n - 9n - (6n-1) = 3n+1 > 3n$.
    $t_3 < 18n - 6n - 3n = 9n$.
    Thus, $t_1, t_2, t_3 \in [3n, 9n]$.

In both cases, we have found two diagonals that divide the polygon into three parts, each containing between $3n$ and $9n$ triangles. $\square$
