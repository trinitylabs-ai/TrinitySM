To determine all pairs of distinct positive integers $(a, b)$ such that we can make the numbers on the blackboard equal after a finite number of steps, we analyze the reachable numbers.

Let $S_n(x)$ be the set of numbers reachable from $x$ in exactly $n$ steps. The available operations are $f_1(x) = x+2$ and $f_2(x) = 3x$. Any element $y \in S_n(x)$ is obtained by applying $k$ multiplication operations and $n-k$ addition operations. Specifically,
\[ y = 3^k x + 2 \sum_{j=1}^{n-k} 3^{p_j}, \quad 0 \le p_1 \le p_2 \le \dots \le p_{n-k} \le k \]
where $p_j$ is the number of multiplications occurring after the $j$-th addition. Let $S = \sum_{j=1}^{m} 3^{p_j}$ where $m = n-k$. A known property of sums of powers of 3 is that $S$ can be represented as a sum of $m$ powers of 3 (each $\le 3^k$) if and only if:
1. $S \equiv m \pmod 2$
2. $m \le S \le m \cdot 3^k$
3. $\text{sd}_3(S) \le m$, where $\text{sd}_3(S)$ is the sum of the digits of $S$ in base 3.

We seek $n, k_1, k_2, S_1, S_2$ such that $3^{k_1} a + 2S_1 = 3^{k_2} b + 2S_2$, where $S_1$ is a sum of $m_1 = n-k_1$ powers ($\le 3^{k_1}$) and $S_2$ is a sum of $m_2 = n-k_2$ powers ($\le 3^{k_2}$).
The parity condition $S \equiv m \pmod 2$ implies:
\[ 3^{k_1} a + 2m_1 \equiv 3^{k_2} b + 2m_2 \pmod 4 \]
Substituting $m_1 = n-k_1$ and $m_2 = n-k_2$:
\[ 3^{k_1} a + 2n - 2k_1 \equiv 3^{k_2} b + 2n - 2k_2 \pmod 4 \]
\[ 3^{k_1} a - 2k_1 \equiv 3^{k_2} b - 2k_2 \pmod 4 \]

**Case 1: $a, b$ have different parity.**
Then $3^{k_1} a$ and $3^{k_2} b$ have different parity, so the equation $3^{k_1} a + 2S_1 = 3^{k_2} b + 2S_2$ is impossible because the LHS and RHS would have different parities. Thus, $a \equiv b \pmod 2$ is necessary.

**Case 2: $a, b$ are both even.**
Let $a=2A, b=2B$. The condition becomes $2 \cdot 3^{k_1} A - 2k_1 \equiv 2 \cdot 3^{k_2} B - 2k_2 \pmod 4$, which simplifies to $3^{k_1} A - k_1 \equiv 3^{k_2} B - k_2 \pmod 2$, or $A - k_1 \equiv B - k_2 \pmod 2$. This can always be satisfied by choosing $k_1, k_2$ appropriately. For large $n$, the conditions $\text{sd}_3(S) \le m$ and $m \le S \le m 3^k$ are easily satisfied.

**Case 3: $a, b$ are both odd.**
The condition is $3^{k_1} a - 2k_1 \equiv 3^{k_2} b - 2k_2 \pmod 4$.
Testing $k \pmod 2$:
- If $k$ is even, $3^k \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ and $2k \equiv 0 \pmod 4$.
- If $k$ is odd, $3^k \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ and $2k \equiv 2 \pmod 4$.
Possible values for $3^k x - 2k \pmod 4$ when $x$ is odd:
- $k$ even: $1 \cdot x - 0 = x \pmod 4$.
- $k$ odd: $3 \cdot x - 2 = 3x - 2 \pmod 4$.
If $x \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, values are $\{1, 3(1)-2=1\}$.
If $x \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, values are $\{3, 3(3)-2=7 \equiv 3\}$.
Thus, for odd $a, b$, the condition $3^{k_1} a - 2k_1 \equiv 3^{k_2} b - 2k_2 \pmod 4$ is satisfied if and only if $a \equiv b \pmod 4$.
If $a \equiv 1, b \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ (or vice versa), then $a-b \equiv 2 \pmod 4$, and the condition can never be met.

Thus, the numbers can be made equal if and only if $a \equiv b \pmod 2$ and, in the case where they are odd, $a \equiv b \pmod 4$. This is equivalent to saying $a \equiv b \pmod 2$ and $a \not\equiv b+2 \pmod 4$ for odd $a, b$. More simply, $a \equiv b \pmod 2$ and if $a, b$ are odd, $a \equiv b \pmod 4$.
Combining these, we find that the condition is $a \equiv b \pmod 2$ and $(a, b \text{ even})$ or $(a \equiv b \pmod 4)$. This is equivalent to $a \equiv b \pmod 2$ and $a \not\equiv b+2 \pmod 4$. However, if $a, b$ are even, $a \equiv b+2 \pmod 4$ is possible, but the parity condition $A-k_1 \equiv B-k_2 \pmod 2$ always works.
The final condition is $a \equiv b \pmod 2$, and if $a, b$ are odd, $a \equiv b \pmod 4$.

The pairs $(a, b)$ are those where $a \equiv b \pmod 2$, and if $a, b$ are odd, then $a \equiv b \pmod 4$.
