Let the number of rows be $H = 3002$ and the number of columns be $W = 3001$. Peter selects a set $S$ of 3000 cells such that exactly one cell is selected from each row $r \in \{2, 3, \dots, 3001\}$, and at most one cell is selected from each column $c \in \{1, 2, \dots, 3001\}$. Since there are $H-2 = 3000$ selected cells and $W = 3001$ columns, and each selected cell must be in a distinct column, exactly one column $C^* \in \{1, \dots, 3001\}$ contains no selected cells.

If James knows the index $C^*$, he can move the stone from $(1, C^*)$ down to $(H, C^*)$, avoiding all cells in $S$ and reaching the goal with 0 penalties. However, James does not know $C^*$. He discovers a cell $(r, c)$ is in $S$ only by entering it, which results in 1 penalty and resets the stone to $(1, 1)$.

Every time James hits a cell $(r, c) \in S$, he gains the information that $c$ is not the safe column $C^*$. Because each column contains at most one selected cell, a penalty at $(r, c)$ eliminates column $c$ as a candidate for $C^*$ and provides no information about whether any other column $j \neq c$ is $C^*$. Specifically, the knowledge that $c_r = c$ (where $c_r$ is the selected cell in row $r$) only implies $C^* \neq c$.

To guarantee that he has found $C^*$, James must eliminate all other $W-1$ candidate columns. Since each trip can result in at most one penalty (due to the reset), and each penalty eliminates exactly one column, James may need up to $W-1$ penalties to uniquely identify $C^*$. After $W-1$ penalties, only one column $j$ remains as a candidate; this column must be $C^*$, and James can then walk down it to reach the last row.

In the worst case, Peter can choose $C^*$ to be the last column James attempts. For instance, if James tests columns $1, 2, \dots, W$ in order, Peter can set $C^* = W$. James will then incur a penalty in each of the first $W-1$ columns. Thus, the maximum number of penalties James might incur is $W-1 = 3001 - 1 = 3000$.

If he hits a cell in $j_1$, he incurs his $(W-1)$-th penalty and knows that $j_2$ must be $C^*$, allowing him to reach the goal. Thus, he can always reach the goal with at most $W-1 = 3000$ penalties.

Since he can guarantee success with 3000 penalties, the smallest $n$ such that he achieves his goal before receiving $n$ points is $n = 3000 + 1 = 3001$.
