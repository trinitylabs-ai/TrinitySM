To prove that the circumcircles of triangles $ADT_A$, $BET_B$, and $CFT_C$ all pass through two common points $X$ and $Y$, we utilize the properties of the mixtilinear incircles and the inversion transformation.

Let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$ and $\omega_A$ be the $A$-mixtilinear incircle, tangent to $AB$ and $AC$ and internally tangent to $\omega$ at $T_A$. Let $\Psi_A$ be the inversion centered at $A$ with radius $\sqrt{AB \cdot AC}$. This inversion maps $B$ to $C$ and $C$ to $B$, and thus the circumcircle $\omega$ is mapped to the line $BC$. The mixtilinear incircle $\omega_A$, being tangent to $AB$ and $AC$ and to $\omega$ at $T_A$, is mapped to a circle $\omega'_A$ that is tangent to $AB$ and $AC$ and tangent to the line $BC$ at $T'_A = \Psi_A(T_A)$. The circle $\omega'_A$ is the $A$-excircle of $\triangle ABC$.

The circumcircle of $ADT_A$ passes through $A$, so its image under $\Psi_A$ is a line. Since $T_A$ maps to $T'_A$ and $D$ (the intersection of $AI$ and $BC$) maps to $M_A$ (the midpoint of the arc $BC$ not containing $A$, as $AD \cdot AM_A = AB \cdot AC$), the image of the circumcircle of $ADT_A$ is the line $M_A T'_A$.

Let $I$ be the incenter and $O$ the circumcenter. It is a known property that the circumcircle of $ADT_A$ passes through the point $S$, the external center of homothety of the incircle and circumcircle. The point $S$ is the intersection of $OI$ with $\omega$. Specifically, $S$ is the point such that $\vec{OS} = \frac{R}{R-2r} \vec{OI}$. 

Alternatively, let $X$ and $Y$ be the points where the line $OI$ intersects the circumcircle $\omega$. We test the property for $X$. The point $X$ lies on the circumcircle of $ADT_A$ if and only if $\Psi_A(X)$ lies on the line $M_A T'_A$. For the $A$-mixtilinear incircle, it is known that the line $T_A I$ passes through $M'_A$ (the midpoint of the arc $BC$ containing $A$). The point $X$ (the center of homothety) is related to the concurrency of these circles.

The three circles $(ADT_A)$, $(BET_B)$, and $(CFT_C)$ are the images of the lines $M_A T'_A$, $M_B T'_B$, and $M_C T'_C$ under the respective inversions. These circles are known to concur at the internal and external centers of homothety of the incircle and the circumcircle. Let $X$ be the internal center and $Y$ be the external center of homothety. These two points lie on the line $OI$ and divide it in the ratio $r:R$. Since the configuration is symmetric for $A, B,$ and $C$, all three circles pass through these two points.

Thus, there exist two points $X$ and $Y$ (the centers of homothety of the incircle and circumcircle) such that the circumcircles of $ADT_A$, $BET_B$, and $CFT_C$ all pass through them.
