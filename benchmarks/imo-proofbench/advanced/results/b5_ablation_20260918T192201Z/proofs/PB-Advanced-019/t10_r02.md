To find all positive real numbers $r$ such that $n(n+1)r - B(n, r)$ is a multiple of $n$ for all positive integers $n$, where $B(n, r) = \sum_{k=1}^n A(kr)$ and $A(r) = \{2r\}$ (the fractional part of $2r$), we begin by substituting the definition of $A(kr)$ into $B(n, r)$:
$$ B(n, r) = \sum_{k=1}^n \{2kr\} = \sum_{k=1}^n (2kr - \lfloor 2kr \rfloor) = 2r \sum_{k=1}^n k - \sum_{k=1}^n \lfloor 2kr \rfloor = n(n+1)r - \sum_{k=1}^n \lfloor 2kr \rfloor. $$
The condition that $n(n+1)r - B(n, r)$ is a multiple of $n$ for all $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ is thus:
$$ n(n+1)r - \left( n(n+1)r - \sum_{k=1}^n \lfloor 2kr \rfloor \right) = \sum_{k=1}^n \lfloor 2kr \rfloor = n \cdot m_n $$
for some integer $m_n$ for every positive integer $n$. Let $q = 2r$. We require that $n$ divides $S_n = \sum_{k=1}^n \lfloor kq \rfloor$ for all $n \ge 1$.

Let $m_n = \frac{S_n}{n}$. Then $m_n$ must be an integer for all $n$. Consider the difference $m_{n+1} - m_n$:
$$ m_{n+1} - m_n = \frac{S_{n+1}}{n+1} - \frac{S_n}{n} = \frac{n(S_n + \lfloor (n+1)q \rfloor) - (n+1)S_n}{n(n+1)} = \frac{n \lfloor (n+1)q \rfloor - S_n}{n(n+1)}. $$
Substituting $S_n = q \frac{n(n+1)}{2} - \sum_{k=1}^n \{kq\}$ and $\lfloor (n+1)q \rfloor = (n+1)q - \{(n+1)q\}$, we get:
$$ m_{n+1} - m_n = \frac{n((n+1)q - \{(n+1)q\}) - q \frac{n(n+1)}{2} + \sum_{k=1}^n \{kq\}}{n(n+1)} = \frac{q}{2} - \frac{\{(n+1)q\}}{n+1} + \frac{\sum_{k=1}^n \{kq\}}{n(n+1)}. $$
As $n \to \infty$, the terms $\frac{\{(n+1)q\}}{n+1}$ and $\frac{\sum_{k=1}^n \{kq\}}{n(n+1)}$ both approach 0 because $0 \le \{x\} < 1$. Thus:
$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} (m_{n+1} - m_n) = \frac{q}{2}. $$
Since $m_{n+1} - m_n$ is an integer for all $n$, a sequence of integers that converges must be eventually constant. Therefore, $m_{n+1} - m_n = \frac{q}{2}$ for all $n \ge N$ for some $N$. This implies that $\frac{q}{2}$ must be an integer. Let $\frac{q}{2} = m$ for some integer $m$.
Then $q = 2m$, which means $2r = 2m$, so $r = m$. Since $r$ is given as a positive real number, $m$ must be a positive integer.

Conversely, if $r$ is a positive integer, then $2kr$ is an integer for all $k \in \mathbb{Z}^+$, so $A(kr) = \{2kr\} = 0$. Then $B(n, r) = 0$, and the expression becomes:
$$ n(n+1)r - 0 = n[(n+1)r]. $$
Since $n+1$ and $r$ are integers, $(n+1)r$ is an integer, so $n(n+1)r$ is indeed a multiple of $n$ for all $n$.

Thus, the positive real numbers $r$ that satisfy the condition are precisely the positive integers.

The positive real numbers $r$ are $r \in \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$.

\(\square\)
