If $AB,CD$ are parallel to each other then $(W_{1})$ maps to $(W_{4}),(W_{2})$
 maps to $(W_{3})$ via a reflection w.r.t. the center $O$ of the
 parallelogram $ABCD$.

 If $AB,CD$ are not parallel to each other then let $X$ be the intersection
 of $AB,CD$. Consider the inversion with power $XA.XC$ and then reflect
 it w.r.t. to the internal bisector $\angle AXD$, we have $A\rightarrow C$,
 $B\rightarrow D$ (1). Note that inversion preserves the angle between
 the circles and the lines, and so $(W_{1})$ is mapped to $(W_{4})$
 since we have (1) and the angle between $(W_{1})$ and $AB$ is $\alpha$,
 which is also the angle between $(W_{4})$ and $CD$. Similarly, we
 have $(W_{2})$ is mapped to $(W_{3})$. Since $(W_{1})$ is tangent
 to $(W_{2})$, we have $(W_{3})$ is tangent to $(W_{4})$. Hence
 proved.
