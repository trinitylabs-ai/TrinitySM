The analytic setup is essentially correct. The solution correctly proves \(A\) is acute, obtains
\[
AI'=\frac{r\cos A}{\sin(A/2)},
\]
derives the correct radius required for the rhombus,
\[
r_0=\frac{r\cos A}{2\cos^2(A/2)},
\]
and verifies that \(r_0\) satisfies the external-tangency quadratic.

There is, however, a small but important unproved final assertion: the solution merely states that \(r_0\) is the smaller root and therefore corresponds to the given circle \(W\). Neither the root comparison nor the connection with the condition that \(W\) is closer to \(A\) is established. For example, writing
\[
s=\sin\frac A2,\qquad q=\cos\frac{B-C}{2},
\]
Vieta’s formulas give the other root as
\[
r_1=2Rs(q+s),
\]
from which \(r_0<r_1\) can be checked. One must then additionally justify that the larger-root circle cannot be the circle specified in the problem (by comparing distances from \(A\), or by showing its tangency point lies beyond a side).

There is also a minor imprecision in saying that the similarity \(\triangle ABC\sim\triangle AEF\) is a homothety: it is a homothety composed with reflection in the angle bisector. The conclusion about the incenter remains valid because the incenter lies on that bisector.

These are locally repairable gaps, while the core computation and strategy are sound.
