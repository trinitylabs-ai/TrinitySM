The main argument is correct: for \(A<90^\circ\), the similarity \(\triangle AEF\sim\triangle ABC\) gives the correct value of \(AI'\), and the rhombus condition is correctly reduced to
\[
\rho=\frac{r\cos A}{2\cos^2(A/2)}.
\]
The coordinate computation for the Euler-circle center and the resulting tangency equation are also correct, and this value of \(\rho\) indeed satisfies it.

There is, however, a minor logical gap in identifying this root with the given circle. Positivity of both roots does not by itself show that the displayed root is the smaller one. By Vieta, the other root is
\[
4R\sin\frac A2\cos\frac B2\cos\frac C2,
\]
which is larger than \(r\), whereas the displayed root is smaller than \(r\); this supplies the missing comparison. The connection between the “closer to \(A\)” condition and selection of that root should also be stated explicitly.

Additionally, before proving \(A<90^\circ\), the angle used in the law-of-cosines argument is incorrect for obtuse \(A\), since \(I'\) lies on the opposite half of the angle-bisector line. This is harmless after reordering the independently proved acute-angle argument.

Thus the solution has a sound, essentially complete core with locally repairable gaps.
