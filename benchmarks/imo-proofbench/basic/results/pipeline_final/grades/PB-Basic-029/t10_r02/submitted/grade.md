The construction is essentially valid: for sufficiently small, strictly increasing, strictly concave \((\delta_k)\), the lines are in general position, and the level calculations give exactly \(m+1\) vertices of level \(m\). Hence it realizes \(\binom{p+2}{2}\) red points.

However, the lower-bound proof has a major flaw. A projective transformation sending \(O\) to infinity does not necessarily send every segment \(OX\) to a downward vertical ray. Depending on which side of the inverse image of the line at infinity \(X\) lies, it may map to the upward ray instead. There need not be a line through \(O\) placing all intersection points on one side—for example, \(O\) may lie inside the triangle formed by three lines. Thus the ordinary-level induction does not apply to an arbitrary original configuration.

Since the correct answer and an optimal construction are provided, but the essential lower bound is unproved, the specific partial-credit criterion applies.
