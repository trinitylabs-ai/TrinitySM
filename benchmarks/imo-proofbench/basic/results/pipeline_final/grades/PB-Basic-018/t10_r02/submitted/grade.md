The algebraic transformation to
\[
3u^2-2v^2=1,\qquad u=4x+1,\quad v=6y+1
\]
is correct. The congruence analysis, closed forms, and the identity
\[
3u_{2k-1}+2v_{2k-1}+1=6u_k^2
\]
are also correctly derived, yielding \(2x+2y+1=u_k^2\) for solutions in the stated recurrence.

However, the solution does not justify the crucial assertion that **every** positive solution of \(3u^2-2v^2=1\) is generated from \((1,1)\) by that recurrence. The usual classification of solutions to \(z^2-6w^2=1\) does not by itself establish this for the inhomogeneous norm equation. A short descent using
\[
(u,v)\mapsto(5u-4v,\,5v-6u)
\]
would fill the gap.

Thus the argument is essentially correct but has a genuine, localized omission in the Pell classification.
