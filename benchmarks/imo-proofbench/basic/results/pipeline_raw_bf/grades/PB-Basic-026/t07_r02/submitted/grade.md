The radical-axis argument in Step 2 is valid: \(P_a\) lies on the tangents at \(D\) and \(X\), so \(DX\) is the polar of \(P_a\) with respect to the incircle. However, this does not connect \(X\) to \(D'\) or to a common point on \(OI\).

The decisive assertion in Step 4—that \(T,D',X\) are collinear—is neither proved nor true in general. The polar statement does not imply this incidence; saying that the construction “ensures” it simply assumes the main missing claim.

For example, take
\[
A=(0,0),\quad B=(4,0),\quad C=(0,3).
\]
Then
\[
I=(1,1),\quad D=\left(\frac85,\frac95\right),\quad
D'=\left(\frac95,\frac85\right).
\]
The external homothety center of the incircle and circumcircle is
\[
T=\left(\frac13,\frac23\right).
\]
The relevant tangency point is
\[
X=\left(\frac{32}{185},\frac{81}{185}\right).
\]
But
\[
\operatorname{slope}(D'X)=\frac57,\qquad
\operatorname{slope}(D'T)=\frac7{11},
\]
so \(D',X,T\) are not collinear.

There are also inaccuracies in Step 3: under the homothety the correct parallelism is \(OM_{BC}\parallel ID\), not \(IM_{BC}\parallel ID\), and the claimed formula \(\angle DIA=90^\circ-A/2\) is not generally true.

Thus the central argument is false. The correct concurrence point is the centroid of the contact triangle \(DEF\), as in the reference solution, not the external homothety center. The valid polar observation does not establish the inversion/centroid milestone or equivalent information required for partial credit.
