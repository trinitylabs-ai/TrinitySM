The main trigonometric argument is sound: the rhombus condition is correctly reduced to
\[
r_W=\frac{r\cos A}{2\cos^2(A/2)},
\]
and the Euler-circle tangency equation and proposed root are correct for \(\cos A>0\).

There are, however, minor rigor gaps:

- \(\triangle AEF\) is not the image of \(\triangle ABC\) under a homothety centered at \(A\); the similarity also involves reflection across the angle bisector. The conclusion \(AI'=|\cos A|\,AI\) is correct, but the stated justification is inaccurate.
- The proof assumes \(\cos A>0\) without deriving this from the configuration, then reintroduces \(|\cos A|\) without checking the non-acute case.
- It asserts without proof that the obtained value is the smaller root and hence corresponds to the specified circle \(W\). This is readily repairable using Vieta’s formulas and a short inequality.

These are local, repairable issues rather than defects in the core argument, so the solution is almost correct but not fully rigorous.
