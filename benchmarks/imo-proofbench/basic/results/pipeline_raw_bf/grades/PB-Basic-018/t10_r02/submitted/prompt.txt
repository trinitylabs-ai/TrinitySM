**PROBLEM STATEMENT**

Let $x$ and $y$ be positive integers satisfying $2x^2 + x = 3y^2 + y$. Prove that $2x+2y+1$ is a perfect square.

**GROUND-TRUTH SOLUTION**

Lemma: If $ab$ is a perfect square for coprime integers $a,b$ then they are perfect squares.

 Observe that $2x^2+x=3y^2+y$ is equivalent to $y^2 = 2x^2-2y^2+x-y = (x-y)(2x+2y+1)$. Let $d$ be the greatest common divisior of $x-y,2x+2y+1$, we have $d | x-y$ and $d | 2x+2y+1$. On the other hand, observe that $y^2 = (x-y)(2x+2y+1)$. Hence, we must have that $d^2 | y^2$, which implies $d | y$. Since $d | y$ and $d | x-y$, we have $d | x$. Furthermore, since $d | x, d | y, d | 2x+2y+1$, we have that $d | 1$ and thus, $d = 1$, meaning that $x-y,2x+2y+1$ are coprime integers. From the Lemma, we have that $x-y,2x+2y+1$ are perfect squares. Hence proved.

**PROPOSED SOLUTION**

To prove that $2x+2y+1$ is a perfect square for positive integers $x$ and $y$ satisfying $2x^2 + x = 3y^2 + y$, we begin by transforming the given equation into a form that allows us to characterize all possible solutions.

### 1. Transformation to a Pell-like Equation
The given equation is $2x^2 + x = 3y^2 + y$. To complete the squares, we multiply the entire equation by 24:
\[ 48x^2 + 24x = 72y^2 + 24y \]
We can rewrite this as:
\[ 3(16x^2 + 8x + 1) - 3 = 2(36y^2 + 12y + 1) - 2 \]
\[ 3(4x+1)^2 - 3 = 2(6y+1)^2 - 2 \]
\[ 3(4x+1)^2 - 2(6y+1)^2 = 1 \]
Let $u = 4x+1$ and $v = 6y+1$. The equation becomes:
\[ 3u^2 - 2v^2 = 1 \]
Since $x$ and $y$ are positive integers, we seek positive integer solutions $(u, v)$ where $u \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ and $v \equiv 1 \pmod 6$.

### 2. General Solutions of the Equation
The fundamental solution to $3u^2 - 2v^2 = 1$ is $(u_1, v_1) = (1, 1)$. The solutions to the related Pell equation $z^2 - 6w^2 = 1$ are generated by the fundamental solution $(5, 2)$. All positive solutions $(u_n, v_n)$ to $3u^2 - 2v^2 = 1$ can be generated by the recurrence:
\[ u_{n+1} = 5u_n + 4v_n, \quad v_{n+1} = 6u_n + 5v_n \]
Starting with $(u_1, v_1) = (1, 1)$, we check the conditions for $x, y$ to be integers:
- $u_{n+1} = 5u_n + 4v_n \equiv u_n \pmod 4$. Since $u_1 = 1$, $u_n \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ for all $n$.
- $v_{n+1} = 6u_n + 5v_n \equiv -v_n \pmod 6$. Since $v_1 = 1$, we have $v_n \equiv 1 \pmod 6$ if $n$ is odd, and $v_n \equiv 5 \pmod 6$ if $n$ is even.

Thus, $x$ and $y$ are integers if and only if $n$ is odd. Let $n = 2k-1$ for $k \in \mathbb{Z}^+$. For $x, y$ to be positive, we require $n \geq 3$ (i.e., $k \geq 2$).

### 3. Proof that $2x+2y+1$ is a Square
We express $2x+2y+1$ in terms of $u_n$ and $v_n$:
\[ 2x+2y+1 = 2\left(\frac{u_n-1}{4}\right) + 2\left(\frac{v_n-1}{6}\right) + 1 = \frac{u_n-1}{2} + \frac{v_n-1}{3} + 1 = \frac{3u_n + 2v_n + 1}{6} \]
Let $\lambda_1 = 5 + 2\sqrt{6}$ and $\lambda_2 = 5 - 2\sqrt{6}$. The closed-form expressions for the recurrence are:
\[ u_n = \frac{3-\sqrt{6}}{6} \lambda_1^n + \frac{3+\sqrt{6}}{6} \lambda_2^n, \quad v_n = \frac{\sqrt{6}-2}{4} \lambda_1^n - \frac{\sqrt{6}+2}{4} \lambda_2^n \]
Substituting these into the expression for $n = 2k-1$:
\[ 3u_{2k-1} + 2v_{2k-1} + 1 = \left( \frac{3-\sqrt{6}}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{6}-2}{2} \right) \lambda_1^{2k-1} + \left( \frac{3+\sqrt{6}}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{6}+2}{2} \right) \lambda_2^{2k-1} + 1 \]
\[ = \frac{1}{2} \lambda_1^{2k-1} + \frac{1}{2} \lambda_2^{2k-1} + 1 \]
Now, consider the square of $u_k$:
\[ u_k^2 = \left( \frac{3-\sqrt{6}}{6} \lambda_1^k + \frac{3+\sqrt{6}}{6} \lambda_2^k \right)^2 = \frac{(3-\sqrt{6})^2 \lambda_1^{2k} + (3+\sqrt{6})^2 \lambda_2^{2k} + 2(3-\sqrt{6})(3+\sqrt{6})(\lambda_1\lambda_2)^k}{36} \]
Since $\lambda_1\lambda_2 = 1$ and $(3 \pm \sqrt{6})^2 = 15 \pm 6\sqrt{6}$:
\[ u_k^2 = \frac{(15-6\sqrt{6})\lambda_1^{2k} + (15+6\sqrt{6})\lambda_2^{2k} + 6}{36} = \frac{(5-2\sqrt{6})\lambda_1^{2k} + (5+2\sqrt{6})\lambda_2^{2k} + 2}{12} \]
Using $5-2\sqrt{6} = \lambda_2$ and $5+2\sqrt{6} = \lambda_1$:
\[ u_k^2 = \frac{\lambda_2 \lambda_1^{2k} + \lambda_1 \lambda_2^{2k} + 2}{12} = \frac{\lambda_1^{2k-1} + \lambda_2^{2k-1} + 2}{12} \]
Multiplying by 6:
\[ 6u_k^2 = \frac{\lambda_1^{2k-1} + \lambda_2^{2k-1} + 2}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \lambda_1^{2k-1} + \frac{1}{2} \lambda_2^{2k-1} + 1 \]
Comparing the two results, we find that $3u_{2k-1} + 2v_{2k-1} + 1 = 6u_k^2$. Therefore:
\[ 2x+2y+1 = \frac{6u_k^2}{6} = u_k^2 \]
Since $u_k$ is an integer, $2x+2y+1$ is a perfect square. \(\square\)
