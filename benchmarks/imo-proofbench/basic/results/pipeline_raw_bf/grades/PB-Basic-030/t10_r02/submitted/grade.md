The solution has a sound analytic core: after correcting the coordinate notation, it establishes the invariant
\[
O_3O_4^2-(R_3^2+R_4^2)=O_1O_2^2-(R_1^2+R_2^2),
\]
together with \(R_1R_2=R_3R_4\).

However, there are minor but genuine issues:

- The displayed normal vectors are incorrect: with \(A=(a,h),B=(b,0)\), the appropriate normal involves \(x_B-x_A\), not \(y_B-y_A\). The intended first components, and hence the subsequent dot products, are nevertheless correct.
- The proof assumes without justification that the original tangency is external. Internal tangency is possible. In that case one must use
  \[
  O_1O_2^2-(R_1^2+R_2^2)=-2R_1R_2,
  \]
  and the same invariant then gives
  \[
  O_3O_4=|R_3-R_4|,
  \]
  proving internal tangency.
- The quantities \(d_i\) should be described as signed offsets when an angle is obtuse, rather than ordinary distances.

All these defects are locally repairable, and the main argument is valid. Thus the solution is almost correct but not fully rigorous as written.
