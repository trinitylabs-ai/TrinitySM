The contradiction setup is correct: if every \(P_k\) had at least \(k\) distinct real roots, then it would have degree \(k\), so every \(c_k\neq0\).

The cited Newton inequality and its limiting consequence are useful. Under the additional assumption that the coefficients all have the same sign—or alternate in sign—the argument deriving
\[
|c_k|\le \frac{|c_1|^k}{k!\,|c_0|^{k-1}}
\]
correctly contradicts the nonzero integrality of \(c_k\).

However, the treatment of general sign patterns is fundamentally incomplete:

- Descartes’ rule does not reduce arbitrary nonzero sign sequences to the cases considered.
- The alternatives \(c_{i-1}c_{i+1}>0\) for every \(i\) and \(c_{i-1}c_{i+1}<0\) for every \(i\) do not exhaust mixed sign patterns.
- Even in the latter period-four case, checking one particular polynomial with coefficients \(\pm1\) says nothing about arbitrary coefficient magnitudes.
- The final assertion that adjusting the coefficients forces them to be nonintegral or zero is entirely unproved.

Thus the proof does not establish the theorem for general sequences. It nevertheless contains a valid useful coefficient inequality and a correct contradiction for substantial special sign cases, meriting partial credit under the specific guideline.
