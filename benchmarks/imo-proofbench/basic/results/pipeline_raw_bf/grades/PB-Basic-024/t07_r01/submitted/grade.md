The special cases \(a=0\), \(b=0\), and \(c=0\) are handled correctly. The modulo \(5\) argument also correctly shows that, in the positive case, \(c\) must be even, and the subsequent factorization is a useful reduction.

However, the central remaining case is not proved. The assertion that
\[
2^k+5^j=253^m
\]
has no solutions for \(m\ge2\), “verified by growth and modular constraints,” is unsupported. Proving such an exponential Diophantine assertion is a major component, not a minor omitted detail. There are also smaller issues: the claimed equation \(1+5^j=253^m\) when \(k=0\) initially ignores a possible power-of-\(2\) factor, and the assignments of \(X,Y\) in the \(m=1\) case are reversed since \(X<Y\).

Consequently, the proof does not eliminate all positive cases. Moreover, it does not completely analyze either the even-\(a\) case or the odd-\(a\) case, as required for the specified partial-credit award. The valid reductions therefore do not meet the stated threshold for 1 point.
