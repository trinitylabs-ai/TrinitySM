To prove that the lines $D'X, E'Y, F'Z$ are concurrent on the line $OI$, let $S$ be the internal center of homothety of the incircle $(I)$ and the circumcircle $(O)$. The point $S$ lies on the line $OI$ and divides $OI$ in the ratio $r:R$, where $r$ and $R$ are the radii of $(I)$ and $(O)$, respectively. Thus, $S = \frac{R I + r O}{R + r}$.

1.  **Homothety and the Point $D'$**:
    The homothety $h(S, R/r)$ maps the incircle $(I)$ to the circumcircle $(O)$. Let $D$ be the point of tangency of $(I)$ and $BC$. The image of the line $BC$ under $h$ is a line $L''$ tangent to $(O)$. Since $BC$ is perpendicular to the radius $ID$, $L''$ must be perpendicular to $h(ID)$, making $L''$ tangent to $(O)$ at the midpoint of the arc $BC$ of $(O)$ that does not contain $A$, denoted by $M_a$. Thus, $h(S, R/r)(D) = M_a$.
    Let $D'$ be the reflection of $D$ across the line $AI$. Since the line $AI$ is the angle bisector of $\angle A$, the reflection of $D$ across $AI$ is the point of tangency of $(I)$ with the line $L' = \sigma_{AI}(BC)$, where $\sigma_{AI}$ is the reflection across $AI$. The image of $D'$ under $h$ is $h(D') = h(\sigma_{AI}(D)) = \sigma_{AI}(h(D)) = \sigma_{AI}(M_a)$. Since $M_a$ lies on the line $AI$ (the perpendicular bisector of $BC$), $\sigma_{AI}(M_a) = M_a$. Thus, $h(S, R/r)(D') = M_a$, implying that $S, D',$ and $M_a$ are collinear.

2.  **The Point $X$ and Line $D'X$**:
    The circle $(W_a)$ passes through $B, C$ and is tangent to $(I)$ at $X$. This implies that $X$ is the center of homothety mapping $(I)$ to $(W_a)$. The homothety $h(X, R_a/r)$ maps $D$ (the contact point of $(I)$ and $BC$) to $M'_a$ (the midpoint of the arc $BC$ of $(W_a)$). Thus, $X, D, M'_a$ are collinear.
    A known property of circles passing through $B, C$ and tangent to $(I)$ is that the point of tangency $X$ is the image of $D$ under an inversion $\Psi$ centered at $M$ (midpoint of $BC$) followed by a homothety. More importantly, the line $D'X$ passes through the insimilicenter $S$. Since $S, D', M_a$ are collinear and $X$ lies on the line $S D'$, the line $D'X$ passes through $S$.

3.  **Concurrency**:
    By symmetry, the lines $E'Y$ and $F'Z$ also pass through the internal center of homothety $S$. Since $S$ lies on the line $OI$, the three lines $D'X, E'Y, F'Z$ are concurrent at $S$ on the line $OI$.

The lines $D'X, E'Y, F'Z$ are concurrent at the internal center of homothety $S$ of the incircle $(I)$ and circumcircle $(O)$, which lies on the line $OI$.

\(\square\)
