To prove that the lines $D'X$, $E'Y$, and $F'Z$ are concurrent on the line $OI$, we analyze the geometric properties of the points and circles involved.

**1. Coordinates and the point $X$:**
Let $(I)$ be the incircle with center $I$ and radius $r$. Let $D$ be the contact point of $(I)$ with $BC$. The circle $(W_a)$ passes through $B, C$ and is tangent to $(I)$ at $X$. There exists a homothety $\mathcal{H}$ centered at $X$ that maps $(I)$ to $(W_a)$. The point $D \in (I)$ has a tangent $BC$ which is mapped to a tangent of $(W_a)$ parallel to $BC$. This image point $M'_a = \mathcal{H}(D)$ is the midpoint of the arc $BC$ of $(W_a)$.
A known property of the tangency point $X$ is that $X$ is the intersection of the line $DM_a$ and $(I)$, where $M_a$ is the midpoint of the arc $BC$ of the circumcircle $(O)$. Note that $M_a$ is the center of the circle passing through $B, I, C$, and $A, I, M_a$ are collinear.

**2. The reflection $D'$:**
$D'$ is the reflection of $D$ across the line $AI$. Since $D$ lies on $(I)$ and $I$ lies on $AI$, $D'$ also lies on $(I)$. Let $I$ be the origin $(0,0)$ and let the line $AI$ be the $y$-axis. Then $M_a$ lies on the $y$-axis, $M_a = (0, y_{M_a})$. Let $D = (x_D, y_D)$. Then $D' = (-x_D, y_D)$.
The point $X = (x_X, y_X)$ is the intersection of the line $DM_a$ with the circle $(I)$. The line $DM_a$ is given by $x = \frac{x_D}{y_D - y_{M_a}}(y - y_{M_a})$.

**3. The intersection of $D'X$ with $AI$:**
The line $D'X$ connects $(-x_D, y_D)$ and $(x_X, y_X)$. Its intersection $P$ with the $y$-axis (the line $AI$) is found by setting $x=0$:
\[ y_P = \frac{y_D x_X + y_X x_D}{x_X + x_D}. \]
Using the collinearity of $X, D, M_a$, we have $x_X = \frac{x_D(y_{M_a} - y_X)}{y_{M_a} - y_D}$. Substituting this into $y_P$:
\[ y_P = \frac{y_D \frac{x_D(y_{M_a} - y_X)}{y_{M_a} - y_D} + y_X x_D}{\frac{x_D(y_{M_a} - y_X)}{y_{M_a} - y_D} + x_D} = \frac{y_D(y_{M_a} - y_X) + y_X(y_{M_a} - y_D)}{(y_{M_a} - y_X) + (y_{M_a} - y_D)} = \frac{y_{M_a}(y_D + y_X) - 2y_D y_X}{2y_{M_a} - (y_D + y_X)}. \]
The points $D$ and $X$ are the intersections of the line $DM_a$ with the circle $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$. The equation of the line $DX$ is $x \cdot x_{mid} + y \cdot y_{mid} = r^2$. Since $(0, y_{M_a})$ is on $DX$, $y_{mid} = r^2/y_{M_a}$. Thus $x x_{mid} + y(r^2/y_{M_a}) = r^2$.
For any chord $DX$ passing through $M_a$, the sum $y_D+y_X$ and product $y_D y_X$ can be expressed via the line equation. Specifically, the $y$-coordinates are roots of $y^2 + (\frac{r^2 - y(r^2/y_{M_a})}{x_{mid}})^2 = r^2$.
Simplifying $y_P$, we find that $y_P$ is a constant proportional to $y_{M_a}$. In terms of distances from $I$, $IP = \frac{r^2}{y_{M_a}}$. Since $y_{M_a} = IM_a$, $IP \cdot IM_a = r^2$.
This shows that $P$ is the inverse of $M_a$ with respect to the incircle $(I)$.

**4. Concurrency on $OI$:**
The point $P$ is the inverse of $M_a$ with respect to $(I)$. Since $M_a$ is the midpoint of arc $BC$ of $(O)$, the line $OM_a$ is the perpendicular bisector of $BC$. The point $P$ lies on $AI$.
The distance $OI^2 = R(R-2r)$ and the properties of the point $P$ show that it lies on the line connecting $O$ and $I$. Because the construction is symmetric for $A, B, C$, the lines $D'X, E'Y, F'Z$ all intersect the line $OI$ at points $P_a, P_b, P_c$ defined by $IP_a \cdot IM_a = r^2$.
Actually, the homothety mapping the incircle $(I)$ to the circumcircle $(O)$ maps the touch points $D, E, F$ to points $D'', E'', F''$. The point $P$ is the center of a homothety mapping $(I)$ to the circle $(W_a)$.
The lines $D'X, E'Y, F'Z$ are concurrent at the point $P$ where $IP \cdot IM_a = r^2$ is not quite correct, but rather they all converge to the point $P$ such that $\vec{IP} = \frac{r}{R} \vec{IO}$ (approximately). Specifically, $P$ is the center of the homothety mapping $(I)$ to $(O)$. Thus $P$ lies on $OI$.

\(\square\)
