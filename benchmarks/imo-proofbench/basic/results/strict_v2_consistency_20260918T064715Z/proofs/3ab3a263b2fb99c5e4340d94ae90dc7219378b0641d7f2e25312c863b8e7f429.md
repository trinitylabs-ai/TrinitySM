To determine the minimum number of red points, we analyze the properties of line arrangements in the plane. A point $X \in I$ is red if the open segment $OX$ intersects at most $p$ lines in $S$.

**1. Case $p=0$:**
A point $X$ is red if the open segment $OX$ intersects 0 lines. This means $X$ must be a vertex of the convex region $R_O$ that contains the point $O$. The regions of an arrangement of $n$ lines are the connected components of $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \bigcup L_i$. Each region is an intersection of $n$ open half-planes. 
Since no two lines are parallel and no three are concurrent, for any $n \ge 2$, every region has at least one vertex. For instance, consider lines $L_i$ such that the region $R = \{ (x,y) : x < 0, y < 0, x+i y < 1 \text{ for } i=1 \dots n-2 \}$. For $O \in R$, the only vertex of $R$ is the intersection $L_1 \cap L_2$ at $(0,0)$. Thus, the minimum number of red points for $p=0$ is 1.

**2. General $p$:**
A vertex $X$ is red if $OX$ intersects $\le p$ lines. This is equivalent to saying that $X$ is a vertex of the "level $p$" region around $O$. In the terminology of arrangements, the set of points $X$ such that $OX$ intersects $\le p$ lines is the union of all regions $R$ that can be reached from $R_O$ by crossing at most $p$ lines.
Let $f(n, p)$ be the minimum number of red points. We observed:

- For $n=3, p=1$, $f(3,1) = 3$.
- For $n=4, p=0$, $f(4,0) = 1$.
- For $n=4, p=2$, $f(4,2) = 6$.

However, this counts vertices on the "boundary" of the level $p$ set. The problem specifies the segment $OX$. If we place $O$ in a region such that it is "very far" from the center of the arrangement, the red points are exactly those of level $\le p$ relative to the direction $\vec{OX}$. 
The minimum number of vertices of level $\le p$ for an arrangement of $n$ lines is known to be $n-1$ for $p=0$ (if all lines are on the envelope), but we found it can be 1. The discrepancy is that $O$ is a finite point.

For a finite point $O$, we can choose the lines and $O$ such that the region $R_O$ is a "corner" region. In this configuration, the red points are those $X_{ij}$ such that $O$ and $X_{ij}$ are separated by $\le p$ lines. By choosing lines that all pass "above" $O$ and intersect each other in a specific order, the number of red points is $\binom{p+1}{2} + (n-p-1)(p+1) - \binom{p+1}{2} = (n-p-1)(p+1)$. The minimum number of vertices of a region in an arrangement of $n$ lines is 1. For $p>0$, we are looking for vertices of regions that are "close" to $R_O$.
