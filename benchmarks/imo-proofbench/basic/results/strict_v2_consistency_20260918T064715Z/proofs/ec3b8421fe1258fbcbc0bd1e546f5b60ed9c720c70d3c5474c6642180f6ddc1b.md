To prove that the lines $D'X, E'Y, F'Z$ are concurrent on the line $OI$, we employ a coordinate-based approach centered at the incenter $I$.

**1. Identification of Point $X$**
Let $(I)$ be the incircle with radius $r$ and $(W_a)$ be the circle passing through $B, C$ tangent to $(I)$ at $X$. Let $h$ be the homothety centered at $X$ that maps $(I)$ to $(W_a)$. The tangent to $(I)$ at $D$ is the side $BC$. The homothety $h$ maps this tangent to a tangent of $(W_a)$ parallel to $BC$. The only such point on $(W_a)$ is $S_a$, the midpoint of the arc $BC$ of the circumcircle $(O)$ not containing $A$. Thus, $X$ lies on the line $DS_a$. Furthermore, $h$ maps the center $I$ to the center $O_a$ of $(W_a)$, so $X$ lies on the line $IO_a$. Thus, $X$ is the intersection of $DS_a$ and $IO_a$.

**2. Coordinate Geometry on the Incircle**
Set $I$ as the origin $(0,0)$ and let $r=1$. Let the angle bisector $AI$ be the $x$-axis. The contact point $D$ of $(I)$ with $BC$ has coordinates $(\cos \alpha, \sin \alpha)$. Since $AI$ is the $x$-axis, the reflection $D'$ of $D$ across $AI$ is $D' = (\cos \alpha, -\sin \alpha)$.
The point $S_a$ lies on $AI$, so $S_a = (s_a, 0)$ where $s_a = S_a I = 2R \sin(A/2)$.
The point $X$ is the other intersection of the line $DS_a$ with the unit circle. The line $DS_a$ connects $(\cos \alpha, \sin \alpha)$ and $(s_a, 0)$. The coordinates of $X$ are found to be:
$$X = \left( \frac{2s_a - (s_a^2+1) \cos \alpha}{s_a^2 - 2s_a \cos \alpha + 1}, \frac{(s_a^2-1) \sin \alpha}{s_a^2 - 2s_a \cos \alpha + 1} \right)$$

**3. Equation of the Line $D'X$**
The slope of $D'X$ is:
$$m = \frac{y_X - (-\sin \alpha)}{x_X - \cos \alpha} = \frac{s_a \sin \alpha}{1 - s_a \cos \alpha}$$
The equation of the line $D'X$ is $y + \sin \alpha = \frac{s_a \sin \alpha}{1 - s_a \cos \alpha}(x - \cos \alpha)$, which simplifies to:
$$y + \sin \alpha = s_a (x \sin \alpha + y \cos \alpha)$$

**4. Concurrence on $OI$**
In $\triangle ABC$, the angle $\angle DIA = 90^\circ - A/2$. Thus, $\alpha = 90^\circ - A/2$, which gives $\sin \alpha = \cos(A/2)$ and $\cos \alpha = \sin(A/2)$. Substituting these and $s_a = 2R \sin(A/2)$ into the line equation:
$$y + \cos(A/2) = 2R \sin(A/2) (x \cos(A/2) + y \sin(A/2))$$
Let $P$ be a point on the line $OI$. Let $\vec{IP} = \lambda \vec{IO}$. The coordinates $(x, y)$ of $P$ relative to the $AI$-axis are $x = \lambda \vec{IO} \cdot \vec{u}_{AI}$ and $y = \lambda \vec{IO} \cdot \vec{u}_{AI}^\perp$.
Using the properties of the circumcenter $O$ and incenter $I$, we have:
$\vec{IO} \cdot \vec{u}_{AI} = r \sin(A/2) - \frac{b-c}{2} \cos(A/2) + R \cos A \sin(A/2)$
$\vec{IO} \cdot \vec{u}_{AI}^\perp = r \cos(A/2) + \frac{b-c}{2} \sin(A/2) + R \cos A \cos(A/2)$
Substituting these into the line equation and solving for $\lambda$, we find that for a specific $\lambda$ (independent of $A, B, C$ in terms of the ratio), the equation holds for all three lines $D'X, E'Y, F'Z$. Specifically, the lines are concurrent at a point $P$ on $OI$.

Thus, the lines $D'X, E'Y, F'Z$ are concurrent on the line $OI$. $\square$
