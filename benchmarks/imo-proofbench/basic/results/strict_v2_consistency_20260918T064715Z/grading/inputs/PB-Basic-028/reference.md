Consider the inversion centered at $A$ with power $\sqrt{\frac{1}{2} AE \times AC}$. Under
 this inversion, the Euler circle remains unchanged, while $B$ and
 $C$ are mapped to $B'$ and $C'$, which are the midpoints of $AF$
 and $AE$, respectively.


 Observe that if $AXI'Y$ is a rhombus, then $(W)$ is the image of
 the mixtilinear incircle of $\triangle AEF$ under a homothety centered
 at $A$ with ratio $\frac{1}{2}$. The $A$-excircle of $\triangle ABC$,
 which is tangent to $AB,AC$, and $BC$, is mapped to the circle $(V)$,
 which is tangent to $AB,AC$, and $(AB'C')$, i.e., the mixtilinear
 incircle of $\triangle AEF$.


 It remains to prove that $(V)$ is tangent to the Euler circle, which
 would imply that $(V)$ coincides with $(W)$. Indeed, by Feuerbach's
 theorem, the $A$-excircle is externally tangent to $(E)$. Thus,
 under the inversion, $(V)$ is also tangent to $(E)$, which implies
 that $(V)$ coincides with $(W)$, as desired.
