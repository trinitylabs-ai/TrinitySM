Lemma: Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle with circumcircle $\Omega$.
 Let $B_{0}$ be the midpoint of $AC$ and let $C_{0}$ be the midpoint
 of $AB$. Let $D$ be the foot of the altitude from $A$ and let $G$
 be the centroid of the triangle $ABC$. Let $\omega$ be a circle
 through $B_{0}$ and $C_{0}$ that is tangent to the circle $\Omega$
 at a point $X\not=A$. Prove that the points $D,G$ and $X$ are collinear.

 This is the well-known IMO Shortlist 2011 G4 problem, we omit the
 proof here.

 Consider an inversion center $I$ with power $r^{2}$ where $r$ is
 the inradius. This maps $B,C$ to the midpoint $M_{B},M_{C}$ of $DF,DE$
 respectively. Moreover, we have $DD'$ is parallel to $EF$. Let $G$
 be the centroid of $\triangle DEF$ and $H_{D}$ be the foot of $D$
 onto $EF$. We have $D',G,H_{D}$ are collinear from Thales Theorem,
 and $X,G,H_{D}$ are collinear. And so, we have $XD'$ passes through
 $G$. Similarly, we have $YE',ZF'$ passes through $G$ as well. Finally,
 it is well-known that $I,G,O$ are collinear. And so, we have $D'X,E'Y,F'Z$
 are concurrent on the line $OI$.
