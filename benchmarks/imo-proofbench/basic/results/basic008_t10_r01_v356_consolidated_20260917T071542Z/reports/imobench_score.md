The substitution and Lagrange-multiplier calculations are correct. The argument that every interior critical point has at least two equal coordinates is valid, and—accepting the exact root-classification lemma—the one-variable analysis correctly shows that all such interior minima have nonnegative value, with equality at \(x=y=z=1/\sqrt3\).

However, the boundary analysis is incomplete. When, for example, \(z=0\), the boundary consists of all pairs satisfying \(x^2+y^2=1\), not merely the two endpoint configurations arising from the slice \(x=y\). Thus the assertion that the endpoints of \(g\) and their symmetric counterparts cover the whole boundary is false.

This is a relatively small, repairable gap. Indeed, on \(z=0\), put \(u=xy\in[0,\tfrac12]\). Then
\[
F(x,y,0)=\sqrt{1+2u}-3\sqrt3\,u^2.
\]
Its positivity follows by squaring, since
\[
1+2u-27u^4>0
\]
on \([0,\tfrac12]\): this function is concave, and its endpoint values are \(1\) and \(5/16\). The other boundary faces follow by symmetry.

Thus the proof has the correct core and needs only a short missing boundary justification, warranting almost full credit.
