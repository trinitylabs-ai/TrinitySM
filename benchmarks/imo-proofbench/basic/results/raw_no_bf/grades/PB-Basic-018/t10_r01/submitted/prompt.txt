**PROBLEM STATEMENT**

Let $x$ and $y$ be positive integers satisfying $2x^2 + x = 3y^2 + y$. Prove that $2x+2y+1$ is a perfect square.

**GROUND-TRUTH SOLUTION**

Lemma: If $ab$ is a perfect square for coprime integers $a,b$ then they are perfect squares.

 Observe that $2x^2+x=3y^2+y$ is equivalent to $y^2 = 2x^2-2y^2+x-y = (x-y)(2x+2y+1)$. Let $d$ be the greatest common divisior of $x-y,2x+2y+1$, we have $d | x-y$ and $d | 2x+2y+1$. On the other hand, observe that $y^2 = (x-y)(2x+2y+1)$. Hence, we must have that $d^2 | y^2$, which implies $d | y$. Since $d | y$ and $d | x-y$, we have $d | x$. Furthermore, since $d | x, d | y, d | 2x+2y+1$, we have that $d | 1$ and thus, $d = 1$, meaning that $x-y,2x+2y+1$ are coprime integers. From the Lemma, we have that $x-y,2x+2y+1$ are perfect squares. Hence proved.

The given equation is $2x^2 + x - 3y^2 - y = 0$. To eliminate the linear terms and create squares, we multiply the entire equation by 24:

We complete the squares for $x$ and $y$:

$$3(4x + 1)^2 - 3 - 2(6y + 1)^2 + 2 = 0$$

Let $A = 4x + 1$ and $B = 6y + 1$. The equation becomes:
$$3A^2 - 2B^2 = 1$$
Since $x, y$ are positive integers, $A$ and $B$ must be positive integers such that $A \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ and $B \equiv 1 \pmod 6$.

**2. Solving the Pell-like Equation**
The equation $3A^2 - 2B^2 = 1$ is a Pell-like equation. The fundamental solution is $(A_0, B_0) = (1, 1)$. All solutions $(A_n, B_n)$ are generated by the relation:
$$A_n\sqrt{3} + B_n\sqrt{2} = (1\sqrt{3} + 1\sqrt{2})(5 + 2\sqrt{6})^n$$
The recurrence relations for $A_n$ and $B_n$ are:
$$A_n = 5A_{n-1} + 4B_{n-1}$$
$$B_n = 6A_{n-1} + 5B_{n-1}$$
Checking the constraints on $n$:
- $A_n \equiv A_{n-1} \pmod 4$, and since $A_0 = 1$, $A_n \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ for all $n$.
- $B_n \equiv -B_{n-1} \pmod 6$. Since $B_0 = 1$, $B_n \equiv 1 \pmod 6$ if and only if $n$ is even.
Thus, for $y$ to be an integer, we must have $n = 2m$ for some integer $m \ge 1$ (since $x, y > 0$ excludes $n=0$).

**3. Evaluating the Expression $2x + 2y + 1$**
Expressing $2x + 2y + 1$ in terms of $A_n$ and $B_n$:
$$x = \frac{A_n - 1}{4}, \quad y = \frac{B_n - 1}{6}$$
$$2x + 2y + 1 = 2\left(\frac{A_n - 1}{4}\right) + 2\left(\frac{B_n - 1}{6}\right) + 1 = \frac{3(A_n - 1) + 2(B_n - 1) + 6}{6} = \frac{3A_n + 2B_n + 1}{6}$$
Let $S_n = \frac{3A_n + 2B_n + 1}{6}$. We use the closed forms for $A_n, B_n$ with $\alpha = 5 + 2\sqrt{6}$ and $\beta = 5 - 2\sqrt{6}$:
$$A_n = \frac{3+\sqrt{6}}{6}\alpha^n + \frac{3-\sqrt{6}}{6}\beta^n, \quad B_n = \frac{2+\sqrt{6}}{4}\alpha^n + \frac{2-\sqrt{6}}{4}\beta^n$$
Calculating $3A_n + 2B_n$:
$$3A_n + 2B_n = \left(\frac{3+\sqrt{6}}{2} + \frac{2+\sqrt{6}}{2}\right)\alpha^n + \left(\frac{3-\sqrt{6}}{2} + \frac{2-\sqrt{6}}{2}\right)\beta^n = \frac{5+2\sqrt{6}}{2}\alpha^n + \frac{5-2\sqrt{6}}{2}\beta^n = \frac{\alpha^{n+1} + \beta^{n+1}}{2}$$
