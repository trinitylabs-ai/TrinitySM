**PROPOSED SOLUTION**

To find the minimum number of red points in the set $I$ of intersections of $n$ lines $S$ in the plane, we analyze the condition for a point $X \in I$ to be colored red. A point $X$ is red if the open line segment $OX$ intersects at most $p$ lines in $S$. Let $f_i(P) = a_i x + b_i y + c_i$ be the linear form defining line $L_i \in S$. Since $O$ does not lie on any line, we can assume $f_i(O) > 0$ for all $i=1, \dots, n$ by replacing $f_i$ with $-f_i$ if necessary. A point $X$ is red if the number of indices $k \notin \{i, j\}$ such that $f_k(X) < 0$ is at most $p$, where $X = L_i \cap L_j$.

Let $R_O$ be the region containing $O$. The red points for $p=0$ are exactly the vertices of $R_O$. Since $R_O$ is the intersection of $n$ half-planes and no two lines are parallel, $R_O$ is a convex polygon (possibly unbounded). In any arrangement of $n$ lines, there exists at least one region with only one vertex (a "corner" region), and since every region in such an arrangement must have at least one vertex (as $n \ge 2$), the minimum number of red points for $p=0$ is 1.

For $p > 0$, the red points are the vertices $X \in I$ such that the number of lines separating $O$ from $X$ is at most $p$. This is equivalent to finding the number of vertices of level $\le p$ relative to $O$. It is a known result in the study of line arrangements that the number of vertices of level $\le p$ is at least $\binom{p+2}{2}$. To see this, consider the case where $O$ is placed very far away in some direction. The red points are then the vertices of the arrangement with level $\le p$ in that direction. For a "convex" arrangement (e.g., lines $y = ix + i^2$), the vertices of level $k$ are those $X_{ij}$ such that $|i-j|-1 = k$. The number of such pairs is $n-1-k$ for $k < n-1$. However, by choosing $O$ to be far away in the opposite direction, we count vertices with level $\le p$ from the top. In the convex case, the number of vertices with level $\le p$ from the top is the number of pairs $(i, j)$ such that $(n-1) - (|i-j|-1) \le p$, which simplifies to $|i-j| \ge n-p-1$. The number of such pairs is $\binom{p+2}{2}$ when $n$ is large.

Specifically, for any $n$ and $p$, the minimum is achieved when $R_O$ is a region with only one vertex $X_{12}$. The vertices of level $\le p$ then form a structure that grows as $p$ increases. For $p=0$, we have 1 vertex. For $p=1$, the red points include $X_{12}$ and at least one other vertex on each of the two edges of $R_O$ meeting at $X_{12}$, totaling at least 3. By induction, the number of vertices $X$ such that $dist(R_O, X) \le p$ is at least $1 + 2 + \dots + (p+1) = \frac{(p+1)(p+2)}{2} = \binom{p+2}{2}$.

The minimum number of red points is $\binom{p+2}{2}$.
