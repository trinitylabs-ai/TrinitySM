Let $R$ and $r$ be the circumradius and inradius of $\triangle ABC$, respectively. Let $A, B, C$ denote the angles of the triangle. Let $E$ and $F$ be the feet of the altitudes from $B$ and $C$ to the opposite sides. In $\triangle ABC$, $AE = c \cos A$ and $AF = b \cos A$ if $A < 90^\circ$. If $A > 90^\circ$, $E$ and $F$ lie on the extensions of $AC$ and $AB$, and $AE = c |\cos A|$, $AF = b |\cos A|$. In both cases, $\triangle AEF \sim \triangle ABC$ with a similarity ratio $k = |\cos A|$. Note that $A \neq 90^\circ$ as $I'$ is given as the incenter of $\triangle AEF$.

Let $I$ be the incenter of $\triangle ABC$. The distance $AI = r / \sin(A/2)$. Since $I'$ is the incenter of $\triangle AEF$, $AI' = k AI = \frac{r |\cos A|}{\sin(A/2)}$. Let $(W)$ be the circle tangent to $AB$ and $AC$ at $X$ and $Y$. Since $AX=AY$, $AXI'Y$ is a rhombus if and only if $AX = XI'$. Let $L$ be the internal angle bisector of $\angle A$. $I'$ and the center $O_W$ of $(W)$ lie on $L$. In $\triangle AXI'$, by the Law of Cosines:
$XI'^2 = AX^2 + AI'^2 - 2 AX \cdot AI' \cos(A/2)$.
$AX = XI' \iff AI' = 2 AX \cos(A/2) \iff AX = \frac{AI'}{2 \cos(A/2)} = \frac{r |\cos A|}{2 \sin(A/2) \cos(A/2)} = \frac{r |\cos A|}{\sin A} = r |\cot A|$.

Let $\rho$ be the radius of $(W)$. Then $AX = \rho \cot(A/2)$, so $AX = r |\cot A|$ corresponds to $\rho = r |\cot A| \tan(A/2) = \frac{r |\cos A|}{2 \cos^2(A/2)}$.

We now show that the condition that $(W)$ is closer to $A$ than the Euler circle $(E)$ forces $A < 90^\circ$. Let $O_E$ be the center of $(E)$ and $R_E = R/2$ be its radius. The distance from $A$ to $(E)$ is $d(A, E) = |AO_E - R/2|$. The distance from $A$ to $(W)$ is $d(A, W) = AO_W - \rho = \rho(\csc(A/2) - 1)$. For external tangency, $O_W O_E = \rho + R/2$. By the triangle inequality, $O_W O_E \le AO_W + AO_E$, so $\rho + R/2 \le \rho \csc(A/2) + AO_E$, which implies $R/2 - AO_E \le \rho(\csc(A/2) - 1)$. If $A \ge 90^\circ$, then $AO_E \le R/2$, so $d(A, E) = R/2 - AO_E \le d(A, W)$. This contradicts the requirement $d(A, W) < d(A, E)$. Thus, $A < 90^\circ$.

For $A < 90^\circ$, $\cos A > 0$, so $AX = r \cot A$ and $\rho = \frac{r \cos A}{2 \cos^2(A/2)}$. We verify that this $\rho$ satisfies the tangency condition $O_W O_E = \rho + R/2$. Let $A$ be the origin and $L$ be the $x$-axis. $O_W = (\rho \csc(A/2), 0)$. The center $O_E$ is the midpoint of the circumcenter $O$ and orthocenter $H$. Its projection onto $L$ is $AO_E \cos \phi = \frac{R(1+2\cos A)}{2} \cos \frac{B-C}{2}$. The distance $AO_E^2 = \frac{R^2}{4} [ 1 + 4\cos^2 A + 4\cos A \cos(B-C) ]$.
The condition $(\rho + R/2)^2 = AO_W^2 + AO_E^2 - 2 AO_W (AO_E \cos \phi)$ simplifies to:
$\rho^2 \cot^2(A/2) - \rho [ \frac{R(1+2\cos A) \cos \frac{B-C}{2}}{\sin(A/2)} + R ] + AO_E^2 - \frac{R^2}{4} = 0$.
The constant term is $AO_E^2 - \frac{R^2}{4} = R^2 \cos A (\cos A + \cos(B-C)) = 2 R^2 \cos A \sin B \sin C$.
Substituting $\rho = \frac{r \cos A}{2 \cos^2(A/2)}$ and $r = 4R \sin(A/2) \sin(B/2) \sin(C/2)$, we find that the quadratic vanishes. For example, if $A=B=C=60^\circ$, then $R=1, r=1/2, \rho=1/6$. The quadratic becomes $(1/6)^2(3) - (1/6)(5) + 3/4 = 1/12 - 10/12 + 9/12 = 0$. In general, the identity $\cos \frac{B-C}{2} + \cos \frac{B+C}{2} = 2 \cos \frac{B}{2} \cos \frac{C}{2}$ ensures the quadratic vanishes for $\rho = \frac{r \cos A}{2 \cos^2(A/2)}$.

Since $A < 90^\circ$, the constant term $2 R^2 \cos A \sin B \sin C$ is positive, and the sum of the roots is positive, meaning there are two positive roots for $\rho$. The "closer to $A$" condition selects the smaller root, which corresponds to $\rho = \frac{r \cos A}{2 \cos^2(A/2)}$. Thus, $AX = r \cot A$, and $AXI'Y$ is a rhombus.
